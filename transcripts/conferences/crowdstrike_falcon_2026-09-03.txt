CrowdStrike (CRWD) used Thursday, 03 September 2026, at Fal.Con Day 3 to outline a broad shift toward AI-driven cybersecurity, pairing new research tools with major platform changes. The company presented a strong innovation story, but it also described a threat environment in which attacks are faster, cheaper and harder to stop.

The keynote focused on how CrowdStrike aims to defend against that shift through frontier AI models, endpoint-level inference and a redesigned sensor architecture. The stock rose 4.4% to $212.37, near the top of its 52-week range of $85.68 to $233.88. The shares have surged nearly 97% over the past year and 108% over the last six months, though InvestingPro analysis indicates the stock is currently overvalued relative to its Fair Value&mdash;placing it among companies on the Most Overvalued list.

The keynote did not include revenue, profit or other earnings metrics. CrowdStrike kept the presentation focused on product development and technical strategy rather than financial performance.

Bartley Richardson, CrowdStrike&rsquo;s Chief AI and Autonomous Systems Officer, introduced what the company called the world&rsquo;s first Cyber Superintelligence Lab. The lab is designed to build frontier AI models for cybersecurity only, rather than general-purpose AI.

Richardson said the effort reflects a simple idea: &ldquo;Focus is a superpower.&rdquo; He added that CrowdStrike wants to answer a central question for defenders: what gives them back an &ldquo;insurmountable advantage&rdquo; against attackers.

CrowdStrike said its AI work is built on the scale of its Falcon platform data. The company argued that the size and quality of its telemetry give it an edge over rivals that rely on smaller datasets or more general AI tools.

Richardson said the company&rsquo;s goal is to turn open-source AI models into specialized security tools that can be used in production.

SafeMind is built around three parts: an offensive model called Red Tempest, a defensive model called Blue Solano and a cyber environment that acts as a digital twin. CrowdStrike said the system is designed for rigorous evaluation, transparency and trust.

The company described the setup as a form of adversarial co-evolution. Offensive and defensive agents are tested against each other, and the results are used to harden both sides.

Red Tempest is CrowdStrike&rsquo;s offensive AI model. The company said it is a 27 billion parameter dense model with a 256K context window, expandable to 1 million for longer tasks.

Blue Solano is the defensive side of SafeMind. CrowdStrike said it is based on a post-trained Nemotron-3 Super 128B parameter mixture-of-experts model, with 12 billion active parameters.

The model uses a Nemotron 3 Ultra reasoning orchestrator with a context window of up to 1 million. CrowdStrike said it was trained with supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, using 3.1 million working hours of expertise from detection engineers.

CrowdStrike said it has built a large testing environment to validate SafeMind. The company emphasized that the models are being tested against real telemetry and validated adversarial objectives, not only synthetic examples.

Alex Ionescu, CrowdStrike&rsquo;s chief technology innovation officer, described a major redesign of the company&rsquo;s endpoint architecture. The changes are meant to make the platform more flexible, more modern and less dependent on kernel-level code.

CrowdStrike said it is working with Microsoft on the Windows Endpoint Security Platform, or WESP, which is in private preview and expected to reach general availability soon. The goal is to provide kernel-equivalent protection from user mode.

Ionescu said, &ldquo;With WESP, just like with eBPF and the macOS Endpoint Security Framework, you can be in user mode and still be the one deciding what gets into the kernel.&rdquo;

Ascent is CrowdStrike&rsquo;s effort to modernize the sensor&rsquo;s internal design. The company said it is replacing custom systems with standard tools and protocols.

The company described the shift as moving from &ldquo;bespoke by necessity&rdquo; to &ldquo;standards by choice.&rdquo;

Modularity is meant to separate the sensor&rsquo;s internal modules so they can move on independent schedules. CrowdStrike said this will let customers deploy only the capabilities they need.

CrowdStrike also discussed local inference on the endpoint, which it said is generally available in its latest Windows 8.10 sensor. The company framed the endpoint as a reasoning machine that can make decisions at inference speed.

Ionescu said, &ldquo;The endpoint is reigning again. Now, the endpoint is also reasoning at inference speed.&rdquo;

CrowdStrike repeatedly returned to the changing economics of cybercrime. The company said attackers now have access to more tools, more automation and faster tradecraft sharing than before.

Richardson said, &ldquo;We just commoditized defense. Defense is commoditized.&rdquo; The company&rsquo;s broader message was that defenders now need systems that can match the speed and scale of AI-driven attacks.

CrowdStrike said several products and capabilities were already generally available, while others are moving toward release.

Michael Sentonas closed the keynote by pointing to the week&rsquo;s announcements and the company&rsquo;s focus on continuous innovation. CrowdStrike said more than 10,000 people attended Fal.Con 2026, and it plans to return with Fal.Con 2027.
