moderator, Morgan Stanley: Kind of following up on those acquisitions you mentioned, especially looking at Modular and Fibrebond, I guess when you’re looking at your share of RFPs and how those are moving to prefab and modular, how do these acquisitions change your competitive positioning?

Paulo Ruiz, CEO, Eaton: Modularization is a clear trend in the industry, especially in data centers. Not only, but especially in data centers. The big question is why is it so important to operators? There are three main reasons. The first one, everyone knows about labor scarcity for plumbers and electricians. As much as we can move that activity away from stick-built construction in the data center to a factory environment, we deal with that constraint. That’s one of the key value propositions of Modular. The second one is speed. Even if you have all those electricians and those plumbers, it will take 18 months for you to build what you need to do in a data center. A module can be built in 9 months, so it’s half the time. The third one, some operators decide to use, some don’t, but it’s an extra bonus here.

When you have all this equipment put into one module, delivered as one box or one container, you can decide to put this outside the building and use most of the space inside the building for servers, where the data center operators generate revenue. They can decide to put this underground. There are many other options, but the three measures are important, and this is part of the menu of our customers using modular solutions. To give you a proof point, because this is just a theory, but the proof point, when we acquired Fibrebond, their fiscal year, they produced $375 million in revenue, the 12 months prior acquisition. The 12 months after acquisition were over $600 million, so from $375 million to $600 million.

We had in our investment thesis an expansion of their existing plant, and the build-out of a new plant was already in the works when we acquired the company. That is what we put in our financial model. That happened very quickly because they are already working on the expansion of existing site, and we just announced the build-out of a new factory in Arkansas. Think about doubling the capacity once in their existing site they operate today and doubling again with a new site. There is a lot of traction there. That is a proof point. Look outside the U.S. to see similar trends for modular, and this is why we ended up having this partnership, a joint venture with NordicEPOD AS in Europe. It is a clear trend, and it is growing.
