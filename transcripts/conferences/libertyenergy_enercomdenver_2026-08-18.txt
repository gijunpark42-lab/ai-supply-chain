On Tuesday, 18 August 2026, Liberty Energy (LBRT) used the EnerCom Denver &ndash; The Energy Investment Conference to outline a bigger ambition: move beyond hydraulic fracturing and become a broader energy company tied to power generation, artificial intelligence and long-term global fuel demand. The message was upbeat on growth, but it also acknowledged a tougher reality for the legacy oilfield services business, where efficiency gains mean the company must keep expanding just to hold its ground.

The stock fell 3.82% to $21.39 from a previous close of $22.24, leaving shares well below the 52-week high of $34.48 but above the low of $9.9. Despite the recent pullback, shares have delivered a strong 107% return over the past year. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock currently trades slightly above its Fair Value of $20.24, placing it on the Most Overvalued list.

In his keynote, President and Chief Executive Officer Ron Gusek framed Liberty&rsquo;s strategy around a simple view of the global energy market: demand keeps growing, and no single new source is replacing oil, gas and coal fast enough to change that.

Gusek also tied energy use to human development. He said the &ldquo;lucky one billion people&rdquo; in Western Europe, North America, Japan, South Korea, Australia and New Zealand consume about 13 barrels of oil per person each year, while the other 7 billion people consume about 3 barrels per person.

He used South Korea and China as examples of rising demand tied to economic growth:

He argued that this math supports a &ldquo;massive&rdquo; long-term outlook for oil and natural gas.

Liberty did not present a quarterly earnings update at the conference, but Gusek highlighted long-term operating metrics that he said show the company&rsquo;s scale and resilience.

Gusek also said the broader industry has become much more efficient since Liberty was founded in May 2011 by Chris and Jim Brady. At that time, North America had about 450 frac crews and 1,800 drilling rigs. Today, he said, record oil and gas production is being delivered with about 200 frac crews and roughly 600 rigs.

That productivity is good for the industry, he said, but it also creates a challenge for a service company that wants to keep growing. &ldquo;Grow to stay flat&rdquo; was the implication: Liberty needs new businesses because the old one is becoming more efficient.

Liberty marked its 15th anniversary in 2024 and said it has grown from a startup into a 6,000-person company. Gusek said the original goal was simple: &ldquo;to build the best damn frack company, period.&rdquo; He said that has now evolved into a broader goal: &ldquo;to be the best damn energy company, period.&rdquo;
