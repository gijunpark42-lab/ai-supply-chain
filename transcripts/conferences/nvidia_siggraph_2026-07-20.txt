On Monday, 20 July 2026, NVIDIA (NVDA) used its SIGGRAPH 2026 Keynote to present a broad push into AI-driven graphics, physics simulation and world models. The company highlighted strong technical progress and new product releases, while also underscoring the challenge of keeping artistic control, real-time performance and physical accuracy in balance.

Jensen Huang opened the keynote by placing the presentation in the context of NVIDIA&rsquo;s 30-year history in computer graphics. He pointed to milestones such as the programmable GPU in 2001, CUDA in 2006 and RTX in 2018. His message was that graphics is no longer only about visual quality. It is also becoming a core tool for training AI systems that must understand and act in the physical world.

Across all three areas, NVIDIA argued that AI should extend existing graphics and simulation methods rather than replace them. The company said the goal is to improve realism, speed and control at the same time.

NVIDIA described DLSS 5 as a new stage in real-time graphics. The company said it adds a third category of AI use in rendering: generation. That sits alongside reconstruction and function approximation, which are already used in graphics pipelines.

According to NVIDIA, DLSS 5 combines traditional physics-based rendering with learned generative models to produce photorealistic images in real time. It does not change the geometry of the render frame. Instead, it enhances appearance while keeping the underlying frame structure intact.

The company said the system uses renderer internal buffers such as albedos, surface normals and lighting information to stay close to the creator&rsquo;s intent. It also uses motion vectors and causal frame-by-frame processing to reduce shimmer, drifting and swimming artifacts.

The company said DLSS 5 improves subsurface scattering, ambient occlusion, contact shadows, reflections, translucency and foliage realism. It also said the system is distilled from larger foundation models into a compact one-step pixel-space diffusion transformer. DLSS 5 is scheduled to ship in fall 2026.

Edward Liu also framed the technical challenge in artistic terms. He said, &ldquo;DLSS 5 must change the image in a way that it does not change the story.&rdquo; He added that the model should not decide what the game should look like; that choice belongs to the creators.

Neil Ashton presented NVIDIA&rsquo;s work on using AI to speed up physics simulation. He said the approach can reduce the cost of large simulations by orders of magnitude while keeping useful accuracy.

One example involved aircraft aerodynamics. NVIDIA said a dataset of 2,000 aircraft simulations with more than 1 billion degrees of freedom produced 200 terabytes of data. The AI model compressed that into a 200 megabyte checkpoint, a reduction of about 10,000x. The company said the model still achieved 1% to 2% accuracy on unseen geometries and boundary conditions.

Ashton said the broader point is that AI can act as a surrogate for expensive simulations, producing answers in seconds on modest GPU hardware such as an RTX 6000 instead of taking hours or days on traditional systems. He called the compression &ldquo;remarkable&rdquo; and said, &ldquo;you have around a million times compression of that 200 terabytes down to 200 megabytes.&rdquo;
