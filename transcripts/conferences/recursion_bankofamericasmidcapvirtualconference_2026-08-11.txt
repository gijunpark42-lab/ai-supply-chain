On Tuesday, 11 August 2026, Recursion Pharmaceuticals (RXRX) used the Bank of America SMID Cap Virtual Conference to present a familiar but still unproven message: artificial intelligence can reshape drug discovery if it is paired with proprietary data and clinical execution. Management pointed to progress in its pipeline and partnerships, but also to the long road ahead before the platform can be fully judged.

Ben Taylor, Recursion&rsquo;s chief financial officer and president, said the company sees AI less as a buzzword and more as a better analytical system for biology. He compared it to the arrival of computers and spreadsheets in business analysis.

Taylor said the company is not trying to use AI only to speed up old processes. Instead, it is trying to change the odds of success in drug development. He said approved drugs cover only about 3.5% of the genome, while drugs in development would bring coverage to only about 13%.

Recursion emphasized that its partnerships are structured to reduce cash burn and share risk with larger pharmaceutical companies.

Management said the business is designed to operate at breakeven or better on a direct cost basis because partners pay expenses in advance. That structure, the company said, helps keep the platform capital efficient while preserving upside through milestones and royalties.

Recursion spent much of the presentation on REC-4881, its lead asset in familial adenomatous polyposis, a rare disease marked by recurring polyp growth throughout the gastrointestinal tract.

Recursion said it is in discussions with the Food and Drug Administration on pivotal trial design. The company also said it built a large language model using more than 250,000 patient records to establish a natural history baseline and standard clinical practice. It added that a Dutch university database tracking FAP patients for 20 years shows continuous annual polyp growth averaging more than 50%.

The company plans to present longer follow-up data at a FAP-focused conference in November, including more detail on side-effect management.

Beyond FAP, Recursion highlighted several oncology programs and a neuroscience milestone with Roche.

On the Roche neuroscience collaboration, the company said the first target selected from its biology maps has moved from the query stage to the design stage. Management described the target as novel and not previously known in the literature. It said the target was experimentally validated in disease models before molecule design began.

Recursion framed that milestone as evidence that its platform can uncover new biology. Management noted that approved drugs cover only a small portion of the genome, leaving most of it unexplored.

Management repeatedly stressed that the company&rsquo;s edge is not a generic AI model. It said the real advantage comes from proprietary data, compute power and the ability to generate new data through experiments.

The same conference also featured Upstart Holdings, whose chief executive, Paul Gu, described a similar thesis in a very different market. He said AI has not been fully applied to consumer lending, where roughly half of Americans are underserved because of cost, approval rates or speed.

Upstart described its second quarter as a period of inflection, with record contribution profit despite a difficult macro environment.

Gu said the company is now focused on proving that its core personal loans business can keep growing without structural margin pressure, while new products move toward profitability. He said the company is guiding to 35% compounded annual growth over the coming years.

Upstart said it has shifted its home and auto products from a growth-first approach to a unit-economics-first approach.

Gu said the bank should allow the company to expand into more states and increase offer volume. He added that the company believes it has enough capital to support the launch.

Upstart said macro conditions remain a headwind, especially through its UMI unemployment proxy, which moved toward the top end of its guidance range of 1.4 to 1.5.

Gu said the market is more skeptical of the business than management is. He said investors still want proof on operating expenses, equity capital needs, the bank charter and whether the profitability improvement can last beyond one quarter.

He also said the company is using stock buybacks when the expected internal rate of return is higher than other uses of capital, including convertible bond repurchases.

Questions from analysts and investors focused on how each company defines its edge and how it plans to prove it.

For Recursion, the next set of catalysts will come from clinical data, FDA discussions and additional partnership milestones. The company expects more updates on REC-4881, RBM39 and PI3K in coming quarters, along with further evidence that its platform can find new targets and create differentiated medicines.

For Upstart, the focus is on proving that the second leg of the business can be as durable as the first: faster growth in core lending, profitability in secured products, progress on the cash line, and an eventual bank launch in early 2027.
