Tuesday, 11 August 2026 &mdash; Lattice Semiconductor (LSCC) used the Technology Leadership Forum 2026 to outline a stronger role in data center and AI infrastructure, while also pointing to supply chain constraints and a still-developing physical AI opportunity. Chief Financial Officer Lorenzo Flores said the company is benefiting from rising server volumes, higher attach rates and richer product mix, even as it works to scale production and integrate its AMI acquisition.

Flores said Lattice&rsquo;s communications and compute business posted growth of more than 80% in the most recent quarter. He described the company&rsquo;s current expansion as the result of several forces working together:

He said AI now represents the high 20% range of Lattice&rsquo;s overall business, with even greater penetration in the compute segment. The company also said the broader server market has expanded from an estimate of 15 million units at the start of the year, with a 20% AI mix, to 20 million units in the latest three-week estimates, with higher AI penetration.

Flores said the FPGA business, which generated about $500 million last year, is expected to end this year at roughly twice that level. He added that the company expects growth to continue next year.

Lattice said its customer discussions around the AMI acquisition have broadened across the ecosystem. The company has engaged with about 100 different customer types, including hyperscalers, OEMs, ODMs and partners.

Management said the combined FPGA and AMI offering could create what Flores called a &ldquo;one plus one equals three&rdquo; effect. The goal is to pair programmable logic with advanced firmware and manageability features to address customer needs such as:

Flores said leak detection, for example, has gone from being a non-existent application 18 months ago to a critical use case today, creating a new attach point for the company. Despite the strong growth story, investors should note that Lattice currently trades at a P/E ratio of nearly 498, and InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock is overvalued relative to its Fair Value. The company appears on InvestingPro’s Most Overvalued list, reflecting premium multiples across revenue, EBIT and book value.

On the supply chain side, Lattice said it has reduced lead times from more than a year to about 40 days. The company has also tightened channel inventory to gain better visibility into end-demand and said its book of business has grown in dollar value and in time horizon.

Fab capacity is described as comfortable because Lattice uses older semiconductor nodes, but assembly, test and packaging remain the main bottleneck. The company has invested heavily in tester capacity for final test and customer shipment.

Flores said unit growth should remain the main driver of FPGA expansion over the next three to five years, with ASP gains as a secondary contributor. He said attach rates should keep rising as customers add functions such as leak detection, management and power optimization.

For the AMI business, management sees management capabilities as a larger growth driver than simple footprint expansion. For the combined solution, the company expects higher-value revenue than either component could generate alone.

Flores said the integrated FPGA-AMI offering is not just a packaging exercise, but a greenfield opportunity. The company believes the same customer problem statements can be addressed with much greater value capture through a combined platform.

Management also pointed to several structural reasons it believes FPGA content can remain durable in rack-scale systems:

Flores said the company expects meaningful physical AI revenue to begin in late 2025. He said this market could resemble data center infrastructure in that there may be multiple socket opportunities per instance. Lattice is also expanding its footprint in robotics companies through a software-like programming environment.

Outside data center, the industrial business is recovering. Flores said industrial revenue rose about 20% year over year and should continue growing next year, helped by improving global purchasing manager indexes, except in Japan. He said Lattice has minimal exposure to automotive, which reduces cyclical risk. The company maintains a solid financial foundation, with a current ratio above 3.0 and a gross profit margin of 69%, according to InvestingPro data. For deeper analysis, investors can access Lattice’s comprehensive Pro Research Report, one of 1,400+ available reports that transform complex data into clear, actionable intelligence.

To support the expected growth, the company said it is building supply chain infrastructure for more than 2x FPGA capacity growth.

During questions, Flores said both general-purpose and AI servers are positive for Lattice. He said the company benefits from a &ldquo;multiplier effect&rdquo; as unit volumes rise, attach rates expand and ASPs increase across both server types.

On the sustainability of FPGA content in rack-scale deployments, Flores said Lattice&rsquo;s position differs from older FPGA models because the company is not trying to replace the core processor. Instead, it is focused on areas where ASIC conversion is not economical, such as I/O expansion, management and security.

He also said the company&rsquo;s role is helped by the faster pace of design changes in data centers. As rack designs move from two-year cycles to 18 months and then 12 months, customers need more flexibility, which supports FPGA use.

He confirmed that the FPGA-AMI opportunity is a greenfield one, with the same customer problems but a more integrated solution and higher value capture.

On visibility and lead times, Flores said bookings extend through Q4 2025, but that this reflects visibility rather than a hard capacity limit. He said the company can support additional demand beyond current bookings.

He also clarified that the industrial growth figure refers to year-over-year growth, not sequential growth.
