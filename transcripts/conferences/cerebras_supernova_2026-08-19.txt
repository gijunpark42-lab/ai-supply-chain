Cerebras (CBRS) used its Supernova 2026 conference on Tuesday, 18 August 2026, to argue that speed has become a defining feature of AI products, not just a technical measure. The company paired that message with new hardware, fresh customer examples and a wider partner network, even as its shares fell sharply in regular trading before edging higher after hours.

Andrew Feldman, Cerebras&rsquo;s chief executive and co-founder, said the industry is moving beyond speed as a benchmark and toward speed as a product requirement.

The company&rsquo;s message was that AI systems should not force users to choose between intelligence and responsiveness. Speakers repeatedly said the goal is to make frontier models feel immediate enough for real-time work.

Cerebras unveiled CS-4, its next-generation system architecture built on the Nexus rack-scale platform. The company said the system is designed for hyperscale deployment and future upgrades through 2027.

The company said the CS-4 backpack design uses a vertical modular enclosure with direct vertical power delivery, giving 2 times more power and 2 times more cooling. Cerebras said the design is meant to support future generations of wafer-scale compute, including CS-5 in 2027.

The most visible software announcement was GPT-5.6 Sol Ultrafast mode, a collaboration between Cerebras and OpenAI. The companies said the mode runs OpenAI&rsquo;s most intelligent models at up to 14 times faster than the standard tier.

Feldman said the launch shows a new top end for the market. Referring to a competitive chart, he said, &ldquo;The only thing up here is GPT-5.6-Sol Ultrafast running on Cerebras. In the entire industry, the only thing here.&rdquo;

Cerebras used several customer demonstrations to show how lower latency can change product behavior.

Cerebras also framed its growth around a disaggregated infrastructure model, in which different parts of inference run on different systems.

Cerebras did not present a traditional earnings update at the conference, but it did give several scale and deployment figures that point to its business momentum. The company reported revenue of $680.67 million in the last twelve months as of Q2 2026, representing growth of roughly 91% year-over-year. According to InvestingPro data, 9 analysts have revised their earnings upwards for the upcoming period, suggesting growing confidence in the company’s trajectory despite its current unprofitability.

OpenAI said last week that it has 15 million weekly active users running Codex and ChatGPT agents, while ChatGPT has surpassed 1 billion total users. The company said agentic workflows are still a small part of usage, but they are growing quickly.

Throughout the event, Cerebras contrasted its wafer-scale design with GPU-based systems.

The company said its benchmark result on Humanity&rsquo;s Last Exam showed the same point. Cerebras said it completed the 2,500-question exam in 11 hours, 11 minutes and 26 seconds, less than half a day, while competitors took more than three days.

Cerebras presented a roadmap that extends beyond the CS-4 launch.

Cerebras said the broader market opportunity is not just faster chips, but a new layer of AI infrastructure that can support agents, real-time collaboration and low-latency decision-making.

Cerebras shares closed the regular session at $219.92, down 12.73% from the previous close of $251.98. After hours, the stock rose 0.21% to $220.39.

The stock has traded between $160.81 and $386.34 over the past 52 weeks.

Cerebras used Supernova 2026 to present a simple message: in AI, speed is becoming part of the product itself.
