Wednesday, 09 September 2026 &mdash; Rolls-Royce (RR) used the Jefferies Global Industrials Conference 2026 to present a company that has moved from survival concerns to stronger margins, better operations and new growth options. Chief Executive Tufan Erginbilgi&ccedil; said the turnaround has created both resilience and expansion potential, while also flagging supply chain inflation, execution risk and the need to keep improving.

Erginbilgi&ccedil; said the company&rsquo;s change is broader than a traditional restructuring. He described it as a move from point A to point B, where the business gains new capability, new culture, new mindset and new financial strength.

Erginbilgi&ccedil; also said the company&rsquo;s progress has come from self-help and operational improvement, not just from a stronger market backdrop. He noted that flying hours are only 13% above 2019 levels, so execution has been important.

Rolls-Royce highlighted a sharp improvement in profitability across the group and in its key divisions.

Erginbilgi&ccedil; said he does not measure growth only by volume, but by the company&rsquo;s ability to create value and improve performance. He said the profit improvement reflects that broader shift.

Rolls-Royce said operational performance has improved across its businesses, with reliability and service quality now at best-in-class levels.

Erginbilgi&ccedil; said this operational base gives the company credibility with customers and supports future growth.

Defence was one of the clearest growth stories in the presentation. Management said the business is benefiting from legacy programs, new orders and emerging markets.

Rolls-Royce said it has market leadership in gas turbine-based autonomous systems, not in lower-end electricity or diesel-based systems. It said the MQ-25 contract has been awarded and flight tested, and is moving to production. It also said it has collaborative combat aircraft contracts with Germany and the U.K. for medium-sized gas turbine-based autonomous systems.

The company said it has a joint venture for smaller autonomous systems, and that the U.S., Germany and the U.K. are all funding a full range of autonomous systems development. Management said the market is expected to move from development to production, with more opportunities likely from other countries. InvestingPro data shows Rolls-Royce maintains a robust current ratio of 93.61 and a gross profit margin of 59%, underscoring the financial foundation supporting these growth initiatives. The platform identifies the company as a prominent player in the Aerospace & Defense industry, with access to detailed peer comparisons and health scores available to subscribers.

Power Systems is being reshaped around demand from hyperscalers and data centers. Management said this is now a major growth engine with long visibility.

Management said the business is not overly dependent on one product line, because services already make up about half of Power Systems profit. It said data center services and industrial services are still small, but should contribute more over time.

Erginbilgi&ccedil; said external cost pressure is becoming more important, especially in elemental and rare earth materials.

Management said long-term service agreements are signed continuously and are not a new development. It said the 2023 deal framework was introduced after the company&rsquo;s earlier financial distress, when pricing had been too low. Under the current model, the manufacturer carries operational and cost risk, so pricing has been reset to better reflect that exposure.

Rolls-Royce said its next phase of growth will come from all three divisions, with Civil Aerospace, Defence and Power Systems each offering different opportunities.

In Defence, the company expects major production programs to begin from 2028 onward, led by MV75 and the B-52 re-engining effort. It also expects GCAP, AUKUS and autonomous systems to move further from development to production.

In Power Systems, management kept its 25% CAGR target through 2030 for power generation. It said gas engines should grow from less than 10% of the power generation segment to 15% to 20% by then, while service growth for Power Systems should continue at a double-digit pace. It also said partnerships with hyperscalers and more digitization should support a more durable business model.

During questions, Erginbilgi&ccedil; addressed competition, pricing and program strategy.

Erginbilgi&ccedil; closed by saying the company&rsquo;s focus remains on safety culture, flawless execution and continued organizational progress, even as it pursues growth across the portfolio.
