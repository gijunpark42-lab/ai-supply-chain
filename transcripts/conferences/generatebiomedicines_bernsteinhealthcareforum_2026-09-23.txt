Generate Biomedicines (GENB) used Wednesday, 23 September 2026, at the Bernstein Insights: Healthcare Leaders and Disruptors &ndash; 3rd Annual Healthcare Forum to make a broad case for its AI-driven drug discovery platform, while also acknowledging the limits of its current model. Chief Executive Mike Nally said the company has moved quickly from concept to clinic, but he also pointed to opportunities to improve patient identification and site selection in large global clinical trials.

Nally framed the company as an early mover in protein design, built before the rise of AlphaFold and ChatGPT, and said the main long-term advantage will come from data rather than software alone. Generate&rsquo;s pipeline now includes a wholly owned late-stage asthma drug and a second clinical program aimed at a side effect of cancer treatment, while the company continues to partner with large drugmakers for early discovery work.

Nally said Generate was founded in 2018 to use machine learning to design protein therapeutics, at a time when the field was still early and before generative AI became mainstream. He said the company&rsquo;s core idea is to combine computational design with experimental validation, because biology does not offer the kind of built-in scoring system that helps train models in games, coding or math.

Nally argued that AI models themselves will become commoditized, much as large language models compete closely with one another. He said the durable advantage will come from data sets that are hard to replicate, especially in areas such as function, developability, manufacturability and immunogenicity.

He also compared the coming shift in drug discovery to earlier changes in the technology industry, saying the field may move from an artisanal craft to an engineering discipline over the next five to 10 years. In his view, discovery could become scalable for the first time, which may change how value is split between biotech companies and large pharmaceutical groups.

Generate did not provide a full financial update in the session, but it described a business model that mixes wholly owned development with partnered discovery programs. The company said this structure helps fund its platform while preserving upside in selected assets.

Generate said it uses partnerships to monetize early discovery work while keeping GB-0895 in-house to prove the platform. The company said that balance helps it answer skeptics while still generating revenue from pharma collaborators. The approach has helped the company maintain a strong balance sheet. With a market capitalization of $2.24 billion and revenue of $26.5 million over the last twelve months, Generate holds more cash than debt and maintains a current ratio of 8.3, indicating liquid assets well exceed short-term obligations.

GB-0895 is an anti-TSLP antibody for severe asthma and is now in Phase III. Nally said the molecule was designed five years and three months ago and is already about one year into the pivotal study, which he described as one of the fastest paths from concept to Phase III for a chronic care biologic. Investors have responded positively to the company’s progress, with the stock gaining 39% over the past six months and 11% in the last week alone. According to InvestingPro analysis, which tracks over 1,400 additional metrics and provides exclusive ProTips for thousands of stocks, analysts have set price targets ranging from $22 to $30 per share, suggesting potential upside from current levels.

Generate said the trial used the Food and Drug Administration&rsquo;s Model-Informed Drug Development pathway. It also said the move from Phase I directly to Phase III was supported by safety and pharmacokinetic data, a wide historical dose range that did not change exacerbation rates, and biomarker results that matched or exceeded a marketed comparator.

The company said the Phase III study is running across about 400 sites in 40 countries.

Generate&rsquo;s second clinical asset, GB-4362, is designed to reduce peripheral neuropathy in patients receiving antibody-drug conjugates, or ADCs. The company said the first focus is urothelial cancer patients treated with enfortumab vedotin plus KEYTRUDA, a combination it described as highly effective but limited by nerve damage.

Nally said the program reflects a broader shift for the platform, moving beyond improving known therapies and into biological areas that were previously considered difficult to drug.

Generate described major gains in its research engine over the past several years. The company said its experimental verification cycle has fallen from three months to seven days, and that its cryo-EM investment has helped it move from static protein images to dynamic views of protein behavior.

The company said it hires people from both technology and pharmaceutical backgrounds and looks for mission-driven talent interested in building new experimental workflows. Nally said the pitch is attractive to people who want to work on medicines with real patient impact, not just consumer technology products.

Generate said it expects the industry to reach an inflection point in which drug discovery becomes more scalable through AI and data. Nally said the company believes molecular design itself may become commoditized within five to 10 years, which would shift value toward the owners of the best data and the best experimental systems.

Nally said the company is still weighing whether it should eventually commercialize medicines globally or continue to partner for late-stage development and sales. He noted that large pharmaceutical companies already have global commercial networks, but said a more discovery-focused model could give Generate stronger negotiating leverage over time. The strategic decision comes as analysts do not anticipate the company will be profitable this year, with projected losses of $2.14 per share for 2026. InvestingPro data indicates the stock is currently trading slightly above its Fair Value estimate, placing it among companies investors should monitor closely. During questions, Nally returned to the company&rsquo;s central argument that data, not models, will define the next phase of AI in biology. He said the rapid pace of change in the broader AI market shows how quickly model leadership can shift, while curated biological data sets are much harder to build and replace.

Nally also explained why the company moved GB-0895 quickly from Phase I to Phase III. He said the biomarker data, the FDA pathway and the broad historical dose range made a separate Phase II dose-finding study unnecessary. He added that the company drew inspiration from other long-acting biologics that used biomarker modulation as a surrogate for clinical benefit.

On GB-4362, he said the program was inspired by the success of KEYTRUDA plus enfortumab vedotin, but also by the burden of neuropathy that limits how long patients can stay on treatment. He said the goal is to preserve the anti-cancer benefit while removing the nerve damage caused by circulating payload.

Generate said it is building a platform meant to speed discovery, expand into new biological targets and create proprietary data that competitors cannot easily copy. Readers can refer to the full transcript below for more detail.
