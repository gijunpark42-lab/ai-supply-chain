Gianluca Pirozzi, Head of Development, Rare Disease, and Safety, Alexion: Thank you, Marc. I’m Gianluca Pirozzi, the Head of Development, Rare Disease, and Safety at Alexion. HPP is a rare inherited lifelong disease caused by deficient alkaline phosphatase activity, affecting patients at all ages. The disease is heterogeneous and multisystemic, with a range of skeletal and functional manifestations significantly impacting quality of life. In its perinatal and infantile forms, HPP can be life-threatening and is often fatal before age 2 without treatment. In children, rickets, impaired growth, deformities, and fractures can affect development throughout life. Adult patients with HPP, their disease is generally milder, but it’s far from benign. They may experience slow-healing fractures, tooth loss, chronic pain, muscle weakness, fatigue, and progressive disability, all of which can affect work, independence, and daily activities. Next slide, please.

Efzimfotase alfa is a great example of patient-centered innovation, a second-generation enzyme replacement therapy developed from more than a decade of experience with Strensiq. Both medicines replace the deficient alkaline phosphatase enzyme, but efzimfotase alfa has four key differences to reduce treatment burden, improve tolerability, and support broader patient access. It has enhanced enzyme activity, allowing for a similar effect as Strensiq, but a lower simple weight-banded dosing regimen with a smaller injection volume. Its optimized Fc design reduces off-target interactions, while enhanced glycosylation extends the mean half-life to around 6 days, supporting dosing once every 2 weeks rather than 3 to 6 times per week as per Strensiq. Finally, an optimized manufacturing process improves production efficiency and reduces wastage, supporting broader global access. Next slide, please. The efzimfotase alfa development program was intentionally designed to capture all patients living with HPP.

Recognizing that pain and fatigue are among the most prevalent symptoms experienced by patients, the program expanded beyond traditional skeletal endpoints to evaluate outcomes that directly affect daily functioning and quality of life. The phase III program enrolled 196 patients across 22 countries, representing the largest clinical program in HPP to date. Importantly, it was the first to include patients with adult-onset disease and those without skeletal manifestation, using tailored endpoints to generate data that reflects all patients living with HPP. The program includes treatment-naive children in MULBERRY, children transitioning from Strensiq in CHESTNUT, and treatment-naive adolescents and adults in HICKORY. Next slide, please. These slides provide a recap of results from MULBERRY and CHESTNUT pediatric studies, which were presented at ICCBH in June.

CHESTNUT investigated the safety switching between two enzyme replacement therapies.
