On Thursday, 23 July 2026, AMD (AMD) used its Advancing AI 2026 Keynote to present a broad plan to win more of the fast-growing artificial intelligence market, from data centers to personal devices and robotics.

The company paired new products and major customer commitments with a bullish view of future demand, while also acknowledging the hard limits of power, cooling and execution in a crowded field.

AMD did not report quarterly earnings at the event, but it spent much of the keynote on market size and demand trends. The message was that AI infrastructure spending is still early in its cycle and could grow much larger than many investors expect.

AMD also pointed to its own momentum in servers. It said EPYC server CPU market share reached a record 46% in the latest quarter, and more than 60% of Fortune 100 companies now use AMD EPYC processors. Public-cloud virtual machine consumption has grown at a 76% compound annual rate over the past three years, the company said.

At the same time, the stock market appeared cautious. AMD shares were last at $533.7, down 3.37% from the previous close of $552.33. The stock remains near the top of its 52-week range of $149.22 to $584.73. The company’s market capitalization stands at $870 billion, reflecting its position as a prominent player in the semiconductor industry. However, InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock appears overvalued relative to its Fair Value estimate, placing it among companies on the Most Overvalued list. The stock trades at a P/E ratio of 178 and a PEG ratio of 1.49, reflecting high investor expectations for future growth.

AMD&rsquo;s product announcements were designed to show that it is building for the full AI stack, not just individual chips. The centerpiece was Helios, a rack-scale system aimed at frontier-model training and inference.

AMD said the hardware story depends on software, and it used the keynote to show how it is trying to make its platform easier to use for developers and large customers.

AMD introduced ROCm AI, which it described as an agentic AI platform for AI-assisted GPU programming. The system is designed to work with coding tools such as Cursor, Claude and Codex, and uses HyperLoom to analyze workloads, tune kernels and improve parallelism.

OpenAI&rsquo;s Philippe Tillet said agents are now capable of writing GPU kernels at a high level of quality, changing how developers work with low-level code. AMD said this is part of a broader move to make its software stack more open and more accessible to AI tools.

AMD also used the event to show that it wants to serve more than hyperscale data centers. The company introduced products for enterprise servers, personal AI machines and robotics.

AMD&rsquo;s broader argument was that AI is entering a new phase. The company said the industry is moving from millions of agents to billions, and that these systems will need many reasoning steps, tool calls and data lookups. That, in AMD&rsquo;s view, will push compute demand much higher than the chatbot wave did.
