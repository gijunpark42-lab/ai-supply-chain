On Thursday, 23 July 2026, AMD (AMD) used its Advancing AI 2026 Investor Roundtable to argue that the artificial-intelligence buildout is still in an early stage, even as the company faces heavy competition and long deployment cycles. Executives said demand for AI servers, accelerators and supporting software is growing faster than earlier forecasts, but they also pointed to power, manufacturing and integration challenges that could shape the pace of future sales.

AMD did not provide new near-term financial guidance at the event, noting that its second-quarter earnings report is due within 10 days. The company, which commands a market capitalization of $875.83 billion, is scheduled to report earnings on August 4, 2026. Instead, the company focused on long-term market sizing and customer demand.

Lisa Su said the company&rsquo;s projections are based on customer discussions, workload analysis, adoption rates and customer capital spending. She said the estimates also account for power availability, supply capacity and productivity gains from AI tools.

AMD outlined a broad set of product and deployment updates across CPUs, GPUs, networking, memory and services.

The company highlighted several large customer relationships that it said extend well beyond pilot programs.

Su said customers do not want to choose an accelerator for only one generation because integration work is substantial. She said AMD is already discussing what comes after MI450 with its largest customers, including Anthropic.

AMD used the roundtable to describe a multiyear product path across CPUs and accelerators.

Management said the company&rsquo;s long-term view assumes continued expansion in AI infrastructure, but also acknowledges that the pace of deployment depends on supply chain readiness and customer capital budgets.

AMD framed its strategy as a broad platform approach rather than a single-chip contest.

AMD devoted significant attention to software, saying its ROCm.ai platform is becoming a more important part of the company&rsquo;s AI pitch.

Su said the company sees the software work as central to making its hardware platform easier to adopt at scale.

AMD also discussed how memory and interconnect choices affect the economics of AI systems.

Executives said AMD has been preparing its supply chain for much larger volumes.

AMD said its work with Cerebras is progressing and could support future token-service deployments.

During the question-and-answer session, analysts pressed AMD on market size, deployment timing and product strategy.

AMD shares fell 2.32% in the regular session to $539.51, after closing the previous day at $552.33. In after-hours trading, the stock rose 3.57% to $558.95 as of 8:23 p.m. UTC, suggesting investors were still weighing the scale of the company&rsquo;s AI plans against the execution risks.
