Thursday, 10 September 2026 &mdash; At the Wells Fargo 21st Annual Healthcare Conference, Amgen (AMGN) said it is still growing through patent pressure and tougher competition, helped by a larger portfolio and several late-stage programs. The company also signaled that it may accept slightly lower margins to fund innovation, even as it keeps a firm grip on profitability.

Chief Financial Officer Thomas Dittrich said Amgen&rsquo;s second quarter showed that the company can still deliver growth across a wide portfolio.

He said the company is trying to balance profitability and investment rather than choose one over the other. Productivity gains, process streamlining and artificial intelligence are expected to help free up resources for research and growth spending. The company’s disciplined approach appears to be resonating with investors. Trading at a P/E ratio of 23.83 and a PEG ratio of 0.77, Amgen looks attractively valued relative to its growth trajectory. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock is currently undervalued based on Fair Value calculations, placing it among compelling opportunities on the platform’s Most Undervalued list. InvestingPro data shows that 13 analysts have revised their earnings upwards for the upcoming period, reflecting growing confidence in the company’s execution.

Amgen&rsquo;s biggest clinical update was positive phase III data for IMDELLTRA in combination with durvalumab as first-line maintenance treatment in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer.

Management called the data an important inflection point for IMDELLTRA and said the drug is already becoming a standard of care in the second-line setting, supported by survival data, clinical differentiation, NCCN recommendations and rapid site adoption.

Amgen also reported other clinical and regulatory progress:

Several products continued to drive sales growth, led by Repatha and UPLIZNA.

Amgen said Repatha&rsquo;s growth is being helped by evolving lipid guidelines, including the ACC/AHA multi-society guideline and a new endorsement from the American Academy of Family Physicians. The company said at least 100 million people are still not at guideline-based LDL levels despite standard therapy, leaving room for more PCSK9 use. It also argued that oral PCSK9 drugs entering the market may expand the category rather than take share from Repatha, since overall penetration remains in the low to mid-single digits.

UPLIZNA, acquired through the Horizon deal, is now used across three main indications: NMOSD, IgG4-related disease and generalized myasthenia gravis. Amgen said the drug is gaining broad acceptance in the gMG community and is also moving into autoimmune hepatitis and chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy.

Management said the company has several late-stage programs that could support growth later in the decade and beyond.

Paul Burton, Amgen&rsquo;s chief medical officer, said MariTide is a highly differentiated medicine because of its dosing profile and broad development plan. He said the company sees it as a potential option for patients who need longer-term maintenance after initial weight loss.

Amgen highlighted progress in rare disease and biosimilars as part of its broader growth story.

Amgen said UPLIZNA is also expanding into autoimmune hepatitis and CIDP, adding to its existing use in NMOSD, IgG4-related disease and gMG.

During questions, executives returned to several recurring themes: market expansion, pipeline depth and capital discipline.

Dittrich said Amgen is a different company from the one he knew in his earlier tenure, with a broader portfolio and more ways to grow. He said the company is not trying to buy growth just to fill gaps, but will act when assets meet its standards.

Burton said Amgen expects sustainable growth through the rest of the decade and into the next, supported by both large and smaller patient populations. Kaveh, senior vice president for global marketing and access, said MariTide may address a real problem in obesity care, where many patients start and stop therapy and may need a different maintenance option.

Amgen said the combination of commercial momentum, late-stage clinical data and disciplined capital allocation gives it room to keep investing while preserving strong margins.
