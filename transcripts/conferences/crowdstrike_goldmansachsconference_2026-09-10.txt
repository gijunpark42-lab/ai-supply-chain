On Thursday, 10 September 2026, CrowdStrike (CRWD) used the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference 2026 to argue that artificial intelligence is reshaping cybersecurity faster than many companies expected. Chief Executive George Kurtz said the shift is creating new demand for CrowdStrike&rsquo;s platform, even as it raises the stakes for customers facing faster, more automated attacks.

The company highlighted strong product momentum, a growing commitment-based sales model and a widening role for security in enterprise AI plans. At the same time, Kurtz warned that agentic attacks are accelerating breakout times and forcing companies to move from traditional detection tools to continuous, automated response.

The cybersecurity firm now commands a market capitalization of $214 billion, though InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock is currently overvalued relative to its Fair Value, placing it among companies on the Most Overvalued list. Still, InvestingPro Tips highlight that 16 analysts have revised their earnings upwards for the upcoming period, and net income is expected to grow this year. Subscribers can access 12 additional ProTips for deeper investment insights.

Kurtz framed the current moment as a turning point for cybersecurity. He said the industry is moving from endpoint detection and response to agent detection and response, or AIDR, as companies prepare for a world where AI agents operate alongside people and systems.

Kurtz said the company is not trying to compete with frontier model makers on broad artificial intelligence benchmarks. Instead, he said CrowdStrike is focused on security-specific use cases where its own data and architecture matter more.

A central theme of the discussion was CrowdStrike&rsquo;s large security data set. Kurtz said the company processes 7 trillion security events per day and has spent about 10 years building annotated data that can train its models.

Kurtz said CrowdStrike&rsquo;s approach combines a red offensive model, Red Tempest, a blue defensive model, Blue Solano, and a harness that links them together. He said that structure is the basis of SafeMind, the company&rsquo;s agentic security system.

He added that the company has found a cost advantage in security-specific AI work. According to Kurtz, CrowdStrike can carry out a remediation activity for about $0.03, compared with about $10 for frontier model solutions, while still achieving equivalent results for the task at hand.

CrowdStrike used the conference to outline several recent and upcoming product moves. Kurtz said the company&rsquo;s Falcon conference drew more than 10,000 customers and featured speakers from NVIDIA, Anthropic and OpenAI, along with large enterprise customers.

Kurtz said Falcon Guardian was developed by a handpicked internal tiger team over roughly six months; about 200 employees initially volunteered. He said about 80% of the product was built on the existing platform, which reduced the need for new architecture.

On the commercial side, CrowdStrike said its Flex-first sales strategy is gaining traction. Flex is the company&rsquo;s commitment-based licensing model, and Kurtz said it is now the preferred route for most deals.

Revenue grew 24.3% over the last twelve months to $5.4 billion. GAAP gross margin was 75%, and the company was profitable on a GAAP basis. The company is now profitable, a milestone reflected in CrowdStrike’s comprehensive Pro Research Report, one of 1,400+ available for US equities that transforms complex Wall Street data into clear, actionable intelligence.

Kurtz said the model is designed to help customers move faster and plan spending more flexibly. He said the company often starts with the customer&rsquo;s problem, then uses Flex to shape demand planning and speed adoption.

He also said the company is winning business from legacy security vendors, with about half of the endpoint market still on older technology. CrowdStrike has been adding large enterprise customers that are moving away from systems they have used for more than 15 years.

Kurtz said CrowdStrike is also using AI internally across the company. He described a "crawl-walk-run" approach, with the firm applying agentic workflows in legal, marketing, human resources and development.

Kurtz said the company&rsquo;s internal work helps it understand customer needs before selling products externally. That approach, he said, is especially important as customers ask for more visibility, control and discovery tools for AI agents.

Kurtz said the broader market is changing in CrowdStrike&rsquo;s favor because security leaders are gaining more influence over AI spending. He said chief information security officers are getting larger budgets as companies try to deploy AI faster.

Kurtz said the company expects AI spending to rise from about $2.7 trillion now to nearly $6 trillion by 2030. He said that growth should create a durable tailwind for security products, especially those built for agentic systems.

Kurtz said the pace of change in security is likely to stay high, and possibly accelerate. He said the company expects the inflection point for security to arrive sooner and more sharply than the cloud shift did.

He also said the company&rsquo;s partnerships with frontier model labs will continue, rather than direct competition. In his view, CrowdStrike&rsquo;s edge comes from vertical specialization, data and the ability to connect the prompt layer, identity layer and runtime execution layer.

During the question-and-answer session, Kurtz returned to several themes from the prepared remarks.

CrowdStrike shares closed the regular session at $208.86, up 0.51% from the previous close of $207.80. In after-hours trading, the stock rose to $209.45, up 0.28% from the close.
