Honeywell Technologies (HON) used Tuesday, 11 August 2026, at Deutsche Bank&rsquo;s Chicago Industrials Summit to lay out its next phase after completing a three-year portfolio overhaul. Management said the company is now a pure-play automation business with strong order momentum, but it also acknowledged uneven demand in some regions and the need to keep buffers in its forecasts.

CFO Mike Stepniak said Honeywell is &ldquo;off to a good start&rdquo; and is aiming for 4% to 6% organic revenue growth over the next three years, with earnings per share expected to reach $12 by 2029. He said the plan includes margin expansion, higher software and services revenue, and disciplined capital allocation.

Honeywell said it has completed major parts of its portfolio transformation, including the divestitures of Solstice and Honeywell Aerospace. Management described the remaining work as minor &ldquo;hygiene&rdquo; items.

The company now operates three core businesses:

Stepniak said the company will be judged by leading indicators such as order pipelines, execution in key verticals, acquisition integration, new product introductions, customer sentiment and delivery performance.

He said, &ldquo;Our orders pipeline is extremely strong. We are being successful as far as focusing on our verticals. We have done a very good job integrating our acquisitions, which is driving growth.&rdquo;

Honeywell framed its outlook around a three-year plan that calls for stronger profit growth than sales growth.

Management said the guidance includes contingency buffers for possible demand disruptions and inflation pressures. It also said the company expects margin expansion across all three businesses over time.

The company’s shares currently trade at a P/E ratio of 9.35, well below historical averages, while InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock is undervalued relative to its Fair Value. With a market capitalization of $75.3 billion and revenue growth of 11% over the last twelve months, the valuation appears attractive given management’s growth targets. Honeywell said capital allocation will remain disciplined:

Building Automation remains Honeywell&rsquo;s most mature segment and its most advanced in software adoption. The business is currently running at margins north of 27% and is targeting 29% by 2029.

Management said growth in the segment is expected to stay in the high single digits, though it is guiding conservatively to leave room for experimentation and execution risk.

Key points in the segment include:

The company said its data center portfolio includes fire sensing, physical security and monitoring, building management systems, power load management, liquid cooling and heat sensing and measurement.

Management said new product introductions should be accretive within the first two years because they tend to improve pricing or mix. It also said the long-term goal is to build Building Automation into a $10 billion business.

Process Automation and Technology is benefiting from demand tied to liquefied natural gas, the Middle East and broader industrial investment. Honeywell said the business had a 22.5% margin rate for full-year 2024, down 150 basis points from a year earlier because of the Johnson Matthey integration and mix pressure.

The company is targeting a 25% margin by 2029.

Management said the catalyst business, which it described as a $5 billion business within a $67 billion segment, should see stronger demand in the second half of 2024. It expects second-half catalyst demand to be up double digits from the first half, with full-year demand flat and second-half year-over-year growth in the high single digits.

Other points from the segment:

Honeywell said demand is broad-based across Africa, Asia and Latin America, with rebuild activity in the Middle East adding to the outlook. The company said higher oil prices are encouraging facility optimization and reloading, while security and cyber needs are rising because of resilience concerns.

Management said revenue from Middle East rebuild work is expected to show up in 2027. It also said the business is expanding into life sciences, pharmaceuticals, medical devices, cyber, U.S. onshoring and semiconductors.

On Johnson Matthey, management said the acquisition is still dilutive to margins but should create cost synergies and efficiency gains over two to three years.

Honeywell said the Industrial Automation segment is expected to move from single-digit-plus growth in the second half of 2024 to double-digit growth in 2025.

Management said the business is on track for a margin rate of about 22% in the fourth quarter of 2024, with a target of 25% in 2027. Stepniak said he is &ldquo;100% confident&rdquo; in that goal.

He said, &ldquo;On industrial automation? 100% confident. Look, you will see it. You will see it in the fourth quarter, and you will see it next year.&rdquo;

The company said the segment&rsquo;s improvement depends on several operational steps:

Honeywell said its on-time to first promise delivery rate is about 80% and should rise to above 85%. It said the better delivery performance should also support pricing power, which has been limited by weak execution in the past.

The company said it invests 4% to 4.5% of revenue in research and development, describing it as a continuous &ldquo;flywheel&rdquo; that must be kept moving. It said last year included a 50 to 60 basis point headwind from new product and R&D spending, but those investments are now starting to produce returns.

Honeywell said the long-term goal is to grow Industrial Automation from about $4 billion in revenue to $7 billion, with a significant share of that growth expected to come from acquisitions.

A major theme of the call was the growing role of software and services. Honeywell said it wants those businesses to reach 45% of total revenue over time, with Honeywell Forge as the main platform.

Management said the company has connected about 5 million assets so far and plans to reach 9 million connected assets over the next 2.5 years. It said more assets were connected last year than in the previous five years combined.

Honeywell said software revenue is growing at about 15% a year. It also said the platform is beginning to generate premium source margins as it scales.

AI is another key part of the strategy. Honeywell said it is deploying AI tools and Copilots across the company, with expected productivity gains of 2 to 3 hours a week for employees who use the tools well.

Examples included:

Management said customer concerns about data sharing have eased over time as cybersecurity has improved. It said customer response to AI integration with Forge has been &ldquo;very good.&rdquo;

Honeywell said its capital priorities are now centered on balance sheet repair, steady shareholder returns and targeted acquisitions.

Management said it wants leverage below 3.0x by year-end. It also plans to keep the dividend payout ratio at 35% and maintain a flat share count while debt is being reduced.

Capital spending is expected to stay near 3% of revenue, helped by the company&rsquo;s more asset-light profile after the divestitures.

On mergers and acquisitions, Honeywell said it remains active but disciplined. The company is targeting $2 billion to $4 billion of bolt-on deals, especially in Industrial Automation, where the market is fragmented and assets are easier to integrate.

Management said it is not looking for transformational deals. Instead, it wants acquisitions that are accretive to growth and margins and fit within the core sensing and measurement strategy.

Honeywell said it has historically paid about 13 times for acquisitions and wants to earn a 10% return on investment by year five.

During questions, investors pressed management on whether the 4% to 6% growth target was conservative. Stepniak responded by pointing to the strong order pipeline, vertical execution, acquisition integration and new product momentum.

He also addressed concerns about Process Automation and Technology margins, saying the market may be overestimating the current profit rate. He noted that the business is at 22.5%, not the higher levels some investors assumed, and that Johnson Matthey integration should help over time.

On the Middle East, management said rebuild revenue is likely to arrive in 2027, with engineering work already largely complete and manufacturing throughput now the main constraint.

On Industrial Automation, management reiterated its confidence in reaching 25% margins and said investors will see progress in the fourth quarter of 2024 and in 2025.

Honeywell also said its NPI program remains an important engine for growth. Stepniak compared it to a &ldquo;flywheel,&rdquo; saying it must keep receiving investment to keep producing results.

Honeywell said the portfolio reset is largely done and the next phase will be about execution, software growth and margin gains across its three core businesses. The stock has gained 20% year-to-date but remains 52% below its 52-week high of $496.36.
