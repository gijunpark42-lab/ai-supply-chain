W&auml;rtsil&auml; said second-quarter order intake rose to a record EUR 2.8 billion, up 33% from a year earlier, while comparable operating profit climbed 7% to EUR 280 million and cash flow reached EUR 497 million. The Finnish engineering group also said its 12-month rolling operating margin hit 14%, meeting a key financial target. Still, the stock fell 3.73% to $29.42 in pre-market trading, down from $30.56, as investors weighed the strong operating results against the lack of disclosed EPS and revenue actuals in the forecast table and the company&rsquo;s cautious tone on capacity limits and future margin timing.

W&auml;rtsil&auml; reported a strong second quarter, supported by broad demand across its main businesses. Total order intake rose 33% to EUR 2.8 billion, while organic order intake increased 43%. The order book reached EUR 8.9 billion, up 13% from a year earlier.

Net sales were stable at EUR 1.6 billion on a reported basis, but organic sales increased 5%, showing that the underlying business remained healthy despite portfolio divestments and foreign exchange pressure. Comparable operating result improved 7% to EUR 280 million, and operating result rose 14% to EUR 209 million.

The company said Marine and Energy both posted all-time high quarterly order intake. Marine order intake rose 12% year over year, while Energy order intake jumped 82%. Management described the market as &ldquo;hot&rdquo; and said demand remained strong across geographies and applications.

The forecast table pointed to EPS of $0.2832 and revenue of $1.63 billion for the period, but no actual EPS or revenue figures were provided in the same table. That means a formal beat or miss cannot be calculated from the available forecast data.

Even so, the operating picture was clearly strong. Order intake, margin, and cash flow all improved sharply, and management said the 12-month rolling operating margin reached 14%, which was a stated target. The company also said earnings per share increased both quarterly and year to date.

Compared with recent periods, the quarter appears stronger on operations than the first quarter, especially in cash generation. The jump in cash flow from EUR 7 million in Q1 to EUR 497 million in Q2 was particularly notable.

W&auml;rtsil&auml; shares fell 3.73% to $29.42 from a previous close of $30.56. The stock is now about 31% below its 52-week high of $40.75 and about 31% above its 52-week low of $22.54.

The decline suggests investors may be focusing on what comes next rather than the strong headline numbers. The company&rsquo;s strong order intake and margin progress were positive, but management also said capacity is tight and that much of the future margin improvement will take time to reach EBIT. The market may also be reacting to the lack of a simple EPS and revenue surprise in the available figures.

No volume data was provided, so it is not possible to confirm whether the move came with unusually heavy trading.

Management said demand in both Marine and Energy is expected to remain similar to the comparison period, which it described as a very strong environment. The company also said it is now booking orders for 2029 and 2030, while 2028 is essentially sold out. W&auml;rtsil&auml; maintains a strong balance sheet with more cash than debt and has raised its dividend for five consecutive years, demonstrating financial resilience alongside growth. The company earned an overall "GREAT" financial health score of 3.14 out of 5 from InvestingPro, which provides detailed analysis through its comprehensive Pro Research Report&mdash;one of 1,400+ available for top global stocks, transforming complex data into clear, actionable intelligence.

W&auml;rtsil&auml; plans to expand capacity further. It announced an additional EUR 90 million investment to expand production capacity by 30% at its Vaasa facility in Finland. Combined with earlier plans, the company expects operational capacity to rise to 2.2 times 2025 levels by 2029.

The company also flagged a EUR 40 million to EUR 50 million negative operating impact in 2026 from its energy storage joint venture with RCT Solutions, which is expected to close in the third quarter.

Management said the Energy equipment order book gross margin has improved by more than 500 basis points since the start of 2025, but that the benefit will take time to flow through to EBIT, likely more meaningfully in 2028 and beyond. The company expects significant service revenue from new data center projects to begin materializing around 2030.

&ldquo;We continue our journey. Total order intake increased by 33% to EUR 2.8 billion, and that is an all-time high quarter in the history of W&auml;rtsil&auml;,&rdquo; Chief Executive H&aring;kan Agnevall said.

&ldquo;We are now on a rolling 12-month basis operating result as a percentage of net sales at 14%, which is our financial target,&rdquo; Chief Financial Officer Arjen Berends said.

&ldquo;By 2029, we will have increased our operational capacity 2.2 times, so more than doubling our capacity compared to the 2025 operational levels,&rdquo; Agnevall said, underscoring confidence in long-term demand.

Analysts focused heavily on margin improvement, demand strength, and capacity. One key question was how to reconcile a 500 basis point improvement in Energy equipment gross margin with volatility in average selling prices. Management said EUR per kilowatt is not the right measure and that the improvement reflects a mix of pricing, execution, and efficiency.

Another major topic was demand. Analysts asked whether the strong quarter meant guidance was conservative. Management said the outlook is based on a 12-month comparison, not just the strong quarter, and that demand remains very solid.

Questions also centered on data centers and market share. Management said it cannot quantify market share because the market is opaque, but it sees strong demand and believes reciprocating engines are competitive on cost, fuel efficiency, and water use.

Analysts further pressed on capacity. Management said 2028 is sold out, orders for 2029 and 2030 are already being negotiated, and future expansion will be handled step by step to keep the supply chain aligned.
