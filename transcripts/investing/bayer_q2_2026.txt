Bayer said first-half 2026 sales rose 3% on a currency- and portfolio-adjusted basis to EUR 24.3 billion, while core earnings per share increased 3% to EUR 3.66. But the company also reported negative free cash flow of EUR 2.7 billion, weighed down by litigation payouts, and its shares slipped 2.3% to $13.57 in the latest trading, from $13.89 the previous close.

Bayer said its first-half results showed resilience despite a difficult backdrop. Net sales reached EUR 24.3 billion, and EBITDA before special items rose 7% to EUR 6.6 billion. Management said the company is on track for the year and pointed to progress in all three divisions.

Crop Science delivered the strongest operational improvement. Sales rose 5.5% in the first half, helped by seeds and traits and licensing revenue, while the EBITDA margin widened sharply. Consumer Health also grew 3.5%, supported by nutritionals and dermatology.

Pharmaceuticals was more uneven. Sales were flat in the first half, as strong growth from NUBEQA and KERENDIA offset declines in other products. Eylea faced pressure from biosimilar competition, and its 8-milligram version now makes up half of sales.

The company&rsquo;s quarterly forecast in the available data called for EPS of $0.2203 and revenue of $12.31 billion, but no matching actual quarterly EPS or revenue figure was provided in the same dataset. That means a direct beat-or-miss calculation cannot be made from the disclosed forecast line.

What can be measured is Bayer&rsquo;s reported operating trend. H1 core EPS rose 3% year over year, but Q2 core EPS fell 17%, mainly because 2025 included non-recurring tax benefits and 2026 normalized tax reconciliation. That makes the quarter look softer than the half-year result.

The magnitude of the quarterly EPS decline was meaningful, but management framed it as a timing and tax issue rather than a sign of broad operating weakness. Sales growth and EBITDA growth suggest the business remained stable overall.

Bayer&rsquo;s ADRs were last quoted at $13.57, down 2.3% from $13.89. The stock is now about 10.9% below its 52-week high of $15.23 and well above its 52-week low of $7.19.

The move suggests investors were cautious after the report, even as the company reiterated its full-year outlook and lowered debt guidance. The market may also be weighing the weak cash flow, the Q2 EPS decline, and continued pressure in parts of the pharma business against the stronger Crop Science margin and the improved legal backdrop. No trading-volume data was provided, so it is not possible to judge whether the move came with unusually heavy activity.

Bayer reiterated its full-year 2026 guidance for sales, earnings and free cash flow at constant currencies. Management said the outlook reflects the latest view of external factors, including geopolitics, weather and drug-pricing policy. Analysts are optimistic about the company’s turnaround, with InvestingPro data showing earnings per share are forecast to reach $5.07 for fiscal 2026. The platform’s Financial Health score rates Bayer as "GOOD" overall, and one of seven exclusive ProTips notes that net income is expected to grow this year while analysts predict a return to profitability.

The company also cut its net financial debt guidance to EUR 29 billion-EUR 30 billion, helped by a EUR 3 billion equity investment from Apollo that is expected to close in the second half. Bayer said it also placed $5 billion in U.S. dollar bonds, giving it additional financing flexibility.

In pharmaceuticals, Bayer expects to launch asundexian at the end of 2026 or early 2027, pending approvals in the U.S. and China. The company said it intends to launch the drug in as many countries as possible, including Europe and Germany, though pricing talks remain open.

Crop Science is expected to keep improving margins under its five-year framework, while Consumer Health is expected to meet its full-year margin outlook.

Chief Executive Bill Anderson said, &ldquo;The past 90 days have been very important for Bayer, operationally we&rsquo;re on track for the year, and we&rsquo;ve made decisive progress on our long-term strategic priorities.&rdquo;

He also highlighted the company&rsquo;s legal progress, saying the U.S. Supreme Court&rsquo;s ruling in Monsanto v. Durnell was &ldquo;a really important step for limiting litigation in the future.&rdquo; Bayer said the decision strengthens its argument that federal regulators, not state failure-to-warn claims, should govern crop-protection labeling.

Chief Financial Officer Judith Hartmann said the Apollo deal was &ldquo;the right transaction in the right time&rdquo; and called it &ldquo;part of the entire toolbox&rdquo; for managing the balance sheet. She added that the company wants to strengthen the balance sheet while preserving room to invest in future growth.

Analysts focused on Bayer&rsquo;s legal, structural and pricing strategy.

Questions centered on whether Bayer would launch asundexian in Europe despite weak pricing conditions. Management said it intends to launch in every country possible, but pricing discussions with policymakers remain important.

Analysts also asked about the company&rsquo;s Dynamic Shared Ownership model and whether job cuts were complete. Anderson said headcount was roughly flat in Q2 and that the company is not working toward a fixed reduction target.

Another major topic was structure. Bayer said it remains open-minded about spin-offs or divestitures, but it is not ready to shift attention away from five priorities: rebuilding the pharma pipeline, containing litigation, de-leveraging, improving Crop Science profitability and making the company leaner.

Questions also covered Ruvion, Bayer&rsquo;s new semi-independent glyphosate entity. Anderson said the move makes sense given the competitive state of the business, but declined to discuss further structural steps.
