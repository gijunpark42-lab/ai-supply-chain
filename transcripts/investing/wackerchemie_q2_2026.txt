Wacker Chemie AG said second-quarter sales rose 7.8% from a year earlier to &euro;1.52 billion, while EBITDA before special pension effects jumped 53% to &euro;175 million, as cost cuts and pricing actions helped offset a weak demand backdrop. The German chemicals group raised its full-year 2026 EBITDA outlook to &euro;625 million to &euro;750 million from a prior range, and the shares were recently up 1.47% at $87.60, after closing at $86.33. The stock remains below its 52-week high of $106, but well above the low of $61.20. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock appears slightly overvalued relative to its Fair Value estimate. Still, analysts predict the company will be profitable this year, a notable shift given it was not profitable over the last twelve months.

Wacker Chemie delivered a clear improvement in Q2, but management stressed that the gain came mainly from self-help, not from a stronger market. Group sales reached &euro;1.52 billion, up from &euro;1.41 billion a year earlier. EBITDA before special pension effects rose to &euro;175 million from &euro;114 million.

The company said cost discipline, pricing and operational execution drove the result. Cost of goods sold was flat year over year despite higher volumes, while SG&A expenses declined. That points to better operating leverage.

The quarter also included a &euro;37 million non-cash benefit from a change in pension plans. Reported EBITDA was &euro;211 million, and net income reached &euro;350 million, helped by &euro;243 million of non-operating gains tied to Siltronic share sales and revaluation effects.

The performance was broad-based across the portfolio, but not evenly so. Silicones benefited from specialty products. Polymers held up well despite raw material swings. Biosolutions grew from a smaller base. Polysilicon remained mixed, with semiconductor-grade demand improving while the solar market stayed weak.

The supplied forecast data showed expected revenue of $1.49 billion and expected EPS of $0.405, but it did not include actual EPS or actual revenue in the same currency and reporting format. That makes a direct apples-to-apples surprise calculation impossible from the available figures.

Still, the company&rsquo;s own reported numbers point to a stronger quarter operationally. Sales of &euro;1.52 billion were above the forecast revenue level in nominal terms, and EBITDA before special items rose sharply. The main message from the call was that Wacker delivered a solid operational improvement even though end markets remained weak.

Compared with recent quarters, the improvement appears meaningful. Management said the quarter was not driven by a broad market rebound, but by internal actions such as PACE, pricing and better execution. That usually matters to investors because it suggests the earnings base may be more durable than a simple cyclical bounce.

Wacker&rsquo;s shares were recently up 1.47% at $87.60, compared with a previous close of $86.33. The move was modest, which suggests investors welcomed the stronger results and higher guidance but remained cautious about the outlook.

The stock is trading about 17.8% below its 52-week high of $106 and about 43.1% above its 52-week low of $61.20. That range shows the shares have recovered from earlier weakness, but they have not fully returned to peak levels. The stock has delivered a 25.6% return over the past six months and a 25.8% year-to-date gain. InvestingPro assigns Wacker Chemie a Financial Health Score of 2.18, rated as "FAIR," reflecting a balanced mix of strengths in cash flow and price momentum alongside profitability challenges. The muted reaction may reflect the mixed nature of the report. On one hand, Wacker raised guidance and showed strong cost control. On the other, management said demand remains subdued in many markets, and uncertainty around polysilicon trade rules continues to weigh on visibility.

Wacker raised its full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance to &euro;625 million to &euro;750 million. Management said the higher range reflects the stronger second quarter and the pension accounting benefit.

Management expects capital spending of about &euro;300 million for 2026, well below historical levels, as major expansion projects are now complete. Net financial debt is expected to fall to about &euro;500 million by year-end.

The company also plans to hold a Capital Markets Day in London on Sept. 17, where it will present new group-level targets.

CEO Christian Hartel said the quarter showed that Wacker&rsquo;s results were driven by internal actions rather than a market recovery. &ldquo;This was not a result of a broad market recovery. It was a result of actions within our control,&rdquo; he said.

Hartel also pointed to the company&rsquo;s strategic focus. &ldquo;In our chemical divisions, we focus on specialty products, in the polysilicon division of the semiconductor market, and in our life science division, Biosolutions, on innovative biotech applications,&rdquo; he said.

CFO Tobias Ohler highlighted the cost program and balance sheet. He said PACE contributed about &euro;45 million in the quarter and noted that the company&rsquo;s &ldquo;balance sheet remains very strong,&rdquo; with &euro;4.18 billion in equity and &euro;1.57 billion in liquidity.

Analysts focused heavily on polysilicon, pricing and the durability of the quarter&rsquo;s improvement.

Questions centered on whether semiconductor-grade polysilicon can keep growing, how much the solar business depends on U.S. trade policy, and whether Wacker might need to adjust capacity if market conditions worsen. Management said semiconductor demand is growing, but timing can be uneven because of long customer supply chains.

Analysts also pressed on polymers, asking how much of the strong margin performance came from inventory effects and how much came from real pricing power. CFO Tobias Ohler said part of the result reflected a low double-digit inventory valuation effect that will not repeat, but he added that rapid pricing responses and PACE savings also helped.

Another focus was silicones, where management said specialty products are helping insulate the business from weaker commodity pricing in China. Hartel said the company is concentrating on higher-value products rather than expanding upstream commodity capacity.

The call also touched on the company&rsquo;s 1,600-position reduction agreement in Germany. Management said the related savings will become visible mainly from 2027, supporting the broader target of more than &euro;300 million in savings by 2028.
