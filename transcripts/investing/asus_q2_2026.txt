ASUS reported record second-quarter 2026 results, with revenue of TWD 241 billion, operating profit of TWD 19.6 billion and net profit after tax of TWD 19 billion, as demand for AI servers, premium PCs and gaming products lifted margins and sales. The company said revenue rose 39% from a year earlier, while operating profit jumped 230% and EPS reached TWD 25.6. The stock was last up 1.55% at $852, compared with a previous close of $839, leaving shares near the upper end of their 52-week range. According to InvestingPro analysis, the company trades at a P/E ratio of 11.67 with a market cap of roughly $3 billion, while the platform’s Fair Value assessment suggests the stock is trading near its intrinsic value.

ASUS delivered one of its strongest quarters in years, helped by a mix of premium consumer devices, commercial PCs and a fast-growing infrastructure business. The company said it was the only top-five Windows PC brand to post positive growth in the quarter, even as the broader PC market faced pressure from rising memory costs and a weaker demand backdrop.

The results also showed a shift in ASUS&rsquo;s business mix. Infrastructure Solutions Business Group revenue doubled year over year, while the System Business Group, which includes PCs, continued to grow at a healthy pace. The company said its server business is becoming a major earnings driver, supported by demand for AI infrastructure and close ties with NVIDIA.

Management also pointed to strength in commercial PCs, where ASUS has been expanding in markets such as Southeast Asia. That segment has now posted more than 50% year-over-year growth for six consecutive quarters, a sign that the company&rsquo;s push into business customers is gaining traction.

The supplied forecast called for revenue of $237.56 billion and EPS of 15.0, but the company reported results in Taiwan dollars, making a direct apples-to-apples comparison difficult. Even so, the quarter was clearly stronger than a routine beat: ASUS described the period as record-setting across revenue, operating profit and net profit.

On a year-over-year basis, the scale of the improvement was notable. Revenue rose 39%, operating profit surged 230% and net profit climbed 94%. Those gains suggest that ASUS benefited not only from higher sales, but also from a better product mix and stronger pricing power. Management said the company captured more value than the broader market as memory prices pushed up industry ASPs.

ASUS shares were last up 1.55% at $852, compared with $839 at the previous close. That implies a gain of $13. The stock is now about 11.3% below its 52-week high of $964 and about 74.1% above its 52-week low of $490.

The move was positive, but modest relative to the size of the earnings beat and the record results. That may suggest investors had already expected a strong quarter, or that they are waiting for more proof that the server business can keep growing at a rapid pace. No unusual trading volume was provided.

ASUS said it expects Q3 2026 PC revenue to rise 15% to 20% sequentially, with growth split roughly evenly between higher average selling prices and shipment volume. Management said high-end products should perform best because memory costs make up a smaller share of their final selling prices.

For the server business, ASUS said Q3 revenue should rise 10% to 15% sequentially, with year-over-year growth expected to exceed 150%. The company raised its full-year 2026 server growth target from 100% to at least 150%.

Analysts focused on four main areas: PC growth, margins, server mix and commercial PCs.

On PC growth, management said the expected 15% to 20% quarterly increase in Q3 revenue should come from a 50/50 split between price and volume gains. ASUS also said high-end products are better insulated from memory inflation.

On margins, executives said the improvement came from brand strength, a richer product mix, supply-chain control and faster server growth. They said the company uses 4% to 5% operating margin as a baseline for normal profitability, but recent quarters have been well above that level.

On servers, ASUS said HGX systems and full rack systems together account for about 90% of server revenue. The company said supply is still tight, but manageable, and that it expects a more diversified customer base in 2027.

On commercial PCs, management said the business has benefited from local service teams, tailored products and strong brand recognition in regions such as Southeast Asia, where ASUS has used its consumer presence to win business customers.
