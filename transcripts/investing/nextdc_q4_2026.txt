NEXTDC said it delivered record FY26 results, with net revenue of AUD 405 million, statutory total revenue of AUD 496.5 million and underlying EBITDA of AUD 248.8 million, which it said was about 4% above the top end of guidance. The data-center operator also reported a 202% increase in contracted utilization to 740 megawatts on a pro forma basis and a record forward order book of 565 megawatts. Shares rose 2.5% to $13.92, from $13.58, after the update.

Key Takeaways

FY26 was NEXTDC’s largest contracting year, with 495 megawatts of new contracts signed.Underlying EBITDA of AUD 248.8 million beat guidance by about 4% above the top end of the range.FY27 revenue guidance calls for AUD 615 million to AUD 640 million, up more than 50% from FY26 net revenue.The company said its 565-megawatt forward order book is fully contracted, with no reservations or options.Management said demand from hyperscale and AI customers remains strong, while billing utilization is set to rise sharply as projects come online.

Company Performance

NEXTDC’s FY26 results reflected a business moving from development to faster monetization. The company said net revenue rose 16% from FY25, while underlying EBITDA increased 15%. Net profit after tax was AUD 82.1 million, helped by a AUD 129 million fair value gain on investment properties and the recognition of previously unrecognized tax losses.

The company’s core operating metrics also improved sharply. Billing utilization rose 58% to 175 megawatts, while contracted utilization tripled to 740 megawatts. Management said less than a quarter of contracted capacity is currently billing, leaving room for future growth as new facilities are delivered and customer deployments accelerate.

The results come as demand for data-center capacity remains strong across hyperscale cloud, AI and enterprise workloads. NEXTDC said more than 70% of billing capacity now comes from hyperscale workloads, and 95% of contracted capacity is tied to major cloud and AI customers.

Earnings vs. Forecast

No EPS or revenue consensus was provided for the period, so a direct forecast comparison is not available. Still, NEXTDC said underlying EBITDA came in about 4% above the top end of guidance, which is a clear beat.

That outperformance matters because it came alongside record contracting and a sharp increase in utilization. In other words, the company did not simply benefit from accounting gains or one-time items. It also showed stronger operating momentum in the core business.

Management framed FY26 as a year of heavy preparation for future growth. The company said it had spent years building the commercial pipeline that led to the record contract wins. That suggests the latest beat was driven more by execution than by a temporary lift.

Market Reaction

NEXTDC shares rose 2.5% to $13.92 after the announcement, up from a previous close of $13.58. The stock remains below its 52-week high of $18.22, but it is well above the low of $10.91.

The move points to a constructive market response. Investors appeared to welcome the combination of record contracting, stronger-than-guidance EBITDA and a large FY27 revenue target. At the same time, the gain was modest, which suggests the market is still balancing the company’s growth story against its heavy capital needs.

The stock’s position in the middle of its 52-week range also suggests that sentiment has improved but is not yet at a peak. For a business that is spending billions of dollars on new capacity, the market may want to see more proof that revenue conversion and cash generation are accelerating as planned.

Outlook & Guidance

NEXTDC guided FY27 net revenue to AUD 615 million to AUD 640 million and underlying EBITDA to AUD 385 million to AUD 410 million. The company also forecast capital expenditure of AUD 5.25 billion to AUD 5.75 billion, including about AUD 500 million of reimbursable customer fit-out.

Management said FY27 should include 197 megawatts of billing conversion from the forward order book, with another 221 megawatts expected in FY28. The company said it is not providing FY28 guidance because growth could accelerate depending on how quickly sites are delivered and customers are activated.

The company also said its development pipeline is large, with 537 megawatts of built capacity under construction and 130 megawatts in plan. Major projects include M2, M3, S4, KL1 and M4, while S5 is expected to begin construction in FY27. NEXTDC said its total planned capacity across the land bank exceeds 3 gigawatts.

Management said the operating platform is now in place to scale materially through 2028 and beyond. It also said FY27 is primarily an execution year, with contracts signed and capital raised.

Executive Commentary

Chief Executive Craig Scroggie said FY26 was “the largest contracting year in NEXTDC’s history” and said contracted utilization tripled to 740 megawatts.

He also said, “Every megawatt in that forward order book is a binding customer contract,” underscoring the company’s visibility into future revenue.

On the company’s growth path, Scroggie said, “As AI adoption moves from experimentation into production, demand is no longer the question. Demand is shifting towards inference,” adding that NEXTDC’s metro footprint positions it well for that shift.

Chief Financial Officer Oskar Tomaszewski said the company is “no longer subject to any leverage ratio covenants” because of its credit standing, which gives it more flexibility as it funds expansion.

Risks and Challenges

Heavy capital spending: FY27 capex guidance of up to AUD 5.75 billion could weigh on free cash flow and increase investor focus on funding discipline. InvestingPro data shows the company is quickly burning through cash, a key consideration for investors monitoring liquidity. The stock’s beta of 1.95 also signals higher volatility than the broader market, amplifying risk during uncertain periods.Execution risk: The company must deliver large projects on time and on budget while converting contracts into billing revenue.Regulatory uncertainty: Proposed energy reforms in Australia could affect future projects, even if current contracted capacity is not affected.Accounting complexity: Investment property reclassification and straight-line revenue recognition may make reported results harder to compare with earlier periods.Customer concentration: A large share of contracted capacity is tied to hyperscale and AI customers, which makes the business dependent on a relatively small number of large counterparties.

Q&A

Analysts focused on valuation, customer mix, yield on cost and the timing of future project financing.

Questions centered on whether NEXTDC can sustain a 10% or higher yield on cost for hyperscale and AI projects. Scroggie said the economics are now more visible because the company is doing build-to-suit projects at scale, rather than traditional colocation where timing and fill rates were less certain.

Analysts also asked about the mix of customers in the order book. Management said most contracted capacity is with double-A credit-rated counterparties or better, and that neocloud customers are backstopped by Nvidia for a decade or more.

Another topic was the timing of activation. Scroggie said FY27 numbers are already locked in and that the company expects 73 megawatts under construction to activate quickly, though not in a traditional cloud-style ramp.

Questions also touched on debt covenants and financing flexibility. Tomaszewski said subordinated capital is treated as subordinated to senior lenders, and that project-level financing could be used for assets such as S4 and S7.

Analysts further asked about energy regulation and future development approvals. Management said current FY27 guidance does not depend on approvals or power arrangements that are not already in place, though future projects will depend on the regulatory process and customer arrangements.
