Lenovo Group reported record first-quarter revenue of $26.9 billion for fiscal 2026/27, topping expectations by more than 20%, but reported earnings per share came in well below forecasts after a large non-cash warrant revaluation loss. The company said adjusted net income rose 176% from a year earlier to $1.1 billion, while adjusted operating income climbed 141% to $1.5 billion. Shares closed the regular session at $73.77, up 1.79% from the previous close, and were unchanged in after-hours trading.

Lenovo said the quarter was the strongest in its history, with record results across all three major business groups. The company&rsquo;s Intelligent Devices Group, which includes PCs and smartphones, generated $17.1 billion in revenue, up 27% from a year earlier. Infrastructure Solutions Group posted $8.5 billion in revenue, up 98%, while Solutions and Services Group reached $2.9 billion, up 28%.

The results suggest Lenovo is benefiting from a mix of demand for AI infrastructure, continued strength in PCs and smart devices, and a growing services business. The company said AI-related revenue now accounts for more than a third of total sales, a sign that its strategy is shifting from a traditional hardware model toward a broader AI platform.

Lenovo also said it gained PC market share for the 12th straight quarter and widened its lead over the No. 2 vendor for the 10th consecutive quarter. In servers, it said it rose to No. 2 globally in the x86 market. The company framed the quarter as evidence that it can grow while maintaining profitability, even in a difficult cost environment.

Lenovo reported adjusted EPS of -$1.01, compared with analysts&rsquo; forecast of $0.683. That was a shortfall of $1.693 per share, or a negative surprise of 247.88%. Revenue, however, came in at $26.94 billion versus a forecast of $22.41 billion, beating estimates by $4.53 billion, or 20.21%.

The earnings miss was driven by accounting items rather than core operating weakness. Lenovo said its reported loss reflected a $1.7 billion non-cash fair value loss from warrant revaluation and $30 million of notional interest from convertible bonds. On an adjusted basis, the company delivered record profit and margin expansion.

That makes the quarter more complicated than the headline EPS figure suggests. Investors often look past non-cash charges when the underlying business is improving, and Lenovo&rsquo;s adjusted results, revenue beat and margin gains point in that direction.

Lenovo&rsquo;s shares closed at $73.77, up 1.79% from the previous close of $72.47. After-hours trading showed no further move, leaving the stock flat at $73.77. The shares are now near the top of their 52-week range of $21 to $74.59, sitting about 1.1% below the high and roughly 251% above the low.

The muted after-hours reaction suggests investors may have been weighing the strong revenue and adjusted profit performance against the reported EPS miss and the large non-cash loss. The stock&rsquo;s strong run into the results also likely left less room for a sharp upside move.

Management said Lenovo is on track to reach its $100 billion revenue target sooner than originally expected. The company said it now believes it can hit that goal within the current fiscal year, ahead of its earlier two-year timeline.

Executives also pointed to a longer-term target of $130 billion in annual revenue and a net margin goal above 5%. The company said the path to that margin target will be supported by a larger contribution from Infrastructure Solutions Group and the eventual ramp-up of Solutions and Services Group.

For the near term, Lenovo expects the PC market to contract by about 15% in units in the second half of the fiscal year, with commercial demand likely holding up better than consumer demand. It also expects overall smartphone shipments to fall about 20%, though it said Motorola should offset unit declines with higher average selling prices and a richer product mix.

In infrastructure, Lenovo said its AI server pipeline has reached $54 billion, more than doubling from the prior quarter. The company also highlighted new manufacturing capacity in North Carolina and deeper partnerships with NVIDIA, AMD and Intel. &ldquo;Lenovo just delivered the best quarter in our entire history,&rdquo; Chairman and Chief Executive Yuanqing Yang said. He said the results validate the company&rsquo;s strategy and execution, and added that AI is now embedded across products, services and operations.

Chief Financial Officer Winston Cheng said the quarter showed &ldquo;record revenue, record profitability, and AI-related revenue that now represents 35% of the group.&rdquo; He said the results were not a one-time event but reflected the company&rsquo;s long-term operating model.

Luca Rossi, who leads the Intelligent Devices Group, said Lenovo expects the PC market to shrink in the second half of the year but believes the company can keep gaining share and offset lower unit sales with higher prices and a better mix of premium and AI PCs.

Analysts focused on three main issues: the path to Lenovo&rsquo;s long-term margin target, the outlook for memory prices, and the durability of the company&rsquo;s AI infrastructure growth.

On margins, management said Infrastructure Solutions Group is becoming a more important profit engine and should help Lenovo move toward a net margin above 5%. On memory costs, executives said supply is likely to remain tight through at least next year, but argued that Lenovo&rsquo;s scale and global supply chain give it an advantage.

Questions about the AI server pipeline centered on how quickly it can turn into revenue. Lenovo said the $54 billion figure includes customer engagements and future orders, and that demand is being driven by enterprise AI, private AI clouds and a strong server refresh cycle.

Analysts also asked how quickly enterprise AI adoption is reaching the services business. Lenovo said its Solutions and Services Group has now posted 21 straight quarters of double-digit revenue growth, helped by AI-powered workplace tools, AI factory services and long-term contracts.
