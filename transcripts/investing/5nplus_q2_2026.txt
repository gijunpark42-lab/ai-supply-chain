5N Plus reported stronger-than-expected second-quarter 2026 results, with earnings of $0.22 a share on revenue of $122.4 million, beating Wall Street estimates of $0.1619 a share and $113.3 million in sales. The Montreal-based advanced materials company said revenue rose 28% from a year earlier, while net earnings increased to $19.7 million. Shares were up 1.21% to $32.58 in recent trading, leaving the stock well above its 52-week low of $14.18 but still below its high of $49.97.

5N Plus said it delivered a strong quarter despite higher input costs and temporary operational inefficiencies. The company benefited from higher volumes in renewable energy, a more favorable product mix in space power and stronger sales of bismuth-based products in Performance Materials.

The quarter showed broad top-line strength. Revenue climbed to $122.4 million from $95.5 million a year earlier, while year-to-date revenue reached $240.3 million, up 30% from the first half of 2025. Net earnings rose to $19.7 million from $15.2 million, and adjusted EBITDA increased to $26.6 million from $24.2 million.

The company also highlighted strong demand visibility. Specialty Semiconductors remains fully booked for 2026 and 2027, while backlog in that segment is at the maximum defined level of 365 days. Performance Materials also posted solid growth, although management said the business typically weakens in the second half as customers reduce inventories.

5N Plus beat analyst expectations on both earnings and revenue. The company earned $0.22 a share versus a consensus estimate of $0.1619, a surprise of $0.0581 a share, or 35.89%. Revenue came in at $122.36 million, above the $113.28 million forecast by $9.08 million, or 8.02%.

The size of the beat was solid, especially on the bottom line. It came as revenue growth remained strong and the company continued to benefit from demand in renewable energy and space-related applications. At the same time, gross margin narrowed because of higher metal input costs, chemical costs and unplanned maintenance. That means investors may view the quarter as a strong sales beat with some margin caution attached.

Compared with the same period a year earlier, the company also improved meaningfully. EPS rose about 29%, and revenue increased 28%, showing that the business is still growing at a healthy pace even in a difficult cost environment.

The stock was up 1.21% to $32.58 in recent trading. The move was modest, suggesting investors welcomed the earnings beat but remained focused on margin pressure and cautious guidance. Despite the muted daily reaction, shares have surged 161% over the past year and are up nearly 84% year-to-date, according to InvestingPro data. The platform’s Fair Value analysis suggests the stock may be overvalued at current levels, placing it among companies on the most overvalued list.

At the current price, the shares are trading far above the 52-week low of $14.18 and below the 52-week high of $49.97. That places the stock in the middle-to-upper part of its annual range, reflecting a market that has already recognized much of the company&rsquo;s recent improvement.

The limited reaction may also reflect the balance between good demand trends and ongoing cost pressures. Revenue and earnings both beat estimates, but gross margin fell 430 basis points year over year, and operating cash flow was negative in the quarter. Those factors can temper enthusiasm even after a strong report.

Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $100 million to $105 million. Year-to-date adjusted EBITDA of $55.8 million puts the company slightly above the midpoint of that range.

The company said it expects second-half gross margins to remain close to the second-quarter level of 30.3%, within about one percentage point. Management said higher metal input costs and chemical costs should continue to pressure margins, although some metal costs are expected to be recovered with at least a two-quarter lag.
