PVA TePla AG said second-quarter revenue rose 8% from a year earlier and 20% from the first quarter, but profit fell sharply as one-off charges weighed on results and investors sent the stock down 13.33%. The German semiconductor equipment maker reported Q2 revenue of EUR 65.8 million, or about $66 million, slightly below the $67 million revenue forecast, while EBITDA dropped to EUR 3 million from EUR 6.7 million in Q1. Management said order intake climbed more than 80% in the first half and confirmed a stronger second half, but the near-term earnings pressure overshadowed the demand outlook.

PVA TePla&rsquo;s second quarter showed a clear recovery from a soft start to the year. Revenue improved sequentially and year over year, and gross margin rose to 30% from 28.5% in Q1. The company said demand remained solid in both of its main businesses, Material Solutions and Metrology.

Still, profitability weakened. EBITDA fell as the company absorbed non-period expenses, including a provision for possible customer compensation in Material Solutions and a valuation adjustment tied to its long-term incentive program. Management described those items as non-recurring, but they reduced the quarter&rsquo;s earnings power and likely shaped investor reaction.

The business mix also mattered. Metrology continued to gain importance, while Material Solutions remained under pressure from underutilization and projects that are still moving through engineering and sourcing phases. That split helped explain why revenue improved faster than profit.

PVA TePla&rsquo;s reported Q2 revenue of EUR 65.8 million compared with a forecast of $67 million, implying a shortfall of about $1 million, or roughly 1.5%. EPS was not provided in the available data, so a per-share comparison could not be made.

The revenue miss was small, but the market appeared to focus more on profitability. EBITDA fell by more than half from the first quarter, and management said the quarter included nearly EUR 4 million in non-period expenses. That combination likely mattered more than the modest revenue gap.

The quarter also fit a broader pattern of improvement in sales but uneven profit conversion. PVA TePla had a weak first quarter, then posted a strong sequential rebound in Q2. Even so, the company still needs to show that higher order intake can translate into more stable earnings.

The stock fell 13.33% to $29.9 after the report, placing it in the lower half of its 52-week range of $18.65 to $46.74. The decline suggests investors were disappointed by the drop in EBITDA and the near-term earnings profile, even though order trends improved. The sharp move reflects the company’s volatile nature, with a beta of 1.61. Despite the recent pullback, shares remain up 52% over the past year and 31% year-to-date, according to InvestingPro data. The reaction was larger than the revenue miss alone would normally suggest. That points to concern over margins, one-off charges and the size of the expected jump in second-half earnings. The company’s sky-high P/E ratio of 312 suggests investors had priced in strong earnings growth, making any near-term profit disappointment particularly painful. Management said Q3 revenue should be similar to Q2, with a stronger Q4, which may have left some investors waiting longer for a clearer profit recovery.

Management confirmed its full-year 2026 guidance and said EBITDA is expected in the lower half of the range. The company expects Q3 revenue to be roughly in line with Q2, followed by an acceleration in Q4 as larger orders move into production.

CEO Jalin Ketter said the company is executing its plan and pointed to the stronger order environment. &ldquo;We are on track, and this is good news because we are executing what we set to do,&rdquo; he said.

He also highlighted the scale of demand in the first half. &ldquo;Our order intake increased by more than 80% year-on-year, and both segments, Material Solutions and Metrology, supported this outcome,&rdquo; Ketter said.

CFO Markus Gro&szlig; emphasized the quarter&rsquo;s margin pressure but said the company has visibility into the second half. &ldquo;The EBITDA in the second quarter reduced from EUR 6.7 million down to EUR 3 million. Here we are having non-period expenses, which impacted the second quarter of a little less than EUR 4 million,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused on the pace of second-half growth, the size of the Q4 earnings jump and the durability of customer demand.

Several questions centered on Metrology, where management said order intake should rise step by step rather than in a single large burst. Executives said many qualification processes are already complete and that the business has visibility into orders extending into 2028 and partly into 2029.

Another area of interest was indium phosphide, a market tied to AI and data center communications. Ketter said the company sees a base-case market of 500 to 600 furnaces over 2027 to 2030, with pricing in the low single-digit million-euro range per system.

Analysts also pressed management on the sharp implied EBITDA improvement needed in Q4. CFO Markus Gro&szlig; said the company has good visibility on gross margins and order timing, while acknowledging that execution risks remain, including possible delivery delays or customer changes.

Questions about the customer compensation provision in Material Solutions drew a limited response. Management said it was an individual, non-structural issue tied to one customer and not a broader problem.
