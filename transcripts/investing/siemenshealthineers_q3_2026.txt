Siemens Healthineers said third-quarter revenue rose 7.6% from a year earlier, helped by strong demand for imaging and therapy equipment, but it lowered its full-year revenue outlook as Diagnostics remained weak, especially in China. The company also raised its adjusted earnings guidance after tariff refunds lifted profit in the quarter. The shares fell 33.64% to $38.05 in premarket trading, down from a previous close of $57.34, putting the stock near the lower end of its 52-week range.

Siemens Healthineers described the quarter as one of mixed performance. Imaging and Precision Therapy supported growth, while Diagnostics remained under pressure from structural changes in China and dilution from older platforms in North America.

The company said Synergetic Core revenue, which includes Imaging and Precision Therapy, grew 5% in the quarter. Precision Therapy advanced 9%, helped by momentum in Advanced Therapies. Imaging rose only 2.3%, which management blamed on difficult comparisons and temporary revenue shifts into the fourth quarter.

Diagnostics continued to decline, with management pointing to market rebasing in China and legacy platform pressure. China revenue fell 10% in the quarter. EMEA returned to growth, while the Americas and Asia Pacific faced tougher comparisons.

Profitability held up better than revenue. The group adjusted operating margin was 15.4% excluding tariff refunds. Management said tariff refunds added about EUR 0.15 to EPS in the quarter and helped cash generation.

The company did not provide actual EPS or revenue figures in the data available for comparison with the Street forecast. Analysts had expected EPS of $0.5301 and revenue of $5.65 billion.

Even without a direct earnings surprise, the call showed why investors may have reacted negatively. Management lowered the revenue outlook for the year, and the quarter&rsquo;s profit strength was helped by tariff refunds rather than by pure operating leverage alone. That can make the earnings quality look less durable to investors.

The quarter also appeared to be weaker than the company&rsquo;s recent growth pattern in Imaging, where management had expected a stronger run rate. By contrast, the order book and cash flow figures were solid, which may have softened the impact of the weaker revenue outlook.

Siemens Healthineers shares fell sharply in premarket trading, dropping 33.64% to $38.05 from $57.34. That is a decline of $19.29 per share.

The stock is now trading near the lower end of its 52-week range of $32.82 to $49.69. The move suggests investors focused more on the reduced revenue outlook and the weakness in Diagnostics than on the stronger profit and cash flow figures.

The size of the decline is unusual and points to a strong negative reassessment of the company&rsquo;s near-term growth profile. No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to judge whether the move came with unusually heavy turnover.

Management lowered full-year revenue growth guidance to 3.5% to 4% from 4.5% to 5%, citing continued weakness in Diagnostics. It said Synergetic Core remains on track for mid-single-digit growth.

Adjusted EPS guidance for fiscal 2026 was raised to EUR 2.35 to EUR 2.45 from EUR 2.20 to EUR 2.30, reflecting the EUR 0.15 benefit from tariff refunds. Management said the underlying earnings outlook was otherwise unchanged.
