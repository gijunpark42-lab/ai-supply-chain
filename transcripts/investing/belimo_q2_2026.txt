BELIMO reported a strong first half of 2026, with sales rising to CHF 676 million, up 29.7% in local currency and 20.5% in Swiss francs, as data center cooling demand, pricing actions and broad-based growth lifted results. EBIT increased 19% to CHF 153 million and net income rose 23% to CHF 125 million, but the shares fell 4.99% to $762 from $802, suggesting investors were unsettled by softer visibility for the second half and continued heavy investment needs.

BELIMO said the first half was &ldquo;extremely successful,&rdquo; and the numbers support that view. Revenue growth accelerated across the business, with volume and mix contributing 22.3 percentage points and pricing adding 7.3 points, while foreign exchange reduced reported growth by 9.2 points. The company said it was able to offset tariffs and FX pressure through pricing and operational efficiency.

The strongest momentum came from data center cooling, where liquid cooling has become the standard for many hyperscaler builds. The company said the vertical contributed more than half of absolute sales growth in the half. Control valves, the largest product line, posted especially strong growth, while sensors and meters also expanded quickly from a smaller base.

Regionally, the Americas generated CHF 341 million, or half of total sales, and grew 35% in local currency. EMEA contributed CHF 240 million, while Asia-Pacific rose 58% in local currency to CHF 95 million, helped by OEM export business.

No consensus forecast was provided for the period, so a direct earnings surprise cannot be calculated. Even so, the reported figures point to a very strong half-year performance. Revenue growth near 30% in local currency, along with EBIT growth of 19% and net income growth of 23%, suggests BELIMO outperformed a typical industrial growth profile.

The company also preserved profitability. The EBIT margin stayed at 22.5% despite higher costs, FX pressure and tariff-related headwinds. That indicates pricing power and operating leverage remained intact. Compared with a more normal industrial cycle, this is a strong result, especially given the company&rsquo;s comments that growth was driven by both broad demand and a fast-expanding data center market.

The stock fell 4.99% to $762 from $802 after the report. The move left the shares well below the 52-week high of $975, but still above the 52-week low of $608.5. The decline suggests investors focused less on the strong half-year numbers and more on the decision to withdraw quantitative full-year revenue guidance. The valuation backdrop may also be weighing on sentiment&mdash;BELIMO trades at a P/E ratio of 54.3, and InvestingPro analysis indicates the stock is currently overvalued relative to its Fair Value. Investors can explore similar valuation concerns across the market on InvestingPro’s Most Overvalued stocks list.

Management said H2 growth should remain strong in absolute terms, but percentage growth will likely slow because of tougher comparisons and a lower pricing contribution. The company also said capital spending will remain elevated and working capital will take time to normalize. Those points may have weighed on sentiment, even as the underlying demand picture remained healthy.

BELIMO said it expects continued strong demand in the second half, but growth rates should be lower than in H1. Management pointed to two main reasons: tougher year-earlier comparisons and a smaller pricing benefit in H2. The company expects EBIT margin to remain above 20% in the second half.

Capital spending is expected to stay high, with H2 capex likely similar to H1&rsquo;s roughly CHF 45 million. BELIMO also expects elevated investment to continue in 2027 and 2028 as it expands capacity in Switzerland and the U.S.

On the strategic side, the company introduced its first data center-only product in H1, a stainless steel Energy Valve built for liquid cooling systems. It also opened a U.S. data center experience center and said it will continue rolling out new digital products through 2026.

Management withdrew quantitative full-year revenue guidance because of uncertainty around the timing and pace of data center deployment. It said it expects to return to more specific guidance when market visibility improves.

&ldquo;The first half year 2026 was extremely successful for BELIMO,&rdquo; CFO Markus Sch&uuml;rch said, adding that sales rose close to 30% in local currency to CHF 676 million. The comment framed the half as a broad-based success, not just a one-off gain from one market.

Sch&uuml;rch also said the company was able &ldquo;not only [to] compensate for the tariffs, but also offset significant FX effects&rdquo; during the period. That suggests pricing and execution helped protect margins despite a difficult external backdrop.

On data centers, he said the company is a technology leader with first-mover advantages and that the new stainless steel Energy Valve was developed closely with hyperscalers. He also said the market remains global and standardized, which supports BELIMO&rsquo;s position as the segment grows.

Analysts focused heavily on data center growth, working capital and guidance. Questions centered on how much of H2 growth could come from data centers, whether the company was seeing competitive pressure, and why quantitative guidance was removed.

Management said data center demand remains strong, but actual deployment is lagging order entry because of execution constraints and power limits. It said there is no evidence of stocking and that the market is still driven by real build-outs.

Analysts also asked about the mix between hyperscalers and co-locators. BELIMO said hyperscalers remain the main driver of liquid cooling adoption, though some co-locators are moving into the segment. In Europe, the company said retrofit remains a major driver, especially in EMEA, where weak new construction has pushed retrofit to more than 60% of the business.

Questions on competition drew a firm response. Management said BELIMO remains the technology leader and expects current stainless steel technology to stay dominant, despite emerging alternatives such as polymer-based valves. It also said the data center market is global, with broadly similar standards across Western markets.
