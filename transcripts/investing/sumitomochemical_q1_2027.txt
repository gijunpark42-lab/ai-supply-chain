Sumitomo Chemical said first-quarter profit improved sharply in fiscal 2026, with core operating income more than doubling from a year earlier and net income swinging back into the black. Still, the shares fell 5.93% to $482.2 from $512.6, suggesting investors were not fully convinced by the company&rsquo;s unchanged full-year outlook and the temporary nature of some gains. The company reported core operating income of JPY 62.3 billion, up from JPY 27.7 billion a year earlier, while net income attributable to owners of the parent rose to JPY 40.8 billion from a loss of JPY 4.5 billion.

Sumitomo Chemical posted a strong start to fiscal 2026, with sales revenue rising 9.9% year over year to JPY 578.2 billion. Operating income increased to JPY 61 billion from JPY 25.4 billion a year earlier, while core operating income rose to JPY 62.3 billion.

The improvement was broad, but not evenly distributed. Essential & Green Materials benefited from better margins at PetroRabigh and temporary inventory valuation gains. Agro & Life Solutions improved on stronger methionine pricing and better feed-additive margins. ICT & Mobility Solutions was mixed: semiconductor processing materials performed well, but display-related products weakened.

The company also reported a stronger balance sheet. Total assets rose to JPY 3,613.5 billion, interest-bearing liabilities fell slightly, and equity increased to JPY 1,412.4 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio improved to 0.80 times from 0.93 times at the end of fiscal 2025.

The supplied forecast called for revenue of $588.65 billion and EPS of $6.48, but the reported actual EPS and revenue figures were not included in the earnings table. As a result, a direct beat-or-miss calculation against that forecast cannot be made from the available data.

Even without that comparison, the operating results point to a strong quarter. Core operating income rose 125.3% year over year, and net income turned from a loss to a profit. That kind of improvement is significant, especially because the company said the quarter was its second-best Q1 on record for both core operating income and net income.

The scale of the year-over-year gain suggests a much stronger quarter than the same period last year, though investors may have been looking for more clarity on the full-year path.

The stock fell 5.93% to $482.2 from a previous close of $512.6, a decline of $30.4. The move left the shares about 22.9% below their 52-week high of $625 and about 26.5% above the 52-week low of $381.3. Sumitomo Chemical kept its full-year guidance unchanged and said it remains difficult to predict the future amid Middle East turmoil. Some of the quarter&rsquo;s gains also came from temporary factors, including inventory valuation gains that are expected to reverse in the next quarter.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to assess whether the move came with unusual activity.

Management said it expects to reach the full-year net income forecast announced in May by the end of the first half, implying a softer second half. The company did not revise its full-year forecast, citing uncertainty around Middle East developments.

He also noted that the company is not selling much in the spot market. &ldquo;We sell to our customers based on the contract, and we refer to spot price and decide on our prices. There are some time lags,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused heavily on the drivers of the quarter and the outlook for the second quarter and beyond.

Questions centered on Agro & Life Solutions, where management said Q1 profit was driven mainly by methionine feed additives, while crop protection was outside peak season. Executives said Q2 should benefit from stronger crop-protection demand in North America, Latin America and India, with crop protection expected to contribute more than half of the projected JPY 35 billion profit increase from Q1 to Q2.

Another major topic was ICT & Mobility Solutions. Analysts asked why display-related products were weak even as semiconductor materials improved. Management said the segment is being reshaped, with large-size display operations being wound down while semiconductor materials continue to grow. Executives also said fixed-cost increases tied to investment are limiting near-term profit growth.

Questions also focused on Essential & Green Materials, especially the Q1-to-Q2 drop expected after inventory valuation gains fade. Management said PetroRabigh should contribute more in Q2, but the reversal of inventory gains will weigh on results.

Analysts further pressed on methionine pricing and capacity. Management said the company is operating at full 180,000 tons a year and does not sell much in the spot market, instead pricing through contracts with a lag.

Finally, investors asked about Singapore plant utilization. Management said it expects the current operating level to continue and does not plan a major increase in utilization.
