WEG reported second-quarter 2026 earnings that topped Wall Street expectations on both profit and sales, but the market response was cautious. The Brazilian industrial equipment maker posted earnings of $0.3605 a share, slightly above the $0.3585 forecast, and revenue of $10.14 billion, ahead of the $9.97 billion estimate. Even so, the stock fell 2.95% to $45.36 from the previous close of $46.74, suggesting investors were more focused on slower year-over-year growth, tariff pressure and near-term margin risks than on the modest beat.

WEG said second-quarter revenue was pressured by two main factors: the absence of centralized solar project deliveries seen a year earlier and a stronger Brazilian real, which reduced reported results in local currency. Still, the company said demand remained healthy abroad, especially in North America and Europe, where industrial equipment, transformers and data center-related products continued to perform well.

The company&rsquo;s international business grew 11.8% in local currencies and 14.7% in U.S. dollars, helping offset weakness in Brazil. Management said domestic industrial activity improved during the quarter, with stronger demand for short-cycle equipment such as low-voltage motors and gearbox reducers, as well as long-cycle products tied to large pulp and paper projects.

WEG also said its product mix was favorable, with less exposure to lower-margin renewable energy projects than in earlier periods. That helped support earnings quality even as the company invested heavily in capacity expansion.

WEG&rsquo;s earnings per share came in at $0.3605, compared with a forecast of $0.3585. That is a beat of $0.0020 a share, or 0.56%. Revenue reached $10.14 billion versus expectations of $9.97 billion, beating the forecast by $170 million, or 1.71%.

The results were positive, but the size of the beat was not large. In that sense, the quarter looked more like a confirmation of steady execution than a major upside surprise. The company also faced a tougher comparison base, since last year&rsquo;s quarter included centralized solar project deliveries that did not repeat this year.

Management said the quarter&rsquo;s profitability remained strong despite tariff costs, higher raw material prices and increased personnel expense tied to factory expansion. That helped keep the earnings beat intact, even though reported growth was not especially strong.

WEG shares fell 2.95% to $45.36 from $46.74, a decline of $1.38. The stock is now trading in the lower half of its 52-week range of $35.25 to $54.53. The move suggests investors were not fully satisfied with the quarter, despite the earnings beat.

The decline may reflect concern about several issues raised on the call: U.S. tariffs, foreign exchange pressure, and the cost of ramping up new factories. The market also may be waiting for clearer evidence that revenue growth will reaccelerate later in the year.

The stock&rsquo;s drop stands out because the company described demand as strong in several key markets, including data centers, grid infrastructure and industrial equipment. Still, investors often react more to the pace of growth and margin outlook than to a small earnings surprise.

Management said it expects revenue growth to improve in the second half of 2026 as comparisons become easier, especially in Brazil, where the solar project base should normalize. The company also expects international demand to remain strong.

WEG reiterated its BRL 3.6 billion capital expenditure plan for 2026. It has already spent BRL 1.4 billion in the first half and expects more spending in the second half because of seasonal patterns. Investments are going to transformer plants in Mexico, Colombia and the U.S., as well as the Betim expansion in Brazil, a new battery energy storage systems factory in Itaja&iacute; and other capacity projects.

The company said its T&D capacity expansion is roughly 100% above the 2023-2024 baseline when fully completed. Betim is expected to add 10% to 15% of the announced capacity in mid-2026, while the Mexico and Colombia plants should come on line in early 2027. Management said these factories should reach optimized profitability in 2027-2028.

WEG also sees growth potential in battery energy storage systems. It said current capacity is 1 gigawatt, and the new Itaja&iacute; factory should add 2 gigawatts when completed in 2027. The company’s financial foundation remains solid, with InvestingPro assigning a "GREAT" Financial Health score of 3.08 out of 5. Notably, WEG has raised its dividend for eight consecutive years, underscoring management’s commitment to shareholder returns. Andr&eacute; Lu&iacute;s Rodrigues, vice president of finance and administration, said the company largely offset the effects that hurt revenue in the first quarter by growing abroad. He also said ROIC improved, underscoring the quality of WEG&rsquo;s investments and capital discipline.

Andr&eacute; Menegueti Salgueiro, finance and investor relations officer, said the EBITDA margin stayed at 21.8% despite higher raw material costs, U.S. import tariffs and personnel expenses linked to expansion.

Rodrigues also addressed tariffs directly, saying that as of July 22, Section 301 tariffs had risen to 25% and could become more complex depending on how they interact with other rules. He said WEG has responded by shifting production toward Mexico and the U.S. to reduce exposure.

Analysts focused heavily on tariffs, pricing and the timing of new plant ramp-ups. Questions centered on how WEG is handling the move from 50% tariffs earlier in the year to the current 25% Section 301 regime, and how much more pressure could come if rules are applied cumulatively.

Management said it is using parametric pricing and contract-by-contract negotiations to pass through commodity and tariff costs where possible. It also said the company has shifted more U.S.-bound supply to Mexico and its own U.S. plants, reducing the share coming from Brazil.

Another major topic was the expansion of transformer capacity. Management said the Betim plant should start contributing more meaningfully in 2027, while the Mexico and Colombia facilities should follow later. Executives said the new plants should eventually reach profitability similar to WEG&rsquo;s existing T&D operations.

Analysts also asked about data center demand, where WEG sees strong orders for transformers, alternators and cooling-related equipment. Management said it does not sell directly to data centers, but the broader demand trend appears favorable, especially for manufacturers with integrated product offerings.

Battery storage was another focus. WEG said it sees a growing Brazilian BESS market and believes its integrated solution model gives it an advantage as more international players enter the space.
