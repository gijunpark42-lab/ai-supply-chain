ABB Ltd. reported a mixed third quarter, with record margins and strong order growth offset by a miss on earnings and a modest revenue shortfall. The Swiss industrial group posted adjusted earnings of $0.6924 a share, below the $0.792 forecast, while revenue came in at $9.7 billion versus expectations of $9.77 billion. The stock fell 5.19% to $70.82 in premarket trading, down from the previous close of $74.70, as investors appeared to focus on the earnings miss even after the company reported its strongest third-quarter margin on record.

Key Takeaways

ABB said comparable order intake rose 40% to $12 billion, showing broad demand across electrification, motion and automation. Operational EBITA climbed 24% to $2 billion, with the group margin reaching a record 20.8% for a third quarter. EPS fell 2% from a year earlier because of non-cash FX hedge losses tied to the Rotork acquisition and weaker results from discontinued operations. Free cash flow reached $1.6 billion in the quarter and $3.7 billion for the first nine months, putting ABB on track for nearly $5 billion in full-year cash flow. Management highlighted rapid growth in data center-related orders and unveiled a new direct-current portfolio aimed at AI infrastructure.

Company Performance

ABB delivered a strong operational quarter by most measures. Revenue grew 14% on a comparable basis, helped by higher volumes and about 2% net pricing realization. The company said demand was especially strong in electrification, where order growth reached 70% and margins improved sharply.

The results also showed how ABB is benefiting from structural spending in grid upgrades, electrification and data centers. Orders in the Americas rose 70% on a comparable basis, with the U.S. up 82%. Europe grew 10%, while Asia, the Middle East and Africa rose 29%, including 25% growth in China.

ABB’s backlog ended the quarter at $32 billion, nearly equal to a full year of 2025 revenue. Management said about 40% of that backlog should convert into revenue next year, supporting visibility into 2027.

Financial Highlights

Revenue: $9.7 billion, up 14% on a comparable basis. Order intake: $12 billion, up 40% year over year. Operational EBITA: $2 billion, up 24% year over year. Operational EBITA margin: 20.8%, a record for a third quarter. EBIT margin: 19.0%, also a third-quarter record. ROCE: 29.9%, showing strong capital efficiency. EPS: $0.65, down 2% from a year earlier. Free cash flow: $1.6 billion, up 2% year over year.Return on Equity: 33%, demonstrating strong capital efficiency.Market Cap: $155.5 billion, reflecting ABB’s position as a prominent player in the Electrical Equipment industry. Year-to-date free cash flow: $3.7 billion. Backlog: $32 billion at quarter end.

Earnings vs. Forecast

ABB missed the analyst consensus on both earnings and revenue. EPS of $0.6924 came in $0.0996 below the $0.792 forecast, a shortfall of about 12.6%. Revenue of $9.7 billion missed the $9.77 billion estimate by $70 million, or about 0.7%.

The earnings miss was the more important one. Revenue was close to expectations, but the lower-than-expected bottom line likely reflected the market’s focus on reported profit rather than the company’s stronger operating indicators. ABB said the EPS decline was driven mainly by non-cash unrealized FX hedge losses linked to the Rotork acquisition and by lower earnings from discontinued operations.

Even so, the quarter was not weak in an operating sense. Order growth, margin expansion and cash generation all remained strong. The miss therefore looks more like a reporting and timing issue than a sign of broad business weakness.

Market Reaction

ABB shares fell 5.19% to $70.82 in premarket trading after the report, suggesting investors were disappointed by the EPS miss despite the strong operational results. The stock had closed at $74.70 before the release. The drop left the shares well below the 52-week high of $89.14, though still above the low of $53.78.

The reaction indicates that the market may have been expecting a cleaner beat after several quarters of strong demand and margin improvement. ABB’s record profitability and robust order book did not fully offset the weaker-than-expected reported earnings in early trading.

Outlook & Guidance

ABB kept its full-year 2026 outlook constructive. The company said comparable revenue growth should be in the low-teens range for the year, with operational EBITA margin expected to improve by at least 270 basis points from 2025. That outlook includes a gain of about 240 million Swiss francs from a real estate sale expected to be booked in the fourth quarter.

For the fourth quarter, ABB expects comparable revenue growth broadly in line with the third quarter. Management also said margins should decline sequentially, as is typical for the season, but by slightly less than the usual 150-basis-point drop.

By business area, ABB expects:

Electrification: high-teens to low-20% comparable revenue growth in Q4. Motion: revenue growth similar to Q3, with margin slightly improving sequentially. Automation: low- to mid-single-digit comparable revenue growth, with margins broadly stable.

The company also said it plans to keep annual capital spending around $1 billion through 2027 to 2029, as it expands manufacturing capacity and supports demand growth.

Executive Commentary

Chief Executive Morten Wierod said ABB “continued to see a strong market, with orders once again reaching just above $12 billion,” adding that the company “performed well in a vibrant market.”

He also pointed to the company’s profitability, saying the 20.8% operational EBITA margin was “the strongest Q3 margin ever for ABB” and that the 19% EBIT margin was also a third-quarter record.

On strategy, Wierod said ABB’s new joint venture with Mitsubishi Electric would make the company “the low-voltage market leader in Japan,” while also giving it a path to full ownership over time. He called the deal a way to expand ABB’s reach in one of the world’s largest electrification markets.

Wierod also highlighted ABB’s long-term investment in direct current technology, saying the company began solid-state R&D 15 years ago and now sees a major opportunity in AI data centers and other power-intensive applications.

Risks and Challenges

Reported earnings pressure: Non-cash FX hedge losses and discontinued operations reduced EPS, which can weigh on investor confidence. Margin seasonality: Management expects a sequential margin decline in Q4, even if it is smaller than usual. Execution risk in capacity expansion: ABB is spending heavily to expand manufacturing, and new capacity must come online smoothly. Data center concentration: A large share of recent growth is tied to a fast-moving market that can be volatile. Business mix and timing: Large project orders can shift between quarters, especially in automation and other long-cycle businesses.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on the durability of ABB’s data center orders. Management said the company uses standard risk controls for large projects, including advance payments and cancellation clauses, and sees no evidence of artificial order build-up.

Questions also centered on backlog conversion and whether ABB’s own capacity or customer timing would limit revenue growth. Management said both matter, but noted that the company is investing heavily in electrification capacity and is rarely the bottleneck.

Another area of interest was the Mitsubishi Electric joint venture. ABB said the deal is mainly about Japan, where it has had limited reach, and that the transaction gives it a stronger local position in low-voltage distribution.

Analysts also asked about free cash flow, which management said should approach $5 billion for the full year, helped by operating performance and a real estate sale, partly offset by capital spending and working capital needs.

In motion, questions focused on margin pressure and the role of Gamesa-related losses. Management said those issues are improving and expects margins to move higher sequentially.
