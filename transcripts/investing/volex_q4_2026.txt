Volex plc said full-year revenue rose to a record $1.243 billion in FY 2026, up 14.4% from a year earlier, while underlying operating margin improved to 10.2%, above its earlier target range. The company also reported underlying basic earnings per share of $0.435, up 20%, and said it reached its five-year revenue goal a year early. Shares fell 2.71% to $538 from $553 in the latest trading, leaving the stock below its recent levels even after the strong results.

Company PerformanceVolex delivered what executives called a year of “very significant progress,” with growth across several end markets and stronger profitability. Revenue increased 14.4% to $1.243 billion, while organic growth was 14.2%. Underlying operating profit rose 19.9% to $127.3 million.

The company said its performance reflected a mix of higher volumes, better operating efficiency and a richer product mix. That included more business from higher-margin, more complex products such as data center cables and power products.

The results also showed a more balanced business than some investors may assume. Complex Industrial Technology was the largest growth driver, but Off-Highway also grew strongly, and EV and Electrification added to the total. At the same time, Consumer Electricals and Medical were weaker, which shows that Volex is still managing uneven demand across its portfolio.

Earnings vs. ForecastThe company’s reported data did not include a direct EPS or revenue forecast for the period, so a standard beat-or-miss comparison is not available. Even so, the results were clearly strong against the company’s own long-term plan.

Revenue of $1.243 billion came in above the $1.2 billion target Volex had set for its five-year plan, and management said the goal was reached a full year early. Underlying operating margin of 10.2% also exceeded the earlier 9%-10% range. That suggests the company did not just grow, but also improved efficiency and pricing power.

Underlying basic EPS of $0.435 rose 20%, which is a solid gain for a business already operating at scale. The improvement in profit was slightly faster than revenue growth, showing operating leverage. That is usually a positive sign for investors because it means more of each sales dollar is turning into profit.

Despite the strong annual results, Volex shares fell 2.71% to $538 from $553. The stock remains well above its 52-week low of $327.5, but it is still below the 52-week high of $705.

The decline suggests investors may have focused on factors beyond the headline numbers. One possible issue is valuation after a strong run. Another is the company’s exposure to a few large customers, especially in data centers and EVs. Management said no customer outside the two largest accounts accounts for more than 4% of revenue, but concentration risk can still weigh on sentiment.

Broader market conditions may also have played a role. Management pointed to a U.S. tech-led sell-off and said some AIM-related selling could continue as the company prepares to move to the London Stock Exchange’s main market on July 24, 2026. The stock’s move was not extreme, which suggests measured caution rather than a sharp negative reassessment.

Volex said it is entering FY 2027 with strong momentum and strong demand in several end markets. Management described trading to date as in line with expectations.

The company’s medium-term plan calls for revenue of $2 billion and an operating margin of 12%. That plan includes about $500 million of organic growth and about $300 million from acquisitions. Management said the organic growth should come from share gains with existing customers, more complex products and expansion into North America.

Q&AAnalysts focused on whether Volex’s growth was too dependent on one data center customer. Management said the business is broader than that, pointing to 20% growth in Off-Highway, strong EV performance and 12% growth outside data centers in Complex Industrial Technology.

Questions also centered on the durability of data center demand. Rothschild said the opportunity could last five years or longer, while Boaden noted that Volex is also moving into power products for data centers, where its EV-related expertise may help.

Another topic was the move from AIM to the London Stock Exchange main market. Management said the company has outgrown AIM and expects the move to broaden its investor base and improve index inclusion. Rothschild said most of the AIM investor rotation has already happened, though some selling may continue before the switch.

Analysts also asked about Consumer Electricals, where management said the business is shifting toward higher-value harness products that can sell for five to 10 times the price of a power cord. In Medical, management said destocking should stabilize in FY 2027.

Questions on acquisitions drew a cautious response. Management said valuations have risen slightly, but the company remains selective and is working on several opportunities that could add capability, geography or customer access.
