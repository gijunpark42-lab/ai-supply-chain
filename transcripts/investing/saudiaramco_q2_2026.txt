Saudi Aramco reported a sharp rise in second-quarter profit as the world&rsquo;s largest oil exporter navigated one of the most severe disruptions in the history of the energy market. The company said adjusted net income climbed to $33.4 billion in Q2 2026, up 33% from a year earlier, while free cash flow reached $25.9 billion. Shares were last at $27.26, up 0.52% from the previous close of $27.12, leaving the stock near the top of its 52-week range.

Saudi Aramco said Q2 was one of the most difficult quarters in its history, but the company still delivered what it called exceptional financial results. The company benefited from strong crude pricing, resilient demand and the flexibility of its integrated business model.

Upstream adjusted EBIT rose 14% year over year to $50.9 billion, helped by realized crude prices of $108.1 a barrel, up 62% from a year earlier. Downstream also performed strongly, with Q2 adjusted EBIT of $6.2 billion, about twice the level of the same period last year. For the first half of 2026, downstream adjusted EBIT jumped 144% to $11.7 billion.

The company said its performance reflected not only higher prices but also operational resilience. It used its East-West Pipeline, export-route flexibility and geographically diversified assets to keep oil flowing while shipping through the Strait of Hormuz was heavily disrupted.
