Air Liquide said first-half 2026 sales rose as its electronics and large-industries businesses gained momentum, even as revenue came in slightly below Wall Street expectations. The French industrial gases company reported second-quarter revenue of $8.01 billion, missing the $8.11 billion forecast by about 1.23%. The stock was recently at $40.34, up 0.75% and trading close to its 52-week high of $41.24, suggesting investors remained broadly constructive on the company&rsquo;s outlook.

Air Liquide delivered what management described as a resilient first half, with growth supported by industrial demand, pricing gains and a fast-rising electronics pipeline tied to artificial intelligence and semiconductor investment. Comparable sales rose 2.6% in the first half, while Q2 comparable growth reached 3.5%, a clear step up from the first quarter. The company’s market capitalization stands at $125.15 billion, and it trades at a P/E ratio of 31.2. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock currently appears overvalued relative to its Fair Value&mdash;placing it among companies on the Most Overvalued list. An InvestingPro tip notes the company is trading at a high earnings multiple.

The company&rsquo;s performance was strongest in electronics, where project signings surged and demand from AI-related chip production remained robust. Large industries also benefited from new projects in the U.S. and other regions. Healthcare continued to provide steady support, while merchant gas pricing improved across regions.

The results suggest the company is benefiting from structural trends rather than a single short-term cycle. Air Liquide also said its acquisition of DIG Airgas, completed in January, was integrated ahead of schedule and added to growth.

Air Liquide&rsquo;s Q2 revenue of $8.01 billion fell short of the $8.11 billion consensus estimate by $100 million, or 1.23%. EPS was not provided in the available figures, so the comparison is limited to sales.

The miss was modest and came alongside a stronger operating backdrop. Comparable sales growth accelerated, margins improved and recurring profit rose. That combination suggests the revenue shortfall was not the result of broad weakness in the business. Instead, it appears to reflect a relatively small gap against expectations in a quarter where the company continued to execute well.

For investors, the more important signal may have been the company&rsquo;s forward momentum in electronics and its ability to expand margins despite currency headwinds and higher debt costs.

The stock recently traded at $40.34, up 0.75% from the previous close and near the top of its 52-week range of $32.88 to $41.24. That position suggests the market has already been rewarding Air Liquide for its growth profile and defensive qualities.

Because the shares are close to the high end of the range, a small revenue miss may have limited impact unless it is paired with weaker guidance or signs of slowing demand. Instead, investors appeared to focus on the company&rsquo;s stronger margin profile, rising electronics backlog and stable outlook for the second half.

No trading volume data was provided, so unusual activity cannot be confirmed.

Management said it expects second-half 2026 comparable sales growth to be &ldquo;similar or slightly better&rdquo; than the first half. The company also said electronics growth should remain in the 8% to 9% range for the rest of the year.

The company also reiterated its longer-term margin target, saying it remains on track to deliver 560 basis points of cumulative margin improvement between 2021 and 2027.

Air Liquide said its investment backlog stood at an unprecedented 6 billion euros, up 30% from a year earlier, with electronics accounting for about half of the 12-month opportunity portfolio. Management said the company will provide more detail on electronics growth dynamics at a digital Capital Markets Day on Oct. 5.

Fran&ccedil;ois Jackow, Air Liquide&rsquo;s chief executive, said: &ldquo;We are executing today and preparing for tomorrow. This is truly the inherent strength of Air Liquide.&rdquo; The comment reflected the company&rsquo;s focus on balancing current performance with long-term project wins.

He also said: &ldquo;In just the first six months of 2026, our electronics project decisions reached over EUR 1 billion. If you think about it, that’s one and a half times of our total for all 2025.&rdquo; That underscores how quickly the electronics business is scaling.

J&eacute;r&ocirc;me Pelletan, the chief financial officer, said the company&rsquo;s backlog is &ldquo;at an unprecedented EUR 6 billion,&rdquo; adding that it is &ldquo;very much well-balanced across electronics and large industry with strong regional and project diversity.&rdquo; The remark points to a broad pipeline rather than dependence on one market or customer.

Analysts focused heavily on helium supply, electronics growth and margin trends.

Questions centered on whether helium shortages could limit electronics growth in the second half. Management said the issue should remain manageable, citing restarted production in Qatar, storage options and alternative sourcing. Executives stressed that helium is not a constraint for the electronics business.

Analysts also asked why electronics sales growth of 8% to 9% trails semiconductor capital spending growth of about 25%. Management said sales reflect current fab production, while the company&rsquo;s own investment decisions in electronics rose 146% in the first half. It said the revenue benefit from those projects will come later, as new facilities ramp up.

Another topic was pricing. Analysts asked about the gap between stronger merchant pricing in the Americas and more moderate pricing in Europe. Management said pricing is managed globally but reflects local inflation and tariff conditions, with Europe also showing sequential improvement.

Finally, analysts asked about backlog levels and project size. Management said the backlog may stay relatively flat as new signings offset project startups, but emphasized that the pipeline is highly diversified and remains the strongest it has seen in years.
