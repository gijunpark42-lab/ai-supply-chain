Austria Technologie und Systemtechnik AG said first-quarter results for fiscal 2026/27 showed a sharp turnaround, with revenue rising about 40% year over year to roughly EUR 550 million and profitability improving across the business. The company also said it remains on track with full-year guidance, while its shares were up 2.43% to $143.4 in pre-market trading, after closing at $140. The stock remains well above its 52-week low of $17.7, though still below its high of $246.

Austria Technologie und Systemtechnik said the quarter reflected a successful transformation from a pure supplier into what management called a technology and innovation partner. The company benefited from strong demand in semiconductor substrates and related technologies, especially in microelectronics.

Microelectronics was the main growth engine, with revenue up 93% and EBITDA margin above 42%, according to management. Electronics Solutions also improved, though it remained below full-year expectations because of timing issues in passing through cost increases and continued investment in Austrian operations.

The company said its performance was supported by higher factory utilization, pricing gains and a more favorable mix of products tied to AI and advanced computing. Management also stressed that the business is becoming less dependent on any single customer or product line as more clients ramp complex substrate designs.

The company&rsquo;s earnings-vs.-forecast table did not include actual EPS or revenue figures for the quarter, so a direct beat-or-miss calculation versus consensus could not be made from that data alone. Still, the operating results described on the call were strong.

Management said EPS improved to EUR 1.93 from a loss of EUR -1.55 in the prior-year quarter, while revenue rose to about EUR 550 million. That points to a substantial improvement in both growth and profitability. The company also said EBIT in the quarter exceeded the full prior fiscal year, underscoring how sharply earnings momentum has improved.

Even without a direct consensus comparison, the scale of the turnaround suggests the quarter was well received operationally. An InvestingPro tip notes that analysts predict the company will be profitable this year, supporting management’s optimistic outlook despite the company posting a loss of $1.28 per share over the last twelve months. The main question for investors is whether the company can sustain this pace as new capacity comes online and capital spending remains elevated.

The stock rose 2.43% to $143.4 in pre-market trading, up $3.40 from the previous close of $140. The move suggests a positive but measured reaction rather than a sharp post-earnings surge. The stock’s recent momentum has been remarkable, with shares delivering a 619% return over the past year and a 338% gain year-to-date, according to InvestingPro data.

At the current price, the shares trade far above the 52-week low of $17.7 and below the 52-week high of $246. That places the stock in the upper part of its yearly range, reflecting strong investor interest in the company&rsquo;s growth story, but also leaving room for caution after a large run-up from the lows.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to determine whether the move came with unusually heavy activity.

Management confirmed full-year fiscal 2026/27 guidance for revenue growth of 45% to 55% in constant currency and EBITDA margin of 32% to 37%.

The company said growth should be driven mainly by volume from new customer agreements, with pricing contributing about one-third of the original guidance, or roughly 10%. Management said the updated outlook already includes contracts known to affect the current fiscal year, but additional deals could add upside if signed in time.

Capital spending is expected to total EUR 1.0 billion to EUR 1.2 billion this fiscal year. The company said the spending is tied to three major expansion projects:

Management said demand for co-packaged optics is far above current global supply and that the technology could add 5% to 10% to margins on relevant products. It also said integrated optics, the next generation of the technology, is under development and part of the longer-term roadmap.

Chief Executive Michael Mertin said the company is undergoing a broader shift in identity and strategy.

&ldquo;It&rsquo;s a very consequent way of developing a company from a pure supplier into a tech and innovation partner to its customers,&rdquo; he said.

He also pointed to the quarter&rsquo;s financial improvement: &ldquo;We had a very strong start into our new fiscal year, with a revenue of approximately EUR 550 million.&rdquo;

On profitability, Mertin said: &ldquo;Our EBITDA went up by 134% versus previous year, and we ended up with EUR 165 million EBITDA.&rdquo;

Chief Financial Officer Gerrit Steen said the company&rsquo;s expansion plan is backed by customer commitments. &ldquo;Capacity additions are supported, as you know, by long-term customer commitments, providing to AT&S visibility and allowing us to invest in a disciplined manner,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused on guidance conservatism, pricing, customer payments and the durability of demand.

One key question was how much pricing is embedded in the full-year outlook. Management said about one-third of the original guidance, or roughly 10%, came from pricing, while the upgrade mainly reflected new customer agreements and additional volume.

Another topic was customer financing and prepayments. Mertin said the company does not want business cases that create negative cash flow and that deals are structured to avoid diluting profitability over time.

Analysts also asked about the mix inside microelectronics. Management said most of the business is still substrate-based, with a large share tied to server and advanced server applications. The company said the mix can shift month to month depending on customer demand and material availability.

Questions on Kulim Three and future capacity drew a cautious answer. Management said no final decision has been made, but it is preparing for the possibility if customer negotiations and financing support a further expansion.

Finally, analysts pressed on co-packaged optics. Management said demand is much larger than current global capacity and called the technology a major part of the next growth phase, though not a meaningful contributor to this fiscal year.
