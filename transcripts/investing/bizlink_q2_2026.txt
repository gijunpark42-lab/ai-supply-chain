BizLink International said second-quarter revenue and profit rose sharply from a year earlier and improved from the first quarter, with earnings per share of TWD 15.28 topping the TWD 14.88 forecast and revenue of TWD 23.28 billion beating estimates of TWD 22.88 billion. The Taiwan-based cable and connectivity supplier also said net income and EPS reached quarterly records, while gross margin and operating margin expanded as manufacturing efficiency improved. The stock was up 1.11% to $2,275, near the top of its 52-week range.

BizLink reported a strong second quarter, with revenue of TWD 23.28 billion, up 37% from a year earlier and 12% from the first quarter. The company said the improvement reflected higher manufacturing utilization, better efficiency and progress in resolving issues that weighed on first-quarter profitability.

Net income attributable to shareholders reached TWD 2.98 billion, a quarterly high, while EPS climbed to TWD 15.28, also a record for the company. The results suggest BizLink is benefiting from a broader mix of growth businesses, including AI infrastructure, capital equipment and industrial markets.

The company framed its business as moving from a component supplier toward a systems solutions provider. That shift is important because it can raise content per customer program and deepen relationships with large industrial and technology clients. BizLink’s balance sheet supports this expansion, with a current ratio of 3.28 and a debt-to-equity ratio of just 0.01, reflecting minimal leverage. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock appears undervalued at current levels&mdash;placing it among companies on the platform’s Most Undervalued list, alongside comprehensive Fair Value estimates and financial health scores.

BizLink&rsquo;s Q2 EPS of TWD 15.28 came in above the TWD 14.88 forecast, a beat of TWD 0.40 per share. Revenue also exceeded expectations by TWD 0.40 billion, or roughly 1.7%.

The size of the beat was modest, but the quality of the quarter looked stronger than the headline numbers alone. Profitability improved sequentially, and management said the margin recovery reflected not only higher volume but also better manufacturing efficiency, lower material costs and improved productivity. That combination often matters more to investors than a small forecast beat because it suggests the improvement may be more durable.

BizLink shares were last at $2,275, up 1.11% from the previous close of $2,250. The stock added $25 in value and remained close to the upper end of its 52-week range of $912 to $3,010.

The move points to a mildly positive reaction. Investors appeared to welcome the earnings beat and the margin recovery, but the gain was not large enough to suggest a major revaluation. The stock has delivered a 50% return over the past year, reflecting growing confidence in the company’s AI infrastructure positioning. Management said the company remains constructive on demand, especially in AI infrastructure and capital equipment. BizLink said it has reasonable order visibility in capital equipment through the second quarter of 2027, and customer discussions are already starting to extend into 2028.

The company did not present a simple straight-line outlook for margins. Chief Financial Officer Charles Tsai said gross margin may fluctuate quarter to quarter because of product transitions, customer deployment cycles, foreign exchange and business mix. He said the goal is stable and reasonable margins while the revenue base grows.

BizLink also said it will keep investing in engineering, automation, NPI capabilities and selective capacity expansion, but only when customer demand, economics and strategic value justify the spending.

Chief Executive Felix Teng said the company&rsquo;s opportunity is increasingly defined by the problems it can solve rather than by any single product. He said AI infrastructure is moving from a phase focused on securing compute to a phase focused on deploying that compute at scale.

Teng also described optics as a structural extension of BizLink&rsquo;s data connectivity business, saying that &ldquo;almost every path toward larger and denser AI infrastructure requires more sophisticated optical connectivities.&rdquo;

Tsai emphasized discipline on capital spending, saying BizLink will not build capacity simply because demand is strong. &ldquo;Capital has to be supported by customer requirement and expected returns,&rdquo; he said.

During the question-and-answer session, analysts focused on three main topics: competition, optics and margins.

On competition, Chairman Roger Liang said BizLink is not trying to win on price alone. Instead, it aims to build deeper positions in markets where qualification is difficult and reliability matters. He said early participation gives the company an advantage because incumbents carry qualification history and customer relationships into the next technology cycle.

On optics, CEO Felix Teng said the business is seeing rising demand and inquiries. He noted that the opportunity goes beyond faster speeds such as 800G and 1.6T and includes denser architectures, optical circuit switching and MPO/CPO technologies.

On margins and capacity, CFO Charles Tsai said the second quarter reflected the expected recovery from first-quarter weakness, but he warned against assuming a straight-line improvement. He also said BizLink will continue to invest, but selectively, and will not automatically turn every increase in demand into more factory capacity.
