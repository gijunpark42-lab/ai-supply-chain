Comet Holdings AG said first-half 2026 sales rose 5.6% from a year earlier to CHF 239.8 million, with growth accelerating sharply in the second quarter as demand improved across semiconductor-related businesses. The Swiss industrial group reported higher EBITDA, a return to gross margin above 40%, and a book-to-bill ratio of 1.48, but also said free cash flow was negative CHF 19.3 million because of heavy investment in its new Penang facility. The stock fell 0.53% to $335.20 in early trading, after closing at $337, leaving it well below its 52-week high of $452.2 but far above the low of $167.

Comet&rsquo;s first-half results showed a business that is still expanding, but now with a clearer split between growth and investment. Sales rose modestly in the first half, yet the second quarter was much stronger, suggesting that demand improved as the period progressed. Management said the semiconductor market remained highly favorable, and that trend was reflected in order intake and sales momentum.

The company&rsquo;s performance was strongest in its Plasma Control Technologies division, where sales rose 6.9% in Swiss francs, or about 15% in constant currency. Industrial X-Ray Systems also improved, helped by restructuring and growing adoption of the CA20 platform. Industrial X-Ray Modules grew 10.6% in Swiss francs, though currency movements weighed on margins.

Comet&rsquo;s results also showed that the company is moving through a major investment phase. The Penang facility is now complete, but it contributed to higher capital spending, negative free cash flow and a temporary net debt position. Management described that as planned and temporary.

Comet did not provide comparable EPS and revenue figures against the forecast in the materials reviewed, so a direct beat-or-miss calculation was not available. The available forecast showed revenue expected at CHF 300.3 million, but the company&rsquo;s reported figures were presented on a half-year basis rather than as a single quarter result.

Even without a direct forecast comparison, the underlying trend was positive. Sales growth accelerated meaningfully in Q2, EBITDA expanded faster than revenue, and profitability improved. That combination usually matters more to investors than a narrow top-line comparison, especially when a company is in a heavy investment phase.

The stock slipped 0.53% to $335.20 from $337 in the latest session. The move was small, suggesting investors were not sharply surprised by the results. The shares remain in the upper half of their 52-week range, trading about 74.2% above the low and about 25.8% below the high. According to InvestingPro data, the stock has taken a bigger hit over the past week, reflecting the kind of volatility that has characterized its trading pattern. The platform notes that stock price movements have been quite volatile, a factor investors should weigh when considering position sizing.

The muted reaction may reflect a balance between stronger operating trends and near-term cash flow pressure. On the positive side, sales momentum, order growth and margin recovery support the long-term case. On the other hand, the company is spending heavily on capacity, which has pushed free cash flow negative and temporarily increased debt. Still, InvestingPro data shows Comet’s liquid assets exceed short-term obligations, and the company operates with only a moderate level of debt&mdash;suggesting the balance sheet can handle this investment phase without undue stress.

No unusual trading volume data was provided.

Comet kept its full-year 2026 guidance unchanged. It expects net sales of CHF 540 million to CHF 570 million and an EBITDA margin of 14% to 17% of sales. That margin range includes about 3 percentage points of one-time costs tied to the efficiency program and the Penang ramp-up. Excluding those items, adjusted EBITDA margin is expected to be 17% to 20%.

Management said the company should support output above CHF 1 billion a year through 2028 as capacity expansions are completed. CapEx for 2026 is expected to stay clearly above 7% of sales but clearly below 10%, before normalizing to a 4% to 7% range after Penang is fully ramped. The company has also maintained dividend payments for 26 consecutive years&mdash;a track record that speaks to management discipline. Chief Executive Stephan Haferl said the company saw &ldquo;a significant acceleration in momentum from the first quarter into the second quarter,&rdquo; pointing to stronger sales growth as the period progressed. He also said the semiconductor industry continues to show &ldquo;remarkable resilience and growth potential despite ongoing geopolitical challenges.&rdquo;

Chief Financial Officer Christian Witt said gross profit margin &ldquo;returned to a range we are used to, that means above 40%,&rdquo; adding that this was driven mainly by operating leverage. Management also highlighted the order book, with Haferl saying the book-to-bill ratio of 1.48 reflects &ldquo;robust demand and a healthy order pipeline&rdquo; supporting the sales outlook.

Analysts focused on three main areas: order sustainability, capacity, and the pace of new product adoption.

Questions centered on whether the strong book-to-bill ratio could continue. Management said order flow should remain healthy in the second half, although some H1 orders were pulled forward and will not repeat every quarter. It also said some orders already extend into 2027, especially in the PCT division.

Another key topic was capacity. Management said the Penang facility and other planned expansions should be enough to support output above CHF 1 billion a year through 2028. It added that there are no serious supply chain choke points at present.

Analysts also asked about the CA20 X-ray system. Management said the product family has now received more than 10 orders and is seeing repeat business from customers that have moved beyond testing and qualification. Lead times vary from three to four months for simpler applications to more than six months for complex ones.

Questions on pricing and margins brought out a clear message: Comet is using value-based pricing and has already implemented price increases across its product range in 2026, with more to come if needed. Management said 2027 should see lower one-time costs and a better margin profile as efficiency savings begin to flow through.
