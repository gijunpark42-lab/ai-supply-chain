Thales said first-half 2026 sales rose 7.8% organically to 10.9 billion euros, with adjusted EBIT up about 10% and free operating cash flow jumping to 1.8 billion euros, nearly three times last year&rsquo;s level. The French defense and aerospace group also lifted parts of its full-year outlook after a strong order book and better cash generation. In pre-market trading, the stock rose 2.66% to $233.6, up from $227.55, as investors appeared to welcome the stronger demand picture and balance-sheet improvement. The company, valued at a market capitalization of $54.8 billion, trades at a P/E ratio of 28.6. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock appears undervalued based on its Fair Value assessment, suggesting potential upside for investors.

Thales delivered a strong first half, led by defense, where demand remained robust across Europe, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific. Management said the defense business benefited from years of investment in industrial capacity and engineering, especially in sensors and effectors, which helped support higher sales and order intake.

The aerospace business also remained resilient, even after the cancellation of two geostationary telecom satellites reduced sales by 153 million euros. Excluding that one-off effect, underlying organic sales growth would have been 9.6%, according to management.

The company said the space segment was beginning to benefit from a restructuring plan launched in 2024, while cyber showed signs of recovery. That mix helped Thales post broad-based growth across all three segments.

- Effective tax rate: 25.8%, or 21% excluding a temporary French corporate tax effect.

Thales&rsquo; reported operating performance came in stronger than the market may have expected, but the provided forecast data did not include actual EPS or revenue figures for a direct comparison. The forecast called for EPS of $4.56 and revenue of $10.89 billion. Instead, management reported first-half sales of 10.9 billion euros and a sharp improvement in profitability and cash flow.

The most important takeaway was not a formal earnings surprise, but the scale of the operational improvement. Order intake growth, margin expansion and stronger cash generation all pointed to a business that is entering the second half with momentum.

The stock rose 2.66% in pre-market trading to $233.6 from $227.55, adding $6.05 per share. That put the shares about 10.7% above the 52-week low of $211, but still about 16.3% below the 52-week high of $279.3.

The move suggests investors reacted positively to the company&rsquo;s stronger order book, higher cash flow and improved guidance. The gain was notable but not extreme, which may indicate that the market had already expected a solid report. No unusual volume data was provided.

Thales confirmed full-year 2026 organic sales growth of 6% to 7%, equal to revenue of 23.3 billion euros to 23.6 billion euros. It also reaffirmed its adjusted EBIT margin target of 12.6% to 12.8%.

The defense division is expected to deliver low double-digit sales growth, up from a prior high-single-digit outlook. Management also said the space business should post low-single-digit growth for the full year, while cyber is expected to grow in the low- to mid-single-digit range in the second half.

Patrice Caine, chairman and chief executive, said the company&rsquo;s sales growth was driven by &ldquo;the successful ramp-up of our defense activities, especially in sensors and effectors, together with the rebound in cyber and the resilience of our aerospace business.&rdquo; The comment underlined how broad the growth drivers were in the first half.

He also said the company was &ldquo;entering the second half of 2026 with confidence,&rdquo; reflecting the strong order book and improved visibility.

Chief Financial Officer Jeremy Papin said free cash flow was exceptionally strong, but cautioned investors not to simply double the first-half figure to estimate the full year. He noted that capital spending will be higher in the second half and that working capital should normalize.

Analysts focused heavily on whether defense growth can continue at its current pace and how sustainable the margin gains are. Management said the defense ramp-up reflects long-term investment and is supported by demand from multiple regions, not just one-off contracts.

Questions also centered on space, especially the outlook for the IRIS&sup2; program and the impact of the satellite cancellations. Caine said IRIS&sup2; is progressing and described the willingness to make it interoperable with a German constellation as a positive sign.

Another area of focus was the digital business. Analysts asked about the decline in digital margins and the weakness in payments and biometrics. Management said part of the margin decline reflected one-offs from the prior year, while payments should improve as inventory issues ease and digital adoption rises over time.

On cyber, executives said growth of 4.5% in the second quarter was a first step and that the business should improve further in coming quarters. They also said the company sees AI as a driver of future cybersecurity demand.
