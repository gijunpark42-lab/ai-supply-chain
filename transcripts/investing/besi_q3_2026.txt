Besi reported a sharp rise in third-quarter 2026 revenue, orders and profit, but its shares fell 3.32% in premarket trading to $158.10 from $163.53 as investors weighed strong demand against a cautious near-term revenue outlook. The Dutch semiconductor equipment maker said Q3 revenue reached EUR 283.4 million, up 113.6% from a year earlier, while net income climbed to EUR 107.2 million. Orders rose to EUR 342.6 million, and the company said its 12-month rolling backlog increased to EUR 1.2 billion. Besi also guided for fourth-quarter revenue to be roughly flat, plus or minus 5%, versus Q3.

Key Takeaways

Revenue more than doubled from a year earlier, driven by demand in hybrid bonding, photonics and data center applications.Orders rose faster than revenue, supporting a backlog of EUR 1.2 billion.Net margin widened to 37.8%, showing strong operating leverage.Management said Q4 revenue should be flat to slightly higher or lower, despite strong order intake.Besi sees continued momentum in advanced packaging, especially for AI-related applications.

Company Performance

Besi delivered what management called exceptional third-quarter results, with broad-based strength across its advanced packaging portfolio. Revenue climbed to EUR 283.4 million, up 13.4% from the second quarter and 113.6% from the same period last year. Orders increased to EUR 342.6 million, reflecting continued demand from computing end markets.

The company’s profitability improved sharply. Net income reached EUR 107.2 million, up 20.4% sequentially and 323.7% year over year. Net margin expanded to 37.8%, more than double the 18.8% margin in the same quarter last year. Besi said the improvement came from higher revenue, better gross margins and tight cost control.

The results also point to a stronger market position. Management said year-to-date hybrid bonding orders more than doubled from full-year 2025 levels, while customer adoption widened across memory, logic, photonics and edge AI. That suggests Besi is benefiting from the industry’s shift toward more advanced packaging technologies.

Earnings vs. Forecast

The forecast data supplied for the quarter pointed to EPS of $1.27 and revenue of $278.05 million, but the actual EPS and revenue figures were not provided in that same currency set. Based on the earnings call, however, Besi reported strong underlying operating results in euros, with revenue of EUR 283.4 million and net income of EUR 107.2 million.

The company’s reported revenue growth and margin expansion suggest a strong quarter by any standard. Even without a direct apples-to-apples EPS comparison, the scale of the year-over-year improvement was substantial. Revenue more than doubled from a year earlier, and profit rose more than fourfold. That is a much larger move than a routine earnings beat.

For investors, the more important issue may be the pace of future growth. Besi’s Q4 revenue guidance was only flat, plus or minus 5%, versus Q3, which may have tempered enthusiasm despite the strong third-quarter numbers.

Market Reaction

Besi shares fell 3.32% in premarket trading to $158.10, down from the previous close of $163.53. That is a decline of about $5.43 per share. The stock remains above its 52-week low of $120.15, but it is still far below the 52-week high of $333.10.

The early move suggests investors focused on the company’s near-term guidance rather than the strong headline results. A flat Q4 revenue outlook can sometimes disappoint when a company has just posted a sharp rise in orders and profits. The market may also be reacting to the expected increase in operating expenses in the fourth quarter, as well as the timing gap between orders and shipments.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to determine whether the move came with unusually heavy activity.

Outlook & Guidance

Besi said fourth-quarter 2026 revenue should be roughly flat, plus or minus 5%, compared with Q3. That implies revenue of about EUR 269 million to EUR 298 million. Management said the guidance reflects customer delivery timing rather than weaker demand.

Gross margin is expected to remain in a range of 63% to 65%, while operating expenses should rise 5% to 10% from Q3 because of strategic consulting costs tied to a 16-week strategic review. The company said the review is expected to conclude in early January 2027.

Management also pointed to several growth drivers ahead:

Besi said TechInsights now expects the assembly equipment market to grow 37% in 2026 and reach $12 billion by 2028. Management said it expects Besi to grow more than twice as fast as the underlying market. Executive Commentary

Chief Executive Richard Blickman said: “Besi reported strong third quarter and year-to-date 2026 results as favorable order momentum continued for both our traditional and wafer level assembly systems.” The comment underscored that demand is broadening beyond one product line.

He also pointed to operating leverage, saying: “Baseline OPEX as a percentage of revenue decreased from 32% in the third quarter of last year to 16.4% in the third quarter of this year.” That highlights how much profit growth is coming from scale rather than just higher sales.

On the market outlook, Blickman said TechInsights had upgraded its 2026 assembly equipment forecast to 37% growth and now sees the market reaching $12 billion by 2028. He said Besi expects to grow more than double the market rate.

Risks and Challenges

Near-term revenue timing: Q4 guidance is flat despite strong orders, which may raise questions about shipment timing.Higher expenses: consulting costs tied to the strategic review will lift operating expenses in Q4.China exposure: about 40% of Q3 orders came from China, creating geopolitical and demand risk.Customer concentration: large orders from a few leading memory and logic customers can create volatility if programs shift.Technology adoption timing: hybrid bonding and TC Next are promising, but revenue depends on how quickly customers move from development to volume production.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on the timing of hybrid bonding adoption, especially in high-bandwidth memory. Management said development activity has continued at full speed and that all three major memory makers remain engaged. Blickman rejected the idea that adoption depends only on stack height, saying performance gains such as lower energy use, higher bandwidth and less heat are the key factors.

Questions also centered on Q4 revenue guidance. Management said the flat outlook is not a customer push-out, but a normal timing issue in equipment delivery. Some customers want delivery dates aligned with broader production plans, so orders do not always turn into revenue immediately.

Analysts asked about TC Next, which Besi said has seven customers and could become a sizable product line over the next couple of years. Management said the platform is aimed at sub-20 micron bond pad pitches and can serve as a bridge between reflow and hybrid bonding.

Another topic was Besi’s relationship with ASML. Blickman said the two companies are not direct competitors in die-to-wafer bonding and are instead working together on die stress and placement accuracy. He also said the company’s partnership with Applied Materials is deepening through the EPIC Center, where the first joint program will cover hybrid bonding and TC for a major U.S. customer.

Analysts also asked about China, automotive and industrial recovery, photonics demand and the company’s power management opportunity. Management said China remains a long-term growth market and that power management could return to 15% to 20% of revenue as AI data center demand expands.
