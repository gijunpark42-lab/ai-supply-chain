Xiaomi said second-quarter revenue reached CNY 108.9 billion and adjusted net profit came in at CNY 6.2 billion, while its smartphone business faced heavy pressure from high memory costs. The company did not provide EPS or revenue figures in the forecast data supplied for comparison, but the latest stock price rose 1.16% to $26.18 from $25.88, suggesting a modestly positive but cautious investor response. Shares remain well below their 52-week high of $59.9 and above the low of $21.3.

Xiaomi&rsquo;s second quarter showed a company still growing across several businesses, but under clear margin pressure in its core smartphone line. Revenue of CNY 108.9 billion and adjusted net profit of CNY 6.2 billion indicated that the group remained solidly profitable, even as it faced a difficult cost environment.

The smartphone and AIoT segment generated CNY 84 billion in revenue, with a gross margin of 20%. Smartphone revenue was CNY 42.1 billion, or 38.7% of total revenue, while the company shipped 31.2 million handsets globally. Xiaomi said it held the No. 3 global smartphone ranking for a 24th straight quarter.

Beyond phones, the company continued to build scale in smart EVs, AI and internet services. The Smart EV, AI and other new initiatives segment brought in CNY 24.9 billion in revenue, up 17.1% from a year earlier, though it still posted an operating loss as Xiaomi kept spending on development. Internet services remained a high-margin business, supported by a growing user base.

The forecast data supplied for this period pointed to EPS of $0.2249 and revenue of $117.85 billion, but actual EPS and revenue figures were not included in that dataset. As a result, a direct beat-or-miss calculation is not available from the forecast block alone.

What Xiaomi did report on the call was a CNY 108.9 billion revenue base and CNY 6.2 billion in adjusted net profit. The quarter appeared to be more about resilience than surprise. The company held margins together despite a sharp rise in memory costs, and it kept its smartphone business at No. 3 globally even as shipments came under pressure.

That combination suggests the quarter was operationally steady, but not the kind of result that would likely trigger a large re-rating on its own.

Xiaomi shares were last quoted at $26.18, up 1.16% from the previous close of $25.88. The move amounts to a gain of $0.30 a share, a modest rise that points to a measured reaction from investors. The stock has faced headwinds recently, declining nearly 19% over the past six months, though InvestingPro analysis suggests the shares may be undervalued at current levels. The stock remains far below its 52-week high of $59.9, though it is above the 52-week low of $21.3. That positioning suggests the market still sees room for recovery, but also reflects lingering caution about margins, competition and the pace of growth in new businesses. Despite near-term pressures, Xiaomi trades at a P/E ratio of just 9.15, which InvestingPro data identifies as a low earnings multiple&mdash;potentially attractive for value-oriented investors looking at the company’s diversification into EVs and AI.

With no volume data provided, there is no sign from the available information of unusual trading activity. The small advance fits a quarter that was constructive, but not clearly transformative.

Management said the near-term backdrop remains difficult. Memory costs are expected to stay high, though the pace of increases should slow. Consumer demand recovery is still gradual, and competition in smartphones remains intense.

For smartphones, Xiaomi said it will keep balancing scale and profit through product mix changes and pricing adjustments. The company noted that its Redmi flagship launch was moved forward to August and is showing strong momentum, with 62% of sales in the 16-megapixel-and-above range and sales up 60% from the previous generation.

In EVs, Xiaomi said the SkyNomad series is set for launch in September. The company described the models as aimed at family buyers and said pre-orders have been enthusiastic. Xiaomi also plans an overseas EV launch in the second half of 2027.

In AI, Xiaomi said it is still in a heavy investment phase. HyperOS 4 and the upgraded Super XiaoAi 2.0 are being positioned as key pieces of a broader human-car-home ecosystem. The company also said it is working on new models and desktop applications, while monetization remains early.

&ldquo;In Q2 of 2026, external environment remains challenging, with the cost of core components such as memory remaining high, consumer market demand still recovering slowly, and industry competition becoming more intense,&rdquo; William Lu, Xiaomi&rsquo;s partner and president, said.

He added that Xiaomi has &ldquo;consistently achieved a good balance between scale and profit through product mix upgrades, software optimization, and improved operational capabilities.&rdquo;

On AI, Lu said HyperOS 4 marks an important step because Xiaomi has upgraded AI &ldquo;from simple question and answer function in individual software to a user-friendly assistant that can remember user habits, understand user thoughts, and truly help users get things done.&rdquo;

Alain Lam, vice president and chief financial officer, said the company&rsquo;s smartphone gross margin was 8.5% in the quarter and that premium smartphone sales in mainland China reached 32.1% of total sales, a record high.

Analysts focused heavily on Xiaomi&rsquo;s smartphone pricing strategy, EV expansion and AI monetization.

On phones, Morgan Stanley asked whether Xiaomi can keep balancing price, shipment volume and gross margin as memory costs rise. Lu said the company cannot pass all of the cost increase to consumers, so it is rationalizing product lines and adjusting mix. He said the business remains &ldquo;controllable.&rdquo;

On EVs, analysts asked about the SkyNomad series and how it differs from SU7 and YU7. Lu said the new models target a different customer group, with minimal overlap, and are designed around space and family use rather than driver experience.

Goldman Sachs asked about AI monetization. Xiaomi said MiMo V2.5 has been well received and is already generating some revenue through API calls and token plans, but the company is not treating monetization as the main goal yet. It is still focused on model quality, ecosystem integration and product development.

Questions also covered R&D spending, inventory and overseas expansion. Xiaomi said R&D spending reached CNY 9.2 billion in the quarter, with about 30% tied to AI. It also said overseas retail store expansion is on track, and that it sees room for growth in large appliances and premium phones outside China.

Another area of interest was the company&rsquo;s robotics work. Xiaomi said its embodied robots have already reached a 98% success rate in a manufacturing nut-loading task, but it stressed that robotics remains a long-term effort rather than a near-term profit driver.
