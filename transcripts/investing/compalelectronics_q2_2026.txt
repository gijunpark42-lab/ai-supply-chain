Compal Electronics reported strong second-quarter 2026 results, with revenue rising 32% from a year earlier and net profit jumping 59% from the previous quarter, as its AI server business and non-PC products gained momentum. The company said Q2 revenue reached TWD 238.3 billion, while net profit was TWD 3.1 billion and earnings per share came to TWD 0.73. The stock fell 1.5% to $39.3 in the latest session, compared with a previous close of $39.9, suggesting investors were cautious despite the improved operating picture.

Compal delivered a stronger quarter even as the PC market remained under pressure. The company said first-half revenue grew 32% year over year, helped by faster growth in AI servers, AI PCs and other non-PC businesses. Non-PC revenue reached TWD 82 billion in Q2, up 15% from the prior quarter and 52% from a year earlier.

The company is trying to move beyond its traditional role as a PC manufacturer. Chief executive Tony Bonadero said Compal is now &ldquo;not just a PC company anymore,&rdquo; pointing to investments in AI infrastructure, healthcare technology, automotive solutions and other growth areas.

The shift is visible in the numbers. Non-PC revenue now makes up about 35% of total sales, up 5 percentage points from a year earlier. Compal said it wants that share to approach 40% by the end of 2026.

The company&rsquo;s reported local-currency results showed solid growth, but the provided forecast was in U.S. dollars, and no comparable actual dollar figures were supplied. That makes a direct beat-or-miss calculation difficult.

Still, the underlying trend was clearly favorable. Revenue growth of 32% year over year and a 59% sequential rise in net profit indicate that Compal is improving its earnings power. The company also said operating profit rose faster than gross profit, showing better leverage from higher sales volume and a richer product mix.

The main pressure point was margin. Gross margin fell to 4.6% because of rising component costs, especially memory. That suggests Compal is growing, but not without cost pressure. An InvestingPro tip notes the company "suffers from weak gross profit margins," a structural challenge that predates the current quarter. For investors seeking deeper insight, InvestingPro offers 7 additional exclusive tips, along with advanced metrics and Fair Value analysis to help assess whether the margin pressure is temporary or systemic.

Compal shares fell 1.5% to $39.3 from $39.9 in the latest session. The stock remains well above its 52-week low of $26.7, but it is still below the high of $47.75.

The modest decline suggests investors may be weighing two opposing forces: strong growth in AI servers and non-PC businesses, versus continued pressure on PC margins and component costs. The move was not large enough to suggest panic, but it does show some caution after the earnings update.

Despite the pullback, InvestingPro analysis indicates the stock appears undervalued based on its Fair Value assessment, placing it among candidates for the platform’s Most Undervalued stocks list. The company’s Financial Health score of 3.22 earns a "GREAT" rating, while its extremely low P/E ratio of 0.41 suggests the market may be underpricing the stock relative to earnings.

No unusual trading volume was provided, so there is no evidence here of a major trading spike.

Compal said its AI server business should remain the main growth driver in the second half of 2026. Management expects AI server revenue to rise by high double digits quarter over quarter in Q3, while PC units are expected to stay flat sequentially.

Looking further ahead, management said server revenue could represent 30% to 40% of total company revenue in 2027, with most of that coming from AI servers. The company expects larger rack-scale systems to begin ramping in early 2027.

Bonadero said Compal&rsquo;s transformation is already underway, not just planned. &ldquo;We are not just a PC company anymore,&rdquo; he said, adding that the company is &ldquo;making bets, executing on those bets, writing checks, building buildings, hiring people.&rdquo;

He also highlighted the scale of the company&rsquo;s digital transformation. &ldquo;We have identified 70-plus use cases for digital,&rdquo; he said, noting that these tools are already being used in R&D, manufacturing, procurement and supply chain operations.

On the market backdrop, Bonadero warned that the PC industry is facing a &ldquo;fundamental margin reset&rdquo; as component costs rise faster than customers will accept price increases. That comment helps explain why Compal is pushing harder into AI servers and other higher-growth businesses.

Analysts focused heavily on the PC market, AI servers and the company&rsquo;s long-term mix shift.

Management said AI server demand remains strong, with new customers expected to ramp in early 2027. It also said Compal is focused more on absolute dollar profit than gross margin percentage, since product mix and customer models vary widely across the business.

Bonadero said the market for agentic AI infrastructure is still early. &ldquo;The world&rsquo;s built less than 1% of the compute power that we need to,&rdquo; he said, arguing that demand for AI infrastructure should remain strong for years.
