Mycronic AB said second-quarter results were among the strongest in its history, with record order intake, higher sales and wider margins across several divisions. Net sales rose 17% from a year earlier to SEK 2.4 billion, while order intake jumped 119% to SEK 2.9 billion. EBIT came in at SEK 698 million, equal to a 29% margin, and gross margin reached 57%. Even so, the shares fell 13.4% to $333.4 from a previous close of $385, leaving the stock just below its 52-week high of $336.4.

Mycronic said the quarter showed strength across most of its business lines, led by Global Technologies, High Volume and Pattern Generators. The company described the period as exceptional, with broad demand and improved execution.

Pattern Generators posted sales of SEK 900 million and EBIT of SEK 500 million, helped by the contribution from Cowin DST and strong demand for semiconductor mask writers. Global Technologies delivered record EBIT of SEK 250 million on sales of SEK 700 million, with demand tied to photonics, optical communication and PCB test. High Volume generated sales of SEK 513 million and EBIT of SEK 68 million, while PCB Assembly Solutions remained under pressure, with sales of SEK 303 million and a negative EBIT of SEK 44 million.

The company&rsquo;s rolling 12-month sales reached SEK 8.65 billion, with a 26% EBIT margin. Management said the result reflected both strong demand and better operational efficiency.

No EPS or revenue forecast-versus-actual comparison was provided for the quarter, so a formal surprise calculation is not available. Still, the reported numbers point to a very strong operating performance.

Sales increased 17% year over year, while order intake rose 119%. That pace is unusually strong for a mature industrial technology company and suggests demand accelerated meaningfully during the quarter. The company also said sales were the second-best quarterly level in its history, while orders were the highest ever.

The scale of the order growth stands out more than the sales growth. It implies that future revenue visibility improved sharply, even though some business lines, especially Pattern Generators and PCB Assembly Solutions, still face uneven demand or restructuring needs.

Mycronic shares fell 13.4% to $333.4 from $385 after the results, a drop of $51.6. The stock is still near the top of its 52-week range of $185.9 to $336.4, which suggests the selloff came after a strong run and may reflect profit-taking as much as concern about the quarter itself.

The decline was notable because the operating results were strong. Investors may have focused on the fact that Global Technologies&rsquo; 36% EBIT margin is expected to normalize closer to 30%, which could limit future margin expansion. They may also have reacted to the uneven outlook in Pattern Generators and the ongoing restructuring in PCB Assembly Solutions.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to assess whether the move came with unusually heavy turnover.

Mycronic raised its full-year 2025 revenue outlook by SEK 500 million to SEK 9.25 billion. Management said the increase came roughly one-third each from stronger demand in High Volume, stronger demand in Global Technologies, especially AI-related products, and favorable currency effects.

Management said long lead times in PCB test, now above 18 months, reflect strong demand and constrained supply. It also said 2027 and early 2028 already look solid based on current orders and down payments. Chief Executive Anders Lindqvist said the company had &ldquo;a record order intake up 119%,&rdquo; adding that orders reached SEK 2.9 billion, &ldquo;we have never been higher.&rdquo; He said the quarter showed contributions from all divisions, especially Global Technologies, High Volume and Pattern Generators.

On margins, Lindqvist said Global Technologies&rsquo; 36% EBIT margin was &ldquo;a little bit on the high side&rdquo; and should normalize around 30% as planned investments are made. That comment suggests the company sees current profitability as strong, but not fully sustainable at the latest level.

Chief Financial Officer Pierre Brorsson said the company &ldquo;increased year-on-year with 17%&rdquo; in sales and highlighted that aftermarket revenue crossed SEK 2 billion on a rolling 12-month basis, or 24% of net sales. He also said the quarter-on-quarter improvement came from gross margin gains in both Global Technologies and High Volume.

- Pattern Generators demand remains cyclical: management said the display market follows normal cycles and can be hard to predict.

Analysts focused on the drivers of the upgraded full-year outlook, the sustainability of Global Technologies&rsquo; margin and the outlook for PCB test capacity.

Questions also centered on Pattern Generators, where management said the business is difficult to sell when customers do not need new equipment, and on the long lead times in PCB test. Lindqvist said the company is not the bottleneck in the market and expects capacity improvements to help stabilize delivery times.

Other questions addressed High Volume&rsquo;s growth drivers, especially AI-related applications, which management said account for less than 20% of division revenue. Analysts also asked about the PCB Assembly Solutions restructuring plan, the role of recent acquisitions such as Surfix and Cowin DST, and the company&rsquo;s willingness to pursue more M&A. Management said it has no capital constraints and will invest if it finds the right technology at the right price.
