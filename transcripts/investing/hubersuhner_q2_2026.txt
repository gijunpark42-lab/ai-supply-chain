Huber+Suhner said first-half sales rose and orders reached a record, but profit margins narrowed as the Swiss industrial group spent heavily to build capacity for its new optical connectivity business. The company kept its full-year outlook unchanged, saying it still expects at least 10% organic sales growth in 2026 and an EBIT margin in the upper half of its long-term target range of 10.5% to 12%. Shares fell 5.36% to $192.6 from a previous close of $203.5, even as the stock remained above its 52-week low of $106.2.

Huber+Suhner delivered a mixed first half, with demand clearly stronger than profitability. The company said organic order intake grew 8.5%, while reported order intake rose 5.1% after foreign exchange and copper effects. Sales increased 2.6% year over year, or 6% on an organic basis, but growth lagged orders because of timing differences in the optical connectivity solutions, or OCS, business.

The strongest performance came from the industry segment, where order intake reached a record CHF 242 million and sales rose 22% to CHF 189 million. Aerospace and defense and test and measurement were the main drivers. The transportation segment was stable, while the communication segment was weighed down by weak mobile and fixed network demand and by the cost of building out OCS production.

Management said the company is well positioned across several long-term themes, including electrification, AI and security. It also said the data center market remains strong, with no sign of peaking yet.

No EPS or revenue forecast was provided alongside the half-year results, so a direct earnings surprise cannot be calculated. The company&rsquo;s reported numbers nonetheless point to a familiar pattern for a business in transition: solid demand, but lower near-term profitability because of investment spending.

The most important signal was not a beat or miss against consensus, but the gap between strong order intake and softer earnings quality. Record orders and backlog suggest future revenue support, while the decline in EBIT margin shows that the current ramp-up phase is still costly. In that sense, the half-year results were more about execution and timing than about a simple earnings surprise.

The shares fell 5.36% to $192.6 from $203.5, a decline of $10.9 in the session. The stock remains well above the 52-week low, but it is also below the top of its yearly range, which extends to $288. According to InvestingPro analysis, the stock currently appears overvalued relative to its Fair Value estimate, which may explain some investor caution. The company trades at a P/E ratio of 45.78, reflecting premium valuation expectations despite near-term margin pressure.

The decline suggests investors focused on the pressure points in the report: weaker margins, negative free cash flow and the expense of scaling OCS production. The strong backlog and reaffirmed guidance were not enough to offset concerns about near-term profitability. An InvestingPro tip notes that the stock has fallen significantly over the last three months, though it still boasts an impressive 89.79% return over the past year. No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to judge whether the move came with unusually heavy activity.

Management said the second half should be much stronger than the first, driven mainly by higher OCS output and continued momentum in the industry segment. The company also said the new INGUN acquisition, announced in July, should close toward the end of the third quarter and contribute at least one quarter of sales in 2026.

The company sees OCS as a strategic growth platform for data center connectivity. It has expanded automated production in Poland and said the ramp-up is on track. Management also said the non-data center communications business appears to have reached a bottom, with no further decline expected from today&rsquo;s perspective.

Chief Executive Urs Ryffel said the order intake was especially strong because it came without the large U.S. hyperscaler orders that boosted the prior year. &ldquo;We have, for the third time, a semester with order intake above the CHF 500 million mark,&rdquo; he said.

He also pointed to the company&rsquo;s backlog as a support for future sales. &ldquo;The strong order backlog from last year and the high book-to-bill in the first semester results in a high backlog of CHF 517 million,&rdquo; Ryffel said.

On the outlook, he said the company remains confident in its full-year plan. &ldquo;We continue to expect organic sales growth of at least 10% and an EBIT margin in the upper half of our long-term objective,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused heavily on OCS, asking how much the company has invested in the new production setup in Poland and how quickly sales could follow. Management said the ramp-up is progressing on track, but it declined to give detailed investment figures or a precise sales forecast.

Questions also centered on the timing of additional hyperscaler orders. Ryffel said the company has discussions with other hyperscalers, but the timing of new orders is difficult to predict. He said the first customer relationship remains stable and demand is unchanged or possibly improving.

Another topic was the optical fiber market. Management said shortages are being watched closely, but Huber+Suhner has not been affected so far and is planning ahead.

Analysts also pressed on the communication segment&rsquo;s weak EBIT. Management said the decline was mainly due to upfront OCS spending and lower sales in the legacy mobile and fixed network businesses. The company said the segment should return to double-digit EBIT margins over the medium term once the ramp-up is complete.

Questions about the INGUN acquisition focused on synergies and margins. Management said the deal is not primarily about cost cuts, but about expanding market access and adding complementary test-and-measurement capabilities. INGUN is expected to be accretive at first and then move gradually toward the industry segment&rsquo;s margin level over time.
