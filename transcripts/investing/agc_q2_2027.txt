AGC Inc. reported higher profit in the fiscal second quarter of 2026, but investors sent the stock lower after the company kept its full-year outlook unchanged and pointed to limited visibility in the second half. Revenue came in at JPY 562.64 billion, slightly below the JPY 566.22 billion forecast, while earnings per share were 59.72. The shares fell 7.88% to $5,585 from the previous close of $6,063, leaving the stock well below its 52-week high of $8,315.

AGC said the quarter was stronger than a year earlier across most major metrics, with sales and profit helped by a weaker yen, higher shipments in chemicals, and better performance in strategic businesses. The company&rsquo;s strategic businesses now account for 54% of group operating profit, reflecting a shift toward higher-return areas such as electronics materials, performance chemicals and life science.

The results also showed a mixed picture by segment. Chemicals and architectural glass posted gains, while electronics and automotive faced cost pressure. Life science narrowed its loss, but remained in the red. Management said the business is still in a transition phase, moving from recovery to resilience.

AGC missed the revenue forecast by a narrow margin. Actual revenue of JPY 562.64 billion was JPY 3.58 billion below the JPY 566.22 billion estimate, a shortfall of 0.63%. EPS was reported at 59.72, but a consensus comparison was not available.

The miss was modest and came against a backdrop of strong year-over-year profit growth. Operating profit rose nearly 20%, and net income attributable to owners more than doubled. That suggests the quarter was operationally solid, even if sales fell a little short of expectations.

The stock fell sharply after the report, dropping 7.88% to $5,585 from $6,063. The decline pushed the shares to about 32.9% below the 52-week high and about 25.2% above the low. Despite the selloff, InvestingPro analysis suggests the stock is currently undervalued, with a Fair Value estimate indicating potential upside from current levels. The company trades at a P/E ratio of 13.9 and offers a dividend yield of 3.5%, backed by an impressive track record of maintaining dividend payments for 35 consecutive years&mdash;one of several InvestingPro Tips highlighting the company’s shareholder-friendly policies.

The move was larger than the revenue miss alone would imply. Investors appeared to focus on management&rsquo;s decision to keep the full-year forecast unchanged despite a strong first half. The company said it still faces many uncertainties and does not yet have clear visibility into the second half. That caution likely outweighed the quarter&rsquo;s profit growth.

AGC left its full-year fiscal 2026 outlook unchanged. Management said the company typically sees a stronger second half, but it did not raise guidance because of uncertainty in several businesses and limited visibility into the months ahead.

AGC also reiterated long-term growth plans in semiconductors, including a target to double semiconductor-related sales to JPY 200 billion by 2030 from about JPY 100 billion in 2025. The company said it has already approved capacity expansion of 20% to 50% by 2028 across several product lines.

President and CEO Yoshinori Hirai said the company is building a stronger base for future growth. &ldquo;We are surely strengthening our management foundation to support the future growth and transformation,&rdquo; he said.

On semiconductor materials, Hirai said customer demand is tightening. &ldquo;Frankly speaking, from our customers, we are getting very strong order inquiries and request for more supplies,&rdquo; he said. He added that supply is becoming tighter and that demand should keep growing.

On display pricing, Hirai said the industry is changing. &ldquo;Customers now have the mindset that as an industry, how profit can be attained, the panel manufacturers are thinking about price increase as well,&rdquo; he said.

Chief Financial Officer Yoshio Takegawa said the company kept its forecast unchanged because of uncertainty. &ldquo;Many uncertainties remaining. We don’t have clear visibility into the second half yet, and that is the reason why we did not change our full-year forecast,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused on the gap between first-half strength and the unchanged full-year outlook. Management said the company normally has a second-half-heavy earnings pattern, but it was not ready to lift guidance because of uncertainty.

Questions also centered on semiconductor materials. Executives said EUV mask blanks are seeing very strong customer inquiries, with volume expected to exceed last year&rsquo;s level and further growth likely next year. They also said the business is shifting toward more advanced, higher-value products, which should support pricing over time.

Life science was another major topic. Management said a long-term biopharmaceutical contract should bring the new Yokohama site to about 50% utilization and that roughly half of next year&rsquo;s projected sales are already secured as firm orders.

Analysts also pressed the company on display pricing and foreign exchange. AGC said the industry still uses yen-based pricing and that recent changes in the market make price increases more acceptable across panel makers. On chemicals, the company said improved PVC and ethylene spreads were helped by higher prices and changes in Chinese tax refunds, though it warned that the market remains volatile.
