VAT Group AG reported a sharp rise in third-quarter sales and orders, with Q3 2026 revenue of CHF 376 million, up 46% from a year earlier, while orders tripled and backlog climbed to CHF 980 million. The company’s revenue was slightly below the 377.46 million forecast provided in the data, but the gap was small. Despite the strong operating update, the stock fell 5.07% to $602.8 in pre-market trading from the previous close of $635.

Key Takeaways

Q3 sales rose 46% year over year to CHF 376 million, driven by strong demand in semiconductors and services.Orders tripled from a year earlier, and the book-to-bill ratio rose to about 1.9 times.Backlog reached CHF 980 million, up 280% year over year, giving the company strong visibility.Management said demand is based on real capacity needs, not double bookings.The company expects Q4 sales of CHF 420 million to CHF 450 million and sees continued growth into 2027.

Company Performance

VAT Group delivered what management called an exceptional quarter, with broad-based demand across its semiconductor-related businesses. Sales rose 29% sequentially and 46% from a year earlier, reflecting strong spending on advanced chip manufacturing equipment. Orders were especially strong in semiconductors, where unit orders increased 37% from the prior quarter and 270% from a year earlier.

The company also saw healthy demand in its service business, where orders rose 52% sequentially and 88% year over year. Sales in that unit grew more slowly because customers were maximizing output from new chip capacity and had less downtime for maintenance. Advanced industrials were flatter, but scientific instruments and analytical products helped support that segment.

Management said the current demand wave is being driven by the industry’s move to more advanced manufacturing, including gate-all-around transistors and sub-2 nanometer process technologies. It also pointed to AI infrastructure spending as an important catalyst, though it said the broader trend is the continued rise in semiconductor complexity.

Earnings vs. Forecast

Revenue came in at CHF 376 million, compared with a forecast of 377.46 million. That is a shortfall of about CHF 1.46 million, or roughly 0.4%. EPS was not provided in the available data, so a full earnings-per-share comparison is not possible.

The revenue result was essentially in line with expectations. The small miss is modest when set against the scale of the company’s operating momentum. Sales rose 46% year over year, orders tripled, and backlog expanded sharply. In that context, the quarter looks more like a strong operational update with a slight top-line shortfall than a true disappointment.

Market Reaction

The stock fell 5.07% to $602.8 in pre-market trading, down from the previous close of $635. The decline came even as the company reported record demand and a strong outlook. The shares are now about 17.9% below their 52-week high of $727.2, but still nearly 98% above the 52-week low of $305.1.

Investors may have reacted to a combination of factors: the revenue figure was only slightly below forecast, growth in factory output is expected to slow from the very rapid pace of the current ramp, and the stock had already enjoyed a strong run. In addition, some traders may be waiting for more proof that the current demand surge can continue without margin pressure from inflation and expansion costs.

Valuation concerns may also be weighing on sentiment. The stock trades at a P/E ratio of 85.65, and according to InvestingPro analysis, VAT appears overvalued relative to its Fair Value—placing it among companies on the Most Overvalued list. Despite the premium valuation, the company has delivered a year-to-date return of roughly 67% and a one-year gain of about 79%.

Outlook & Guidance

For the fourth quarter, VAT Group guided for sales of CHF 420 million to CHF 450 million, implying another step up from Q3. Management also expects order intake of CHF 500 million to CHF 600 million, after adjusting for the longer order window seen in the quarter. The company said it expects a book-to-bill ratio above 1.0 in Q4.

Management said factory output is on track to reach a quarterly run rate of CHF 450 million by the end of 2026. It also said 2026 should be a record year for orders, sales, free cash flow, EBITDA margin and net income.

Looking further ahead, the company said sales in 2027 should be significantly above its earlier guidance of CHF 1.5 billion to CHF 1.7 billion. It did not give a new full-year number, but said growth should continue through 2027 as new capacity comes online and service revenue expands with the installed base.

Analysts forecast earnings per share of $12.55 for fiscal 2026, supporting expectations for continued profitability. InvestingPro assigns VAT a "GOOD" Financial Health score of 2.67 out of 5, with particularly strong marks for profitability and cash flow. Key capacity projects include Malaysia 1B, which is scheduled to come online in the first quarter of 2027, and the continued integration of the Romania site into a fully finished-products manufacturing location. Management also said it will make decisions on the next phase of capacity expansion during 2027.

Executive Commentary

“Order volumes tripled in Q3 compared to a year ago, reaching record levels as new production capacity for leading-edge semiconductors remains highly sought after and continues to drive significant demand for VAT products,” Chief Financial Officer Fabian Chiozza said.

He also said, “We are not seeing double bookings, but orders that are driven by real capacity demand.” That comment was aimed at easing concerns that the order surge might be inflated by customer behavior rather than genuine end demand.

On the broader industry outlook, Chiozza said, “AI is clearly accelerating demand, but the more fundamental trend is the continued advancement of semiconductor technology itself.” He added that “Moore’s law remains very much alive,” underscoring the company’s view that demand is structural, not temporary.

Risks and Challenges

Margin pressure from inflation: Management cited higher material, labor, transportation and energy costs, which could weigh on profitability.Execution risk: The company is expanding capacity quickly across Switzerland, Malaysia and Romania, which raises operational complexity.Semiconductor cycle risk: Demand is strong now, but equipment spending can slow if chip makers delay projects.China exposure: Orders from China were slightly above 30% of sales, leaving the company exposed to geopolitical and trade risks.FX volatility: A stronger Swiss franc could pressure reported results over time.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on capacity, order quality and margins. Questions centered on how quickly VAT can ramp Malaysia 1B, whether more capacity will be needed beyond the current footprint, and how much of the demand surge is tied to pricing versus true end-market growth. Management said the main driver is underlying demand, not price increases.

Several analysts asked about the service business, where orders were especially strong. Management said that segment should continue to benefit from a larger installed base, more retrofit work and higher fab utilization.

There were also questions about China demand, with management saying the region continues to develop in line with expectations and that Chinese orders represent slightly above 30% of sales. Another topic was gross margin, with management saying margins should hover around 66% as efficiency gains and production ramp-ups help offset inflation.

Finally, analysts asked about the broader AI and semiconductor investment backdrop. Management said it is watching market noise but remains focused on customer plans, which it said still point to about 150 fabs under construction or planned over the next three years.
