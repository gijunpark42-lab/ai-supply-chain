BASF SE reported stronger second-quarter and first-half results, led by higher margins, volume growth and lower costs, and raised its full-year EBITDA outlook. The German chemical company said Q2 EBITDA before special items jumped 54% from a year earlier to 2.4 billion euros, while H1 EBITDA rose 17.5% to 4.8 billion euros. Net income increased to 5.1 billion euros, helped by a 3.5 billion-euro after-tax gain from the Coatings sale. The stock closed at $13.92, up 1.53% from $13.71, and was unchanged in after-hours trading.

BASF said its first half of 2026 showed a clear improvement in operating performance, with earnings growth across all segments except Surface Technologies. The company benefited from higher contribution margins, continued volume growth and lower cash fixed costs as its restructuring program gained traction.

The strongest contributions came from Materials, Chemicals and Industrial Solutions. Agricultural Solutions also performed well, with volume growth in all regions and an EBITDA margin before special items of about 29%. Surface Technologies was the main weak spot, as Battery Materials faced the end of subsidies and Precious Metal Services saw lower earnings.

The company also said its new Verbund site in Zhanjiang, China, helped drive volume growth in Greater China. Management described the site as an important competitive advantage because it supports local-for-local production and supply chain resilience.

The provided forecast called for revenue of 18.87 billion and EPS of 0.2396, but BASF did not provide comparable actual revenue and EPS figures in the materials reviewed here. The company did, however, report a strong operating performance through EBITDA, which is the main measure management emphasized on the call.

On that basis, the quarter was clearly stronger than the company&rsquo;s recent baseline. Q2 EBITDA before special items rose 54% year over year, and BASF raised full-year EBITDA guidance. That points to a meaningful improvement rather than a small one. The company also said earnings grew in all segments except Surface Technologies, which suggests broad-based momentum.

The lack of a direct EPS and revenue comparison makes the headline forecast test incomplete, but the operating data indicate a solid quarter.

BASF shares ended the regular session at $13.92, up 1.53% from the previous close of $13.71. The stock was unchanged in after-hours trading. The move was modest, suggesting investors welcomed the stronger operating results but remained cautious about cash flow and the broader macro backdrop.

At the current level, the stock sits about 14.3% above its 52-week low of $11.93 and about 14.3% below its 52-week high of $16.25. That places the shares in the middle of the recent trading range, not far enough to signal a full rerating.

The limited reaction may also reflect the fact that free cash flow remained negative and the company kept its free cash flow guidance unchanged, even as it raised EBITDA expectations. For some investors, that likely tempered enthusiasm.

BASF raised its full-year 2026 EBITDA before special items guidance to 6.9 billion euros to 7.7 billion euros, citing better-than-expected first-half performance. The company left its free cash flow guidance unchanged at 1.5 billion euros to 2.3 billion euros.

Management said capital spending should come in below the prior 3.4 billion-euro forecast and reiterated its plan to reduce capex by 20% over the next four years. BASF also said it expects capex to stay below depreciation through 2028.

The company said its Agricultural Solutions business remains on track for IPO readiness by mid-2027. It also announced a 1 billion-euro share buyback program to run from August 2026 through April 2027, as part of a broader plan to return at least 4 billion euros to shareholders by the end of 2028.

The buyback announcement aligns with what InvestingPro data shows: management has been aggressively buying back shares, contributing to a high shareholder yield. CEO Markus Kamieth said, &ldquo;We are successfully implementing our Winning Ways strategy,&rdquo; pointing to the company&rsquo;s first-half progress and the stronger operating trend.

He added that BASF&rsquo;s &ldquo;unique setup provided a clear competitive advantage,&rdquo; especially during the volatile second quarter, when customers made advance purchases amid Middle East disruption.

CFO Dirk Elvermann said the company is &ldquo;really focusing on net cash fixed cost savings,&rdquo; underscoring that the new CoreShift program is aimed at lowering costs in a more durable way than earlier restructuring efforts.

Analysts focused on several issues during the call, including M&A, Rhine River logistics, cash flow and the outlook for Agricultural Solutions.

Management said BASF is watching consolidation in the chemical industry and sees more potential for value-accretive deals, though it remains focused on strengthening the core business. On Rhine water levels, executives said the company is much better prepared than in 2018 and expects only minor disruptions.

Questions also centered on why free cash flow guidance did not rise along with EBITDA guidance. BASF said higher earnings are being offset by cash tied up in working capital, especially receivables and inventories. The company also said some cash will be collected later in the year.

Analysts asked about Agricultural Solutions pricing, and management said pressure is likely to continue because of weak farm economics and generic competition. The company also said some South American sales were brought forward ahead of an ERP conversion, which will affect third-quarter comparisons.
