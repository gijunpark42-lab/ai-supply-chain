Zealand Pharma reported strong first-half 2026 results, backed by 4.5 billion DKK in revenue, 3.5 billion DKK in net profit and a 14.5 billion DKK cash position. The company kept full-year 2026 guidance unchanged and highlighted progress across its obesity, liver disease and immunology pipeline. The stock rose 2.4% to $336.9 in pre-market trading, after closing at $329, as investors reacted to the clinical updates and capital return plans.

Zealand Pharma said the first half of 2026 was one of the busiest periods in its history, with multiple clinical programs advancing at once. The company is still best known as a development-stage biotech, but the latest update showed a stronger balance sheet and a broader set of assets moving forward.

The company&rsquo;s main near-term story remains petrelintide, its amylin-based obesity drug. Management said the therapy delivered double-digit weight loss in Phase II and did so with a side-effect profile that compared favorably with placebo. That matters in a market where many obesity drugs still face dropouts because of gastrointestinal side effects.

At the same time, Zealand is building a second pillar around survodutide, a GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist partnered with Boehringer Ingelheim. The latest Phase III results showed meaningful weight loss and improvements in body composition and liver fat, which could help the drug stand out in obesity and metabolic liver disease.

The company is also pushing earlier-stage programs in immunology and GIP biology, showing that it wants to remain more than a single-asset obesity story.

The forecast table pointed to EPS of $0.94 and revenue of $824.9 million, but no actual figures were provided in that format. As a result, a direct beat-or-miss calculation is not available from the reported data.

Still, the company&rsquo;s broader financial update was solid. Zealand said revenue reached 4.5 billion DKK in the first half, supported by a large Roche milestone and a collaboration payment. Net profit came in at 3.5 billion DKK, while cash remained very strong at 14.5 billion DKK.

The lack of a clean EPS comparison makes the market&rsquo;s focus shift to execution. On that measure, the update looked constructive: guidance stayed unchanged, the pipeline advanced, and the company continued to return capital while preserving a large cash cushion.

Zealand Pharma shares were up 2.4% at $336.9 in pre-market trading, compared with the previous close of $329. That puts the stock about 44.4% above its 52-week low of $233.2, but still roughly 39.3% below its 52-week high of $555.3.

The move suggests investors welcomed the clinical progress and balance-sheet strength, but did not treat the update as a major surprise. The stock&rsquo;s gain was modest, which often signals confidence without excess enthusiasm.

No volume data was provided, so it is not possible to determine whether the move came with unusually heavy trading. Even so, the early reaction appears consistent with a market that sees Zealand as a promising but still high-risk biotech story.

Zealand left its 2026 guidance unchanged. The company continues to expect collaboration revenue of 4.5 billion DKK and operating expenses of 2.7 billion DKK to 3.3 billion DKK, with spending still driven mainly by research and development.

Analysts focused heavily on petrelintide, survodutide and the company&rsquo;s capital strategy.

One major topic was how petrelintide should be positioned versus GLP-1 drugs. Steensberg said the company sees a shift toward a &ldquo;weight loss journey&rdquo; model, where patients start with a more tolerable therapy and move to combinations only if needed. Analysts also asked about the Kv1.3 blocker ZP9830 and whether it could eventually be combined with metabolic drugs. Management said that was an interesting possibility, but it is too early to define the path beyond the current psoriasis study.

More importantly, up to 98% of participants successfully escalated to their target maintenance dose. Together, these data point to the potential of petrelintide to fill a critical gap in chronic weight management, namely a highly tolerable therapy that delivers the weight reduction that the majority of those seeking weight management desire, with exceptional tolerability and the potential to support long-term adherence.
