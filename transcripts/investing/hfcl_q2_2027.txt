HFCL said its second quarter of fiscal 2027 was the best in its history, with revenue rising 143.62% year over year to INR 2,541.79 crores and consolidated profit after tax jumping 448.14% to INR 394.22 crores. The company did not provide earnings-per-share or revenue consensus figures in the materials reviewed, but the operating results and upgraded guidance appeared to support a positive market response, with the stock up 5% and trading near its 52-week high.

Key Takeaways

HFCL called Q2 FY2027 its strongest quarter ever, driven by sharp growth in optical fiber, cable and connectivity solutions.Revenue rose 143.62% year over year, while EBITDA increased 223.55% to INR 658 crores.Profit before tax climbed 406.49% to INR 538.60 crores, and PAT rose 448.14% to INR 394.22 crores.Management lifted full-year FY2027 revenue growth guidance to at least 80%, up from 20% at the start of the year and 40% after Q1.The company ended the quarter with an order book of INR 28,900 crores, giving it multi-year revenue visibility.

Company Performance

HFCL’s second quarter showed a steep acceleration in both growth and profitability. Revenue, margins and profits all expanded sharply, helped by strong demand for optical fiber and cable products, especially from data center and hyperscaler customers. Management said the quarter should not be viewed as a peak, but as the start of a longer growth phase.

The company’s EBITDA margin widened to 25.89% from 19.49% a year earlier, while PAT margin improved to 15.51% from 6.93%. That suggests HFCL is converting more of its sales growth into bottom-line profit, helped by product mix, operating leverage and premium pricing in higher-end connectivity products.

The performance also reflects a broader industry trend. Demand for data center infrastructure remains strong, and management said telecom operators are also beginning to increase fiber spending after a two-year slowdown. HFCL said roughly 70% of its INR 18,500 crores optical fiber and data center interconnect order book comes from hyperscalers, with the rest from telecom operators.

Earnings vs. Forecast

The company did not disclose a consensus EPS or revenue forecast in the supplied data, so a direct beat-or-miss comparison is not available. Even without that benchmark, the quarter was clearly strong by historical standards.

Revenue growth of 143.62% year over year is unusually high for a large industrial and telecom equipment company. The profit growth was even more striking: PBT rose more than fourfold and PAT rose nearly 4.5 times from a year earlier. That points to a much stronger earnings profile than in recent quarters.

Management also said the quarter was not driven by one-off “diamonds falling from the sky,” implying the improvement came from normal business operations, better pricing and a richer product mix. That makes the result more meaningful than a purely one-time gain.

Market Reaction

HFCL shares were up 5% in live trading at $268.81. The stock is now close to the top of its 52-week range of $59.82 to $275.91, suggesting investors have already rewarded the company for its growth story but still saw room for further upside after the results.

The move higher appears consistent with the company’s strong numbers and raised outlook. Investors may also have reacted to the large order book, the rapid growth in connectivity solutions and management’s confidence that growth will continue into FY2028.

The stock’s position near its 52-week high also suggests expectations are elevated. That can support the share price if execution remains strong, but it can also leave less room for error if growth slows or margins weaken.

Outlook & Guidance

Management raised its full-year FY2027 revenue growth target to at least 80%, up from an initial 20% and then 40% after the first quarter. The company said it remains conservative in its guidance and prefers to surprise on the upside.

Executive Commentary

Managing Director Mahendra Nahata said the results reflect the company’s scale and momentum. “These results reflect the increasing scale, strength, and momentum of our business and reinforce our confidence in HFCL’s ability to deliver sustained growth and create enduring value for our stakeholders,” he said.

He also emphasized that the quarter was not a peak. “I want to emphasize that we do not see this quarter as a peak, but as the sustained phase of strong growth,” Nahata said.

On guidance, he said the company is now expecting at least 80% revenue growth this year. “It is always good to be conservative. Give you a lower number and surprise you by better numbers,” he told analysts.

Nahata also pointed to the structural demand behind the business. “Data centers are a point of necessity,” he said, arguing that AI-led infrastructure spending should continue even if some debt-heavy developers slow expansion.

Risks and Challenges

EPC losses: The EPC segment posted a loss of about INR 121 crores, mainly because of billing delays tied to milestone-based projects.Customer concentration: A large share of the order book is linked to hyperscalers, which could make demand more sensitive to spending cycles in data centers.Pricing volatility: Management said fiber prices rose 20% to 25% quarter over quarter, and preform prices remain elevated.Execution risk: The company is counting on major capacity expansion, new product qualification and defense order conversion to support future growth.Macro and geopolitical risk: Management acknowledged that geopolitical issues could affect supply chains or export flows.

Q&A

Analysts focused on the company’s order book, margin outlook, demand durability and defense expansion.

One key question was how much of the optical fiber and data center interconnect order book comes from hyperscalers. Management said about 70% of the INR 18,500 crores order book is tied to hyperscalers, mostly export orders, while the rest comes from Indian telecom operators.

Analysts also pressed management on margins. HFCL said it sees no downside risk to margins and expects improvement from higher utilization, more connectivity solutions and backward integration through its planned preform facility.

Another major topic was the defense business. Management said defense revenue could reach INR 500 crores to INR 600 crores in FY2027, with a path to at least INR 2,000 crores in FY2028, supported by thermal weapon sights, radar programs, aerospace acquisition and an ammunition complex.

Questions also centered on the EPC segment. Management said losses should narrow over the next two quarters and that profitability could return in Q1 FY2028 once contract approvals and billing issues are resolved.

Finally, analysts asked about preform supply and pricing. Management said it is already negotiating long-term supply arrangements and does not expect a major margin hit from germanium or preform sourcing, even though current preform prices are unusually high.
