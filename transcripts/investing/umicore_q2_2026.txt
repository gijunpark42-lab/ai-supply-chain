Umicore said first-half 2026 profit and cash generation improved sharply, prompting the materials company to raise its full-year outlook and sending the stock up 3.12% in premarket trading to $21.80. The Belgium-based recycler and specialty materials producer reported adjusted EBITDA of &euro;577 million, up 33% from a year earlier, while revenue rose 7% to &euro;1.9 billion. Adjusted earnings per share doubled to &euro;1.14. Management now expects full-year adjusted EBITDA to come in slightly above &euro;1 billion, compared with a prior target to approach that level.

Umicore delivered a broad improvement across its main businesses in the first half, with gains in Catalysis, Specialty Materials and Recycling. The company said the results reflected three roughly equal drivers: stronger activity, ongoing efficiency measures and favorable prices for precious and minor metals.

The performance marks a clear step up from the prior year. Adjusted EBITDA margin widened by 600 basis points to 30.2%, while return on capital employed improved 660 basis points to 23%. The company also reported a stronger balance sheet, with leverage at 1.52 times and liquidity supported by &euro;1.8 billion in cash and &euro;1.1 billion in unused credit lines.

Catalysis remained a core contributor, with adjusted EBITDA margin at 28.3%. Specialty Materials was described by management as a &ldquo;hidden gem,&rdquo; helped by strong demand in cobalt, germanium and electro-optic materials. Recycling also performed well, with an adjusted EBITDA margin of 52% as metal prices stayed elevated and volatile.

The company did not provide a directly comparable quarterly EPS or revenue figure in the same format as market forecasts, so a standard beat-or-miss calculation is not available from the reported data. The available consensus figures pointed to EPS of $1.30 and revenue of $1.98 billion, but the call focused on semiannual results reported in euros.

Even so, the operating trend was clearly stronger than the market likely expected. Adjusted EBITDA rose 33%, adjusted EPS doubled, and free cash flow turned positive by a wide margin. For investors, the most important signal was not a narrow quarterly surprise, but the combination of higher profitability, better cash generation and an upgraded full-year outlook.

Umicore shares rose 3.12% to $21.80 from a previous close of $21.14. The stock remains well above its 52-week low of $12.80 and below the high of $26.96, leaving it about 19.6% under the peak and 70.3% above the low.

The move suggests investors welcomed the stronger-than-expected operating momentum and the guidance increase. At the same time, the gain was measured rather than dramatic, likely because management also warned that the second half would not match the unusually strong first half. No trading volume data was provided, so unusual volume patterns could not be assessed.

The stock’s valuation remains attractive, with shares trading at a P/E ratio of 13.7 and a PEG ratio of just 0.1, suggesting the company is undervalued relative to its growth prospects. The company’s market capitalization stands at approximately $6.05 billion.

Management lifted full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to slightly above &euro;1 billion from a prior view of approaching that level. The company said the new target assumes stable metal prices versus June 2026 levels and no major deterioration in the macro environment.

By segment, Catalysis is expected to deliver full-year adjusted EBITDA in line with its record 2025 level. Recycling should remain significantly above 2025, though the second half is expected to be weaker than the first. Specialty Materials is also expected to finish well above last year, even if momentum eases in the second half.

Battery Materials remains a longer-term story. Umicore kept its 2028 adjusted EBITDA target of &euro;275 million to &euro;325 million, supported by long-term customer contracts, take-or-pay clauses and a push to diversify its customer base. Management said the European battery market is still developing and that platform ramps have been slower than expected.

The company also said capital spending should remain around 2025 levels and that it will continue selective investments in foundation businesses, including germanium capacity and stationary catalysts.

&ldquo;We delivered broad-based earnings growth with adjusted EBITDA up 33%, a margin of 30%, strong cash conversion, and a robust balance sheet,&rdquo; CFO Juan Pe&ntilde;arrubia said. &ldquo;The foundation business performed well, and we continued to exercise discipline on costs, capital, and cash.&rdquo;

CEO Bart Sap said the company&rsquo;s &ldquo;circular multi-metal platform is unique and well-positioned as it continues to benefit from the growing strategic momentum behind critical raw materials.&rdquo; He added that &ldquo;volatility is the new normal of the world we operate in,&rdquo; a comment that reflects the company&rsquo;s exposure to metal prices and shifting supply chains.

Sap also said the company is raising guidance because of the first-half performance. &ldquo;We are increasing our adjusted EBITDA guidance to slightly above &euro;1 billion,&rdquo; he said, citing confidence from the results.

Analysts focused on several themes during the call. Questions centered on the tungsten opportunity, the pace of European battery partnerships, and the outlook for battery cathode materials volumes. Management said tungsten recovery is being expanded through the Olen facility and is mainly a European opportunity for now.

Analysts also pressed for details on cobalt pricing and hedging. Management said the key indicator is the &ldquo;revenue pool&rdquo; from refining charges and premiums, not just the spot price, and added that 2027 precious metals hedge levels should be neutral for the recycling segment.

Another area of interest was germanium and stationary catalysts. Management said demand for germanium remains strong because of export controls and supply constraints, while stationary catalysts could more than double EBITDA over time as data center backup power demand grows.

On battery materials, executives said the business is still in a transition phase. They pointed to take-or-pay contracts, customer diversification and new partnerships as the main supports for the 2028 target.
