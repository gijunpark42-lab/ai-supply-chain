Schneider Electric said third-quarter revenue rose to a record €11.5 billion, up 17% organically from a year earlier, as demand surged across data centers, energy management and industrial automation. The company also lifted its full-year outlook for the second time in 2026, but the stock was down 1.38% to $254.45 in pre-market trading, suggesting investors were weighing the strong operating performance against a high bar for future growth.

Key Takeaways

Revenue reached a quarterly record of €11.5 billion, the highest in the company’s history.Organic sales grew 17%, with reported growth of 17.9% including currency tailwinds.Data center demand posted triple-digit growth for a third straight quarter.Management raised full-year 2026 guidance for organic revenue growth to 12% to 14%.Pricing actions taken earlier in the year were fully flowing through in the quarter, helping offset inflation and tariffs.

Company Performance

Schneider Electric said the quarter reflected broad strength across its businesses, regions and end markets. Energy Management revenue rose 19% organically to €9.6 billion, while Industrial Automation grew 8% organically to €1.8 billion. The company said all four regions contributed to growth, with China and East Asia up 26% organically, North America up 20%, South Asia and International up 16%, and Europe up 9%.

Management said the result was not driven by one-off factors. Product growth accelerated to 17% organically, systems business growth reached 22%, and software and services also advanced. The company said year-to-date organic growth was 20%, showing momentum through the first nine months of 2026.

The strongest engine remained data centers, where demand has stayed exceptionally high as cloud providers, AI infrastructure builders and colocation operators expand capacity. Schneider Electric said it is seeing visibility into 2027 and 2028 from this pipeline.

Financial Highlights

Revenue: €11.5 billion, a quarterly record, up 17% organically and 17.9% reported.Energy Management revenue: €9.6 billion, up 19% organically.Industrial Automation revenue: €1.8 billion, up 8% organically.Systems business growth: 22% organically.Product growth: 17% organically.Software and services: 7% organic growth.China and East Asia revenue growth: 26% organically.North America revenue growth: 20% organically.South Asia and International revenue growth: 16% organically.Europe revenue growth: 9% organically.

Earnings vs. Forecast

The company did not provide comparable EPS and revenue actuals in the supplied forecast data, so a precise beat or miss versus consensus cannot be calculated here. The available forecast showed revenue of $11.18 billion, while Schneider Electric reported €11.5 billion in quarterly revenue. Because the figures are in different currencies, they are not directly comparable without an exchange-rate conversion.

Even so, the operating update points to a strong quarter that likely exceeded market expectations on growth momentum. Management emphasized that Q3 pricing contribution alone matched the value of the entire first half, and that demand in data centers continued to grow at triple-digit rates. The company also said the quarter was a new all-time record, which usually signals a meaningful step up from prior periods.

Market Reaction

Schneider Electric’s shares fell 1.38% to $254.45 in pre-market trading, down from the previous close of $258. The stock remains near the upper half of its 52-week range of $220.40 to $312.30, sitting about 18.5% below the high and 15.4% above the low.

The modest decline suggests investors may be taking profits after a strong run, or waiting for more clarity on how long the current growth pace can last. The market may also be reacting to the absence of directly comparable EPS and revenue surprise figures in the data provided. Still, the stock’s move was limited, which points to a generally constructive view of the quarter.

Outlook & Guidance

Schneider Electric raised its full-year 2026 guidance for the second time this year. The company now expects organic revenue growth of 12% to 14%, up from earlier guidance, and adjusted EBITA margin expansion of 100 to 120 basis points. That implies a full-year adjusted EBITA margin of about 19.7% to 19.9%.

Management said Q4 trends are largely unchanged from the July outlook, though the comparison becomes harder because the fourth quarter of 2025 was the company’s strongest quarter of that year and one of the best in its history. The company also said it expects broad-based demand to continue across regions, with China now joining the U.S. and India as a key growth driver.

Looking further ahead, Schneider Electric said data center demand should remain strong into 2027 and 2028, supported by ongoing investment in AI infrastructure, hyperscale computing and prefabricated solutions. The company also sees continued opportunity in EV chargers, heat pumps, retrofit projects and digital energy-management tools.

The company’s financial foundation supports this growth ambition, with a return on equity of 18% and gross profit margins of 42% over the last twelve months. Executive Commentary

Chief Executive Olivier Blum said, “Q3 shows that momentum continuing and strengthening. You will see that we delivered €11.5 billion in terms of revenue, which is a new record for Schneider Electric, not just for the third quarter, but for any quarter in our history.”

Chief Financial Officer Nathan Fast said, “Data center continues to be exceptionally strong with another quarter of triple digit demand growth in Q3, further improving our visibility into 2027 and 2028.”

Fast also said pricing actions were now fully in place: “The proactive price increases we made at the start of the year are now at full run rate, and price realization is ramping up on the supplementary actions we continue to take.”

The comments underscore two themes that shaped the quarter: demand strength in high-growth markets and disciplined pricing to protect margins.

Risks and Challenges

Slower comparisons ahead: The company faces tougher year-over-year comparisons, especially after a very strong fourth quarter in 2025.Input-cost inflation: Management cited pressure in memory, semiconductors and copper, which could affect margins if pricing does not keep pace.Tariff and supply-chain risk: Schneider said it is offsetting tariff effects now, but trade conditions can change quickly.Dependence on data center growth: The data center business is a major driver, and investors may want proof that demand can stay elevated beyond the current buildout cycle.Macro weakness in some markets: Residential construction remains subdued, and higher interest rates still affect parts of the building market.

Q&A

Analysts focused heavily on three issues: data center conversion, China demand and pricing.

Questions centered on how quickly triple-digit data center orders will turn into sales, and whether Schneider Electric has enough capacity to meet demand. Management said it is expanding manufacturing and regional hubs, including liquid cooling capacity in North America, India, Italy and China, and is confident it can keep up.

Analysts also pressed for more detail on China, where growth was especially strong. Management said the market is being driven by AI infrastructure, data centers and semiconductors, with demand flowing through panel builders and equipment manufacturers rather than direct end customers. Executives said they expect demand to remain strong through 2027.

Another focus was pricing. Management said earlier price increases are now fully running through the business and that more pricing flexibility may be used in 2027 if input costs rise again. On the building market, executives said residential demand is still weak, but retrofit, EV charging and heat pumps are creating new opportunities.
