Technoprobe said second-quarter revenue hit a record EUR 277 million, up 64.3% from a year earlier and above the top end of its outlook, as demand tied to artificial intelligence continued to drive growth. The Italian semiconductor test-equipment maker also raised its full-year revenue and margin guidance, but the stock was little changed in early trading, slipping 0.35% to $28.68 from $28.78.

Technoprobe delivered a strong first half of 2026, with revenue of EUR 464.1 million, up 42.4% from the same period a year earlier. The company said the growth came mainly from AI volumes, along with a gradual recovery in consumer, automotive and industrial markets.

The second quarter was especially strong. Revenue rose 64.3% year over year, while gross profit and EBITDA grew much faster than sales. Management said the results reflected both higher volumes and better efficiency across the business.

The company also said AI-related products now account for more than half of total revenue. That mix has helped Technoprobe benefit from the rapid buildout of data centers and next-generation computing platforms.

- Capital expenditure: about EUR 90 million in H1, as the company accelerated its expansion plan.

The supplied forecast data showed revenue expectations of $227.98 million, while the company reported Q2 revenue of EUR 277 million. Because the figures are in different currencies, a direct numerical beat calculation is not meaningful from the available data. Still, management said revenue came in above the high end of guidance, which indicates a clear outperformance.

No EPS forecast or actual EPS figure was provided. On the revenue side, the quarter appears to have been a meaningful beat, not a small one. The company also exceeded its margin outlook, which often matters as much as sales in a manufacturing business with operating leverage.

Compared with a typical earnings beat, this one looks larger because it came with record revenue, a sharp margin gain and an upward revision to full-year guidance.

Technoprobe shares were down 0.35% to $28.68, versus a previous close of $28.78. The move was modest and suggested a restrained reaction rather than a strong post-earnings rally.

At the current price, the stock trades about 25.7% below its 52-week high of $38.58 and about 384% above its 52-week low of $5.92. That places the shares well above their bottom but still below the peak reached over the past year. The small decline suggests investors may be weighing the strong results against the company&rsquo;s large capital-spending plan and the expected slowdown in GPU-related demand later in the year.

Technoprobe raised its full-year 2026 guidance. The company now expects revenue of EUR 1.05 billion to EUR 1.1 billion and an EBITDA margin of 46% to 48%.

For the third quarter, management guided to revenue of about EUR 314 million, plus or minus 3%, with a gross margin of 61.5% plus or minus 200 basis points and an EBITDA margin of 52% plus or minus 200 basis points.

Management said the fourth quarter should still be strong, but GPU volumes are expected to pause after a heavy first-half production cycle and restart in 2027. CPUs and ASICs are expected to partly offset the decline. The company said it does not expect major margin dilution from the product mix change, though some pressure could come from labor awards, agency fees and China ramp-up costs.

Technoprobe also said it is increasing its capacity target to a EUR 1.6 billion run rate by the end of the first quarter of 2027, up from a prior EUR 1.4 billion target. Total investment for the 15-month period from January 2026 to March 2027 is now expected to reach EUR 350 million.

Chief Executive Stefano Felici said the company&rsquo;s performance reflects its position in a structural growth market. &ldquo;Our performance demonstrates not only outstanding financial execution, but also strength of our positioning in what we believe is one of the most attractive structural growth markets in the semiconductor industry,&rdquo; he said.

He also pointed to the role of AI demand. &ldquo;AI data center remains by far the most important growth engine for Technoprobe,&rdquo; Felici said. &ldquo;We continue to see exceptional investment across the AI ecosystem.&rdquo;

Chief Financial Officer Stefano Beretta emphasized the scale of the quarter and the company&rsquo;s profitability. &ldquo;Revenues recorded in the second quarter marked another all-time record for Technoprobe, exceeding EUR 277 million,&rdquo; he said. He added that EBITDA reached EUR 137 million, with a margin of over 49%.

Beretta also said the company is moving ahead with a large capacity program. &ldquo;We have farther accelerated the total investment to be deployed by the end of Q1 2027 to approximately EUR 350 million,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused on three main issues: the second-half revenue pattern, the company&rsquo;s capacity plans and the outlook for new technologies.

Questions centered on why Q4 revenue is expected to be softer than Q3. Management said the answer lies in GPU timing, with major campaigns concentrated in the first half and a pause later in the year before volumes restart in 2027. CPUs and ASICs should help offset some of that decline.

Analysts also pressed the company on its capacity expansion. Beretta said the new EUR 1.6 billion run rate target reflects added investment in equipment and automation, mostly in Italy but also in Taiwan, Korea and China. He said the company remains committed to the full EUR 350 million plan.

Another focus was HBM, where revenue was only about EUR 10 million in the first half. Management said the technology is ready, but customer adoption will determine when volumes scale. Silicon photonics drew similar questions. Felici said the market is still in characterization and that mass production is likely to begin in 2027, though the size of the opportunity remains unclear.
