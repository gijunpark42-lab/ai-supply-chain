Fujitsu said first-quarter profit improved sharply as its core services business continued to grow, while the company kept its full-year outlook unchanged. The Japanese technology group reported revenue of 779.3 billion yen, up 3.9% from a year earlier, and adjusted operating profit of 54.9 billion yen, a 56% increase. Net profit was held back by the absence of a large one-time gain booked a year earlier. The stock rose 1.51% to $3,902 in after-hours trading, compared with a previous close of $3,844.

Fujitsu&rsquo;s first quarter showed a clear split between its growing services business and weaker legacy segments. The company said Service Solutions, its largest and most important division, continued to deliver higher revenue and profit. That helped offset pressure in Hardware Solutions and a sharp decline in Ubiquitous Solutions.

The company also said it is reorganizing Service Solutions around industries rather than regions. Management said the change is meant to make industry knowledge a core competitive advantage. The shift comes as Fujitsu pushes deeper into digital transformation, cloud modernization and AI-related services.

The results suggest Fujitsu is making progress in its effort to build a more stable earnings base. The company&rsquo;s operating profit improved even as net profit was affected by the absence of a large gain from the sale of Shinko Electric shares in the prior year.

Fujitsu&rsquo;s supplied forecast called for earnings per share of 16.99 and revenue of 750.2 billion in currency terms, but the company did not provide matching actual EPS and revenue figures in the material reviewed here. That means a direct beat-or-miss calculation is not possible from the available numbers.

Even so, the operating results point to a solid quarter. Revenue rose 3.9% and adjusted operating profit increased 56%, while management said the business was progressing in line with plan. The company also kept its full-year outlook unchanged, which usually signals that results were broadly consistent with internal expectations.

The strongest part of the quarter was profitability. Service Solutions delivered higher revenue and a 31% increase in operating profit, while Uvance continued to scale quickly. Those trends suggest Fujitsu&rsquo;s core business is improving even if the headline net profit was distorted by a missing one-time gain from last year.

Fujitsu shares were up 1.51% to $3,902, compared with a previous close of $3,844. The stock is now about 16.8% above its 52-week low of $3,016 and about 16.4% below its 52-week high of $4,668.

The move points to a mildly positive investor response. The market appeared to welcome the stronger operating profit and the continued growth in services, but the gain was limited, likely because the company did not report a clear EPS or revenue surprise in the supplied figures. The stock&rsquo;s reaction also suggests investors remain focused on whether Fujitsu can keep expanding margins while managing weaker hardware and legacy businesses.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to say whether the move came with unusually heavy activity.
