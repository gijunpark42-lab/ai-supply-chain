Nanya Technology posted a sharp jump in third-quarter profit as higher DRAM prices drove margins to unusually high levels, with earnings per share of TWD 25.4 topping the forecast of TWD 23.51 by about 8%. Net sales rose to TWD 113.6 billion, up 61.9% from the prior quarter, while the stock slipped 0.94% to $527 in the latest session, leaving it just below the previous close of $532 and near the top of its 52-week range.

Key Takeaways

Q3 EPS of TWD 25.4 beat the forecast of TWD 23.51 by TWD 1.89, or about 8%.Net sales climbed to TWD 113.6 billion, up 61.9% from Q2 and 611.7% from a year earlier.Gross margin improved to 85.7%, while operating margin reached 80%, reflecting strong pricing power.Average selling prices rose more than 60% sequentially and more than 500% year over year.Management said AI infrastructure and server products now account for more than 20% of revenue and could rise to 40% to 50% by 2029 or 2030.The company plans major capacity expansion, including a new fab scheduled for equipment move-in in Q1 2027.

Company Performance

Nanya Technology delivered one of its strongest quarters in recent years, with profit rising faster than sales as DRAM prices surged. The company said the main driver was a sharp increase in average selling prices, helped by renewed long-term agreements signed when prices were much lower.

Gross profit reached TWD 114.4 billion, up 74.5% from Q2, while net income climbed to TWD 87.6 billion, up 74.6% sequentially. Earnings for the first nine months of 2026 totaled TWD 163.9 billion, with accumulated EPS of TWD 49.28 per share.

The results point to a market still tight on supply, especially for DRAM used in AI infrastructure, servers, and other high-value applications. Management said AI computation, including GPU, TPU, CPU and ASIC demand, is now a major force behind memory demand growth.

Financial Highlights

Net sales: TWD 113.6 billion, up 61.9% from Q2 and 611.7% from a year earlier.Gross profit: TWD 114.4 billion, up 74.5% sequentially.Gross margin: 85.7%, compared with 79.4% in Q2.Operating income: TWD 106.8 billion, with an 80% margin.EBITDA: TWD 110 billion.Net income: TWD 87.6 billion, up 74.6% from Q2.EPS: TWD 25.4, versus TWD 14.66 in Q2.Book value per share: TWD 118.99, up from TWD 93.45 in Q2.Free cash flow: TWD 75.2 billion.Ending cash balance: TWD 288.5 billion, up from TWD 217.6 billion at the start of the quarter.Return on equity: 9%, reflecting efficient capital deployment.Dividend yield: 4.42%, providing income alongside growth.

Earnings vs. Forecast

Nanya Technology reported Q3 EPS of TWD 25.4, above the forecast of TWD 23.51 by TWD 1.89. That equals a beat of about 8.0%.

The company did not provide a comparable revenue forecast in the supplied figures, so a formal revenue surprise calculation is not available. Still, the reported sales growth was striking. Revenue rose 61.9% from Q2, and profit growth outpaced sales because of higher prices and strong margin expansion.

The beat was meaningful, not just a small upside surprise. It followed a very strong Q2, when EPS was TWD 14.66, showing that the company’s earnings momentum accelerated again in the third quarter.

Market Reaction

The shares were last quoted at $527, down 0.94% from the previous close of $532. The stock remains close to the top of its 52-week range of $85 to $569, suggesting that investors have already priced in much of the company’s recent strength.

The modest decline indicates that the market may be balancing the strong earnings against the company’s heavy investment plans and limited near-term shipment growth. Management said shipments fell mid-single digits in Q3 because inventory was depleted, and bit shipments are expected to stay flat for several quarters until new fab output begins in the second half of 2027.

No volume data were provided, so unusual trading activity cannot be confirmed from the available information.

Outlook & Guidance

Management said the shortage in the DRAM market is likely to continue into the December quarter, with average selling prices expected to improve gradually.

For 2027, the company expects the market to remain tight because new capacity takes a long time to build and equip. Management said bit shipments are likely to stay flat for several quarters, with little growth expected in early 2027. New fab equipment is scheduled to move in during Q1 2027, and wafer output is expected to begin in the second half of 2027.

The company also outlined a long-term growth plan tied to AI infrastructure:

Capital spending will remain heavy. Management said one fab will require about $16 billion in investment, including construction, clean room, equipment and future EUV technology. A separate Pingtung Science Park fab for 3D wafer packaging and testing is expected to require about $10 billion over the long run, with equipment move-in targeted for the second half of 2028.

The company also said it will continue paying dividends next year, while balancing that policy against large capital needs. No share buyback plan has been announced. Executive Commentary

President Pei-Ing Lee said the quarter’s sales and profit gains were driven mainly by pricing. “ASP increased by greater than 60% quarter-over-quarter and year-over-year greater than 500%,” he said, adding that shipment volume fell because of depleted inventory.

Lee also pointed to the company’s AI exposure. “AI infrastructure and server contribute to Nanya’s more than 20% of our revenue,” he said, noting that the company expects that share to rise over time.

On capacity, Lee said, “Our new fab equipment is scheduled for move in Q1 2027. We plan to ramp up to 30K by the end of 2028 and target up to 50K by the end of 2030.”

He also said the company already has some revenue from AI-related wafer-to-wafer bonding and 3DIC projects, though the contribution is still small. “We already have some revenue. Not big, but it is an important business for Nanya to work on,” he said.

Risks and Challenges

Heavy capital spending: The planned fab investment of about $16 billion could weigh on cash flow and limit flexibility.Flat shipments near term: Management expects little bit shipment growth until new output starts in the second half of 2027.Inventory depletion: Low inventory can support pricing, but it also limits the company’s ability to raise shipments quickly.Pricing cycle risk: The current profit surge depends heavily on strong DRAM pricing, which may not last forever.Competitive pressure: Larger DRAM makers remain far bigger players, even as Nanya benefits from tight supply conditions.

Q&A

Analysts focused on three main themes: pricing, AI demand, and capital spending.

Questions centered on how much of the ASP increase came from renewed long-term agreements, and management said more than half of the more than 60% sequential rise was tied to those renewals. Analysts also asked about the outlook for DDR4, where management said demand remains strong even though the product mix is gradually shifting toward DDR5.

Another major topic was AI-related demand. Management said AI infrastructure revenue includes DDR4, DDR5, low-power DRAM, enterprise SSDs, communication chips, storage chips, system management chips and RDIMMs. The company expects that business to grow steadily and eventually reach 40% to 50% of revenue by 2029 or 2030.

Analysts also pressed management on capital allocation. Nanya said it has no buyback plans and will continue to balance dividends with the large investment needed for new fabs. The company reiterated that long-term contracts remain fully committed, with a 100% fulfillment rate for signed orders.
