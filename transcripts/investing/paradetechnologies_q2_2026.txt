Parade Technologies reported higher second-quarter revenue and stronger demand for its integrated display products, but profit fell as repeated outsourcing and assembly cost increases squeezed margins. The Taiwan-based chip designer said Q2 2026 revenue rose to $134.48 million, up 6.61% from the prior quarter and 0.73% from a year earlier. Net income dropped 17.6% year over year to $18.78 million, while EPS fell to $0.24 from $0.29 a year earlier. The shares rose 1.39% to $584 in live trading, after closing at $565, suggesting investors focused more on the company&rsquo;s growth outlook than on the margin pressure.

Parade&rsquo;s second quarter showed a mixed picture. Sales continued to grow, but earnings weakened because costs rose faster than the company could offset them. Revenue increased from $133.50 million a year earlier, reflecting steady demand across several product lines. But gross profit fell 6.06% year over year to $54.06 million, and net income declined to $18.78 million from $22.79 million.

The company said the main issue was a sharp rise in OSAT, or outsourced semiconductor assembly and test, costs. Management said those costs increased multiple times during the quarter, creating an unexpected drag on profitability. Parade is trying to respond by negotiating price increases with customers and shifting to lower-cost manufacturing partners.

The company maintains a solid financial foundation despite the margin pressure. According to InvestingPro data, Parade holds more cash than debt on its balance sheet, with a current ratio of 5.9 indicating strong liquidity to weather near-term cost headwinds.

Product mix remained an important support. High-speed products made up about 45% of revenue, display-port products 30%, touch controller products a little above 10%, and TTED integrated display solutions slightly below 15%. TTED was the standout, with shipments running ahead of plan.

The forecast data provided for the period does not line up with the company&rsquo;s reported quarterly scale, so a standard beat-or-miss calculation is not meaningful here. Using the company&rsquo;s reported results, the quarter showed revenue growth but lower profitability.

On a year-over-year basis, revenue rose 0.73%, while EPS fell 17.2%. That means the company grew sales, but not enough to protect margins. In practical terms, the quarter looked stronger on the top line than on the bottom line.

Compared with the prior quarter, revenue improved 6.61%, which is a solid sequential gain. But gross profit rose only 6.35%, showing that cost pressure limited operating leverage. The result suggests demand was healthy, but earnings quality was weaker than revenue growth alone would imply.

Parade shares rose 1.39% to $584 in live trading, up from a previous close of $565. The stock is still far below its 52-week high of $929, but it has recovered about 26% from the 52-week low of $463.5.

The modest gain suggests investors were encouraged by the company&rsquo;s growth outlook and by signs that TTED products are gaining traction. At the same time, the stock&rsquo;s move was not large, which fits a report that mixed revenue growth with weaker profit margins. No unusual trading volume was provided.

For Q3 2026, Parade guided for revenue of $139 million to $153 million, with gross margin of 40% to 44% and operating expenses of $33 million to $36 million. Management said Q3 margins should improve slightly from Q2, which it hopes marked the trough.

Management also said full-year 2026 growth is now expected to be only a few percentage points above 2025, down from earlier expectations for mid-single-digit to 10% growth. The company blamed weaker notebook demand on high DRAM prices and earlier CPU shortages. Over the last twelve months, Parade achieved revenue growth of 9.5%, though the company now expects that pace to moderate.

Chief Executive Dr. Ji Zhao said the company expects TTED to remain a major growth engine. &ldquo;Our TT, which includes our TTED device, will continue to grow in the Q3, the percentage also will increase,&rdquo; he said. He added that the product supports both normal touch and active stylus use, which he said helps Parade increase silicon content in each notebook.

On margins, Zhao said OSAT costs rose repeatedly during the quarter and that the company is working with customers and suppliers to offset the impact. &ldquo;We are addressing the cost increase and by two direction,&rdquo; he said, referring to price adjustments and more cost-efficient manufacturing.

He was also upbeat about the custom-chip program. &ldquo;The project is moving forward and doing well,&rdquo; Zhao said, adding that the company is working closely with the customer on system design and emulation ahead of tape-out.

Analysts focused on four main issues: revenue mix, notebook demand, margin pressure and the company&rsquo;s new product pipeline.

Questions centered on TTED growth, which management said has exceeded expectations and now represents nearly 15% of revenue. Analysts also pressed for more detail on the notebook market, where management said demand has been hurt by memory prices but should improve in 2027 as CPU supply normalizes and new chips from NVIDIA, Qualcomm, AMD and Intel reach the market.

Margin questions took up much of the call. Management said Q2 gross margin may have been the low point and that it is working to pass through some cost increases while shifting to cheaper OSAT and foundry partners.

Analysts also asked about the ASIC-like custom chips. Parade said the first chip should tape out later in Q3 2026, with revenue expected in the second half of 2027. The company described the devices as large and complicated, but said progress has been strong.

Other questions covered data center cables and automotive products. Management said cable testing results were encouraging and that automotive revenue is growing as EV customers adopt more Parade content per vehicle.
