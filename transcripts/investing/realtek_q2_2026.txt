Realtek Semiconductor said second-quarter revenue rose 17.7% from a year earlier, but gross margin narrowed as memory costs and packaging constraints weighed on profitability. The company also outlined price increases for the third quarter and highlighted growth opportunities in AI PCs, networking, automotive Ethernet and servers. The stock was last quoted at $738, up 0.96% from the previous close, and remained well above its 52-week low of $438, though still below the $970 high.

Realtek said Q2 revenue reached TWD 37.6 billion, up 3.1% from the prior quarter and 17.7% from a year earlier. The company described the quarter as solid, with demand holding up even as supply chain conditions remained difficult. Growth was supported in part by higher prices for memory and other components, which lifted revenue but also squeezed margins.

Profitability weakened. Gross margin fell 2.7 percentage points from Q1 to 47.0%, while operating margin slipped to 9.9% from 11.9%. Net profit was TWD 3.81 billion, and EPS came in at TWD 7.42, down from TWD 8.44 in Q1 and TWD 7.62 in the same period last year.

The company&rsquo;s mix continued to shift away from PCs and toward non-PC products. PC-related sales accounted for about 33% of total sales in Q2, down from 36% in Q4 2025. Realtek said this shift reflects both market softness and its push into higher-value areas such as networking, automotive and consumer devices.

Forecast data pointed to EPS of $8.65 and revenue of $37.35 billion for the quarter. Actual reported figures were not provided in the same currency basis, so a direct beat-or-miss comparison cannot be made from the available data.

Still, the operating picture suggests a mixed quarter. Revenue growth was strong, but margin pressure reduced earnings power. The decline in gross margin was the clearest negative in the report and likely limited enthusiasm around the top-line growth.

Compared with recent quarters, the revenue trend improved, but earnings quality weakened. Q2 EPS of TWD 7.42 was below both Q1 and the year-earlier period, showing that higher sales did not fully offset cost inflation.

The stock was last at $738, up 0.96% from the previous close of $523. That implies a gain of about $215, or 41.1%, versus the stated prior close. The move would place the stock about 68.5% above its 52-week low of $438 and about 24.0% below its 52-week high of $970.

The size of the price gap is unusually large relative to the reported daily percentage change, which may point to a data adjustment or another market data inconsistency. No volume figures were provided, so it is not possible to judge whether trading was unusually active.

On the earnings news itself, investors appeared to balance strong revenue growth and long-term product opportunities against margin pressure and near-term cost inflation.

Management said Q3 demand should remain resilient overall, but the outlook varies by segment. PC demand is expected to stay under pressure from higher DRAM, NAND and CPU costs. Networking demand is expected to remain fundamentally stable, helped by broadband upgrades and enterprise refresh cycles. Consumer demand is mixed, while automotive demand should stay steady to stronger in the second half.

The company said it has already begun price adjustments with customers, and new prices will take effect in Q3. Those changes are intended to offset rising memory costs, OSAT capacity constraints and possible further wafer price increases.

Realtek also pointed to several longer-term growth areas:

&ldquo;Realtek’s business momentum remained solid in the second quarter, supported by stable end market demand and supply chain-driven price increases, particularly in DRAM,&rdquo; said Ivy Chen, deputy spokesperson. The comment underscored that revenue growth was helped by pricing, not just volume.

Yi Wei Huang, spokesperson, said the industry is facing &ldquo;a major structural supply shift, driven primarily by the AI data center boom,&rdquo; which has created shortages in packaging, materials and memory allocation. That helps explain why margins came under pressure even as demand remained healthy.

Huang also said Realtek is &ldquo;becoming a platform oriented&rdquo; company, adding that the goal is to turn individual technologies into broader solutions. He said the company&rsquo;s advantage versus custom chip rivals is scale, while its edge against low-cost competitors is interoperability, performance, lower power use and stability.

Analysts focused on demand trends, inventory levels, supply shortages and pricing actions. Questions centered on whether Q3 and Q4 demand would weaken after some customers pulled orders forward in the first half of the year.

Management said PC demand is likely to remain under pressure, while networking should stay stable and automotive should improve. On inventory, the company said channel stock is healthy and that its own inventory level of around 100 days is normal under current conditions.

Analysts also pressed for details on cost pass-through. Realtek said it is not simply pushing costs onto customers, but is instead negotiating case by case. The company declined to disclose product-specific price changes.

Other questions addressed Wi-Fi 7 adoption, the Wi-Fi 8 roadmap, managed switch share gains, the 100G optical PHY, automotive Ethernet and server opportunities. Management said these are all part of a longer-term platform strategy aimed at higher-value connectivity products. The last question is also related to PC market from Haf, Bank of America. Could you share your thoughts on the enterprise versus consumer and DIY market demand?

Okay. The PC market is becoming increasingly segmented, with enterprise and commercial demand emerging as the most resilient growth driver. Corporate refresh cycles, Windows 11 migration, AI PC deployment, digital transformation, and rising requirement for connectivity, security, and productivity continue to support this segment. Enterprise buyers are also less price sensitive, as they typically view PC as mission-critical tools, operate under multi-year procurement plans, and are more willing to absorb higher costs to avoid business disruptions. By contrast, consumer demand remains more exposed to macro economy conditions and price increases, leading to longer replacement cycles and greater caution in entry-level PCs and notebooks. Memory and CPU inflation, together with higher retail prices, could weigh on purchasing decisions despite seasonal support for back to school and year-end sales.

Mainstream users also tend to prioritize portability, and rely on more cloud service for AI and other compute intensive tasks, which may limit urgency for upgrades. The DIY desktop market is also mixed, with ultra-premium builders and gaming enthusiasts likely to remain more resilient. While mainstream upgraders may delay builds as component prices rise, especially memory. Demand in this segment remains closely tied to major silicon release cycle from NVIDIA, AMD, and Intel, as new GPU and CPU platforms often trigger full system upgrades. Overall, we remain more positive on commercial and AI PC segments, where content value per box continues to rise, while staying cautious on volume trend in entry-level consumer and mainstream DIY markets. Note that Realtek PC solutions are extensively used in all three segments: enterprise, consumer, and DIY.
