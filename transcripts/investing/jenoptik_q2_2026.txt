Jenoptik said it returned to revenue growth in the first half of 2026 and raised its full-year outlook after stronger-than-expected demand in semiconductor equipment and biophotonics helped lift profitability. The German photonics company reported H1 revenue growth of 1% year over year, or about 3% excluding currency effects, while EBITDA rose about 25% to roughly EUR 99 million. Jenoptik also lifted its 2026 revenue growth target to 5% to 9% and its EBITDA margin forecast to 20% to 21%. The stock was up 3.05% to $40.34 in the latest session, above the prior close of $39.07 and closer to the upper end of its 52-week range of $16.05 to $48.42.

Jenoptik&rsquo;s first half showed a clearer recovery in both growth and profitability. Revenue rose modestly, but the more important story was the improvement in margins and order momentum. The company said the semi-ramp is &ldquo;in full swing,&rdquo; and that customer activity remained strong in both lithography and semi-inspection.

The company also benefited from a lower cost base after a 2025 restructuring program. At the same time, it faced mixed conditions across its portfolio. Smart Mobility Solutions grew about 11%, while Metrology & Production Solutions remained under pressure from a weak European automotive market. Biophotonics posted lower sales, but order intake in that unit rose 45% year over year, supported by defense and MedTech demand.

Overall, Jenoptik&rsquo;s results suggest a business that is gaining operating leverage as demand improves in key end markets, particularly semiconductors and defense-related optics.

- Full-year EBITDA margin guidance: 20% to 21%

The company’s strong liquidity position is reflected in its current ratio of 2.29, while revenue over the last twelve months reached $1.21 billion. Jenoptik&rsquo;s supplied forecast called for EPS of $0.3887 and revenue of $274.33 million for the period, but the actual quarter-level figures were not provided in the earnings data. That makes a direct beat-or-miss calculation impossible from the available consensus comparison.

Even so, the company&rsquo;s reported H1 performance points to a stronger-than-expected operating trend. EPS rose 64% year over year to EUR 0.69, while EBITDA climbed more than 25%. The company also raised full-year guidance, which usually signals that management sees demand and execution improving enough to offset weaker areas such as automotive-related businesses.

The most important takeaway for investors is that the company&rsquo;s earnings power appears to be improving faster than revenue growth alone would suggest. That often matters more than a small top-line change.

Jenoptik shares rose 3.05% to $40.34 in the latest session, up from a previous close of $39.07. The move suggests a positive investor response to the stronger margin profile and higher guidance.

The stock remains below its 52-week high of $48.42, but it has recovered sharply from the 52-week low of $16.05. This impressive recovery has delivered a 112.71% return over the past year and a 44.44% gain over the last six months. InvestingPro data highlights this as a "high return over the last year," part of the platform’s comprehensive suite of metrics, expert insights, and ad-free analysis tools available to subscribers. That places the shares in the upper part of their yearly trading range, reflecting a more constructive view of the company&rsquo;s turnaround.

The gain was not dramatic, which may indicate that investors had already expected some improvement after recent strength in semiconductors and defense optics. Still, the combination of better profitability, a larger backlog and an improved outlook appears to have supported buying interest.

Jenoptik raised its full-year 2026 revenue growth forecast to 5% to 9%, from a previous single-digit growth outlook. It also moved its EBITDA margin target to 20% to 21%, the upper half of its original 19% to 21% range.

Management said the semiconductor and advanced manufacturing business is expected to keep growing, though not at the exceptional order levels seen in H1. The company is also expanding capacity in Jena and Dresden to support semi-inspection and lithography demand. Those investments are expected to help in coming quarters, although some facilities will not have a material impact immediately.

The company said H2 should be better than H1 in Metrology & Production Solutions, helped by seasonality and a better order picture. Smart Mobility Solutions is also expected to keep growing, while optical data communications should continue to benefit from data-center demand.

Jenoptik also said its strategic review is still under way, with findings expected no earlier than the end of 2026.

CEO Dominic Dorfner said photonics has &ldquo;a lot of growth potential&rdquo; and described joining Jenoptik as a &ldquo;dream,&rdquo; underscoring his confidence in the company&rsquo;s long-term position.

CFO and investor relations head Prisca Havranek-Kosicek said the company saw &ldquo;exceptionally strong order intake dynamics&rdquo; in its OEM businesses and added that the semi ramp-up is &ldquo;in full swing.&rdquo; She also said the company is focused on &ldquo;adding capacities and our ability to deliver.&rdquo;

On strategy, Dorfner said Jenoptik is reviewing its businesses, with outcomes expected toward the end of the year at the earliest. That suggests the company is still shaping its portfolio and long-term priorities.

Analysts focused heavily on the strength and durability of semiconductor demand. Management said the strong order intake in semi reflects normal customer behavior during the industry ramp-up, rather than a one-time pull-forward. It also said customers may be securing capacity, but that H1 levels should not be assumed to continue unchanged.

Questions also centered on capacity and staffing. Jenoptik said it is generally able to find the workers it needs, although skilled operators remain harder to recruit. The company said supply chain partners are expected to expand in step with its own growth.

Another area of interest was the biophotonics business, where Jenoptik disclosed a large MedTech order and said defense demand remains strong. Management declined to give more detail because of the concentrated nature of the customer base.

Analysts also asked about the optical data communications business, where demand is being helped by data-center spending. Jenoptik said the business is growing from a smaller base and serves multiple customers, which reduces concentration risk.

Finally, questions touched on the company&rsquo;s stance on Chinese semiconductor OEMs. Management said Jenoptik does not sell to Chinese suppliers in its semi business and that the decision remains unchanged.
