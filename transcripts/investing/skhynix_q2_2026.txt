SK hynix reported record second-quarter 2026 results, with revenue rising to KRW 79.3 trillion and operating income reaching KRW 60.5 trillion, both all-time highs. The company said demand from AI infrastructure and tight supply conditions drove the surge, while DRAM and NAND prices climbed sharply. Despite the strong quarter, the stock fell 8.98% in regular trading to $130.17 and slipped another 2.05% after hours to $127.5, leaving it near the low end of its 52-week range.

SK hynix delivered a rare combination of rapid growth and unusually high profitability in the second quarter. Revenue and operating income both hit record levels, helped by strong pricing, a better product mix and continued demand for AI memory chips.

The company said the market for memory is changing as AI moves from training to inference and becomes more widely used in search, coding and productivity tools. Management described this as a structural shift in demand, with both AI memory and conventional memory growing at the same time.

The results also showed how important high-value products have become. Sales of enterprise SSDs doubled from the previous quarter, and Solidigm&rsquo;s high-capacity enterprise SSD revenue rose more than threefold. On the DRAM side, sales were led by HBM3E and AI server DRAM products, while the company also began supply of SOCAMM2 products.

- Debt-to-equity ratio: 7%, down 5 percentage points from the previous quarter end.

The company’s financial strength is reflected in its impressive gross profit margin of 68%, while return on assets reached 43% over the last twelve months as of Q1 2026, according to InvestingPro data. No forecast figures were provided for the quarter, so a direct comparison with analyst expectations was not available. Even without a formal estimate, the scale of the results points to a very strong quarter.

Revenue of KRW 79.3 trillion and operating income of KRW 60.5 trillion were record highs, and the company said both figures reached all-time levels. The operating margin of 76% also marked a new peak. Compared with the company&rsquo;s recent history, the quarter stood out not just for growth, but for the speed of the improvement.

The main drivers were clear: higher DRAM and NAND prices, stronger AI-related demand, and a richer mix of premium products. Non-operating gains also lifted bottom-line profit, including KRW 63.3 trillion from the sale and valuation of investment assets.

The stock moved in the opposite direction of the earnings report. SK hynix closed the regular session at $130.17, down 8.98% from the previous close of $143.02. In after-hours trading, it fell another 2.05% to $127.5, or $2.67 lower.

That put the shares near the bottom of their 52-week range of $128.38 to $194.8, and the after-hours price slipped slightly below that low. InvestingPro data confirms the stock is trading just 1% above its 52-week low, with shares down 21% over the past week alone. The platform’s Financial Health Score rates SK hynix as "GREAT" with an overall score of 3.71 out of 5, suggesting strong fundamentals despite the recent selloff. The decline suggests investors may have been focused less on the record quarter and more on what comes next, including capital spending, pricing durability and how much of the current strength is already reflected in the share price.

No trading volume data was provided, so it is not possible to determine whether the move came with unusually heavy activity.

For the third quarter of 2026, SK hynix said DRAM shipments should rise about 10% from the second quarter, with the company actively responding to demand, especially in server products. NAND bit shipments are expected to rise by a low single-digit percentage.

Management also said 2026 capital spending is expected to reach the high KRW 40 trillion range, as the company accelerates investment and pulls forward the mass production schedule at its M15X facility. The clean room at Yongin Phase 1 is expected to open in early 2027.

Song Hyeon-jong, president of the corporate center, said the quarter reflected broad demand strength. &ldquo;Second quarter revenue increased by 51% quarter-on-quarter and 257% year-on-year, reaching KRW 79.3 trillion, marking a record high revenue following the previous quarter,&rdquo; he said.

He also framed AI demand as a long-term driver. &ldquo;AI technology is evolving into an agentic form that performs complex tasks on behalf of users for extended periods,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;As AI spreads across various services such as search, coding, and productivity tools, the scope of demand is widening from a memory perspective.&rdquo;

On supply discipline, he added: &ldquo;Our capacity expansion will be executed flexibly in alignment with confirmed customer demand, we do not believe our medium to long-term investment plans will lead to oversupply right away.&rdquo;

CFO Kim Woo-hyun addressed investor concerns about AI infrastructure spending, saying the company sees current trends as a shift in how existing infrastructure is used. &ldquo;We view these developments not as a sign of AI investment slowdown, but more as a transition towards higher utilization of the AI infrastructure that has already been built at scale and as well as accelerated monetization efforts,&rdquo; he said.

Analysts focused on several key issues during the call.

One major topic was whether AI infrastructure spending is slowing. Management said it sees the trend differently, arguing that customers are using existing infrastructure more efficiently and pushing for faster monetization, not cutting back on investment.

Another focus was long-term capacity expansion. Management said its plans are based on structural AI demand and customer discussions, including long-term agreements. It said it has already completed LTA negotiations with around 10 customers and will expand capacity in stages to avoid oversupply.

Analysts also asked about HBM pricing for 2027. The company said talks are ongoing with major customers and that pricing will reflect not only conventional DRAM trends, but also the higher resource needs, technical complexity and value of HBM.

Questions on HBM4 competitiveness drew a strong response. Management said its advantage comes from more than performance alone. It pointed to scale, stable yields, quality and customer trust as key strengths that are difficult for rivals to copy quickly.

Finally, analysts asked about shareholder returns after the company&rsquo;s cash position improved. Management said it is reviewing additional measures and aims to share a plan with the market within the year.
