Aalberts Industries said first-half 2026 revenue rose 5% organically and profit margins improved, helped by stronger demand in semiconductors and steady gains across its three businesses. The Dutch industrial group reported EUR 1.5 billion in revenue, EBITA of EUR 225 million and earnings per share of EUR 1.47, up EUR 0.09 from a year earlier. The shares were little changed in early trading, at $41.68, up 0.1% from the previous close of $41.64 and near the top of their 52-week range.

Aalberts said its first-half performance reflected broad-based demand and the benefits of portfolio reshaping through acquisitions and divestments. Revenue grew to EUR 1.5 billion, while net profit rose to EUR 158 million. The company also said its added value margin increased by more than 200 basis points to about 65.5%, helped by pricing discipline and a better mix of businesses.

The results point to a company that is still growing, but also improving quality of earnings. That matters in industrial groups, where investors often look for both volume growth and margin discipline. Aalberts said its balance sheet remained resilient, with debt-to-EBITDA at 1.9 times, unchanged from year-end 2025.

The company&rsquo;s three segments all contributed to growth, but the semiconductor business drew the most attention. Management said demand remained strong in both front-end and back-end markets, supported by AI-related investment and customer requests for additional capacity.

The company&rsquo;s earnings release did not include a reported EPS or revenue figure for the specific forecast period shown in the market data, which had revenue expectations of $755.5 million. Because no actual consensus-comparable EPS or revenue number was provided for that period, a direct beat-or-miss comparison is not possible.

Still, the half-year results were clearly stronger than the prior year. EPS rose to EUR 1.47 from EUR 1.38, while EBITA margin expanded and free cash flow improved. For investors, that combination is often more important than a narrow quarterly surprise, especially when a company is in the middle of portfolio changes and capacity investment.

Aalberts shares were trading at $41.68, up 0.1% from the previous close of $41.64. The stock was near its 52-week high of $41.78 and far above its 52-week low of $25.78, suggesting the market has already rewarded the company for its recent execution.

The small move indicates a restrained reaction so far. That may reflect the absence of a clear earnings surprise in the available market data, even as the underlying operating update was solid. The stock&rsquo;s position near the top of its range suggests investors may already be pricing in some of the company&rsquo;s stronger trends, especially in semiconductors and margin improvement.

Management said it is entering the second half with &ldquo;positive momentum&rdquo; and a &ldquo;very healthy order book,&rdquo; and reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook for improved organic growth and EBITA margin versus 2025.

The company kept full-year capital expenditure guidance at EUR 190 million, in line with last year. It said spending will rise in the second half as major projects move forward, including the Dronten factory in the Netherlands and capacity additions in Penang, Malaysia.

Management said the semiconductor business should continue to grow at roughly the same pace in the second half as in the second quarter, around 16% organic growth, with further upside possible as new capacity comes online in 2027 and beyond.

Chief Executive St&eacute;phane Simonetta said the company&rsquo;s first half showed &ldquo;positive organic revenue growth in our three segments, particularly in semicon,&rdquo; and added that the portfolio changes made over the past year are contributing to both growth and margins.

He also said Aalberts is benefiting from major long-term trends, including urbanization, technology acceleration, reshoring and decarbonization. &ldquo;We continue to see positive end market dynamics,&rdquo; he said, pointing to the company&rsquo;s position in industrial markets and its ability to win business through speed and execution.

On the semiconductor side, Simonetta said the company is seeing &ldquo;accelerated growth&rdquo; and strong demand in both front-end and back-end markets. He said customer capacity requests are increasing and that the company&rsquo;s expansion plans are aligned with that demand.

Chief Financial Officer Frans den Houter said acquisitions contributed EUR 24.4 million to EBITA, while divestments reduced EBITA by more than EUR 15 million. He said the acquired businesses carried margins above 18%, while divested businesses had margins of a little over 8%.

Analysts focused on three main topics: the Building segment&rsquo;s margin pressure, the pace of semiconductor growth, and the company&rsquo;s capital spending plans.

Questions on Building centered on weak residential demand in the UK and the impact of the Middle East. Management said the segment was hurt by shipping issues in the Middle East, low residential activity in Europe and some one-off costs.

In semiconductors, analysts pressed for visibility on future growth. Management said second-half 2026 organic growth should be similar to the second quarter, around 16%, and that 2027 should be in a similar range, with potential acceleration as new capacity comes online.

Analysts also asked about CapEx and the Dronten plant. Management said first-half spending was low because of timing, not a structural shift, and that full-year CapEx should still reach EUR 190 million. It said the Dronten site is expected to ramp in 2027 and should add about EUR 6 million in annual depreciation once operational.

Questions on data centers drew attention as well. Management said the opportunity is still small, at about 2% of Building revenue, but that it has a EUR 1.5 billion addressable market and is growing at double-digit rates, especially in North America.
