Siemens Energy said its fiscal third-quarter results showed a sharp improvement in profitability, with profit before special items tripling from a year earlier and the margin before special items rising to 14.2%. The company also reported EUR 17.9 billion in quarterly revenue and said free cash flow reached EUR 7.2 billion in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, leaving about EUR 800 million to meet its full-year target of EUR 8 billion. Shares rose 3.57% to $156.24, up from the previous close of $150.86.

Siemens Energy said its third quarter reflected broad improvement across the business, driven by better execution, cost control and stronger demand in several markets. Management described the company as being in the middle of a major electrification cycle, with demand supported by power generation, grid investment and industrial customers.

The company&rsquo;s wind business remained a work in progress, but the quarter marked an important step. Wind posted a positive result for the first time in 15 quarters, although the segment still reported a small double-digit loss over the first nine months of the fiscal year. Management said the turnaround is progressing, but more work is needed before the unit reaches sustained profitability.

The company also pointed to strong demand in the Middle East and from data centers. It said the Middle East accounted for about 30% of gigawatt capacity orders in the quarter, while data centers represented about 20% of gas turbine order capacity. Management said these trends support its long-term growth outlook.

The company did not provide directly comparable actual EPS and revenue figures against the analyst forecast in the available data. The forecast called for EPS of $1.16 and revenue of $11.16 billion for the reported period, but the transcript focused on the company&rsquo;s euro-denominated operating results and margin performance.

Even without a direct EPS comparison, the quarter appears to have been stronger than the market may have expected on a profitability basis. Profit before special items tripled year over year, and the margin before special items reached 14.2%, which suggests the company delivered a meaningful beat in operating performance. The improvement was especially notable because it came alongside continued progress in the wind turnaround and strong cash generation.

Siemens Energy shares rose 3.57% to $156.24 after the update, compared with the previous close of $150.86. The stock has delivered a 17.86% return over the past week and is up 30.42% year-to-date, with a one-year return of 58.65%. The stock traded near the middle of its 52-week range of $83.32 to $191.66, leaving room above current levels but well above the low point of the past year. The company currently trades at a P/E ratio of 64.6, reflecting investor optimism about future growth prospects.

The move suggests investors welcomed the stronger profitability, improved cash flow and signs that the wind business is stabilizing. The stock&rsquo;s rise also reflects confidence in management&rsquo;s comments about demand in the Middle East, data centers and electrification. No unusual volume data was provided.

Siemens Energy kept its full-year guidance intact and said it remains confident in meeting its targets. Management said free cash flow guidance of EUR 8 billion for fiscal 2026 remains achievable, with about EUR 800 million still expected in the fourth quarter.

The company also said its margin guidance remains in the 10% to 12% range, with nine-month performance already at 12.6%, putting it at the upper end of the target. Management said Q4 will include significant capital spending, which is normal for the business and may affect quarterly cash flow timing.

Looking ahead, management said early signs for 2027 are positive and that demand should remain constructive. A more detailed update on fiscal 2027 and the medium-term outlook is expected in November. Siemens Energy also said Siemens Gamesa expects break-even in 2026 and cash break-even in 2028.

Chief Executive Christian Bruch said the company&rsquo;s customer relationships are long term and built over decades, not on short-term gains. He said, &ldquo;We have a long-term relationship with our customers, and that’s very important because we’re in a business here that is done for decades and is not a short-term optimization thing.&rdquo;

Bruch also pointed to the company&rsquo;s broader market position, saying, &ldquo;We’re at the beginning of electrification, this major wave of electrification, we see that the share of electricity in the overall energy demand is growing continuously.&rdquo;

On the company&rsquo;s portfolio strategy, he said, &ldquo;The beauty of Siemens Energy at the moment is that both segments are very profitable. They’re growing, they’re very successful, they’re battling for the same resources when it comes to capital investments.&rdquo;

He added that the company is taking a measured approach to any changes: &ldquo;Nothing’s going to happen rashly overnight and quickly because what we are talking about is so important and when things are up for discussion, we take our time.&rdquo;

Analysts focused on several themes during the call, including the strength of demand in the Middle East, the role of data centers, and the company&rsquo;s portfolio strategy.

One question centered on whether Siemens Energy could raise reservation agreements above the current 10% to 15% range. Bruch said the company is not trying to squeeze customers, but rather to capture positive market momentum while keeping the balance sheet protected.

Another area of focus was the possible restructuring of the Transformation of Industry business. Bruch said the company is reviewing how to keep both major segments competitive over the next three to 10 years, but stressed that no decisions have been made and that the process will take time.

Analysts also asked about wind industry consolidation in Europe and competition from Chinese manufacturers. Bruch said European companies cannot match Chinese asset costs and that any broader solution would need to involve policymakers, not just manufacturers.

Questions about data centers and the order backlog also drew attention. Bruch said data centers are an important market, but not as dominant as some reports suggest, and confirmed that the EUR 162 billion backlog includes firm orders only, not reservation agreements.
