WEG S.A. reported third-quarter 2026 earnings that came in slightly below expectations, with earnings per share of $0.3969 versus a forecast of $0.4067 and revenue of $10.86 billion versus $10.92 billion expected. The Brazilian industrial equipment maker said revenue still rose 5.7% from a year earlier, while EBITDA increased 3.1% to BRL 2.3 billion. Shares were down 1.02% to $44.75, leaving the stock below its recent close and well under its 52-week high of $54.53.

Key Takeaways

WEG missed both earnings and revenue estimates, but by a narrow margin.Revenue rose 5.7% year over year, supported by stronger international sales.EBITDA margin held at 21.6%, though it fell 130 basis points from the second quarter.International revenue reached 62.5% of total sales, the highest level ever for the company.Management pointed to strong demand in generators, transformers, and data center cooling systems.Cost inflation, tariffs, and ramp-up expenses remained the main pressure points.

Company Performance

WEG delivered a solid quarter by operating standards, even if the headline results fell short of Wall Street’s forecast. The company said net operating revenue increased 5.7% from the same period a year earlier, helped by strong international demand that offset weakness in Brazil.

The company’s performance reflected a split picture. International markets posted double-digit growth in dollar terms, while the domestic market was softer in several areas, including short-cycle equipment and industrial products tied to Brazil’s slower economy and high interest rates.

The quarter also highlighted WEG’s growing global footprint. International revenue accounted for 62.5% of total sales, the highest level ever in the company’s generation, transmission and distribution business. That mix helped support growth, even as cost pressures intensified.

Earnings vs. Forecast

WEG’s third-quarter results missed expectations on both the top and bottom lines. EPS came in at $0.3969, below the $0.4067 consensus estimate by $0.0098, or 2.41%. Revenue totaled $10.86 billion, missing the $10.92 billion forecast by $0.06 billion, or 0.55%.

The shortfall was modest, not dramatic. That matters because the company still posted growth in revenue, EBITDA, and return on invested capital. The market is likely weighing the small miss against the more important issue of margin pressure from raw materials, tariffs, and new plant ramp-up costs.

Compared with a company that is still growing revenue and maintaining a margin above 21%, this was not a severe disappointment. But the sequential margin decline may have been enough to keep investors cautious.

Market Reaction

WEG shares were down 1.02% at $44.75, compared with a previous close of $45.21. That puts the stock about $0.46 lower on the day and roughly 18% below its 52-week high, while still about 11% above its 52-week low of $40.19.

The move suggests a restrained negative reaction rather than a sharp selloff. Investors appeared to focus on the earnings miss and on margin compression, even though the company delivered solid year-over-year growth. The stock’s position near the middle-to-lower part of its annual range indicates that sentiment remains cautious.

No unusual trading volume was provided, so it is not possible to determine whether the decline came with heavier-than-normal activity. Outlook & Guidance

Management said it remains confident in continued revenue growth, supported by traditional businesses and newer growth areas. The company expects international markets to keep delivering double-digit growth, while Brazil may stabilize as political uncertainty eases and demand improves.

Management said it expects margins to remain healthy, though cost inflation and tariffs may continue to create pressure in the near term. It also said pricing actions and productivity gains should help offset some of those costs over time.

Executive Commentary

Chief Financial and Administrative Officer André Luís Rodrigues said international sales are now the company’s biggest strength. “International revenue accounted for 62.5% of total revenue, the highest level ever in energy generation, transmission, and distribution,” he said.

Finance and Investor Relations Director André Menegueti Salgueiro said the generator business was a key driver of the quarter. “The novelty of this quarter is the good performance of the generator sector. This is what most contributed to the growth of the sector, more than T&D,” he said.

Rodrigues also stressed WEG’s competitive position. “No company in the world has reached the level of verticalization that WEG has reached,” he said, adding that innovation remains central to competing with Chinese and other global manufacturers.

Risks and Challenges

Raw material inflation: Copper prices were up 40% year over year and steel more than 20%, raising production costs.Tariff pressure: U.S. Section 232 tariffs on transformers exported from Mexico added cost and uncertainty.Brazil slowdown: High interest rates and political uncertainty are weighing on domestic demand.Ramp-up costs: New plants in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia are still in the early stages and are pressuring margins.Freight and logistics: Geopolitical disruptions have pushed freight costs higher.Competition: Chinese rivals remain aggressive in industrial equipment and related markets.

Q&A

Analysts focused on margins, the Brazil slowdown, tariffs, and the company’s investment plans.

Questions centered on why EBITDA margin fell 130 basis points from the second quarter, despite strong growth in North American transformers. Management pointed to raw material inflation, tariffs, personnel costs, and pre-operational expenses tied to new facilities.

Another area of interest was the company’s tariff reimbursement process. Management said reimbursements are still being worked through with authorities and will likely come in tranches over time rather than in one payment.

Analysts also asked about the BESS business and the upcoming auction in Brazil. WEG said the market is an important long-term opportunity and that its expanded plant investment reflects confidence in demand.

Several questions addressed WEG’s international strategy, especially in China and North America. Management said the company is building local capabilities in China and continues to invest in capacity where demand is strongest, including data center cooling, generators, and transformers.
