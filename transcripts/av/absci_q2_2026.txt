We continue to anticipate an interim proof-of-concept readout in the form of 13-week data later this year. Then, we anticipate our full proof-of-concept data in the form of 26-week data in early 2027. As a reminder, the HEADLINE trial is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. The primary endpoints are safety and tolerability, while secondary endpoints include PK, PD, immunogenicity, target area hair count, target area hair width, and target area darkening/pigmentation. We will also collect patient-reported outcomes data in this study. The prolactin receptor mechanism for hair growth is unique. Unlike minoxidil, finasteride, or nutraceuticals, the mechanism underlying ABS-201 is regenerative for the hair follicle: protecting and promoting hair follicle stem cells, restoring CD34 progenitor cells, and stimulating key growth factors IGF-1 and FGF-7 while decreasing the catechin-driving TGF-beta 2. This regenerative mechanism is unique to the anti-prolactin receptor pathway and supports significant durable effects with the potential to grow new hair and dormant follicles. Our clinical study is designed to showcase this unique mechanism with our 13-week assessment, which is interim in nature and intended to provide a directionally positive signal — i.e., has the mechanism begun to take hold? Is it building the machinery and building blocks we see responsible for regenerating the hairs? A directionally positive signal of hair growth at 13 weeks would give us further confidence in seeing more robust hair growth at 26 weeks and beyond, as is supported by preclinical data in the stump-tailed macaque. Altogether, the preclinical stump-tailed macaque data as well as our human ex vivo data from skin biopsies of both male and female donors provide robust support for this mechanism and its ability to provide durable hair growth. Arseniy Shabashvili (Analyst (BTIG)): Hi, team. Thank you so much for taking the question. It appears from your modeling that ABS-201 can maintain greater than 90% prolactin receptor occupancy without cycling. How should the more sustained receptor occupancy impact efficacy compared to other prolactin receptor antibodies in pattern hair loss, and then secondarily in endometriosis? Thanks so much.

Sean McClain (Founder and CEO): Yeah, great question. We published updated modeling in the corporate deck today that illustrates this clearly. If you look at HMI-115 dosing once every two weeks at 240 milligrams, you see an oscillation that occurs between roughly 60% to 70% receptor occupancy. From our perspective, effective blockade of the prolactin receptor requires well above 90% receptor occupancy to significantly blunt signaling; even a little receptor left can permit substantial signaling. We believe HMI-115's oscillation and lower occupancy contributed to a lack of robust efficacy because they were unable to achieve prolonged anagen phase. With ABS-201 and the PK profile we've observed, two to three doses provide sustained occupancy well above 90%, which should allow the follicle to remain in prolonged anagen to rebuild the stem cell niche. Growth does not happen overnight — you need a sustained period of blockade to get regrowth. The stump-tailed macaque data showed that when you achieved greater than 90% receptor occupancy, you saw very robust efficacy. We are targeting a 30-hair increase but believe that hitting the receptor appropriately and sustaining the anagen state could provide upside beyond that. In short, the sustained receptor occupancy is central to achieving meaningful regenerative effects in pattern hair loss.

Ransi Somaratne, FACC (Chief Medical Officer & Head of R&D): Happy to add on endometriosis: the same principle applies. When you look at other prolactin receptor antibodies, you can see some effect at the highest doses on symptoms like dysmenorrhea. We think you need to achieve a high level of receptor occupancy to get efficacy in endometriosis as well. Although the disease biology differs, the occupancy-efficacy relationship appears to be consistent across indications.
