Lilly's oral GLP-1, Foundayo (orforglipron), was associated with significant reductions in predicted long-term risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease

October 1, 2026

PDF Version

In post-hoc analyses of ATTAIN-1, Foundayo 17.2 mg was associated with a predicted 57% and 18% reduction in risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease vs. placebo

Combined with recent research on the savings tied to weight loss, these findings suggest Foundayo could help reduce long-term spending for patients and healthcare systems

INDIANAPOLIS , Oct. 1, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Eli Lilly and Company (NYSE: LLY), the maker of Zepbound (tirzepatide), today announced results from post-hoc analyses of ATTAIN-1 showing that Foundayo was associated with significant reductions in predicted 10-year risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease in adults with obesity or overweight with a weight-related medical problem. Obesity is strongly associated with an increased risk of developing both cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes. 1 Through its proven benefit in weight loss and its ability to improve certain cardiovascular risk factors like blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol, Foundayo may be a meaningful treatment option for the more than 100 million U.S . adults living with obesity. 2 The findings of these analyses were published in Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism .

"For many people living with obesity or overweight, the concern isn't just what the scale says today, it's whether their future could include type 2 diabetes or heart disease," said Thomas Seck , M.D., senior vice president of product development, Lilly Cardiometabolic Health . "These additional analyses of ATTAIN-1 show that Foundayo was associated with a lower predicted long-term risk of both conditions, in addition to meaningful weight loss. We've also seen evidence that treating obesity and reducing the risk of its downstream consequences, like cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes, can meaningfully lower healthcare costs. 3,4,5 That gives us real optimism about what long-term Foundayo use could mean, both for patients and for the broader healthcare system."

In these analyses, modeling showed that Foundayo was associated with clinically meaningful improvements in predicted 10-year risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease across all three doses. 6 At 72 weeks, participants taking Foundayo 5.5 mg, 9 mg and 17.2 mg showed an estimated 45.0%, 50.0% and 57.0% reduction, respectively, in predicted type 2 diabetes risk versus placebo. 7,8 Participants taking Foundayo 17.2 mg also showed an estimated 18.0% reduction in predicted cardiovascular disease risk versus placebo.
