Why It Matters

The teams shipping the most interesting AI products right now are small, fast, and spending a growing share of their budget on tokens. For an early-stage company, inference cost is a product decision. It shapes which models you can afford to put in front of users, how many requests you can serve per dollar, and how quickly you can move from a working demo to paying customers.

GMI Cloud was built for exactly this stage. One API key opens 200+ models, including Claude, GPT, Gemini, and the leading open-source models, at pricing that typically runs 10 to 30 percent below official token rates. Teams switch models with a single parameter change and scale to dedicated endpoints once traffic settles into a steady pattern. Underneath all of it sits GMI's own GPU infrastructure, recognized by NVIDIA as an Exemplar Cloud on GB300 NVL72 systems.

By joining the a16z speedrun marketplace, we get to put that infrastructure in the hands of some of the most promising AI companies at the moment it matters most: while they are still deciding what to build and how much it will cost to run.

How GMI Cloud Works

GMI Cloud is an AI inference platform with its own GPU infrastructure underneath. Teams pick the layer that fits how they work:

Inference Engine : OpenAI-compatible API access to 200+ models. Keep your existing client code, change the model name, and pay standard token rates on the model you choose.

Dedicated endpoints : Reserved capacity for production workloads that need predictable latency and throughput.

Bare-metal GPU compute : Dedicated NVIDIA infrastructure, including GB300 NVL72 systems, for training, fine-tuning, and large-scale serving.
