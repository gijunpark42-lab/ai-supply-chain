Press Release 19 October 2026 Siemens AG Munich

Siemens partners with NVIDIA to advance

AI-driven, autonomous factories

New AI solutions showcased at Siemens’ Erlangen factory advance its role

as a global blueprint for AI-driven, autonomous manufacturing

Fully

virtual production ramp-up reduces engineering effort by up to 85 percent

Agent-based

production planning enables more adaptive manufacturing and cost savings of up

to 20 percent

Siemens has

unveiled new AI solutions at its Electronics Factory in Erlangen, Germany,

marking a new milestone in its partnership with NVIDIA to advance AI-driven,

autonomous manufacturing. The applications that are currently unique in the manufacturing

industry use AI and digital twins to accelerate production ramp-up and enable

more adaptive production planning. Both companies announced at CES in Las Vegas

in January 2026 that they would build the world’s first fully AI-powered

manufacturing sites. Since then, both companies have been co-developing and

testing applications in live production at the Erlangen plant.

Siemens has

unveiled new AI solutions at its Electronics Factory in Erlangen, Germany,

marking a new milestone in its partnership with NVIDIA to advance AI-driven,

autonomous manufacturing. The applications that are currently unique in the manufacturing

industry use AI and digital twins to accelerate production ramp-up and enable

more adaptive production planning. Both companies announced at CES in Las Vegas

in January 2026 that they would build the world’s first fully AI-powered

manufacturing sites. Since then, both companies have been co-developing and

testing applications in live production at the Erlangen plant.

Erlangen serves as a living lab for industrial AI and a blueprint

for scaling these capabilities across industries. Siemens and NVIDIA aim to

make artificial intelligence the operating system of manufacturing,

transforming how products are designed, manufactured and delivered across the

entire value chain. Further applications are planned in the coming months.

“The

historic opportunity for Germany and Europe lies in bringing artificial

intelligence out of the data centers and into the factories – and applying it

on an industrial scale,” says Cedrik Neike, member of the Managing Board of

Siemens AG and President Digital Industries. “Today, together with NVIDIA

at our Siemens Electronic Works in Erlangen, we are showing how this can be

done. With an annual productivity increase of seven percent, Erlangen is one of

the most advanced manufacturing plants in the world. At the same time, with

around 3,000 customer visits every year, it is a global meeting place for

industrial innovation. Here, the future of industry is not just discussed; it

is becoming reality today.”

“Factories

need to handle change as well as they handle repetition. AI and simulation make

that possible,” said Rev Lebaredian, Vice President, Physical AI Simulation at

NVIDIA. “At Erlangen, Siemens and NVIDIA are putting years of collaboration to

work on the factory floor – training inspection systems before physical

prototypes exist, designing and testing production plans in simulation before

changing the real world. Together, we’re building a blueprint for autonomous

factories everywhere.”

The joint Siemens and NVIDIA team developed the first

new AI offerings in less than ten months,

combining NVIDIA’s AI,

simulation and accelerated computing

technologies with Siemens’ industrial software, automation and manufacturing expertise. Testing the solutions directly in a production

environment enabled the teams to develop solutions around real manufacturing

requirements. These new technologies can deliver unprecedented improvements in

areas such as production planning and product development.

For new printed circuit

boards (PCB) assemblies – the core of the drives and frequency converters

manufactured in Erlangen – virtual production

preparation can reduce engineering

effort by up to 85 percent and cut time-to-market by up to 40 percent.

The workflow addresses a critical bottleneck: preparing quality

inspection previously took several weeks, because teams had to wait for

physical prototypes to train vision systems and manually configure inspection equipment.

AI now generates photorealistic representations of PCB assemblies from their

digital twins to train the vision systems, enabling engineers to prepare

inspection before physical prototypes are available.

In the plant’s

production planning, new AI technologies also promise to master the balancing

act between capacity, material availability, delivery dates and production lines

much faster and more efficiently. Previously, planners had to weigh various

factors against each other based on their experience to make the best possible

decision. At the Siemens Electronics Factory in Erlangen, an AI agent now

supports this process. When new orders arrive, it automatically generates

various production scenarios and shows planners the impact of each option based

on defined KPIs. Before implementation, these proposals are validated using a Digital

Twin of the entire production facility. This end-to-end agent-based production

planning can enable cost savings of up to 20 percent, while allowing

manufacturing operations to respond more flexibly to new orders, material

shortages, or shifting customer requirements.

These

advances combine Siemens’ industrial software and manufacturing expertise with

NVIDIA technologies. NVIDIA Cosmos world models generate realistic training

data for quality inspection. The NVIDIA Factory Operations Blueprint supports

AI agents that create production plans and help factories respond to changing

production needs, while NVIDIA Omniverse libraries enable teams to test those

plans virtually using real factory data.

Established

in 1971, the Siemens Erlangen plant in Southern Germany employs approximately

1,000 people and manufactures power electronics and automation technology for

industry and infrastructure – including machine tools, battery production, as

well as the process and discrete manufacturing industries. For more than a

decade, new digital manufacturing technologies have been tested, developed and

deployed at the site during live operations. Currently, more than 100 AI

algorithms are already in active use across production, logistics, quality

inspection and maintenance. The location is set for further expansion through

2030, with Siemens investing €500 million in the transformation.

Siemens AG (Berlin and Munich) is a leading technology company focused on industry, infrastructure, mobility, and healthcare. The company’s purpose is to create technology to transform the everyday, for everyone. By combining the real and the digital worlds, Siemens empowers customers to accelerate their digital and sustainability transformations, making factories more efficient, cities more livable, and transportation more sustainable. A leader in industrial AI, Siemens leverages its deep domain know-how to apply AI – including generative AI – to real-world applications, making AI accessible and impactful for customers across diverse industries. Siemens also owns a majority stake in the publicly listed company Siemens Healthineers, a leading global medical technology provider pioneering breakthroughs in healthcare. For everyone. Everywhere. Sustainably. In fiscal 2025, which ended on September 30, 2025, the Siemens Group generated revenue of €78.9 billion and net income of €10.4 billion.
