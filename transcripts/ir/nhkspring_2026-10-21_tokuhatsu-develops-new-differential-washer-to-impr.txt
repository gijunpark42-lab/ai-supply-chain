TOKUHATSU Develops New Differential Washer to Improve Vehicle Energy Efficiency

Micron-scale Microgrooves Created Using "ULTRA FINE TEXTURE" Reduce Costs and Meet Demand

for Smaller Differentials

TOKUHATSU CO.,LTD. (Head office: Itami, Hyogo, Japan; President: Yasuhiro Shinkai), a group

company of NHK SPRING CO., LTD. (Head office: Yokohama, Japan; President & COO: Kazuhisa

Uemura), has jointly developed with a Japanese automobile manufacturer a new model of the

“differential washer,” which is a sliding component used inside a differential. TOKUHATSU has

established a mass-production system for this product at its Sanda Plant and has already begun

supplying it to the market.

Differential washers are components that slide between the differential case and side gears, and are

subjected to high levels of friction and wear. Recent years have seen rising demand for higher

efficiency and smaller size in differentials. TOKUHATSU has responded by applying its proprietary

ultrafine groove processing technology, “ULTRA FINE TEXTURE” (hereinafter referred to as “UFT”)

*1, to

this the new product. The result is formation of a stable oil film even when using low-viscosity

lubricating oil. Compared with conventional products employing surface treatment (PTFE composite

electroless nickel plating*2), the new product achieves equivalent seizure resistance at a lower cost.

Guided by its corporate philosophy of “Key Parts, Driving the World Forward,” the NHK SPRING Group

will continue to develop and introduce new technologies and engage in manufacturing that drives

society forward.

 Development Background

Recent years have seen intensifying demand for the automotive, industrial machinery, and

precision equipment industries to deliver greater space savings and weight reduction through the

miniaturization of sliding components, as well as higher efficiency through the use of lower-

viscosity lubricating oils. To meet these challenges, TOKUHATSU has developed a mechanism that

retains oil in grooves by forming UFT on the sliding surfaces of its products through press

processing. This technology provides exceptional seizure-prevention performance even under

high-load conditions that arise from the miniaturization of differentials. The new product has been

developed for use in electric vehicles.

 Product Features

1. High durability

Oil reservoirs (recesses) are formed on the surfaces of sliding components. By retaining lubricating

oil in these grooves, a stable oil film can be formed even under severe lubrication conditions,

thereby improving seizure resistance.

Conventional product (surface treatment) Newly developed product (UFT)

2. High productivity

Because UFT is formed through press processing, high productivity can be achieved. In addition,

eliminating the surface treatment process previously required also helps to reduce production

costs.

3. Compatibility with miniaturization

The smaller the washer, the greater the contact pressure on its sliding surfaces, making seizure

more likely to occur. By forming a stable oil film using ultrafine grooves, this product demonstrates

outstanding seizure resistance even under high contact pressure conditions. This helps designers

to make differentials ever smaller and lighter.

 TOKUHATSU 's proprietary technology

One challenge in forming UFT through press processing has been ensuring the reproducibility of

fine groove geometries during mass production. Applying both its proprietary die manufacturing

technology and high-precision press processing technology enables TOKUHATSU to consistently

form ultrafine groove geometries even during mass production. This minimizes variation between

production lots.

*1 ULTRA FINE TEXTURE is a technology developed by TOKUHATSU for forming fine grooves on sliding surfaces.

*2 A surface treatment in which PTFE (fluororesin) is incorporated into electroless nickel plating. It is used to reduce friction and

improve seizure resistance in sliding components.
