Accton Promotes Solitary Bee Education to Conserve Urban Biodiversity

ESG Spotlight / Environmental 🌄 / Accton E-Charity, ESG Office / September 29th, 2026

The alarming decline of bee populations has long drawn global attention. Yet more than 80% of bee species in nature are solitary bees, which are also facing significant population declines due to habitat loss.

Solitary bees play a vital role in urban ecosystems as pollinators and natural biological control agents, making them essential to maintaining urban biodiversity. However, public stereotypes continue to pose challenges to bee conservation and ecological education. In response, Accton aims to leverage its influence to promote a better understanding of bees through ecological education, helping transform public concerns into awareness and understanding. By installing solitary bee hotels, Accton is also working to gradually restore breeding habitats for solitary bees in urban areas and foster a more biodiversity-friendly urban environment.

Building Awareness Through School Education

The Accton E-Charity partnered with Urban Ark Studio to promote education on solitary bees at three schools in Hsinchu City: Nanliao Elementary School, Dahu Elementary School, and Fuli Junior High School. By introducing the differences between social bees and solitary bees, as well as the behavior of solitary bees, the program helps students develop accurate knowledge about bees from an early age and gain a deeper understanding of solitary bee.

In addition, the Accton E-Charity donated a solitary bee hotel to each school, enabling teachers to incorporate hands-on observation into their curricula. Through close-up observation of solitary bees, students can deepen their understanding and develop greater awareness of and appreciation for bee-friendly environments.

“We would like to thank the instructor for delivering such an engaging and lively course. The students learned a great deal about bees and, through the solitary bee hotel, were able to observe the fascinating ecology of solitary bees up close.” — Director Wang, Dahu Elementary School, Hsinchu City

Engaging Employees from Within

Accton hosted an ecological education seminar on solitary bees, inviting Urban Ark Studio to guide employees in exploring the fascinating world of solitary bees. Employees learned that solitary bees account for more than 80% of bee species worldwide. Unlike social bees, they do not build large hives or live in colonies with defined roles, yet they quietly serve as vital pollinators within ecosystems. Each female solitary bee independently finds a nesting site, collects pollen, lays eggs, and seals her nest, all without a social hierarchy or division of labor.

During the seminar, employees also assembled solitary bee hotels that will be installed on Jiùgǎng Island in Hsinchu City, adding their own creative touches by painting the hotels in vibrant designs. At the same time, Accton installed three solitary bee hotels around its facilities to provide nesting and breeding spaces for solitary bees in the urban environment, while giving employees opportunities to observe their ecology up close.

“Learning about solitary bees and their importance has changed the way I see bees. Instead of simply feeling afraid of them, I now have a better understanding of their role in nature.” — Employee participant in the ecological education seminar

Extending Our Impact into the Community

Building on Accton’s Food Forest Project launched in 2025, the Accton E-Charity installed solitary bee hotels on Jiùgǎng Island and in Jiangjun Village in Hsinchu City. Through volunteer-led educational activities and opportunities for the public to observe solitary bees up close, the initiative aims to deepen public understanding of their ecology and behavior and help transform fear of bees into greater awareness and appreciation. By fostering understanding, Accton hopes to promote coexistence between people and nature in urban environments.

Achievements

2026 Solitary Bee Ecological Education Program:

Schools: 3 sessions held across 3 schools in Hsinchu City, with 100 students participating.

Company: 1 session held, with 16 employees participating.
