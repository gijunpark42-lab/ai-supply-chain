Bristol Myers Squibb Announces Phase 3 ALOFT-IPF Trial Evaluating Admilparant in Adults with Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis Met Primary Endpoint

October 12, 2026

PDF Format (opens in new window)

In the trial, admilparant at the 60 mg dose demonstrated a statistically significant benefit in absolute change in forced vital capacity (FVC) from baseline to Week 52 compared with placebo

Admilparant demonstrated a favorable safety and tolerability profile

The totality of the findings supports the potential of admilparant as a clinically meaningful first-in-class treatment

PRINCETON, N.J.--(BUSINESS WIRE)--

Bristol Myers Squibb (NYSE: BMY) today announced topline results from the pivotal Phase 3 ALOFT-IPF trial evaluating the efficacy, safety and tolerability of admilparant, a potential first-in-class, orally administered, potent and selective lysophosphatidic acid receptor 1 (LPA 1 ) antagonist, in adults with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). The trial evaluated two dose levels of admilparant, 60 mg and 120 mg, twice daily for ≥52 weeks against placebo (1:1:1).

The trial met its primary endpoint at the 60 mg dose, with admilparant demonstrating a statistically significant improvement compared to placebo in absolute change in forced vital capacity (FVC) from baseline at Week 52. At the 120 mg dose, admilparant slowed the decline of FVC but was not statistically significant.

Notably, admilparant demonstrated a well-tolerated safety profile across both doses in the ALOFT-IPF trial. In both admilparant arms of the trial, discontinuations due to adverse events were low and similar to placebo.

“Taken together, these topline data reinforce the profile of admilparant as a promising, clinically meaningful investigational therapy for IPF – one that reflects the innovation of targeting the LPA 1 pathway as a first-in-class approach,” said Cristian Massacesi, MD , executive vice president, chief medical officer and head of development, Bristol Myers Squibb. “We also look forward to sharing the topline results from the ALOFT-PPF trial early next year, as we continue working to improve outcomes for patients with pulmonary fibrosis.”

“IPF remains one of the most complex and challenging lung diseases to treat, with unpredictable progression, irreversible scarring and a profound impact on patients’ daily lives,” said Fernando Martinez, MD, chair of the ALOFT program clinical steering committee and the Joseph D. Early Chair in Biomedical Research, interim chair and professor of medicine at UMass Chan Medical School. “These admilparant results represent an important advancement as there remains a need for therapies that are both effective and tolerable for patients living with IPF.”

The company will complete a comprehensive evaluation of the Phase 3 ALOFT-IPF trial data and engage with health authorities to support planned regulatory submissions, with detailed results to be presented at a future medical meeting.

Admilparant is also being studied in the fully enrolled Phase 3 ALOFT-PPF trial in progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF), with topline results anticipated in early 2027.

Bristol Myers Squibb thanks the patients and investigators who participated in the ALOFT-IPF clinical trial.
