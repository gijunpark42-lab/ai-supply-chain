Press Release 25 September 2026 Siemens AG

Siemens showcases new Picadilly line trains at InnoTrans exhibition in

Berlin

Siemens Mobility and Transport for London presented the new Inspiro London Piccadilly line train at the Piccadilly Showcase Event during InnoTrans 2026

Visitors also experienced Siemens Mobility's digital rail solutions, including Railigent X, showcasing how data-driven technologies can improve performance, and reliability across the railway

80% of Transport for London’s 94-train order for the new Piccadilly line fleet is being manufactured at Siemens Mobility's £240 million Goole Rail Village in the UK.

This week at the biannual InnoTrans exhibition,

Siemens Mobility showcased the new Inspiro London Piccadilly line train.

Following addresses from Siemens Mobility Chief Executive Officer Michael

Peter, Transport for London Commissioner Andy Lord and Aglaja Schneider, Joint

CEO of Siemens Mobility UK&I, attendees were invited aboard the

113-metre-long, nine-car train for a guided tour to provide a first-hand look

at the innovative features and passenger-focused enhancements designed for

London's future Piccadilly line journeys.

Andy Lord, London’s Transport Commissioner, said:

“These new trains will fundamentally change the

experience of travelling on the Piccadilly line when they start entering

service. They will become one of the first things an international visitor will

experience when they come to London, be it through Heathrow or St Pancras

International, and will be more energy efficient and spacious, provide CCTV and

air conditioning and give customers better information during their journeys.

Journeys will be more accessible, safer and faster, and the experience for our

customers will be transformed.”

The new Piccadilly line trains have been designed to

transform the passenger experience with air-conditioning, walk-through

carriages, enhanced accessibility, onboard CCTV and improved onboard digital

information systems. The trains will also be more reliable, increase capacity

by 10% and consume 20% less energy than the existing fleet. The trains will be

digitally connected via our Railigent platform, meaning trains will be able to

send live location data and live component health data to TfL’s depots, enabling

predictive maintenance.

Aglaja Schneider, Joint CEO and Managing Director, Rolling Stock and Customer Services at Siemens Mobility UK&I, said:

“I’m incredibly proud to see the new Piccadilly line

train exhibited at InnoTrans. Having been part of this programme earlier in its

development, it's fantastic to see how far the project has come and to share

that progress with the wider rail industry.

I would like to thank the teams across Siemens

Mobility and Transport for London for their hard work and collaboration. From

design and manufacturing through to testing, teams from both organisations have

worked side by side to bring the project to life. Built at our Goole Rail

Village in East Yorkshire, it also represents our continued investment in UK

manufacturing and skills.”

Alongside the train, Siemens Mobility showcased its

portfolio of digitally connected rail solutions, including Railigent X,

Signaling X and Mobility Software Suite X. Powered by Siemens Xcelerator, these

technologies help operators optimise performance, improve reliability and make

better use of rail infrastructure, supporting the delivery of a more efficient

and sustainable railway.

Notes to editors:

Siemens Mobility is

investing up to £240 million in the Goole Rail Village, a site that spans 67

acres, the size of 35 football pitches. London Underground’s new Piccadilly

line trains will be assembled at Goole before they

begin operating between December 2026 - June 2027.

Overall, Siemens Mobility’s

investment will bring up to 1,000 new jobs to the East Riding of Yorkshire, as

well as around 1,700 supply chain opportunities, promoting localisation and

helping to serve other global markets by 2030.

The Goole Rail Village is a fully

integrated rail hub, consisting of the Train Manufacturing Facility which

assembles and commissions trains, the Components Facility where Siemens

maintains gearboxes, traction motors and other parts for train and tram fleets,

the Logistics Centre warehousing facility, and the Rail Accelerator and

Innovation Solutions hub for Enterprise (RaisE) business centre.

With additional investment of up to £40 million, all of this will be

joined by a Bogie Assembly and Service Centre, which will incorporate and

expand Siemens Mobility’s current capabilities to overhaul bogies from UK

trains.

Siemens Mobility has

supported the local supply chain wherever possible, contracting Leeds-based GMI

Construction for the build of the Goole factory,

Components Facility and the warehouse. The GMI supply chain is based

entirely in the UK with over 70% of it based in Yorkshire. Several components

for the Piccadilly line trains will also come from local Yorkshire and UK

suppliers, such as the exterior lighting from LPA Lighting, just 25 miles away

from the Goole factory.

Siemens Mobility is a separately managed company of Siemens AG. As a leader in intelligent transport solutions for more than 175 years, Siemens Mobility is constantly innovating its portfolio. Its core areas include rolling stock, rail automation and electrification, a comprehensive software portfolio, turnkey systems as well as related services. With digital products and solutions, and the use of industrial AI, Siemens Mobility is enabling mobility operators worldwide to make their infrastructure intelligent, increase value sustainably over the entire lifecycle, enhance passenger experience, and guarantee availability.
