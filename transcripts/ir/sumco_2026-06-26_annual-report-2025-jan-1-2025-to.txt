Message from the President

I was appointed representative director and president of SUMCO on March 27, 2026. Here, I would like to share the Company’s

future direction and lay out the kind of management style I intend to adopt.

Since joining SUMCO, I have served in many different capacities: in research and development, on the factory floor, in sales,

and running overseas subsidiaries. Working in R&D taught me the value of building up technological expertise. Being on the

factory floor taught me the importance of quality and keeping operations running smoothly. My time in sales taught me about

being committed to customers and addressing their needs head-on. Running overseas subsidiaries taught me how to make

prompt decisions and the weight of responsibility. These varied experiences have led me to the conclusion that in business,

the answers are always to be found on the front lines—and that what ultimately generates results is candid, open communication

with frontline staff, customers, and suppliers.

A particularly memorable chapter in my career was turning around an overseas subsidiary. In a challenging business climate,

I engaged in repeated dialogue with the people on the front lines, brought issues to light one by one, identified opportunities

to remediate them, set priorities, and took systematic action. I thus succeeded in setting the business on the path to recovery.

This experience convinced me that the more difficult the situation, the more important it is to face the facts squarely, be honest

with each other, decide what must be done, and then do it.

This conviction has remained unchanged since I assumed the presidency. Adhering firmly to the growth strategy developed

by my predecessor Mayuki Hashimoto, who served as representative director, chairman & CEO, I intend to advance steadily

one step at a time, while seeing reality for what it is. At the same time, I will move quickly to keep pace with the rapidly

changing. These will be my guiding principles.

The semiconductor and silicon wafer markets have entered the recovery phase, but that recovery is uneven. Demand for wafers

for leading-edge logic chips and high bandwidth memory (HBM), buoyed by the increasing spread of artificial intelligence (AI),

remains strong. In addition to wafers for AI training chips, demand is also expanding to related fields such as data centers,

memory, peripheral devices, and power control, fueled by the spread of advanced inference. Areas that play to our strengths

are steadily increasing.

Yet a recovery will still take time in the market for non-leading-edge wafers, including those for automobiles, industrial

equipment, and consumer electronics. This is due to, among other factors, prolonged inventory drawdowns by customers

and expanding Chinese supply. Moreover, sources of uncertainty in the outside environment are proliferating: exchange rates,

commodity prices, energy security, policy trends in different countries, and geopolitical risks. These changes, in my view,

represent not a passing wave but a structural transformation in the market itself. Demand spikes are steeper than they used

to be, with greater variations in demand across products for different applications. That makes it more important than ever to

gather accurate information, detect nascent trends, and stay ahead of the curve in making the necessary preparations.

In this climate, the course we should pursue is obvious. Rather than focus solely on expanding volume, we must achieve

quality-driven growth. “Quality” as I use the term here means being chosen on the virtue of our technology. It means winning

trust with the excellence of our products, consistently being able to supply what is needed when it is needed, and staying on

a growth trajectory while remaining profitable. The first pillar of the SUMCO Vision is to be the “World’s Best in Technology.”

To achieve that goal, we will simultaneously expand our lineup of differentiated products, ensure that supplies remain steady,

and enhance our cost competitiveness.

The same principles will govern capital expenditures. In relation to leading-edge products, a new factory for producing

leading-edge 300 mm wafers—a greenfield investment decided in 2021—is being phased into operation in the Imari district.

At existing plants, meanwhile, production facilities are to be upgraded in response to the changing structure of demand,

enhancing our ability to cater to the market for leading-edge products. On the other hand, in deciding when to invest in the

land in Yoshinogari, which was acquired in anticipation of future demand growth, we will carefully monitor market trends.

Getting too far ahead of demand would result in overcapacity; falling behind would result in lost opportunities. We are

committed to a disciplined approach to investment, which means knowing when to accelerate and when to apply the brakes.

On the financial front, cash flow, rather than short-term fluctuations in profits, will remain a prime consideration in the

running of our business. Despite higher depreciation resulting from the greenfield investment referred to above, we will

maintain our ability to generate steady earnings in the face of significant fluctuations in demand and prepare ourselves for

the next growth opportunity. We will strengthen the foundations of our business with the goal of ensuring “Stable Profitability

Even During Economic Downturns”—the second pillar of the SUMCO Vision.

Our competitive edge is underpinned by our people, our technology, and our ability to work together effectively as an organization.

No matter how good your equipment is, it takes the individual abilities of the people working on the production floor to put it

to effective use. I believe in creating a workplace that empowers employees to think independently and challenge themselves.

Message from the President

When employees fully appreciate the significance of their work and show resourcefulness on the job, it ultimately results in

higher quality and greater customer trust. That in turn enhances our technology and our ability to address problems as they

arise on the factory floor.

One of SUMCO’s strengths is the fact that our factories producing leading-edge 300 mm wafers, our R&D arm, and the AI

Promotion Division are all located in the Imari district. That means that when an issue arises, all concerned can promptly

gather, discuss the problem on the spot, and take immediate action. The rapidity with which decisions are made and

implemented builds trust with customers. To enhance these frontline capabilities, I will strengthen our ability to respond to

the customer’s wants as a single unified team with no walls between R&D, production, and sales.

In addition, I will take integration between the factory floor and digital a step further. The AI Promotion Division has so far

spearheaded the Company’s efforts to utilize quality and process data. Going forward, however, it will be critical to have a cycle

proceeding in both directions. Experts in data analysis need a good understanding of the factory floor, and factory personnel

need to know how to use digital technology. We will boost productivity and quality by rotating personnel between the AI

Promotion Division and our production arm, thus expediting the process of identifying problems and recommending and

implementing solutions.

At the same time, I will focus on creating a workplace where employees feel safe and secure. One of the most important

foundations of trust in our organization is a set of mechanisms encouraging employees to speak up and properly addressing

their concerns. These include health and productivity management initiatives, measures to improve the working environment,

and a whistleblower program, as well as an anonymous suggestion box. The third pillar of the SUMCO Vision is to “Empower

Employee Awareness of Profit.” To achieve that goal, I will cherish our culture of recognizing people for challenging their

limits, while ensuring a workplace where everyone can apply their abilities with confidence.

Contributing to building a sustainable society, I believe, is inseparable from the growth of our business. We will work in good

faith to meet the public’s expectations by steadily improving the way we do business—not to mention delivering value

through our products.

On the environmental front, we consider cutting greenhouse gas emissions a key priority. We have strengthened our net-zero

targets and obtained Science Based Targets (SBT) certification. We are also working to install more renewable energy capacity.

In 2025, we began developing renewable energy capacity under a corporate power purchase agreement (PPA) and installed

additional solar power generation facilities. Further, we are taking steps to enhance responsible sourcing and better ensure

continuity of supply. These are not solely SUMCO initiatives; they also concern the sustainability of our customers and of the

entire supply chain. I consider it a testament to these cumulative efforts that SUMCO was selected for the CDP’s 2025 A List

in both climate change and water security.

As for human resources and the working environment, I value creating a company where everyone can thrive. Besides

hiring, training, and developing people from a wide range of backgrounds, we take steps to empower women, provide career

development support, and offer generous incentive programs for employees wishing to pursue further studies. We have put

in place programs catering to the needs of working parents, including an on-site day-care facility, a childcare support

allowance, and the return-to-work program for former employees. As a result of these efforts, SUMCO was chosen for the

2026 Health & Productivity Stock Selection and again named one of the “White 500” corporations under the Certified Health &

Productivity Management Outstanding Organizations Recognition Program.

As a business rooted in local communities, we continue to make a difference wherever we operate. We conduct educational

outreach classes at universities, colleges of technology, and high schools and donate exhibits to them; engage in conservation

activities; and assist those with special needs to find work. Working with the local community and helping develop the next

generation of talent are key parts of our mission as a linchpin of the semiconductor industry. Going forward, we will continue

to cherish our ties of trust with local communities while aligning our social responsibilities with sustainable business growth.

Our business environment is opaque and volatile. But instead of going on the defensive from an excess of caution, I believe it is

vital to keep moving forward step by step while assessing the facts and not neglecting to make the necessary preparations.

The semiconductor industry is essential to society. As a company underpinning it, we will meet your expectations by steadily

enhancing our technology, quality, and ability to supply a steady stream of products, the soundness of our finances, the training

of our people, and the sustainability of our business.

In closing, let me express my deepest gratitude to our shareholders and investors, our customers and business partners,

the people of the communities where we operate, and the employees who keep our business running day in and day out. I look

forward to your ongoing understanding and support.

●While overall smartphone growth has

slowed, the shift toward edge AI will

continue.

●Demand growth is expected for wafers

for leading-edge logic chips used in the

advanced processors that constitute the

“brains” of smartphones.

●Demand for use in data center AI servers

was the main driver of the semiconductor

and wafer markets in 2025. This demand is

expected to continue growing in response

to the rapid expansion of AI use.

amounts of data, AI servers are equipped

with large numbers of leading-edge

semiconductors, including GPU

*1 and

numerous semiconductors, but a bonding

process is adopted to achieve higher scaling

and functionality, adding up to a large

increase in silicon wafer area per server.

●A shift to cloud-independent personal

computers and other devices with their

own AI capabilities will accelerate, aimed

at more efficient AI use.

●Even amid sluggish growth in the overall

personal computer market, this trend will

drive demand for memory and logic wafers.

Inspection and assembly processes (Back-end process)

The surface of a silicon wafer is oxidized, forming an oxide layer, which is then coated with a light-sensitive material (photoresist).

After exposing the surface to UV light to transfer a circuit pattern to it from a mask, a chemical process selectively removes

unwanted portions of the layers (etching).

After ion implantation to enhance performance, the photoresist is peeled off and the surface is flattened by polishing.

By repeating the processes from photoresist application to flattening, multiple circuit layers are made. Finally, using tiny

needles called probes, the devices (chips) on the wafer are inspected for issues with interconnects and current flow.

After the wafer is cut into individual chips with a diamond blade (dicing), the chips are attached to a base and encapsulated

in a resin package. Finally, the devices undergo many inspections, including electrical properties testing, before being shipped.

Moore's Law is the observation by Gordon Moore in 1965 that the number of transistors on a microchip doubles

every two years (giving the chip twice the functions and performance). The Dennard scaling rule, proposed in

1974 by R.H. Dennard of IBM, stated that as transistors become smaller, allowing microchips to shrink,

devices will become faster. The combined result of these observed trends is that semiconductor chips have

continued to advance through repeated cycles of becoming smaller while their functions are expanded.

As semiconductors advance in scaling and functions, greater use is expected to be made of wafer bonding, whereby two or

more wafers are joined together.

For high-performance semiconductor devices, wafer bonding is a key technique for solving the current scaling limits and

problems of heat and noise, by enabling the advancement from conventional flat integration to 3D structures.

An advantage of 3D structures is the shorter electrical signal transfer distance, reducing latency and electricity

consumption while improving speed and performance. By stacking in 3D multiple circuits or devices in the limited available

surface area, chips can be made smaller and be given more advanced functions.

Moreover, to meet diversifying market needs, devices fabricated with different processes, such as logic, memory, and

sensors, can be integrated to equip products with complex functionality.

Wafer bonding technology will solve semiconductor issues on the way to realizing the next generation of high-

functionality, high-performance chips.

Acclaim from customers around the world is reflected in our high rate of market dominance. Having high market dominance

also means we get to hear from more customers, leading to further value-raising.

Silicon wafers that are used in a wafer bonding process of semiconductor production must have even higher precision and

higher quality properties than before. This is because the wafers need to be aligned to each other with the utmost precision,

since even a slight defect or variation will greatly affect product yield and performance.

For the precise bonding of silicon wafers, surface flatness at the nanometer level is required, microparticles that can cause

poor adhesion must be removed from the surfaces, surface roughness must be completely uniform to minimize electrical

connection defects in wafer bonding, and the layers must be of uniform thickness to ensure dimensional accuracy of the

entire 3D-device structure.

Highly advanced technology goes into the wafer bonding process, for which the quality of the silicon wafers is of crucial

importance. SUMCO will continue to work in close cooperation with our customers to fabricate and supply leading-edge wafers.

Corporate Governance Structure

and auditing has been enhanced through audits conducted by Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members and have

voting rights at Board of Directors meetings. In addition, the effectiveness of internal controls has been enhanced through audits

conducted in cooperation with the Internal Audit Department.

SUMCO's Board of Directors comprises internal Directors with a proven record of performance in their respective areas of responsibility

and outstanding management abilities, along with Outside Directors with expert knowledge and extensive experience, based on the basic

stance of SUMCO to ensure an overall balance and diversity in knowledge, experience, and capabilities of the Board of Directors. The

Board of Directors comprises six Directors (excluding Directors who are Audit & Supervisory Committee Members) and seven Directors

decisions on management strategy, management plans, and other such important matters relating to business. It receives reports from

every Director on the status of the execution of duties and it exercises supervision of compliance, internal controls, risk management, and

important business execution at affiliated companies. With the participation of Independent Outside Directors, the Board of Directors also

engages in the free exchange of opinions regarding the appropriate evaluation of company performance and related matters, and reflects

that evaluation in the personnel administration of senior management members.

It is also our basic company policy that matters relating to financial results and so on, matters relating to management plans, and the

execution of other such important operations should be decided after adequate discussion by the Board of Directors, including the

Independent Outside Directors. For this reason, the Articles of Incorporation do not stipulate that decisions on the execution of important

business can be delegated to each Director.

SUMCO's Audit and Supervisory Committee is made up of seven Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members (six, a

majority, of whom are Independent Outside Directors). In order to ensure the effectiveness of Audit and Supervisory Committee activities,

permanent Audit and Supervisory Committee Members are put in place by resolution of Audit and Supervisory Committee. In addition, it is

SUMCO's basic policy to take steps to ensure that at least one Audit and Supervisory Committee Member has considerable knowledge

and experience pertaining to finance and accounting. Currently, this is Mr. Hyo Kambayashi, who is also a certified public accountant.

Additionally, SUMCO has established the Audit and Supervisory Committee Office and assigns staff to support the activities of the

Committee and facilitate the smooth performance of its audit and supervisory functions.

The Audit and Supervisory Committee exercises its statutory right of investigation and audits the Directors' execution of their duties

from the viewpoints of appropriateness and conformance with laws and regulations and the Articles of Incorporation by inspecting and

confirming the status of compliance with laws and regulations, Articles of Incorporation, and so on, and by monitoring and otherwise

supervising the development of and operational status of the system of internal controls, including the internal control of financial reporting.

In an effort to further enhance its corporate governance, SUMCO has established a Nomination and Remuneration Committee composed

of two Internal Directors (Directors Jiro Ryuta and Mayuki Hashimoto) and three Independent Outside Directors (Directors Shinichiro Ota,

Masahiko Sue, and Amy Shigemi Hatta) to serve as a discretionary advisory body for the Board of Directors. The Nomination and

Remuneration Committee receives requests from the Board of Directors for advice on the process for selection of candidate Directors and

Executive Officers of SUMCO, their qualifications, the reasons for their designation, the structure of remuneration for Directors (excluding

Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members) and Executive Officers, and related matters. The committee deliberates on

the appropriateness and other aspects of the matters referred, including the perspectives of gender and other diversity as well as expert

knowledge and experience, and, also taking evaluations of SUMCO’s performance into account, delivers its advice. The Board of Directors,

receiving the findings of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee, designates candidate Directors and Executive Officers, and

decides on the remuneration and other payments for Directors (excluding Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members).

During the fiscal year 2025, SUMCO held the Nomination and Remuneration Committee five times in total, and deliberated on the

selection of candidate Directors and personnel matters concerning Executive Officers, the systems for remuneration of Directors

(excluding Outside Directors and Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members) and Executive Officers, and individual

remuneration amounts of Directors (excluding Outside Directors and Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members).

The Company’s basic policy for the remuneration of Executive Directors stipulates that the remuneration of Executive Directors comprises

a basic remuneration, performance-linked monetary remuneration based on short-term performance, and performance-linked stock

remuneration based on medium- to long-term corporate value, which are set within the total remuneration amount determined by resolution

of the General Meeting of Shareholders and according to their office and rank. This policy aims to allow Executive Directors to share the

interests and risks with shareholders and encourage them to contribute to the improvement of business performance and the medium- to

long-term enhancement of corporate value.

Outside Directors and Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members receive basic remuneration that is only fixed

remuneration from the perspective of ensuring the appropriate conduct of their duties such as auditing and the supervision of the execution of

business operations. Remuneration for Directors who are Audit and Supervisory Committee Members is determined through deliberations

of the Audit and Supervisory Committee Members within the total amount determined by resolution of the General Meeting of Shareholders,

and by taking into account the role and duties of each such Member and whether they are a full-time or part-time Member.

As an outside director involved in SUMCO's management, what most

impresses me about the company is how its officers and employees all

share a common knowledge and perception of its operating environment,

the challenges facing the company, and other factors. This is a valuable

asset and strength unique to a company specializing in silicon wafers.

SUMCO's operating environment is set to change more rapidly than

ever. Consistently seizing opportunities with the potential for stronger

corporate value while simultaneously minimizing risks will require that

the entire workforce share a wider range of higher quality information.

Everyone at SUMCO must commit fully to that goal.

Further, steadily increasing the company’s corporate value will require

the understanding and cooperation of its shareholders and many other

stakeholders. As the company becomes known to more people, it will

receive both encouragement and criticism. That will instill officers and

employees with greater confidence and keep them on their toes, driving

an increase in corporate value. It will be important to boost name

recognition by strengthening the company’s messaging. While its

communication efforts are already underway, I believe the company must

continue striving to enhance these initiatives further. I look forward to

doing my small part.

The semiconductor industry is the foundation of modern industrial growth

and innovation, a strategic sector that underpins a nation’s long term

competitiveness. In an environment defined by rapid innovation cycles,

often described as Moore’s Law, and by immense capital requirements,

companies must possess exceptionally robust management frameworks.

SUMCO, a leader in high-quality silicon wafers, the core substrate technology

from which every semiconductor chip begins, has remained competitive

through sustained, world-class R&D. Its management team brings decades

of experience balancing technological leadership, disciplined investment,

and competitive pressure, often through difficult industry cycles.

Japanese companies are increasingly aligning with global governance

standards, with stronger expectations for transparency, board independence,

and shareholder engagement. In this context, I provide an independent

perspective on SUMCO's capabilities and have been deeply impressed by

the care and openness that shape management’s decision-making process.

Management listens closely to the Board’s input on technology, capital

allocation, and risk. I also commend recent improvements in information

including document translation and interpretation services.

There is no room for complacency in today’s competitive environment;

success must be continually forged. As an outside director, I am committed

to supporting SUMCO's ongoing evolution as an industry leader.

Compliance

SUMCO is committed to promoting fair business activities and preventing bribery and other forms of corruption, and the SUMCO Charter

of Corporate Conduct clearly provides for legal compliance, prohibition of unfair competition and improper transactions, prohibition of

comprehensive anti-corruption measures such as prohibiting any illegal or improper provision of benefits to and acceptance of benefits

from public servants and any party who has business relationships, and prevention of insider trading.

To promote fair business practice and prevent corruption in particular, the Regulations on Handling the Grant of Benefits prohibit

(1) unlawful political contributions and donations; (2) entertainment or gifts in connection with the duties of public servants and others;

(3) financial benefits in connection with the exercise of shareholders' rights; (4) providing or accepting excessive entertainment, gifts, or

other such benefits that exceed commonly accepted bounds in society to or from customers, suppliers and potential business partners,

and the receiving of personal benefits; and any illegal provision and receipt of benefits; and any unlawful benefits as well as the entire

range of actions that are recognized as wrongful or improper in light of what is commonly accepted in society. The Regulations also

define standards for making donations, providing sponsorship, paying entertainment expenses, giving celebratory or condolence gifts,

paying membership dues to external organizations, and outsourcing operations and ensure the implementation of them under an

appropriate approval process. SUMCO also requires employees to avoid these behaviors when engaging brokers and other agents.

SUMCO makes the Regulations on Handling the Grant of Benefits available at all times on the corporate intranet, and seeks to ensure

all employees are fully aware of our policies on promoting fair business practices and preventing corruption. We also provide training on

the SUMCO Charter of Corporate Conduct to thoroughly familiarize all officers and employees with it, and the status of compliance with

these provisions is regularly reported to the Chief Compliance Officer.

Also note that to date, the SUMCO Group has not paid any fines or made any facilitation payments stemming from political

contributions or corruption, nor has the Group been penalized in any way relating to corruption.

SUMCO has established Internal Reporting Regulations and introduced an internal reporting hotline for reporting any act in violation of the

SUMCO Charter of Corporate Conduct, including conduct in violation of laws and ordinances, human rights infringements and harassment,

comprehensive corruptive acts such as bribery and unfair competition, and improper transactions. SUMCO accepts internal reports from

our suppliers as well as employees.

To ensure that whistleblowers can make reports with peace of mind, we have set up an in-house and external (attorney) contact points for

internal reports as well as a “suggestion box” that goes straight to top management. Anonymous reports are accepted and all disadvantageous

treatment including retaliation against whistleblowers who make reports is strictly forbidden.

Information on how to use the hotline and about contact points for consultation are posted at all times on the premises of the SUMCO Group,

the corporate intranet and our dedicated procurement website, and these matters are also explained to be known for all employees during

training on the SUMCO Charter of Corporate Conduct, which is conducted regularly for all employees.

To ensure thorough export control aimed at maintaining international peace and security, SUMCO has clearly laid out compliance with

export control rules in the SUMCO Charter of Corporate Conduct. The Company has established an appropriate export control system in

accordance with the Security Export Control Rules where it ensures that exports are only executed after the reviews and examinations of

all the requirements to be met, and has implemented measures including regular training on export control for employees.

The SUMCO Charter of Corporate Conduct declares that the Company denounces any kind of relationship with antisocial forces and rejects

unreasonable requests or demands from such forces. This is thoroughly disseminated among all executives and employees of the Company

through education and training.

For the Group's further growth while fulfilling its social responsibility, the SUMCO Group has instituted the SUMCO Charter of

Corporate Conduct to define the norms that the Group's employees should uphold, not only in terms of compliance with laws

and ordinances, but also more broadly in terms of CSR in general. The SUMCO Charter of Corporate Conduct is positioned as

the highest level of SUMCO regulations and is placed above the various in-house regulations and compliance programs

found within the Group.

The SUMCO Group aims to make the SUMCO Charter of Corporate Conduct understood throughout the Group by preparing

it in languages understandable to employees at Group companies overseas, keeping it available on the corporate intranet at

company, regularly reports findings to the Board of Directors, and undergoes review by the Board.

primary responsibility for continually overseeing those laws and ordinances that are related to the execution of the departments'

business. Each responsible department continuously monitors the enactment, amendment, and abolishment of relevant

laws and ordinances, while the auditing department confirms the status of legal compliance through internal audits.

In the event a problem related to compliance is discovered, the departments involved act in cooperation and under the

supervision of the Chief Compliance Officer to immediately investigate the cause of the problem and prevent its recurrence.

Risk Management

The silicon wafer business is a critical industry that supports our IT-oriented society, and the SUMCO Group is responsible for the stable

supply of silicon wafers to semiconductor device manufacturers around the world. For this reason, the SUMCO Group regards business

continuity as a key management concern.

The Business Security Committee (BSC), which is an organization responsible for company-wide risk management, identifies, analyzes,

and assesses risks, implements countermeasures, and promotes risk management activities including the formulation of business

continuity plans (BCP), with the aim of predicting risks in advance, preventing them from being realized, and minimizing damages when a

risk is realized.

SUMCO has set forth basic matters pertaining to risk management

in the Basic Rules on Risk Management, under which we undertake

risk management activities.

Our basic approach to responding to risks involves the following

two points.

1.

Prioritize the handling of risks by likelihood of occurrence

and severity of impact from the viewpoints of optimum

allocation of management resources and the effectiveness

of risk management.

2.

Minimize damage and loss by preventing the suspension or

discontinuation of business activities to ensure business

continuity in the event of an accident or other emergency

situation.

Responsibility for each risk is allocated between departments according to the priority established in (1) above, and the policy on risk

countermeasures is determined via discussion within a company-wide committee known as the BSC. The committee meets every year,

with not only the President and other senior management in attendance but also responsible members of each department, factory, plant,

and group companies worldwide. It decides company-wide policy regarding risk management, deliberates and determines risk prevention

measures, discusses responses on cross-department risks and new risk phenomenon, and exchanges a broad range of information about

risk management in general.

Our Basic Rules on Risk Management define business continuity as an important management issue, and we have been working to develop

and improve our BCP to ensure business continuity.

The details of the BCP and its state of improvements, the results of trainings and drills, and other matters are reported to the BSC, and

the BSC verifies the effectiveness of the BCP and determines policies on future action to be taken. Each department and group company

improves the BCP further according to the policies.

Every year, SUMCO confirms procedures to transfer head office operations elsewhere, the implementation status of drills and exercises

carried out by each department, and the issues to be addressed in the future based on the assumption of a large-scale earthquake beneath

the Tokyo metropolitan area that leads to the disfunction of Tokyo Head Office. We continue with activities aimed at further enhancing

the readiness of the head office's BCP.

We also conduct integrated emergency response drills at each plant, which include activities such as evacuation, firefighting, reporting,

rescue, emergency relief, and transportation in the event of a disaster such as an earthquake. In addition to those drills above, we also

conduct regular BCP training to confirm procedures and the contents of the BCP and enhance our response capabilities. Continuous

improvements are made by reviewing the training results, identifying issues, and revising procedures and other contents after conducting

the drills. For example, at the Miyazaki Plant of SUMCO TECHXIV Corporation, practical simulation training was conducted in anticipation

of the Nankai Trough Earthquake, leading to the confirmation and improvement of disaster BCP procedures. Furthermore, at each site, we

are implementing measures to minimize damage and ensure swift recovery in the event of a disaster such as an earthquake. This

includes facility improvements such as installing earthquake-resistant measures for seismic reinforcement and stockpiling necessary

tools, materials, and equipment to promptly resume production.

The Company understands the importance of information in business operations and clearly stipulates the appropriate use and

management of our trade secrets and all other information including confidential information received from others in the SUMCO Charter

of Corporate Conduct. We have also established the Rules on Information Management along with related regulations, guidelines, and

other materials. We make every effort to appropriately manage and prevent leaks of our trade secrets and confidential information

received from customers, suppliers, and other parties by conducting regular training for all employees on these information management

rules and information security.

In an effort to prevent computer system-related leaks and stop information from leaking outside the Company, the Company also

Two common types of power semiconductors are Power MOSFET and IGBT. SUMCO puts major effort into the development of

silicon wafers for Power MOSFET and IGBT.

IGBT is used in electrified vehicles (xEVs), seen as a growth

market. Use of 300 mm silicon wafers, able to accommodate

more chips per wafer, is needed to increase IGBT production.

SUMCO was early to take up the development of 300 mm

silicon wafers for IGBT, obtained certification for IGBT silicon

wafers for xEVs, with their stringent quality requirements, and

has moved to mass production.

A Power MOSFET is made from a crystal with red phosphorus

additive for the efficient flow of electricity in the silicon

wafer. Using large amounts of red phosphorus, however, can

cause crystal defects to occur in the epitaxial layer. SUMCO

was the first in the world to develop technology that greatly

minimizes such epitaxial layer defects even when ample

amounts of red phosphorus are used, as well as the first to

achieve mass production.

Approximately 8% of the world’s electricity consumption is by data

centers. The worldwide volume of stored and transmitted data is

expected to increase more than 30-fold by 2030 and 4,000 times by

2050. With today’s technology, assuming no further energy

Making transistors smaller with advances in semiconductor

fabrication process scaling shortens the distance electrons must

travel, for greater energy efficiency. Scaling also increases the

number of transistors per surface area, making possible electronic

devices with more advanced functions.

The logic chips used in data center servers see reductions in

electricity consumption of around 20% to 30% with each generational

advance in scaling.

Power semiconductors are used in all kinds of electrical equipment, from air conditioners, refrigerators, and other home electrical

appliances around us to social infrastructure such as industrial motor drives, railways, and power transmission and distribution equipment.

Power semiconductors have the role of switches, changing between on and off states to supply and control electric power. In performing

their role while minimizing power loss, power semiconductors are essential for achieving carbon neutrality.

For the purpose of maintaining and enhancing the Group's levels of safety and health, the SUMCO Group annually convenes the Company-

Additionally, each plant shares safety and health information and improves safety and health issues, and engages in daily activities to

achieve safety and health objectives, through channels such as Safety and Health Committees, Workplace Safety and Health Committees,

and small group workplace safety meetings.

overseas) occurred (three more than in the previous fiscal

prevent such accidents from occurring in the future, with

work (chemicals, heavy objects, high places, rotating

objects, etc.), and also strengthen knowledge and

awareness education on hazards and harmfulness. For

the SUMCO Group and contractors, the number of

deaths due to occupational accidents was zero.

initiatives, including broad introduction of the lockout-tagout safety procedure. Having acquired ISO 45001 certification for its occupational

health and safety management systems, the Group is further developing and enhancing these systems, while carrying out safety awareness-

raising activities based on employee survey results. In these and other ways, the Group is endeavoring to create a workplace environment

that is safe, healthy, comfortable, and pleasant to work in.

SUMCO has established the SUMCO CSR Procurement Policy and promotes responsible procurement activities to fulfill its social

responsibilities across the supply chain. We share various issues related to the supply chain with all suppliers, both domestic and

audits of a given number of suppliers. Along with providing explanations of our CSR procurement policy and environmental policy and asking

for understanding and cooperation, we verify the state of compliance with each of the items on the CSR questionnaire such as human rights,

safety, environment, ethics, information security, and management systems, as well as confirming the status of improvements.

understanding of suppliers and asked them to correct these issues, leading suppliers to improve their operations. The SUMCO Group will

continue to promote CSR management in our supply chain.

service accepts consultations by e-mail or telephone to keep them

confidential. Each consultation request received is investigated in

the relevant department, measures for rectification and prevention

of a recurrence are taken as the situation calls for, and the results

are reported to management, while the consulting party is given

feedback. In addition, with appropriate consideration to privacy, the harassment cases are shared with the Human Rights Awareness and

Promotion Committee, putting them to Group-wide use in measures for preventing a recurrence.

During fiscal year 2025 (January 1, 2025–December 31, 2025), the pace of

recovery varied across the semiconductor market. Demand was steady in

leading-edge fields, most notably AI, while inventory adjustments continued

in non-leading-edge fields, including wafers for consumer products, industry,

and automobiles.

In this market environment, SUMCO’s financial results for fiscal year 2025

were as follows. Net sales were 409.6 billion yen, operating profit was 1.3

billion yen, and loss attributable to owners of parent was 11.7 billion yen.

Increased depreciation costs associated with greenfield investments put

downward pressure on earnings. Depreciation costs are expected to peak in

fiscal year 2026 and then decline in fiscal year 2027 and beyond as assets

depreciate, leading to a recovery in earnings.

On the financial front, net cash provided by operating activities stood at

100.0 billion yen, with free cash flow improving starting from the second half

of fiscal year 2025 as we passed our capital expenditures peak. At the end of

the fiscal year, our shareholders' equity ratio was 51.3%, and our debt-equity

ratio (gross) was 0.61 times. Our financial foundation thus remains sound.

Going forward, we will steadily make the investments necessary for growth

while working to strengthen our financial fundamentals by enhancing our

earning power and reducing borrowings.

The silicon wafers the SUMCO Group manufactures and sells are used

for semiconductor substrates and other parts that are used in various

products, including data centers, personal computers, smartphones,

tablets, other portable terminals, cars, and other consumer products.

This makes them highly susceptible to the market demand for

semiconductor devices. As a result, demand for silicon wafers is

sensitive to rapid progress in technological innovation or product

obsolescence, rapid change in the product mix, falling product prices,

and other factors characteristic of semiconductors and surrounding

industries. With the recent rapid expansion of the semiconductor and

silicon wafer market coverages, the semiconductor device market has

become increasingly intertwined with global macroeconomic trends.

Economic recessions caused by infectious diseases or geopolitical

risks, such as regional conflicts between nations and U.S.-China trade

frictions, may affect demand for semiconductor products.

Furthermore, demands for semiconductor devices and silicon

wafers are expected to grow in the medium to long term due to

technological innovations, such as an increase in data communication

volume, development of generative AI technology, progress in

driverless driving, and digital transformation (DX), and the

semiconductor market growth driven by the increasing adoption of

HEVs and EVs. There is, however, no guarantee that demand for silicon

wafers will increase as expected by the Group. A discrepancy between

the actual market condition and the Group’s expectation may impact

the Group’s business results, etc.

To address these changes in the business environment, the Group is

creating business foundations that can speedily and accurately adapt

to market trends, while endeavoring to further strengthen its financial

fundamentals. If, however, such measures do not have a positive

impact, it may affect the Group’s business results, etc.

other events, downward pressure could be put on the prices of silicon

wafers, which are basic materials of such products, and affect the

Group’s business results, etc.

To increase its ability to deal with market fluctuations, the Group

prepares business plans to offset potential declines in the prices of

such products. These plans include efforts to improve productivity

using AI technology and raise production yields through continuous

technological improvement. These measures, however, may not

produce the expected impact.

Moreover, the Group’s business results, etc., may be affected

by a loss or decline in the Group’s production capacity caused by

developments including the following: falling sales volume or market

share due to reputational damage; discontinuation of or significant

delays in production or lowering yield rates following a large accident

in its facilities, large-scale system errors or other reasons; breakdown

of manufacturing facilities; or disruption of distribution functions.

In response, the Group takes measures to avoid the risks of production

The semiconductor industry is characterized by rapid advances in

technology. As semiconductors feature increasingly high circuit

densities and fine line widths and their applications become more

diverse and advanced, customers’ demands for the silicon wafers that

the Group supplies are growing more diverse and sophisticated. To

meet such customer demands, the Group conducts R&D activities,

focusing on technologies related to leading-edge 300 mm

semiconductor silicon wafers, for which demand is expected to grow

in the medium to long term. By product category, its R&D activities

focus on technologies for high-value-added wafers, such as epitaxial

wafers, and technologies for next-generation wafer products.

If, however, its R&D activities do not produce the expected benefits

or it falls behind its competitors in terms of technology development,

the Group will be unable to respond to technological advances in the

semiconductor industry and find it difficult to meet customer

demands. This may affect the Group’s business development,

business results, etc. It is not easy to develop or mass produce

leading-edge 300 mm semiconductor silicon wafers, which the Group

focuses on. If the Group incurs a higher-than-expected R&D expense or

if it takes long to improve productivity, this may have a negative impact

on the Group’s business results, etc.

To supply wafer products that meet escalating customer demands

in a timely manner, the Group needs to endeavor to constantly update

its understanding of customer needs and technological trends in the

semiconductor industry, and promote R&D activities, anticipating their

future needs. The Group enhances a structure to systematically

comprehend technological trends of the semiconductor industry and

sections in a timely manner. At the same time, the Group conducts

cutting-edge R&D activities utilizing joint research activities with

conferences and other opportunities. To conduct advanced R&D

activities, the capabilities of engineers are no doubt important. The

Group therefore develops engineers through its fine-tuned educational

programs. The Group strengthens these structures so that it does not

fall behind its competitors in meeting customer requirements. If, however,

such measures do not produce a positive impact, this may affect the

Group’s business results, etc.

even if the Group decides to enhance facilities to match the market

demand, the Group may end up having excess production capacity if

the market conditions deteriorate or competitors enhance their

is enhancing its production capacity, due to a time lag between making

such a decision and completing the enhancement. Furthermore, if such

investments do not result in the expected quality or yield rate, the

Group may not be able to secure the projected production volume or

may even post impairment losses.

In addition, the Group supplies products preferentially to customers

long-term contracts or may not conclude or renew long-term sales

contracts under the same terms and conditions.

accidents, the Group prepares a Business Continuity Plan (BCP). The

BCP clarifies the earthquake-resistant and seismic isolation measures

meeting, which oversees risk management in general, and are reviewed

by management. If, however, such measures do not produce a positive

impact, this may affect the Group’s business results, etc.
