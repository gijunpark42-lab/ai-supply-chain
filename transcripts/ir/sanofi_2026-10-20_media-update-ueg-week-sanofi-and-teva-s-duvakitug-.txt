UEG Week: Sanofi and Teva’s duvakitug maintained long-term positive outcomes in new phase 2b data for ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease

In UC, duvakitug maintained high rates of clinical remission, clinical response, and endoscopic improvement at Week 44

In CD, duvakitug maintained high rates of endoscopic response, clinical response, and clinical remission at Week 44

Duvakitug was well tolerated and safety was consistent with the induction study

Paris and Parsippany, NJ. October 20, 2026. New positive data from the RELIEVE UCCD phase 2b long-term extension (LTE) study (clinical trial identifier: NCT05668013 ) in patients with moderately-to-severely active ulcerative colitis (UC) or Crohn’s disease (CD) demonstrated that duvakitug maintained clinically meaningful efficacy improvements across multiple endpoints over 44 weeks. Duvakitug is an investigational human monoclonal antibody that targets TL1A, a protein that amplifies inflammation and potentially drives fibrosis in UC and CD.

In addition, biomarker findings that advance the understanding of how duvakitug may impact inflammation and tissue remodeling in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) were shared. These data were presented today at United European Gastroenterology (UEG) Week 2026 in Barcelona.

The RELIEVE UCCD LTE study evaluated the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of duvakitug through 44 weeks of maintenance treatment after induction in patients with UC and CD, the two most common forms of IBD. Patients (n=130, 72 with UC and 58 with CD) who responded to duvakitug treatment in the 14-week induction study were randomized to receive either duvakitug 450 mg or 900 mg subcutaneously every four weeks (Q4W) for up to 58 weeks of total exposure.

UC Results

At Week 44, maintenance treatment with duvakitug demonstrated high rates of clinical remission and clinical response, as measured by the modified Mayo Score (mMS), meaning active UC symptoms such as rectal bleeding, increased stool frequency, and pain have stopped (remission) or improved (response). Duvakitug treatment also achieved high rates of endoscopic improvement, defined by a Mayo Endoscopic Score of 0 (inactive disease in the colon) or 1 (mild disease activity), which measures the presence and degree of intestinal inflammation. Efficacy outcomes were numerically higher at 900 mg compared to 450 mg, consistent with a dose response.

“Ulcerative colitis can have a significant impact on patients that disrupts even the most basic daily activities. Despite available therapies, some patients continue to experience flares that impact their lives in various ways and may contribute to disease progression,” said Walter Reinisch, MD, PhD, Director of the Inflammatory Bowel Disease Study Group at the Medical University of Vienna and a co-lead investigator of the RELIEVE UCCD LTE study. “These results underscore the potential of duvakitug to deliver effective and durable relief from persistent inflammation and symptoms, supporting better long-term disease management in patients with ulcerative colitis.”

CD Results

Duvakitug maintenance treatment demonstrated high rates of endoscopic response, defined as a 50% or more decrease from baseline in Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn’s Disease (SES-CD) at Week 44. SES-CD assesses mucosal ulcers, the ulcerated surface, the endoscopic extension, and the presence of stenosis in the colon as a gauge of disease severity. Additionally, duvakitug maintained high rates of clinical response and clinical remission as measured by the Crohn’s Disease Activity Index (CDAI), meaning symptoms such as abdominal pain, diarrhea, and fatigue have stopped (remission) or improved (response). Duvakitug appeared to demonstrate a dose response, with numerically higher efficacy results achieved at the higher dose.

“Due to the heterogeneous nature of Crohn’s disease, many patients either stop responding or never respond to available therapies, leaving them with uncontrolled symptoms that cause physical distress,” said Vipul Jairath, MBChB, DPhil, FRCP, FRCPC, Professor of Medicine in the Departments of Medicine, Epidemiology and Biostatistics at Western University and a co-lead investigator of the RELIEVE UCCD LTE study. “The efficacy rates seen with duvakitug in the maintenance period highlight its potential as a new treatment option that could help more patients achieve sustained remission.”

Across both UC and CD, duvakitug was generally well tolerated throughout the maintenance period, with a safety profile consistent with the induction phase and no new safety concerns identified. The most frequently reported adverse events (AEs) occurring in ≥5% of patients across pooled duvakitug dose groups in UC were upper respiratory tract infection and nasopharyngitis, and in CD were upper respiratory tract infection, CD, hypertension, nasopharyngitis, anemia, and influenza. The majority of AEs were mild or moderate in severity. Discontinuation rates due to AEs were low (UC, 4% and 2% in the 450 mg and 900 mg arms, respectively, and in CD were 6% in each arm).

Biomarker Results

Biomarker analyses from the RELIEVE UCCD induction study (clinical trial identifier: NCT05499130 ) were presented at UEG Week 2026. Duvakitug reduced blood levels of markers connected to multiple inflammatory pathways in UC and to the Th17 and Th1 immune pathways in CD, as well as markers of neutrophilic inflammation in both blood and tissue in CD and UC. In patients with CD, duvakitug also reduced markers of tissue remodeling. These findings provide additional mechanistic evidence that TL1A inhibition may address both inflammation and, potentially, fibrosis.

Additionally, clinical results from the RELIEVE UCCD induction study were recently published in The Lancet Gastroenterology and Hepatology for UC and CD .

Duvakitug Clinical Development Program

Duvakitug is being investigated in the phase 3 SUNSCAPE-1 (clinical trial identifier: NCT07184996 ) and SUNSCAPE-2 (clinical trial identifier: NCT07185009 ) studies in moderately-to-severely active UC, and the phase 3 STARSCAPE-1 (clinical trial identifier: NCT07184931 ) and STARSCAPE-2 (clinical trial identifier: NCT07184944 ) studies in moderately-to-severely active CD. Sanofi and Teva have initiated the phase 2 AURALIGHT study (clinical trial identifier: NCT07812116 ) of duvakitug in moderate-to-severe hidradenitis suppurativa and are planning a phase 2 study of duvakitug in fibrostenotic Crohn’s disease.

Duvakitug is currently under clinical investigation, and its efficacy and safety have not been evaluated by any regulatory authority.
