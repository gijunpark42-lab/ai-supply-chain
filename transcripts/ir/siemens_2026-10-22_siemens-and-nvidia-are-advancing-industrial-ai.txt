Press Release 22 October 2026 Siemens AG Munich

Siemens and NVIDIA are advancing Industrial AI

The partners advance the technologies that power AI – from chips to AI factories – and deliver AI applications transforming physical industries

Designing better chips: AI agents automate complex design tasks, enabling chip designers to explore 50 times more design options

Building AI factories faster: Reference designs help simplify planning and deployment, while Siemens–Delta prefabricated power modules reduce data-center time to market by up to 50 percent.

Reducing AI factories’ footprints: Physics-based digital twin technology helps optimize energy use, inform grid assessments and evaluate noise-reduction measures before construction

Putting AI to work in engineering: Engineers can predict product performance up to 1,000 times faster

Putting AI to work in manufacturing: Siemens’ Erlangen factory provides the blueprint for adaptive manufacturing, with successfully piloted AI applications now coming to market

Siemens and

NVIDIA are putting AI to work in product design and production – and helping

build the AI factories that power it. Ten months after expanding their

partnership at CES, the companies are delivering new products and reporting

measurable results from joint projects.

Siemens and

NVIDIA are putting AI to work in product design and production – and helping

build the AI factories that power it. Ten months after expanding their

partnership at CES, the companies are delivering new products and reporting

measurable results from joint projects.

“Industrial AI gives our customers a once-in-a-century opportunity to do what was never possible before,” said Roland Busch, President and CEO of Siemens. “Together with NVIDIA, we are building the Industrial AI Operating System, combining their accelerated computing and AI platforms with our leading hardware, software, industrial AI and data. This will help customers make products smarter, factories more productive and infrastructure more efficient – with the safety, security and reliability industry demands.”

“AI is

driving a new industrial revolution, transforming every industry and redefining

what we can build and how we build it,” said Jensen Huang, founder and CEO of

NVIDIA. “Together with Siemens, we are reinventing how chips are designed and

AI factories are built – and putting that intelligence to work in the physical

world, powering a new era of autonomous engineering and manufacturing.”

Designing the chips that enable AI

AI starts

with chips. To deliver more computing power faster, Siemens and NVIDIA have developed AI agents that automate complex chip

design tasks and verify their results using Siemens’ electronic design

automation (EDA) tools and NVIDIA AI infrastructure. The latest application

extends this work to electrical and thermal analysis.

As

chipmakers stack components to build more powerful chips, heat becomes harder

to manage and can limit performance. With Siemens’ AI-driven electrothermal

workflow, engineers can predict where heat will build up and how it will affect

a chip’s performance before manufacturing begins. AI agents plan and run the

analysis across multiple engineering tools within Siemens’ EDA software

portfolio. NVIDIA CUDA-X libraries accelerate the thermal simulation workflow,

helping engineers evaluate design alternatives faster. The workflow helps

engineers to set up multiple tools in under five minutes instead of several

days and enables 50 times more design exploration than manual methods.

Building the infrastructure powering AI

AI factories

turn data and energy into AI at scale. NVIDIA DSX defines the reference

architecture for the AI factory. As a leading data center infrastructure

provider, Siemens provides the electrical distribution, protection, automation

and controls to the NVIDIA DSX architecture – from the grid connection to the

AI rack – and the digital twin that customers use to design, build and operate

the facility as one system.

Siemens,

NVIDIA and ecosystem partners have introduced the NVIDIA DSX Vera Rubin

Reference Design for the U.S. and European markets. These repeatable

architectures translate NVIDIA DSX requirements into deployable power and

controls infrastructure, bringing together power, cooling and compute

requirements and adapting them to local standards to simplify planning,

construction and operation. Siemens and Delta co-designed prefabricated,

factory-tested electrical power modules that can reduce data-center time to

market by up to 50 percent.

Siemens’

physics-based digital twin, developed with NVIDIA and built using NVIDIA

Omniverse libraries, helps operators plan and optimize this infrastructure as

one system. Even before construction begins, the digital twin shows how a

facility will interact with the grid, where cooling could fall short and how

noise from continuously operating cooling equipment could affect neighbors.

Operators can identify and address potential bottlenecks, evaluate

infrastructure tradeoffs that affect tokens per watt, and test noise-reduction

measures before committing capital – helping them meet local requirements and

build community trust. The digital twin can also provide utilities with

additional visibility to inform grid-connection assessments. During operation,

connected telemetry and simulation can help operators identify opportunities to

improve cooling and electricity use.

Turning AI into real-world impact

Putting AI

to work in industry means helping engineers develop better products faster and

manufacturers run more resilient, efficient operations. To enable engineers to

test more designs before building prototypes, Siemens combines its Simcenter

simulation software with NVIDIA accelerated computing and NVIDIA CUDA-X

libraries to accelerate supported simulation workloads. The package can run

without an internet connection, keeping sensitive data on site. Simulations run

up to ten times faster. Simcenter PhysicsAI uses models trained on simulation

data to predict design performance up to 1,000 times faster than traditional

simulation; local AI agents further accelerate simulation tasks, reducing

manual effort and helping engineers iterate faster.

Siemens’

electronics factory in Erlangen serves as the blueprint for adaptive

manufacturing. Here, Siemens and NVIDIA have developed and piloted AI

applications that coordinate production tasks and prepare quality inspections.

Intelligence Center X and Siemens’ operations software bring together data and

workflows from enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems and the shop floor.

AI agents built with the NVIDIA Factory Operations Blueprint (FOX) use this information to

coordinate and optimize production planning, helping manufacturers adapt faster

to changing conditions and cut costs by up to 20 percent.

For

automated optical inspections, Process Preparation X uses Siemens’ Digital Twin

and NVIDIA Cosmos Data Factory to create training data, eliminating a critical

bottleneck in preparing quality inspections – the need to build physical

prototypes – and helping to shorten time to market by up to 40 percent.

Manufacturers

can also test production changes before implementing them. Digital Twin

Composer – built using NVIDIA Omniverse libraries – connects virtual factory

models to real operational data, and AI-powered factory scheduling allows teams

to optimize production based on component availability, equipment maintenance

and other variables. An initial customer deployment increased throughput by 20

percent. This implementation brings the NVIDIA Factory Operations Blueprint (FOX) to life through Siemens Intelligence Center X for

operations.

Siemens AG (Berlin and Munich) is a leading technology company focused on industry, infrastructure, mobility, and healthcare.
