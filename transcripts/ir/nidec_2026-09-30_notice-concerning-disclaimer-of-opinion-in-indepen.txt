Notice Concerning Disclaimer of Opinion in Independent Auditor's Report for the Securities

Report, Disclaimer of Review Conclusion for the Third Quarter Financial Results,

and Disclaimer of Opinion in Internal Control Audit Report

for the Fiscal Year Ended March 31, 2026

Nidec Corporation (the “Company”) has received an audit report containing a disclaimer of opinion on the

consolidated financial statements and the non-consolidated financial statements in the securities report for the fiscal

year ended March 31, 2026.

The Company hereby also announces that it has received an internal control audit report containing a disclaimer

of opinion with respect to the internal control audit on the internal control report as of March 31, 2026.

１．Name of the audit corporation which was engaged in the audits

PricewaterhouseCoopers Japan LLC

２．Content of the audit report concerning the consolidated financial statements, the non-consolidated financial

statements and the internal control audit report

(1) Content of the audit report concerning the consolidated financial statements

The content of the audit report concerning the consolidated financial statements for the fiscal year ended

March 31, 2026, is as follows:

<Disclaimer of Opinion>

Pursuant to Article 193-2, Paragraph 1 of the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act of Japan, we have been

engaged to audit the consolidated financial statements of NIDEC CORPORATION (the “Company”) and its

consolidated subsidiaries (collectively referred to as the “Group”) included in the “Financial Information” section,

which comprise the consolidated statement of financial position, consolidated statements of income and

comprehensive income, consolidated statement of changes in equity, consolidated statement of cash flows, and notes

to the consolidated financial statements for the consolidated fiscal year from April 1, 2025 to March 31, 2026.

Given the significance of the potential effects of the matters described in the “Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion” on

the consolidated financial statements, we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to provide a

basis for an audit opinion on the consolidated financial statements. Accordingly, we do not express an audit opinion

on these consolidated financial statements.

<Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion>

Corporate Governance

As described in the Notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements, “Status of the Company’s Remediation Measures

Relating to Corporate Governance” and Note 43, Additional Information, “Improper Accounting Practices and Other

Related Matters,” section “(1) Investigations by the Third-Party Committee,” the Company established a Third-Party

Committee on September 3, 2025, received the investigation report from the Third-Party Committee on February 27,

2026, and received the investigation report (supplement) as its final report on April 17, 2026. The investigation reports

describe numerous instances of improper accounting practices carried out at various operating bases within the

Company and its group companies with the involvement or knowledge of management, including premature revenue

recognition, understatement of provisions, overstatement of inventories, avoidance of impairment losses on fixed

assets, and capitalization of expenses. The reports further identified instances in which management or accounting

personnel of the Company and its group companies provided inaccurate or misleading information to the independent

auditor, concealed relevant documents, or provided altered records in order to obtain the independent auditor’s

concurrence. The improper accounting practices identified by the Third-Party Committee have been corrected in the

consolidated financial statements.

In addition, as described in section “(2) Internal Investigation regarding Potential Quality Issues,” the Company

completed an investigation regarding potential quality issues and identified falsification of testing and inspection

records and unauthorized use of non-conforming materials at multiple production bases. Furthermore, as described

in section “(3) Other Internal Investigation,” the Company completed its investigation into matters relating to trade

transactions and tariffs and identified underpaid tariffs associated with import transactions amounting to USD 94.1

million (approximately ¥14,160 million), including interest. Related costs, liabilities and disclosures have been

reflected in the consolidated financial statements.

However, as described in the Notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements, “Status of the Company’s

Remediation Measures Relating to Corporate Governance,” the Company has identified certain officers and

employees of the Company and its group companies who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper

accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, and is evaluating and taking

remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures; however, some of those actions have not been

completed. As a result, certain officers and employees who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper

accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, continue to hold positions of

responsibility within the financial reporting process of the Company and its group companies.

The matters identified in the Third-Party Committee’s reports indicate that material weaknesses existed in the

control environment over an extended period and raise substantial concerns regarding whether the Company’s

governance and control environment relating to financial reporting is operating effectively, including whether the

risk of management override of controls has been sufficiently mitigated. This condition also affects the reliability of

records, documents and information submitted to the independent auditor by, or prepared, responded to, or reviewed

by, individuals involved in the improper accounting practices, as well as the reliability of management representations

provided to the independent auditor.

During the course of our audit, we concluded that information obtained from officers and employees involved in

these improper accounting practices had a significant effect on the reliability of management representations as audit

evidence and on the persuasiveness of the audit evidence as a whole. Because some remedial personnel actions had

not been completed as of the date the consolidated financial statements were prepared and individuals involved in

the improper accounting practices continue to participate in the financial reporting process, we were unable to obtain

sufficient appropriate audit evidence as to whether the improper accounting practices corrected or determined by the

Company to require correction were complete, or whether undetected misstatements arising from the risk of

management override existed as a result of those individuals’ involvement in the financial reporting process and, if

so, the nature, extent and effects thereof.

Accordingly, as described in the Notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements, “Status of the Company’s

Remediation Measures Relating to Corporate Governance,” although the Company is evaluating and taking remedial

personnel actions to determine appropriate measures for individuals involved in the improper accounting practices,

some of those remedial personnel actions have not been completed, and it has not been sufficiently demonstrated that

successor personnel have assumed responsibility for financial reporting and are appropriately performing their

responsibilities. We therefore concluded that the possible effects of any undetected misstatements on the consolidated

financial statements would not be confined to specific line items or disclosures and could be material and pervasive.

As a result, we were unable to determine whether any adjustments to the consolidated financial statements were

necessary.

Impairment

As described in Note 16, “Impairment of Non-Financial Assets,” the Company groups assets based on the smallest

identifiable group of assets that generates cash inflows that are largely independent (a cash-generating unit). Goodwill

acquired in a business combination is allocated to cash-generating units or groups of cash-generating units that are

expected to benefit from the synergies of the combination, and impairment losses are measured based on estimated

future cash flows and other assumptions. As a result, the Company recognized impairment losses of ¥632,221 million

during the current fiscal year. However, as of the date of this report, we have not obtained sufficient appropriate audit

evidence regarding the reasonableness of the accounting estimates relating to the amount and period allocation of

impairment losses for certain cash-generating units or groups of cash-generating units.

IAS 36, Impairment of Assets, requires future cash flow projections to be based on reasonable and supportable

assumptions. However, as described under “Corporate Governance,” information obtained from individuals who may

have been involved in the improper accounting practices has a significant effect on the persuasiveness of audit

evidence relating to impairment of assets submitted to us and on the reliability of management representations.

In addition, the Company has restated the comparative information because the opening balances of non-financial

assets of certain cash-generating units or groups of cash-generating units had been overstated in light of the improper

accounting practices. Under IAS 8, Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors, a prior-period

restatement must be based on information that was available when the original consolidated financial statements were

authorized for issue, without using facts or outcomes identified subsequently. Specifically, revised cash flow

projections and significant assumptions included in the underlying business plans are required to reflect information

available at the relevant dates and not information or assumptions developed subsequently. Because we did not obtain

sufficient necessary explanations from the Company as to whether prior-period corrections were based on information

available at the relevant dates without using hindsight, we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence

supporting that the restatement had been measured based on information available at those dates.

Accordingly, we were unable to determine whether the impairment losses recognized by the Company complied with

the requirements of IAS 36 and IAS 8.

Comparative Information

The Company received the investigation reports from the Third-Party Committee, considered the improper

accounting practices based on those reports, completed its own investigation and the investigation relating to trade

transactions and tariffs, and restated the comparative information based on the results. However, for the reasons

described under “Corporate Governance” and “Impairment,” because individuals involved in the improper

accounting practices continue to hold positions of responsibility within the financial reporting process of the

Company and its group companies, we were unable to determine the appropriateness and completeness of the restated

amounts and disclosures included in the consolidated financial statements for the fiscal year from April 1, 2024 to

March 31, 2025, which are presented as comparative information.

We were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to provide a basis for an audit opinion on the

consolidated financial statements. In addition, we concluded that the possible effects on the consolidated financial

statements of potentially required additional adjustments described under “Corporate Governance,” the amount and

period allocation of impairment losses described under “Impairment,” and the disclosures relating to comparative

information described under “Comparative Information” could be material and pervasive. If undetected

misstatements exist, their possible effects would not be confined to specific line items or disclosures and could be

material and pervasive to the consolidated financial statements as a whole. Accordingly, we were unable to determine

whether any adjustments to the consolidated financial statements were necessary.

Other Matter

As a result of the investigations conducted by the Third-Party Committee and other investigations, the Company

has adjusted opening retained earnings in the comparative information. However, the Company has not corrected the

consolidated financial statements included in the Annual Securities Reports for fiscal years up to and including the

consolidated fiscal year from April 1, 2024 to March 31, 2025.

In preparing the consolidated financial statements, management is responsible for assessing whether it is

(3) Content of the internal control audit report concerning the internal control report

The content of the internal control audit report concerning the internal control report as of March 31, 2026, is

as follows

<Disclaimer of Opinion>

Pursuant to Article 193-2, Paragraph 2 of the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act of Japan, we have been

engaged to audit the Internal Control Report of NIDEC CORPORATION as of March 31, 2026.

Given the significance of the possible effects on the Internal Control Report of the matters described in the “Basis

for Disclaimer of Opinion” section, we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence to provide a basis

for an audit opinion. Accordingly, we do not express an opinion on the Internal Control Report, in which the Company

states that it is unable to express the results of its assessment of internal control over financial reporting as of March

31, 2026.

<Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion>

As described in the Internal Control Report, although the Company is evaluating and taking remedial personnel

actions to determine appropriate measures for officers and employees involved in the improper accounting practices

at the Company and its group companies, some of those actions have not been completed. Consequently, the

Company was unable to evaluate the effects on entity-level controls of the Company and its group companies and

determined that it was unable to express the results of its assessment of internal control over financial reporting as of

March 31, 2026.

As a result, because we were unable to perform significant audit procedures, we were unable to obtain a basis for

expressing an opinion on the Internal Control Report, in which the Company states that it is unable to express the

results of its assessment of internal control over financial reporting as of March 31, 2026.

<Emphasis of Matters>

As described in the Internal Control Report, material weaknesses exist in entity-level controls of the Company and

its consolidated subsidiaries, controls relating to financial closing and reporting processes that should be evaluated

from an entity-wide perspective, and business-process controls. As described under “Corporate Governance” in the

“Basis for Disclaimer of Opinion” section relating to the audit of the consolidated financial statements, certain

officers and employees who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who

provided false explanations to us, continue to hold positions of responsibility within the financial reporting process

of the Company and its group companies. We were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence as to

whether the improper accounting practices corrected or determined by the Company to require correction were

complete, or whether undetected misstatements arising from the risk of management override existed as a result of

those individuals’ involvement in the financial reporting process and, if so, the nature, extent and effects thereof.

Consequently, we concluded that the possible effects of any undetected misstatements would not be confined to

specific line items or disclosures and could be material and pervasive, and we were unable to determine whether any

adjustments to the consolidated financial statements were necessary.
