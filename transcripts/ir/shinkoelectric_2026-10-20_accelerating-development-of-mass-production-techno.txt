TOKYO, Japan, October 20, 2026 – NTT, Inc. (NTT), NTT Innovative Devices Corporation

(NTT Innovative Devices), Japan Semiconductor Corporation* (Japan Semiconductor),

Furukawa Electric Co., Ltd. (Furukawa Electric), and SHINKO ELECTRIC INDUSTRIES CO.,

LTD. (Shinko Electric Industries) today announced that a four-company joint R&D proposal led

by NTT Innovative Devices has been selected under the New Energy and Industrial

Technology Development Organization (NEDO)'s Research and Development Project of the

Enhanced Infrastructures for Post -5G Information and Communication Systems /

Development of Manufacturing Technologies for Advanced Semiconductors. The project aims

to establish mass -production technologies for photonics -electronics convergence  (PEC)

devices that support next -generation AI and data -intensive computing.  The participating

companies will jointly develop mass -production technologies for optical chiplets, with NTT

serving as an advisor.¹

The rapid adoption of generative AI is driving unprecedented growth in demand for data

processing across AI data centers and high -performance computing (HPC) systems. At the

same time, rising power consumption associated with chip-to-chip communications has

emerged as a major challenge. As a result, PEC technologies, which enable high-speed data

transfer with significantly lower power consumption, are attracting increasing attention

worldwide.

Against this backdrop, NTT has been leading research and development of optical chiplet 2

packaging technologies that use light to connect semiconductor packages and semiconductor

chips under NEDO's Research and Development Project of the Enhanced Infrastructures for

Post-5G Information and Communication Systems  / Development of Manufacturing

Technologies for Advanced Semiconductors since fiscal year 2023. NTT serves as the lead

company for this initiative, which has advanced the development of optical chiplet technologies

for next-generation semiconductor systems.3

Under the  project, the participating companies will develop innovative manufacturing

technologies for semiconductor devices incorporating optical chiplets to enable the high -

capacity, low-power data transmission required by next -generation AI computing platforms.

The project aims to achieve more than a tenfold increase in manufacturing productivity

compared with current R&D-stage processes.

To accomplish this goal, the participating companies will combine their expertise in optical

semiconductors, silicon photonics, optical interconnects, semiconductor packaging, and mass

production processes. They will establish and evaluate a pilot production line designed for

future commercial manufacturing. NTT will participate in the project as an advisor, promoting

industry collaboration and supply chain development to help accelerate the commercialization

and large-scale production of PEC devices.

The selected R&D project and the roles of the participating companies are outlined below.

【Roles of Participating Companies】

NTT As an advisor, NTT will promote industry collaboration and

supply chain development to establish mass -production

technologies for PEC devices.

NTT Innovative Devices Development of manufacturing, testing, and packaging

technologies required for the mass production of optical

chiplets.

Japan Semiconductor Development of mass-production technologies for silicon

photonics optimized for heterogeneous material integration

in optical chiplets.

Furukawa Electric Development of mass-production technologies for

compound semiconductor-based optical devices for optical

chiplets.

Shinko Electric Industries Development of optical chiplet packaging  technologies and

mass production processes, including in-process inspection

technologies.

Through the project, the participating companies will establish mass-production technologies

for optical chiplets spanning the entire manufacturing value chain, from photonic device

fabrication and testing to optical interconnects and advanced packaging, in anticipation of the

emerging market for PEC devices.

These efforts will enable the high -speed, high -capacity, and low-power data transmission

required by next -generation AI computing infrastructure while accelerating the

commercialization of PEC devices and strengthening Japan's domestic semiconductor

manufacturing capabilities.

The project will also foster collaboration among companies and research institutions involved

in the development and production of PEC technologies, helping to establish a globally

competitive supply chain. Through these initiatives, the participating companies aim to

contribute to reducing the growing energy consumption of data centers driven by the rapid

adoption of generative AI and to supporting  the realization of a more sustainable information

and communications society.

2. Optical Chiplet

A photonic s-electronics convergence device that combines a photonic integrated circuit (PIC), including photonic

semiconductor devices, with an electronic integrated circuit (EIC) through high -density packaging. It replaces electrical

signals with optical signals for data transmission between processing chips such as CPUs and GPUs. As a core technology

for photonic s-electronics convergence, it overcomes the increases in power consumption associated with transmission

distance and the limitations of bandwidth, enabling high-capacity, ultra-low-power servers and data centers for the AI era.

About NTT Innovative Devices

At NTT Innovative Devices, we have commercialized COSA (Coherent Optical Sub

Assembly), which consists of miniaturized integrated optical transmitter/receiver with silicon-

photonics technology and digital signal processing LSI technologies developed over many

years. Furthermore, we have been developing commercialized co-packaged devices

integrated with a digital signal processing LSI. We will continue to develop PEC-related

technologies and actively develop products that meet the requirements of our customers in

the growing areas of data centers and computing industries in addition to the conventional

optical communication applications.
