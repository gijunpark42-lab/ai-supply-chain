Radioactive Airborne Particulate Monitor Receives a 2026 R&D 100 Award

Enhancing Safety in Nuclear Facility Work Environments through the Rapid and Highly Accurate

Identification of Radioactive Materials

Radioactive Airborne Particulate Monitor

TOKYO, October 8, 2026 – Mitsubishi Electric Corporation (Mitsubishi Electric), Mitsubishi Electric Plant

Engineering Corporation (MPEC), Nagoya University, Tokai National Higher Education and Research System

(Nagoya University), and the Japan Atomic Energy Agency (JAEA)  announced today that the “Radioactive

Airborne Particulate Monitor,” jointly developed by the four organizations , has received a 2026 R&D 100

Award, presented by R&D World in recognition of innovative technological developments.

The Radioactive Airborne Particulate Monitor can rapidly and accurately identify and measure radioactive

materials in the air at nuclear facilities and other locations, thereby improving workplace safety . At facilities

that handle radioactive materials, such as nuclear power plants, radioactive particulates (aerosols) may become

airborne in the event of an accidental release of radioactive substances. To prevent workers from suffering

serious internal radiation exposure, monitoring systems capable of strictly controlling the concent rations of

artificial radioactive materials, including nuclear fuel materials, are required.

With conventional monitors, it has been difficult to accurately distinguish the proportion of  alpha particles

emitted by naturally occurring radioactive materials compared to those emitted by artificial nuclear fuel

materials. Identifying nuclear fuel materials requires precise analysis at specialized facilities. The Radioactive

Airborne Particulate Monitor implements an  unfolding technique that estimates the energy distribution of

2/3

alpha particles and identifies  the type of radioactive material withou t precise analysis. This technology has

significantly improved the detection limit1 and reduced measurement time, making it possible to immediately

detect radioactivity concentrations by type of nuclear fuel material at the work site. The R&D 100 award

recognizes this technology for enabling rapid responses to abnormal events. This technology is expected to

contribute to improved safety not only at nuclear -related facilities, but also at a wide range of facilities that

handle radioactive materials, from resource extraction and mining sites to medical facilities.

Features

1) Implementation of an “unfolding technology ”that enables the separation of naturally occurring

nuclides2 from artificial nuclides, which was previously difficult to achieve

2) Compared with conventional methods, this approach achieves a detection limit that is more than 100

times lower while reducing the measurement time to one-sixth or less

3) Enabling immediate on-site quantification and making a significant contribution to ensuring worker

safety in high -risk environments, including decommissioning operations at the Fukushima Daiichi

Nuclear Power Station

About the R&D 100 Awards

Since 1963, R&D World has sponsored the R&D 100 Awards to recognize the world’s 100 most outstanding

commercial technologies each year. The prestigious awards, often referred to as the “Oscars of Innovation,”

are nominated by industry professionals, university academics, resea rchers and other experts. Winners are

selected as the most technically significant, original and useful technologies brought to market in the previous

year. This year’s award ceremony will be held in the U.S. city of Scottsdale, Arizona on November 20.
