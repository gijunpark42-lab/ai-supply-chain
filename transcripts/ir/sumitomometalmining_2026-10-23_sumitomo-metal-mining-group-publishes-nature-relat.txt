Sumitomo Metal Mining Group Publishes Nature-Related Information Based on the TNFD Disclosure Framework and Other Guidance

October 23, 2026

Sumitomo Metal Mining Co., Ltd.

Tokyo, Japan - Sumitomo Metal Mining Co., Ltd. (TSE: 5713) has set forth "a company that contributes to a nature-positive future" as its vision for 2030 to address "Conservation and Restoration of Nature," one of its material issues. In response to recent global developments in nature-related disclosures, Sumitomo Metal Mining has published nature-related information on the Sumitomo Metal Mining Group with reference to the disclosure framework of the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (TNFD) and the LEAP approach as its guidance.

Sumitomo Metal Mining Group's Nature-Related Disclosures (PDF:619KB)

The Sumitomo Metal Mining Group recognizes that the natural environment, which is the foundation of human health, welfare, and the economy, is deteriorating worldwide, and that biodiversity is decreasing at an unprecedented rate. Accordingly, the Sumitomo Metal Mining Group will contribute to the sustainable development of society by avoiding or minimizing the negative impacts of its business activities on nature and undertaking restoration and conservation efforts.

Sumitomo Metal Mining Group’s Nature-Related Disclosures

1. Introduction

Sumitomo Metal Mining Co., Ltd. (“the Company”) has a history that stretches back more than 430 years and has

contributed to Japan’s modernization and subsequent development through the supply of non- ferrous metals. The

Company’s history has also been one of addressing environmental issues, including afforestation at the Besshi Copper

Mines and measures to address smoke pollution.

Our Group1 recognizes that coexistence with nature is essential to the continuation of our business. We have

identified “Conservation and Restoration of Nature” as one of our material issues in realizing our long-term vision

of becoming the world leader in the non-ferrous metals industry, with “a company that contributes to a nature-positive

future” as a Vision for 2030.

To achieve this, we analyzed the impacts of our Group’s business activities on the natural environment and

identified the associated risks and opportunities. Going forward, we plan to conduct more detailed analyses that take

into account the characteristics of our businesses and to establish new metrics and targets, further strengthening our

efforts to address our material issues.

Furthermore, with regard to our material issues, including “Conservation and Restoration of Nature,”  our Group

views the Vision for 2030 as a medium -term goal and formulates a business  plan every three years to address the

issues. Individual measures are carried out as short-term initiatives planned for each fiscal year.

In FY2026, guided by the disclosure framework developed  by the Taskforce on Nature -related Financial

Disclosures (TNFD) and the LEAP approach 2, we assessed our Group’s directly operated sites in terms of nature -

related risks and opportunities and, in particular, identified the locations and business areas requiring priority action

toward the goal of achieving effectively zero loss of nature (no net loss). The identification process was as follows.

at 36 sites in Japan and overseas where our Group conducts direct operations.

our priority locations, taking into account the nature -related dependencies, impacts, risks, opportunities, business

Our Group has established the Sumitomo Metal Mining Group’s Nature Policy, which clarifies our governance

and promotional framework. Under the supervision of the Board of Directors, we promote sustainability activities

primarily through the Sustainability Committee, chaired by the president and representative director. Nature-related

3-2 Strategy

3-2-1 Identification of Priority Locations

Using the various tools described in the preceding section “2. The Sumitomo Metal Mining Group’s

Understanding of and Approach to Nature,” we analyzed and assessed our Group’s nature-related impacts and

evaluated the degree of nature-related dependencies and impacts of each business and site (including the areas where

directly operated sites are located), as well as the degree of attention required for each location . The results of the

analysis and assessment of the degree of nature-related dependencies and impacts of each business and the degree of

attention required for each location are shown in Figure 2.

The horizontal axis (X-axis) indicates the degree to which each of our Group’s businesses depends on nature; the

further to the right, the greater the degree of dependency. The vertical axis (Y -axis) indicates the degree of impact

that each of the businesses has on nature; the higher the position, the greater the degree of impact. The size of each

bubble indicates the degree of attention required, taking into account material locations in light of business scale and

the results of tool-based analyses and assessments of sensitive locations.

As shown in Figure 2, the degrees of dependency and impact are approximately the same for the smelting &

refining business and the materials business (advanced). However, the smelting & refining business, which is larger

in scale, operates in locations that require a higher degree of attention.

Based on the results of these analyses and assessments, we determined that the smelting & refining business should

be given particularly high priority among our  Group’s businesses in taking action toward the goal of achieving

effectively zero loss of nature (no net loss). Furthermore, taking into account the results of analyzing sensitive

locations, we identified two nickel production sites in the Philippines, Coral Bay Nickel (“CBNC”) and Taganito

HPAL (“THPAL”), as priority locations.

3-2-3 Initiatives to Address Nature-Related Risks and Opportunities in Priority Locations

The following are the main initiatives being undertaken in the priority locations identified through the analyses.

(1) Tailings Dam Management (Addressing Physical Risks)

CBNC and THPAL manage their tailings dams in accordance with the Global Industry Standard on Tailings

Management (GISTM). Tailings dams that have reached the end of their service life are also rehabilitated through

measures such as planting vegetation in an effort to restore ecosystems. These initiatives have been highly praised by

the Philippine government and have received awards including the Philippine Presidential Mineral Industry

7

Environmental Award.

(Reference) Rehabilitation of Tailings Dams https://www.smm.co.jp/en/sustainability/activity_highlights/article_01/

(2) Ecosystem Monitoring (Addressing Physical Risks)

Through baseline surveys conducted prior to commencement and ecosystem surveys conducted once operations

were underway, we confirmed that operations have had no significant impacts on ecosystems. Monitoring surveys

have also confirmed the presence of additional rare species.

(3) Coral Cultivation (Addressing Physical Risks)

As a diverse range of flora and fauna has been confirmed to inhabit the area near CBNC’s wastewater discharge

outlet, CBNC has also begun cultivating coral and is working to conserve the natural environment that supports the

diverse ecosystem.

(4) Support for Indigenous Peoples (Addressing Reputation/Stakeholder Risks)

The Mamanwa people, believed to be the oldest indigenous people in the Philippines, live in the vicinity of THPAL

and possess extensive knowledge about living in harmony with nature. THPAL aided in establishing a school where

Mamanwa children are able to learn about their traditions and culture in the Mamanwa language.

THPAL also provides i ndigenous peoples with various forms of other aid. It s upported the construction of the

Claver Community Hospital, which is open not only to the Mamanwa people but also to members of the local

community, and handed the hospital over to the local government.

(Reference) THPAL Supporting Indigenous People (the Philippines)

https://www.smm.co.jp/en/sustainability/activity_highlights/article_24/

(5) Conservation of Endangered Species (Addressing Physical Risks)

CBNC and THPAL support mangrove planting in habitats of the Indo- Pacific crocodile and the Philippine

crocodile, both of which are on the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. They also

support the planting of narra (Pterocarpus indicus), the national tree of the Philippines, which is also on the IUCN

Red List.

(6) Technological Innovation in the Recovery of Valuable Metals (Effective Use of Resources as an Opportunity)

THPAL has developed technologies for the separation and recovery of chromite from raw ore and the recovery of

scandium from the process of producing mixed nickel -cobalt sulfides, and is conducting recovery operations using

these technologies. Through these efforts, we are promoting the effective use of resources. We are also considering

developing technology to increase the grade of the iron contained in leach residue remaining after the recovery of

nickel for effective utilization as an iron resource (hematite).

4. Management of Risks and Impacts

As mentioned, our Group evaluates locations in terms of the material nature-related dependencies, impacts, risks,

and opportunities identified, as well as in terms of the degree of attention required, and determines priority locations

8

within our direct operations. We also assess the degrees of dependency and impact on nature using tools such as

ENCORE. Risks and opportunities are identified following discussion and consideration by the Nature Conservation

and Restoration Subcommittee, c omprised of relevant parties including from our  Group’s business divisions, and

deliberation by the Sustainability Committee.

However, these assessments will need to be reviewed and updated as appropriate in response to future changes in

our Group’s business and new insights gained through further analysis. We will continue to address nature-related

dependencies, impacts, risks and opportunities associated with our business based on the latest information.

Our Group has established a proprietary risk management system, which we operate in conjunction with the

management of risks and opportunities under our environmental management system . We manage nature- related

risks in the same manner, and we have set up a Risk Management Working Group to implement our risk management

policy and priority measures. The Risk Management Working Group holds discussions and conducts reviews annually,

after which material risks are identified through deliberation by the Sustainability Committee.

The Board of Directors receives reports on these efforts as necessary and engages in discussions on them.

5. Metrics and Targets

Our Group has adopted “a company that contributes to a nature -positive future” as our Vision for 2030 for the

material issue of “ Conservation and Restoration of Nature.”  Each year, we disclose environmental data on raw

material and energy inputs, water resource inputs, water consumption, chemical releases and transfers, and industrial

waste generation by type, and other metrics in our business activities, and through monitoring this data, we advance

initiatives to reduce the impacts of our business activities on nature.
