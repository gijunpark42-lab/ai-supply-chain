(1) Forward-looking statements, such as forecast of consolidated financial performance, stated in this document are based on

(2) Improper accounting practices and other related mattersThe Company recognized the suspicion of improper accounting treatment, that may have a significant impact on the entire

condensed consolidated financial statements, with the involvement or knowledge of its or their management—namely, for

example, that those companies could be construed to have arbitrarily considered when to write down certain assets with risk in

terms of their asset value. Therefore, the Company recognized the need for objective investigations by the Third-Party Committee

independent of the Company. Accordingly, the Company decided to establish the Third-Party Committee in compliance with the

“Guidelines for Third-Party Committees in Cases of Corporate Misconduct” established by the Japan Federation of Bar

Associations on September 3, 2025.

In the Third-Party Committee‘s reports, a large number of instances of improper accounting practices through misconduct and

errors were pointed out at various operating bases in the Company and the group companies. In light of the final report from the

Third-Party Committee, including the results of the self-inspection, and the internal investigations and other internal

investigations regarding potential quality issues, the Company decided that it was necessary to retroactively correct misstatements

associated with the improper accounting practices and other practices in the consolidated financial statements and other related

documents, including corrections of errors related to matters that were not corrected from the viewpoint of materiality in previous

years and matters that were additionally identified in the course of the investigation.

Details of investigations by Third-Party Committee and other internal investigations are described in “4. Consolidated Financial

Statements and Other Information, (5) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements (8. Additional information)”.

(3) Premises for the Preparation of Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company established a Third-Party Committee on September 3, 2025, received the investigation report from the Third-Party

Committee on February 27, 2026, and received the investigation report (supplement) as its final report on April 17, 2026. The

investigation reports describe numerous instances of improper accounting practices carried out at various operating bases within

the Company and its group companies with the involvement or knowledge of management, including premature revenue

recognition, understatement of provisions, overstatement of inventories, avoidance of impairment losses on fixed assets, and

capitalization of expenses. The reports further identified instances in which management or accounting personnel of the Company

and its group companies provided inaccurate or misleading information to the independent auditor, concealed relevant documents,

or provided altered records in order to obtain the independent auditor’s concurrence. The improper accounting practices identified

by the Third-Party Committee have been corrected in the consolidated financial statements.

However, the Company has identified certain officers and employees of the Company and its group companies who participated

in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent

auditor, and is evaluating and taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures; however, some of those

actions have not been completed. As a result, certain officers and employees who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of

the improper accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, continue to hold positions of

responsibility within the financial reporting process of the Company and its group companies.

The matters identified in the Third-Party Committee’s reports indicate that material weaknesses existed in the control environment

over an extended period and raise substantial concerns regarding whether the Company’s governance and control environment

relating to financial reporting is operating effectively, including whether the risk of management override of controls has been

sufficiently mitigated. This condition also affects the reliability of records, documents and information submitted to the

independent auditor by, or prepared, responded to, or reviewed by, individuals involved in the improper accounting practices, as

well as the reliability of management representations provided to the independent auditor.

Although the Company is evaluating and taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures for individuals

involved in the improper accounting practices, some of those remedial personnel actions have not been completed, and it has not

been confirmed that successor personnel have assumed responsibility for financial reporting and are appropriately performing

their responsibilities. Accordingly, the possible effects of any undetected misstatements on the consolidated financial statements

would not be confined to specific line items or disclosures and could be material and pervasive. Such effects, if any, have not been

reflected in the consolidated financial statements.

For details, please refer to " Status of the Company’s Remediation Measures Relating to Corporate Governance" in the Notes to

Consolidated Financial Statements.

1. Overview of Operating Results, Etc.

As described in “4. Consolidated Financial Statements and Other Information, (5) Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements (8.

Additional information)”, the Company established the independent Third-Party Committee in accordance with the “Guidelines for

Third-Party Committees in Cases of Corporate Misconduct” established by the Japan Federation of Bar Associations on September 3,

2025, and received the investigation report from the Third-Party Committee on February 27, 2026, and the investigation report

(supplement) as its final report on April 17 of the same year. In addition, the Company has conducted internal investigations regarding

potential quality issues and issues related to trade transactions and tariffs. In these reports, a large number of instances of improper

accounting practices through misconduct and errors were pointed out at various operating bases in the Company and the group

companies. In light of the final report from the Third-Party Committee, including the results of the self-inspection, the Company

decided that it was necessary to retroactively correct misstatements associated with the improper accounting practices and other

practices, including corrections of errors related to matters that were not corrected from the viewpoint of materiality in previous years

and matters that were additionally identified in the course of the investigation.

(1) Overview of Operating Results for the Year Ended March 31, 2026

1. Overview of Business Environment for the Year Ended March 31, 2026

During the nine months ended March 31,2026, the global economy remained resilient overall and gradually recoveded by various

national policies. On the other hand, outlook remained uncertain due to factors such as the impact of U.S trade and tariff policies,

hightlighted geopolitical risks in the Middle East, and fluctuations in financial markets.

In this economic situation, the initiatives by product group are as follows:

First, in the Small precision motors, we are working to develop and expand net sales of products that respond to the evolution

required to support the explosive growth of the market in our product groups for AI data centers that support the AI society, including

water-cooling modules and near-line HDDs. In addition to these efforts, we are also working to further boost profitability by

reviewing unprofitable models.

Next, in the Automotive products, we will continue to provide the market with mobility innovations that contribute to the

promotion of electrification and automation of mobility through a wide variety of products and related modules. At the same time, we

are working to improve profitability by thoroughly reviewing orders for unprofitable models and reducing fixed costs. Also, with the

integration of the Automotive organic (existing business) into the Appliance Commercial Industrial Motors Business Unit (ACIM),

Nidec Mobility and Nidec Elesys merged their operations, effective April 1, 2025. We will integrate the resources of both companies

and accelerate the restructuring of a system that can reorganize business.

In the Appliance, commercial and industrial products, demand continues to grow for backup power generators for data centers,

Battery energy storage systems (BESS) accelerated by green innovation. To meet these strong demands, NIDEC is actively investing

in enhancing production capacity in India, France, and North and Middle America, and strengthening its recurring business related to

maintenance and inspections downstream of the value chain.

Regarding the Machinery, we are steadily receiving orders amid a global trend toward capital investment aimed at "labor-saving

and unmanned operations" and "higher speed and precision." We aim to become a leader in global manufacturing by steadily

capturing demand for capital investment while creating synergies between the companies we have acquired through M&A in recent

years and our group companies.

Our company and our group will continue to leverage our advanced technological capabilities and manufacturing expertise to

pursue ongoing high profitability as a leading company contributing to the realization of a sustainable, circular society.

2. Management Policies, Business Environment, and Challenges

(1) Basic Management Policies

The Company received a Designation as a Security on Special Alert from the Tokyo Stock Exchange on October 28, 2025. The

Company takes very seriously, from a management perspective, the significant loss of trust from shareholders, investors, customers,

business partners, and other stakeholders caused by improper accounting and inappropriate actions regarding quality and inspections

within the group.

The Company has been positioned to "always do everything right" as its highest management priority. The Company is verifying

and correcting the accounting treatments of prior years to disclose financial information with accuracy and transparency. The

Company is advancing a root cause investigation and clarifying accountability, while executing recurrence prevention measures based

on its improvement plan across the entire group. As a first step, transitioned to a new board of directors system that enhances the

independence and supervisory functions of the board of directors, and executed a substantial reform of its executive system.

The Company is also breaking away from a traditional performance-first approach and an excessive top-down management style.

The Company is advancing a transition to a management structure where each business proactively formulates plans aligned with the

business environment and its own capabilities, executing them following sound discussion and verification. The Company is currently

executing steadily based on the improvement plan, aiming to restore trust as quickly as possible. The Company is continuously

verifying the effectiveness of these measures and working on the appropriate establishment and operation of the internal control

system. The Company is making these improvements with all executives and employees of the group united, aiming for the lifting of

the Designation as a Security on Special Alert through these initiatives.

The Company furthermore revised its previously announced Conversion 2027 plan and announced the basic policy of “Re-

Definition” The Nidec Five-Year Business Transformation Plan for Better Corporate Value, covering the period from FY2026 to

FY2030, on May 13, 2026. The Company is currently formulating a mid-term management plan, including the strategies and

numerical plans for each business, based on this basic policy.

1. Corporate culture & Governance reform

The Company recognizes that the background to the recent improper accounting and inappropriate actions regarding quality lies in

a corporate culture that gives top priority to short-term profits and does not allow targets to be missed, concentration of authority in

specific management, and vulnerabilities in Internal Controls and group company administration. Based on this understanding, the

Company is striving to transition from a conventional top-down structure to an integrated organization where executives and frontline

employees engage in two-way dialogue. The Company will advance its transformation into an organization and culture that is highly

transparent and encourages collaborative discussion, ensuring all employees engage in their duties with a sense of reward and social

significance. Top management is taking the lead in this dialogue by holding town hall meetings to speak directly with employees, and

the Company is conducting "Culture Transformation Lab" activities to foster a culture were "speaking up changes the company",

thereby connecting employee voices directly to management. Furthermore, the Company is strengthening the deployment of training

and education programs, providing continuous education for personnel engaged in accounting, sales, purchasing, and production

control worldwide. Through these measures, the Company will eliminate excessive pressure caused by short term numerical targets

and firmly establish a governance system that promotes sound growth at all locations. Through such initiatives, the Company will

strengthen cross functional collaboration across departments and sites, ensure psychological safety, and foster a free and open

environment, establishing a mechanism where failures, concerns, and signs of irregularities can be reported and corrected without

being hidden.

14

2. New board of directors system & enhancing its supervisory function

The Company takes seriously the findings in the report by the Third-Party Committee regarding the dysfunction of the check and

balance roles of Audit and Supervisory Committee members and outside directors, as well as the lack of diversity among directors.

Accordingly, the Company seeks to strengthen its supervisory functions by diversifying the expertise of the board of directors.

Specifically, “Experience in company management” and “Knowledge in the capital market” have been added as newly required skills

to the member matrix of the board of directors. Based on these new criteria, the Company has appointed experienced corporate

managers with extensive practical experience in corporate rebuilding and governance reform, as well as experts possessing the

perspective of an institutional investor, to serve as outside board of director members.

Furthermore, to ensure a stronger board of directors independence and supervision of executives, the Company is transitioning to a

structure to select the members of independent outside board of directors to chair the board, aiming to build board of directors system

for better corporate value on a sustainable basis.

Regarding the executive system, the Company has revised its selection criteria to strongly emphasize a strong sense of corporate

ethics and compliance, organizational design and human resource development, and capabilities in future strategic planning and

execution, thereby enhancing the transparency and objectivity of the appointment and dismissal process. The Company will firmly

establish a system that clearly separates the supervisory role of the board of directors from the operational responsibilities of the

executive side, ensuring that material management issues and risks are shared and deliberated in a timely manner.

3. “Re-Definition” The Nidec Five-Year Business Transformation Plan for Better Corporate Value

The Company aims to maximize its corporate value by improving ROIC, reallocating management resources across the entire

group, and thoroughly promoting structural reform. Starting from the reconstruction of proper management, and in order to build a

high-profit structure, and evolve into a truly global company, the Company will strengthen the selection and concentration of its

businesses by combining a review of the business portfolio, group reorganization, and investments in growth areas where high

profitability and market growth are expected.

(2) Business Environment and NIDEC's Medium- to Long-term Business Strategies

The Company will promptly initiate the following four initiatives set forth in “Re-Definition” The Nidec Five-Year Business

Transformation Plan announced on May 13, 2026: (1) business reorganization based on the five pillars, (2) review of the business

portfolio,(3) group reorganization, and (4) company-wide IT infrastructure reform.

The Company is currently proceeding to formulate a mid-term management plan covering the period from fiscal 2026 to fiscal

2030 by re-examining the growth potential, profitability, capital efficiency, and feasibility of each business based on these policies.

These five pillars indicate the direction of the medium- to long-term business strategy and management control toward fiscal 2030

and differ from the reporting segments used for financial reporting in the current consolidated fiscal year. Based on the development

status of business operations and management control systems, the Company will gradually establish mechanisms for performance

management and resource allocation centered on the five pillars.

1. Business Reorganization Based on five pillars

The Company will strengthen group management tailored to each business profile by consolidating the sales companies and

manufacturing facilities currently held by individual legal entities in accordance with the five pillars. Going forward, the Company

will promote its Business Reorganization centered around the five pillars: Better Life, Electrification & Energy Infrastructure, Digital

Society Solutions, Manufacturing Solutions, and Mobility Innovation. The Company aims to expand its business by fostering

collaboration and generating synergies, cross-functionally leveraging the diverse strengths previously scattered across Nidec's group

companies on a group-wide basis. At the same time, the Company will consolidate the group's strengths beyond the boundaries of

existing businesses to create new value, while remaining highly conscious of customer perspectives and demands.

In these five business domains, the Company is transitioning to a structure where each pillar assumes accountability for its

consolidated operations to fulfill its accountability to the capital markets. The Company will drive this transformation through the

global HQ; specifically, with the global HQ for compliance, portfolio management, capital allocation, etc. building a strong

governance structure for the entire world.

2. Review of business portfolio

The Company will execute a fundamental restructuring of its business portfolio from the perspectives of growth potential and

profitability by recognizing its core strengths in manufacturing technologies, and reconfirming the deep technological capabilities,

extensive experience, and expertise it has cultivated over many years.

The Company will actively invest in and develop new business models such as solution providing in fields such as AI datacenters,

energy, and semiconductor-related businesses where high profitability and market growth are expected. The Company views

technological innovations and broader social transformations as distinct opportunities for investment in new domains. The Company

will leverage its existing proprietary technologies as a foundation to expand its business into emerging fields poised for future growth

by identifying these new areas within high-growth markets. Conversely, the Company will swiftly proceed with the withdrawal from

our divestiture of low-growth and low-profitability businesses.

The Company intends to continuously improve its cash-generating capabilities and capital efficiency, including ROIC, aiming for

Better Corporate Value on a sustainable basis through these strategic measures. Furthermore, the Company will maintain an

appropriate balance between growth investment and shareholder returns while ensuring its financial soundness.

The Company will execute structural reform across the company based on a review of the business portfolio, striving to achieve

stronger governance, less cost, and improve ROIC. In our Business Transformation Plan discipline announced in May 2026, we have

set targets to consolidate our locations from 248 as of March 2026 to 160–180 locations, and our legal entities from 353 to 160–180

entities. Moving forward, the Company will verify the business necessity, customer support, supply systems, legal and tax matters, IT

systems, and reorganization costs for each location and entity, and proceed with the reorganization sequentially on the premise of

avoiding any disruption to business continuity and risk management.

(3) Status of Implementation of the Improvement plan and progress Report

1. Overview of the Accounting Issue

We sincerely apologize for the significant inconvenience and concern caused to our shareholders, investors and all other

stakeholders by the improper accounting issue involving the Company.

The Company has identified that, in past fiscal years, inappropriate accounting treatments and trade transaction issues occurred

within the Group, including multiple domestic and overseas consolidated subsidiaries. As a result, the Company received an audit

report expressing a disclaimer of opinion on the consolidated financial statements included in the annual securities report for the fiscal

year ended March 31, 2025. In addition, in light of these series of events, Tokyo Stock Exchange, Inc. determined that the Company’s

internal management system and related controls required significant improvement, and designated the Company as a Securities on

Special Alert on October 28, 2025. In response, the Company submitted the “Improvement Plan and the Status Report” on January 28,

2026, and the “Revised Improvement Plan and the Status Report” on April 27, 2026, to Tokyo Stock Exchange, Inc., with the aim of

fundamentally improving its internal management system and related controls and thereby seeking removal from the Securities on

Special Alert designation.

With respect to the “Revised Improvement Plan and the Status Report,” after formulating the Improvement Plan based on the

hearing results of the Nidec Corporate Reform Committee established in November 2025, the Company reviewed the causes that led

to the inappropriate accounting treatments and deficiencies in internal controls in light of the contents of the Investigation Reports

received from the Third-party Committee on February 27, 2026 and April 17, 2026, and formulated a revised version of the

improvement measures for preventing recurrence based on the analysis of those causes.

As set forth in the “Revised Improvement Plan and the Status Report” dated April 27, 2026, the Company believes that the causes

of the issues were the result of a combination of six structural and interrelated factors: (1) excessive shareholder primacy to continue

to demonstrate growth, (2) a corporate culture that gives top priority to short-term profits and does not allow targets to be missed, (3)

concentration of authority on Mr. Nagamori and a culture of prioritizing his opinions, (4) governance vulnerabilities, (5) internal

control weaknesses, and (6) vulnerabilities of the Group company management system.

(v) Ensuring the independence of the accounting function from the business divisions

(a) Ensuring the independence of the accounting function

The Company announced on Jun. 27, 2025 that it would extend the submission deadline for the annual securities report for the fiscal

included an audit report containing a disclaimer of opinion.

- Despite extending the submission deadline for the annual securities report by approximately three months, the Company submitted

premature revenue recognition, understatement of provisions, overstatement of inventories, avoidance of impairment losses on fixed

Company and its group companies provided inaccurate or misleading information to the independent auditor, concealed relevant

documents, or provided altered records in order to obtain the independent auditor’s concurrence. The improper accounting practices

identified by the Third-Party Committee have been corrected in the consolidated financial statements.

investigation regarding potential quality issues and identified falsification of testing and inspection records and unauthorized use of

non-conforming materials at multiple production bases. Furthermore, as described in section “(3) Other Internal Investigation,” the

section “(1)Investigations by Third-Party Committee” or section “(3) Other Internal Investigation,” participated in, directed, or had

knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, and is evaluating and

taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures; however, some of those actions have not been completed. As a

result, certain officers and employees who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who

provided false explanations to the independent auditor, continue to hold positions of responsibility within the financial reporting

process of the Company and its group companies.

reliability of management representations provided to the independent auditor.

(4) Background of the Improper Accounting and Measures to Prevent Recurrence

As previously mentioned, the investigation by the Third-Party Committee and the other internal investigations revealed a large

number of instances of improper accounting practices through misconduct and errors across various bases in the Group.

The identified improper accounting practices involves a wide range of instances, including those by errors, such as: an instance where

loss on valuation of inventory was not recorded by falsely recognizing asset values in raw materials and products with extremely low

55

future usage and sales prospects and no asset value; an instance where impairment was avoided by including projects with low

probability of realization in sales plans used as assumptions for fixed asset impairment tests; an instance where labor costs, for which

it was not appropriate to include in fixed assets as incidental expenses, were recorded as fixed assets and expensed through

depreciation, thereby deferring the timing of expense recognition; an instance where, in the course of preparing the Group’s

consolidated financial statements, provisions related to return of government grants and so on, recorded in the non-consolidated

financial statements of subsidiaries were improperly reversed in the consolidated financial statements; an instance where grants,

which due to their nature, should not be recognized as revenue, were falsely represented and recognized as revenue; and an instance

where provisions for allowance for doubtful accounts of bad debts were not appropriately recorded.

The Third-Party Committee’s investigation concluded that each improper accounting practices discovered was a misconduct

undertaken against a backdrop of excessive pressure to achieve performance targets, particularly operating profit targets. Within the

Group, under the forceful leadership of its founder, Mr. Nagamori, the idea that “losses are unacceptable” had been thoroughly

entrenched over many years, and achieving performance targets had come to be seen as imperative. For many years, the Group’s

performance targets were set by Mr. Nagamori using a top-down approach and allocated to individual business divisions and

subsidiaries. However, these targets were set from the perspective of the level of growth expected by investors, and they exceeded the

capabilities of the business divisions and subsidiaries. On top of that, Mr. Nagamori applied considerable pressure to the Vice

Presidents and the CFO at the head office, who were in charge of the business divisions and subsidiaries, to achieve performance

targets, and this pressure on the Vice Presidents and others at the head office was then passed on to the executives of the business

divisions and subsidiaries. Under such circumstances, the check-and-balance function of the management departments, such as the

internal audit and accounting departments, did not work sufficiently (internal control failure). Consequently, as the excessive

performance pressure was placed on the entire Group and internal controls did not function sufficiently, a large number of instances of

improper accounting practices occurred at various bases.

The Company published its Improvement Plan and Status Report (hereinafter referred to as the “improvement plan”) for the

purpose of fundamentally improving the internal management system, etc. and submitted it to the Tokyo Stock Exchange on January

28, 2026.

Since then, the Company has been working on the development and operation of corrective measures in accordance with the

improvement plan. On February 27 and April 17, 2026, the Company received the investigation report from the Third-Party

Committee and, based on this report and other internal investigation results, re-examined and reviewed the improvement plan. A

revised improvement plan reflecting these contents and the status of corrective measures was submitted to the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

The Company will implement the improvement of the internal management system etc., and the reform of governance and

organizational culture and continuously monitor the status of implementation. At the same time, executives and employees will work

together to implement this revised improvement plan.

More details on the improvement plan are provided in "2. Management Policies, Business Environment, and Challenges”.
