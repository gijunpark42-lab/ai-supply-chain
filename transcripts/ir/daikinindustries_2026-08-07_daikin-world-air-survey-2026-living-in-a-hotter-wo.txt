Living in a Hotter World: How People Are Adapting to Heat

Survey of 1,200 people across 12 cities worldwide finds that nearly 90% feel their area has become hotter, with growing impacts on daily life, health, and cooling needs.

Osaka, Japan, August 7, 2026 - Daikin Industries,

Ltd., a global leader in HVAC&R (Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration) solutions,

reports the results of its second Daikin World Air Survey. The survey gathered responses from 1,200 air

conditioner owners across 12 cities worldwide, exploring perceptions of heat, changes in daily behaviors due

to heat, and air conditioning usage.

Record-breaking temperatures

and heatwaves have become increasingly common around the world in recent years. According to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), 2024 was the warmest year

on record globally, while 2023 and 2025 ranked second and third, respectively.* 1 As rising

temperatures increasingly affect daily life, health and cooling needs, the survey explores how people are

adapting to a hotter world.

What This Survey Finds

Areas feel hotter: 86.5% of respondents say their area has

become hotter compared to five years ago .

Heat is changing daily routines: 59.1% say they have avoided going out or doing outdoor activities because

of the heat.

Health and well-being are affected: 89.9% of respondents report some form

of heat-related physical discomfort.

Cooling seasons are getting longer: 84.1% of respondents say the period

during which they use air conditioning has become longer .

Many people face barriers to cooling: 64.4% of respondents

say there are times when they cannot use air conditioning as much as they would like to

because of costs, power supply, or other constraints .

86.5% feel their area has become hotter

How do people around the world perceive recent changes in heat?

Across the 12 cities surveyed, 86.5% of respondents reported that their area had become hotter over the past

five years. By city, the highest share was recorded in Bangkok, Thailand, where 97.0% of respondents said

conditions had become hotter, followed by, Delhi, India, and Tokyo, Japan , both at

94.0%. Madrid, Spain, and Paris, France, were also above the overall average, with around 90% of respondents

reporting that their area had become hotter.

Notably, the survey was conducted in May 2026, before the major

heatwaves that affected Europe in June and July 2026 , indicating that the perception of rising heat was already widespread at the time of

the survey

How have temperatures actually changed in each city?

Data from the Japan Meteorological Agency show that

Bangkok and Delhi, where many respondents perceived their area to be hotter than five years ago, are

among the warmest of the 12 cities surveyed. While maximum temperatures in Bangkok have continued to

trend upward in recent years, Delhi has experienced a slight decline.

These findings suggest that perceptions of rising heat

are not necessarily driven by maximum temperatures alone. Because temperatures fluctuate considerably

across seasons and years, people may also be influenced by other indicators of heat, including the

frequency and intensity of heatwaves, the number of extremely hot days, and the occurrence of tropical

nights.

Dubai, UAE, recorded the highest average maximum

temperature among the 12 cities, exceeding

40.0 º C/104.0 ℉ . By contrast, Paris, France, had

an average maximum temperature around

26.0 º C/78.8 ℉ , lower than many of the other

cities surveyed, despite experiencing severe heatwaves this year. This highlights the fact that Paris

has historically had a more moderate summer climate than many of the hotter regions included in the

survey.

Living with Heat in Europe

Hotter summers reshaping perceptions of cooling.

According to the Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S), Europe is the fastest-warming continent

globally and June 2026 was the hottest June on record in western Europe *2 . As extreme heat

becomes more frequent, attention is increasingly turning to how homes, buildings and cities can adapt to a

warmer climate.

Although the survey was conducted between 29 May and 5 June 2026, respondents in Paris and Madrid

were already reporting growing exposure to increased heat and longer cooling seasons before the major

heatwaves that affected their region later that summer. Public concern about heat-related health risks,

cooling needs and climate adaptation may have increased further following these extreme weather events.

In Madrid, 93% of respondents said their area had become hotter than it was five years ago, while

89% of respondents in Paris, France, shared the same view. In addition, 87% of respondents in Madrid and 75%

in Paris reported that their cooling season had become longer. These findings suggest that cooling is

becoming an increasingly important aspect of adapting to life in a warmer climate, even in regions that have

traditionally experienced more moderate summer temperatures.

Heat is changing how people live and spend their time

Heat is also influencing daily routines. When asked, “Over the past year, have you or any of the

family members you live with changed your lifestyle or behaviors due to the heat?”, the most common response

across the 12 cities surveyed was, “I have avoided going out during the day or taking part in outdoor

activities,” cited by 59.1% of respondents. The highest shares were recorded in Istanbul, Turkey (74%),

Bangkok, Thailand (73%), and Delhi, India (70%).

The second most common response was, “I have increased the amount of time I spend at home or

indoors,” selected by 40.7% of respondents. Other common adaptations, included changing clothing choices,

showering more frequently, using air-conditioned public facilities, and shifting activities to cooler times of the

day. Together, these findings suggest that heat is increasingly influencing everyday routines and behaviors,

extending its impact beyond physical discomfort alone.

89.9% have experienced heat-related physical discomfort

Heat appears to affect not only how people perceive changes in their local climate, but also their health

and daily lives. When asked, "Have you ever experienced physical discomfort that you think was caused by heat?",

89.9% of respondents across the 12 cities surveyed said they had experienced some form of heat-related physical

discomfort.

The most commonly reported effect was a decline in sleep quality (58.3%), followed by fatigue or

sluggishness (50.9%) and headaches (36.1%). While these symptoms may not be as severe as heatstroke, poor sleep can

affect rest and recovery, contributing to fatigue, daytime drowsiness, reduced concentration and lower productivity.

As periods of extreme heat become more frequent, the findings highlight the importance of protecting

people from heat exposure throughout the day and night. Measures that help maintain comfortable indoor environments

may play an increasingly important role in supporting health, well-being and daily activities in a warming climate.

Cooling and Health

Protecting well-being in a hotter world

Extreme heat affects more than comfort. It can influence sleep quality, daily activities and

overall well-being, while also increasing serious health risks. According to the World Health Organization

(WHO), studies estimate that approximately 489,000 heat-related deaths occur worldwide each year. Heat can

trigger heat exhaustion and heatstroke, and increase a range of health risks, including cardiovascular and

respiratory diseases. *3

In Daikin’s survey, 89.9% of respondents across 12 cities reported

experiencing some form of heat-related physical discomfort. As the world becomes hotter, the effects of

heat are increasingly being felt not only through changing weather patterns, but also in people’s

health, well-being and everyday lives. Ensuring access to energy-efficient and affordable cooling will

increasingly become both a public health and societal challenge.

*3WHO, Heat and Health https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-heat-and-health

Cooling seasons are getting longer

As behavior changes in response to heat, the way people use air conditioning is also changing. When

asked, “Compared with five years ago, how has the period during which you use air conditioning for

cooling, from the start to the end of use within a year, changed?”, 84.1% of respondents across 12 cities

said it has become longer. By city, the highest shares were in Delhi, Bangkok, and São Paulo, Brazil.

When respondents were asked how many hours they use air conditioning for cooling when spending a full

day at home during the hottest time of year, the longest average usage time was in Tokyo, at 14 hours and 12

minutes. This was followed by Houston, United States, at 14 hours and 2 minutes, Dubai at 12 hours and 56

minutes, Shanghai, China, at 11 hours and 56 minutes, and New York, United States, at 11 hours and 36 minutes.

Different Homes, Different Cooling

How air conditioning systems vary around the world

Air conditioning has evolved differently around the world to suit lifestyles and housing styles.

In the United States, central air conditioning is the most common residential system. A network

of ducts distributes cool or warm air throughout the home, making it well suited to larger detached houses.

In contrast, many homes in Asia and Europe use ductless mini-split systems, which allow people to

cool or heat individual rooms as needed.

In Europe, the heating market is gradually shifting from traditional boilers to heat pumps.

Modern air conditioning systems are reversible, providing both cooling in summer and heating in winter. As a

result, technologies that were once used primarily for cooling are increasingly playing a role in year-round

indoor comfort.

64.4% cannot always use air conditioning as much as they would like

The survey found that air-conditioning use is increasing around the world, but not everyone is able

to use cooling as much as they need or would like. When asked, “Do you think everyone who wants to use air

conditioning in your country or region is actually able to do so?”, more than 60% of respondents across 12

cities answered “Yes.” At the same time, in São Paulo, Paris, Lagos, Tokyo, and Madrid, more

than half answered “No,” indicating that many people do not believe everyone who wants to use air

conditioning is actually able to do so.

Looking at the respondents themselves, all of whom owned an air conditioner at home, the survey

asked, “If it were not for high electricity costs or power supply constraints, would you want to increase

your use of air conditioning for comfort or health reasons?” Across the 12 cities, 89.7% of respondents

said they would, either “Very much” or “Somewhat.”

Affordable Cooling

Closing the gap in access to cooling

As temperatures rise around the world, access to cooling is becoming increasingly important. The

International Energy Agency (IEA) points out that only about 15% of people living in hot climates own an air

conditioner, and affordability remains a major challenge in many parts of the world. *4

This challenge is also reflected in Daikin’s survey. Across 12 cities, 64.4% of respondents

said they sometimes cannot use air conditioning as much as they would like because of factors such as

electricity costs or power supply constraints. Addressing access to cooling is not only an energy challenge, but

also a public health and climate resilience challenge, particularly for those most vulnerable to increasing heat

risks.

As cooling demand continues to grow, expanding access to efficient and affordable cooling, together

with reliable power infrastructure and energy-saving practices, will become increasingly important. The

challenge is no longer whether cooling is needed, but how to provide cooling that is affordable,

energy-efficient and sustainable.

About the Daikin World Air Survey

Daikin has conducted the Modern People’s Air Survey in Japan to reveal how people today think

about air and what issues they face, and to encourage broader interest in air, a subject that is often taken for

granted. Since the survey began in 2002, Daikin has conducted 28 surveys and shared a wide range of topics

related to air.

As Daikin’s overseas business ratio now exceeds 80%, the company expanded the survey globally

in 2024 and conducted the first Daikin World Air Survey to encourage more people across borders to pay attention

to air and to air conditioners, which help create comfortable indoor air environments.

The second Daikin World Air Survey was conducted amid growing global interest in heat. It surveyed

people living in 12 cities across 11 countries about their perceptions of recent heat, changes in daily life and

behavior caused by heat, actual use of air conditioning, impacts on sleep and health, energy-saving awareness,

and challenges related to using cooling. As the world becomes hotter, air conditioning is becoming increasingly

important not only as a home appliance that creates a comfortable indoor environment, but also as something that

supports daily life and health. Through this survey, Daikin aims to shed light on how air environments,

air-conditioning usage, and approaches to heat differ by country and region, and to provide an opportunity to

think about air environments that help people around the world live comfortably and healthily.
