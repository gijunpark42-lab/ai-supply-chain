(2) Improper accounting practices and other related mattersThe Company recognized the suspicion of improper accounting treatment, that may have a significant impact on the entirecondensed consolidated financial statements, with the involvement or knowledge of its or their management—namely, forexample, that those companies could be construed to have arbitrarily considered when to write down certain assets with risk interms of their asset value. Therefore, the Company recognized the need for objective investigations by the Third-Party Committeeindependent of the Company. Accordingly, the Company decided to establish the Third-Party Committee in compliance with the“Guidelines for Third-Party Committees in Cases of Corporate Misconduct” established by the Japan Federation of BarAssociations on September 3, 2025.In the Third-Party Committee‘s reports, a large number of instances of improper accounting practices through misconduct anderrors were pointed out at various operating bases in the Company and the group companies. In light of the final report from theThird-Party Committee, including the results of the self-inspection, and the internal investigations and other internalinvestigations regarding potential quality issues, the Company decided that it was necessary to retroactively correct misstatementsassociated with the improper accounting practices and other practices in the consolidated financial statements and other relateddocuments, including corrections of errors related to matters that were not corrected from the viewpoint of materiality in previousyears and matters that were additionally identified in the course of the investigation.Details of investigations by Third-Party Committee and other internal investigations are described in “2. Condensed QuarterlyConsolidated Financial Statements and Other Information, (5) Notes to Condensed Quarterly Consolidated Financial Statements(7. Additional information)”.

(3) Premises for the Preparation of Condensed Quarterly Consolidated Financial Statements

As described in Note 7, Additional Information, “Improper Accounting Practices and Other Related Matters,” to the condensed

quarterly consolidated financial statements, the Company established a Third-Party Committee on September 3, 2025, received

the investigation report from the Third-Party Committee on February 27, 2026, and received the investigation report (supplement)

as its final report on April 17, 2026. The investigation reports describe numerous instances of improper accounting practices

carried out at various operating bases within the Company and its group companies with the involvement or knowledge of

management, including premature revenue recognition, understatement of provisions, overstatement of inventories, avoidance of

impairment losses on fixed assets, and capitalization of expenses. The reports further identified instances in which management or

accounting personnel of the Company and its group companies provided inaccurate or misleading information to the independent

auditor, concealed relevant documents, or provided altered records in order to obtain the independent auditor’s concurrence. The

improper accounting practices identified by the Third-Party Committee have been corrected in the condensed quarterly

consolidated financial statements.

However, the Company has identified certain officers and employees of the Company and its group companies who participated

in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent

auditor, and is evaluating and taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures; however, some of those

actions have not been completed. As a result, certain officers and employees who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of

the improper accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, continue to hold positions of

responsibility within the financial reporting process of the Company and its group companies.

The matters identified in the Third-Party Committee’s reports indicate that material weaknesses existed in the control environment

over an extended period and raise substantial concerns regarding whether the Company’s governance and control environment

relating to financial reporting is operating effectively, including whether the risk of management override of controls has been

sufficiently mitigated. This condition also affects the reliability of records, documents and information submitted to the

independent auditor by, or prepared, responded to, or reviewed by, individuals involved in the improper accounting practices, as

well as the reliability of management representations provided to the independent auditor.

Although the Company is evaluating and taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures for individuals

involved in the improper accounting practices, some of those remedial personnel actions have not been completed, and it has not

been confirmed that successor personnel have assumed responsibility for financial reporting and are appropriately performing

their responsibilities. Accordingly, the possible effects of any undetected misstatements on the consolidated financial statements

would not be confined to specific line items or disclosures and could be material and pervasive. Such effects, if any, have not been

reflected in the consolidated financial statements.

For details, please refer to " Status of the Company’s Remediation Measures Relating to Corporate Governance" in the Notes to

Condensed Quarterly Consolidated Financial Statements.

As described in Note 7, Additional Information, “Improper Accounting Practices and Other Related Matters,” to the condensed

quarterly consolidated financial statements, the Company established the independent Third-Party Committee in accordance with the

“Guidelines for Third-Party Committees in Cases of Corporate Misconduct” established by the Japan Federation of Bar Associations

on September 3, 2025, and received the investigation report from the Third-Party Committee on February 27, 2026, and the

investigation report (supplement) as its final report on April 17 of the same year. In addition, the Company has conducted internal

investigations regarding potential quality issues and issues related to trade transactions and tariffs. In these reports, a large number of

instances of improper accounting practices through misconduct and errors were pointed out at various operating bases in the

Company and the group companies. In light of the final report from the Third-Party Committee, including the results of the self-

inspection, the Company decided that it was necessary to retroactively correct misstatements associated with the improper accounting

practices and other practices, including corrections of errors related to matters that were not corrected from the viewpoint of

materiality in previous years and matters that were additionally identified in the course of the investigation.

1. Overview of Business Environment for the Nine Months Ended December 31, 2025

During

the nine months ended December 31,2025, the global economy remained resilient overall and gradually recovered by

various national policies. On the other hand, outlook remained uncertain due to factors such as the impact of U.S trade and tariff

policies, highlighted geopolitical risks in the Middle East, and fluctuations in financial markets.

In this economic situation, the initiatives by product group are as follows:

First, in the Small precision motors, we are working to develop and expand net sales of products that respond to the evolution

required to support the explosive growth of the market in our product groups for AI data centers that support the AI society, including

water-cooling modules and near-line HDDs. In addition to these efforts, we are also working to further boost profitability by

reviewing unprofitable models.

Next, in the Automotive products, we will continue to provide the market with mobility innovations that contribute to the

promotion of electrification and automation of mobility through a wide variety of products and related modules. At the same time, we

are working to improve profitability by thoroughly reviewing orders for unprofitable models and reducing fixed costs. Also, with the

integration of the Automotive organic (existing business) into the Appliance Commercial Industrial Motors Business Unit (ACIM),

Nidec Mobility and Nidec Elesys merged their operations, effective April 1, 2025. We will integrate the resources of both companies

and accelerate the restructuring of a system that can reorganize business.

In the Appliance, commercial and industrial products, demand continues to grow for backup power generators for data centers,

Battery energy storage systems (BESS) accelerated by green innovation. To meet these strong demands, NIDEC is actively investing

in enhancing production capacity in India, France, and North and Middle America, and strengthening its recurring business related to

maintenance and inspections downstream of the value chain.

Regarding the Machinery, we are steadily receiving orders amid a global trend toward capital investment aimed at "labor-saving

and unmanned operations" and "higher speed and precision." We aim to become a leader in global manufacturing by steadily

capturing demand for capital investment while creating synergies between the companies we have acquired through M&A in recent

years and our group companies.

Our company and our group will continue to leverage our advanced technological capabilities and manufacturing expertise to

pursue ongoing high profitability as a leading company contributing to the realization of a sustainable, circular society.

(4) Designation as a Security on Special Alert

NIDEC CORPORATION (hereinafter referred to as "the Company") received notification from the Tokyo Stock Exchange, Inc. on

October 27, 2025, that its shares would be designated as a Security on special alert starting October 28, 2025, as detailed below.

(i) Reasons for Designation as a Security on Special Alert

The Company has received the following observations from the Tokyo Stock Exchange, Inc.:

The Company announced on Jun. 27, 2025 that it would extend the submission deadline for the annual securities report for the

fiscal year ended Mar. 2025 to Sep. 26 of the same year. This extension was made to allow time for an investigation into issues related

to international trade at its Italian subsidiary. Later, on Sep. 3, the Company further disclosed that it had decided to establish the

Third-Party Committee to investigate newly discovered concerns about inappropriate accounting practices at its Chinese subsidiary

involving a lump-sum payment representing a discount for a purchase from a supplier, as well as concerns that, with the involvement

or awareness of their respective management teams, the Company and its Group companies may have arbitrarily decided the timing

for writing down the value of certain assets.

Subsequently, on Sep. 26, the Company issued an "Important Notice Regarding Annual Securities Reports, etc." stating that the

investigation by the Third-Party Committee was still ongoing, and that it had submitted the annual securities report without the results

of the investigation being reflected in the consolidated financial statements or other included information. The notice also stated that

there had been a material weakness in internal control over these matters. The annual securities report submitted by the Company

included an audit report containing a disclaimer of opinion.

These disclosures, submissions, and other information revealed the following facts:

- Despite extending the submission deadline for the annual securities report by approximately three months, the Company

submitted the report with an audit report that included a "disclaimer of opinion." This suggests that the Company has still not

been able to disclose accurate financial results, and there remains a possibility that past fiscal year accounts may need to be

corrected.

- Since the initial issue was discovered, the scope of the investigation has continued to expand. Even though a considerable

amount of time has passed, the completion date of the Third-Party Committee’s investigation remains uncertain. As a result, the

Company has not been able to provide investors with a clear outlook on when its financial reporting schedule will return to

normal.

- Although the Third-Party Committee’s investigation is still ongoing, deficiencies have already been identified in the Company’s

company-wide internal control systems (particularly in areas related to information and communication), as well as in the internal

controls related to its accounting and financial closing processes. In light of these findings and considering the likelihood that they

could have a significant impact on the Company’s financial reporting, the Company has determined that these deficiencies

constitute material weaknesses that should be disclosed.

As described above, the audit report attached to the financial statements in the annual securities report, which forms the basis for

appropriate investment decisions, contains a disclaimer of opinion, and TSE deems that improvements to the Company’s internal

management system, etc. are highly necessary. As such, TSE designates the stock as a Security on Special Alert.

Furthermore, the investigation by the Company's Third-Party Committee is still ongoing, and its results may reveal new facts or

lead to corrections of past fiscal years’ financial statements. In view of this, Japan Exchange Regulation (JPX-R) will continue its

examination of the Company regarding its disclosure of corporate information and enforcement measures. Should any new issues

come to light, TSE may consider taking additional measures as necessary.

(ⅲ) Period of Designation as a Security on Special Alert

The designation period will generally be one year from October 28, 2025. After one year, the Company will

control system. If no issues are found with the internal control system, the designation will be lifted. However, if

issues are found, the Company will, in principle, be delisted.

appropriately operated (limited to cases where there is an expectation of appropriate operation), the designation as a Security on

system by the review following the end of the business year in which the continuation of the designation was decided (or the next

business year if the period from the decision date to the end of the business year is less than three months). If the internal control

system is deemed to be appropriately established and operated, the designation will be lifted. If the internal control system is not

deemed to be appropriately established or if there is no longer an expectation of appropriate operation, the Company will be delisted.

following the end of the business year in which the continuation of the designation was decided (or the next business year if the

period from the decision date to the end of the business year is less than three months), during which time the review will be

（Status of the Company’s Remediation Measures Relating to Corporate Governance）

As described in the Condensed Quarterly Consolidated Financial Statements Note 7, Additional Information, “Improper Accounting

Practices and Other Related Matters,” the Company established a Third-Party Committee on September 3, 2025, received the

investigation report from the Third-Party Committee on February 27, 2026, and received the investigation report (supplement) as its

final report on April 17, 2026. The investigation reports describe numerous instances of improper accounting practices carried out at

various operating bases within the Company and its group companies with the involvement or knowledge of management, including

premature revenue recognition, understatement of provisions, overstatement of inventories, avoidance of impairment losses on fixed

assets, and capitalization of expenses. The reports further identified instances in which management or accounting personnel of the

Company and its group companies provided inaccurate or misleading information to the independent auditor, concealed relevant

documents, or provided altered records in order to obtain the independent auditor’s concurrence. The improper accounting practices

identified by the Third-Party Committee have been corrected in the condensed quarterly consolidated financial statements.

However, the Company has identified certain officers and employees of the Company and its group companies who participated in,

directed, or had knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, and

is evaluating and taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures; however, some of those actions have not been

completed. As a result, certain officers and employees who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper accounting

practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, continue to hold positions of responsibility within the

financial reporting process of the Company and its group companies.

The matters identified in the Third-Party Committee’s reports indicate that material weaknesses existed in the control environment

over an extended period and raise substantial concerns regarding whether the Company’s governance and control environment

relating to financial reporting is operating effectively, including whether the risk of management override of controls has been

sufficiently mitigated. This condition also affects the reliability of records, documents and information submitted to the independent

auditor by, or prepared, responded to, or reviewed by, individuals involved in the improper accounting practices, as well as the

reliability of management representations provided to the independent auditor.

Although the Company is evaluating and taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures for individuals

involved in the improper accounting practices, some of those remedial personnel actions have not been completed, and it has not been

confirmed that successor personnel have assumed responsibility for financial reporting and are appropriately performing their

responsibilities. Accordingly, the possible effects of any undetected misstatements on the consolidated financial statements would not

be confined to specific line items or disclosures and could be material and pervasive. Such effects, if any, have not been reflected in

the condensed quarterly consolidated financial statements.

Third-Party Committees in Cases of Corporate Misconduct” established by the Japan Federation of Bar Associations. The Company

commissioned the Third-Party Committee: fact-finding investigations into suspected improper accounting practices; calculation of the

amount of impact if improper accounting practices is found; root cause investigations and recommendation of recurrence prevention

measures if improper accounting practices is found; and other matters deemed necessary by the Third-Party Committee.)

In addition, while ensuring a mechanism that an external expert confirms the details of each procedure to ensure objectivity under

the management and supervision of the Third-Party Committee, the Company conducted a self-inspection to investigate the

appropriateness of the accounting treatment from the fiscal year ended March 31, 2020 to the first quarter of fiscal year ended March

31, 2026 for 288 companies, including the Company and group companies, excluding certain bases not subject to performance

management by the Company, such as regional headquarters, dormant companies, companies in liquidation, and shared services. For

this inspection, the Company created a procedure checklist that considers types of improper accounting practices, such as premature

revenue recognition, understatement of provisions, overstatement of inventories, and avoidance of fixed asset impairment losses and

capitalization of expenses, which were identified in the Third-Party Committee’s investigation, issues of improper accounting

practices generally assumed, and improper accounting practices based on methods used in past cases of accounting fraud or improper

accounting practices in the Nidec Group. Based on this checklist, personnel in charge at each base conducted an inspection, which

was cross-checked by personnel in charge of other bases, and confirmed by an external expert. The inspection took a conservative

approach and covered a period of one year earlier than that of the Third-Party Committee investigation (from the fiscal year ended

March 31, 2021 to the first quarter of fiscal year ended March 31, 2026). In addition, the Company corrected misstatements in the

consolidated financial statements and other disclosures for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026.

Corporate Governance

As described in Note 7, Additional Information, “Improper Accounting Practices and Other Related Matters,” to the condensed

quarterly consolidated financial statements, the Company established a Third-Party Committee on September 3, 2025, received the

investigation report from the Third-Party Committee on February 27, 2026, and received the investigation report (supplement) as its

final report on April 17, 2026. The investigation reports describe numerous instances of improper accounting practices carried out at

various operating bases within the Company and its group companies with the involvement or knowledge of management, including

premature revenue recognition, understatement of provisions, overstatement of inventories, avoidance of impairment losses on fixed

assets, and capitalization of expenses. The reports further identified instances in which management or accounting personnel of the

Company and its group companies provided inaccurate or misleading information to the independent auditor, concealed relevant

documents, or provided altered records in order to obtain the independent auditor’s concurrence. The improper accounting practices

identified by the Third-Party Committee have been corrected in the condensed quarterly consolidated financial statements.

However, as described in the note “Status of the Company’s Remediation Measures Relating to Corporate Governance,” the Company

has identified certain officers and employees of the Company and its group companies who participated in, directed, or had

knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who provided false explanations to the independent auditor, and is evaluating and

taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures; however, some of those actions have not been completed. As a

result, certain officers and employees who participated in, directed, or had knowledge of the improper accounting practices, or who

provided false explanations to the independent auditor, continue to hold positions of responsibility within the financial reporting

process of the Company and its group companies.

The matters identified in the Third-Party Committee’s reports indicate that material weaknesses existed in the control environment

over an extended period and raise substantial concerns regarding whether the Company’s governance and control environment

relating to financial reporting is operating effectively, including whether the risk of management override of controls has been

sufficiently mitigated. This condition also affects the reliability of records, documents and information submitted to the independent

auditor by, or prepared, responded to, or reviewed by, individuals involved in the improper accounting practices, as well as the

reliability of management representations provided to the independent auditor.

During the course of our interim review, we concluded that information obtained from officers and employees involved in these

improper accounting practices had a significant effect on the reliability of management representations as evidence and on the

persuasiveness of the evidence as a whole. Because some remedial personnel actions had not been completed as of the date the

condensed quarterly consolidated financial statements were prepared and individuals involved in the improper accounting practices

continue to participate in the financial reporting process, we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence as to whether the

improper accounting practices corrected or determined by the Company to require correction were complete, or whether undetected

misstatements arising from the risk of management override existed as a result of those individuals’ involvement in the financial

reporting process and, if so, the nature, extent and effects thereof.

Accordingly, as described in the note “Status of the Company’s Remediation Measures Relating to Corporate Governance,” although

the Company is evaluating and taking remedial personnel actions to determine appropriate measures for individuals involved in the

improper accounting practices, some of those remedial personnel actions have not been completed, and it has not been sufficiently

consolidated financial statements would not be confined to specific line items or disclosures and could be material and pervasive. As a

result, we were unable to determine whether any adjustments to the condensed quarterly consolidated financial statements were

Impairment

Under IAS 36, Impairment of Assets, an impairment loss is required to be recognized for non-financial assets presented in the

condensed quarterly consolidated statement of financial position when the recoverable amount falls below the carrying amount.

The Company groups assets based on the smallest identifiable group of assets that generates cash inflows that are largely independent

(a cash-generating unit). Goodwill acquired in a business combination is allocated to cash-generating units or groups of cash-

generating units that are expected to benefit from the synergies of the combination, and impairment losses are measured based on

estimated future cash flows and other assumptions. As a result, the Company recognized impairment losses during the nine-month

period. However, as of the date of this report, we have not obtained sufficient appropriate evidence regarding the reasonableness of

the accounting estimates relating to the amount and period allocation of impairment losses for certain cash-generating units or groups

of cash-generating units.

IAS 36, Impairment of Assets, requires future cash flow projections to be based on reasonable and supportable assumptions.

However, as described under “Corporate Governance,” information obtained from individuals who may have been involved in the

improper accounting practices has a significant effect on the persuasiveness of evidence relating to impairment of assets submitted to

us and on the reliability of management representations.

In addition, the Company has restated the comparative information because the opening balances of non-financial assets of certain

cash-generating units or groups of cash-generating units had been overstated in light of the improper accounting practices. Under IAS

8, Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors, a prior-period restatement must be based on information that

was available when the original consolidated financial statements were authorized for issue, without using facts or outcomes

identified subsequently. Specifically, revised cash flow projections and significant assumptions included in the underlying business

plans are required to reflect information available at the relevant dates and not information or assumptions developed subsequently.

Because we did not obtain sufficient necessary explanations from the Company as to whether prior-period corrections were based on

information available at the relevant dates without using hindsight, we were unable to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence

supporting that the restatement had been measured based on information available at those dates.

Accordingly, we were unable to determine whether the impairment losses recognized by the Company complied with the

requirements of IAS 36 and IAS 8.
