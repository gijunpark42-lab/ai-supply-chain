Accton Technology Honored with “Bio-Power 30×30 Biodiversity Action Award”

ESG Spotlight / Environmental 🌄 / ESG Office, Accton E-Charity / May 29th, 2026

Accton Technology has been honored with the “Bio-Power 30×30 Biodiversity Action Award” in May 2026, recognition centered around its core initiative, “Connecting People and Nature through Urban Co-learning.” In response to the growing disconnection between people and the environment caused by urbanization,Accton believes that the genesis of connection lies not in remote wilderness, but within everyone’s daily life.

With this philosophy as our launchpad, we have deployed a dual-track strategy: Internally, we awaken employees’ environmental mindfulness through diverse environmental education, daily life observations, and hands-on practices. Externally, we forge cross-sector collaborations with local communities, professional teams, and public authorities. By maximizing resource sharing and encouraging localized actions by our staff, we amplify the educational impact of biodiversity, ultimately fostering a profound, inside-out connection between humanity and nature.

Internal Empowerment: Awakening Daily Observation of Nature

The first step to understanding nature begins on the daily commute. The “Accton Little Forest series” utilizes the existing ecological environment around our office buildings, combining citizen science project designs to guide employees in observing nature within their daily lives. We organized the “iNaturalist Nature Treasure Hunt” competition, leveraging digital species-identification tools and competitive mechanics to further stimulate interest in exploring the surrounding environment. Additionally, the “Accton Nature Photography Contest” was held to capture the vibrant greenery through lenses, revealing through our colleagues’ unique perspectives, how natural ecosystems have quietly woven themselves into the daily workplace.

To deepen awareness of urban ecology, we turned our gaze toward the bats in our skies. We led employees on field surveys across five distinct locations in the greater Hsinchu area to track urban bat populations. Furthermore, we partnered with the Feng He Zi Association to design three sessions of the “Dōnghǎikū Bat Studies: Learn by Doing” series workshop at Dōnghǎikū in Zhúběi. This initiative intertwined bat tracking and ecological knowledge with local Hakka traditional craftsmanship, enabling employees to comprehend the symbiosis between species and cultural heritage while rebuilding an emotional bond with the land.

Moreover, we extended our care to insects—the often-overlooked creatures in urban corners. Accton first invited the Bee and WASP Conservation Association of Taiwan to conduct professional lectures and a “Solitary Bee Box DIY Workshop.” This initiative built foundational knowledge among employees regarding the critical role solitary bees play in ecological balance and the agricultural economy, culminating in the hands-on creation of three solitary bee hotels. We also collaborated with the Urban Ark Studio to offer the “Discovering the Tiny City Creatures” lecture, consistently fostering accurate perceptions of insects and solitary bees to overturn conventional stereotypes. Through this sequence of classroom learning and hands-on practice, our workforce has deepened their capacity to practice biodiversity conservation step-by-step.

Partner Synergies: Sowing Seeds of Ecological Education

Beyond equipping employees with nature-observation skills and practical experience internally, Accton simultaneously connects with local communities and public sector partners. By utilizing diverse media to convey sustainability concepts, we aim to raise environmental awareness among the general public.

Jiǔgǎng Island | Community Co-prosperity and Resource Circulation

Our deep engagement with Jiǔgǎng Island began in 2024 through a collaboration between Accton E-Charity and the “Island Port Nest” team of Hsinchu Community College. Through cultural walking tours of Jiǔgǎng Island and book donations to the Jiǔgǎng Bookstore, we guided employees to understand the ecological challenges facing the Tóuqián River; concurrently, we supported the “Tóuqián River Arts Festival” and the “Windward Series” soundscape art creations, leveraging music and local soundscapes to build emotional bonds between our staff and the community.

In 2025, Accton E-Charity further collaborated with the “Island Port Nest” team of Hsinchu Community College to launch the “Jiǔgǎng Island Food Forest Project”. Abandoned wooden pallets and green-belt fallen leaves from our Hsinchu branch were transported to the island to construct composting areas. By transforming corporate materials into vital nutrients, we collectively built a food forest that safeguards biodiversity; employees also took part in co-planting activities to practice sustainable actions firsthand. To amplify the educational benefits, the “Island Port Nest” team designed environmental education curricula, extending this site to nearby local primary and secondary schools for field trips, thereby turning this urban corner of biodiversity into a tangible, accessible environmental textbook for the public.

Forestry and Nature Conservation Agency | Supporting Watershed Bat Ecological Surveys

Aligning with the Taiwan Ecological Network, Accton launched the “Bats of Hsinchu, Harmonious Coexistence of the Urban Dwellers and Nature” project (hereafter referred to as “Bats of Hsinchu” project), becoming one of the first enterprises to respond to the FANR ESG Matchmaking Platform. In addition to leveraging environmental education for internal empowerment, we supported the Bat Association of Taiwan in conducting bat biodiversity surveys along the Fèngshān River and Tóuqián River. The survey results provide a foundational basis for the Hsinchu District Office’s future planning. Furthermore, this project was featured as a case study at the Hsinchu District Office’s “Northwestern Taiwan Ecological Green Network Conservation Strategy Implementation and Cross-Sector Expansion Program” Presentation, expanding our external impact.

Taroko National Park | Connecting Post-Disaster Ecological Education

Solitary bees are closely linked to human life yet are easily overlooked. Being non-aggressive and highly efficient pollinators, they serve as an ideal starting point for post-earthquake ecological recovery and education. Accton E-Charity supported the educational outreach program of Taroko National Park by donating 13 solitary bee hotels. The Taroko National Park Headquarters installed these hotels at the Taroko Cultural and Creative Industry Park and integrated them with the “Delivery to Schools” initiative, enabling citizens and students to observe ecological interactions from an up-close perspective.

Future Outlook: Moving Toward Symbiosis Between Humanity and Nature

Through our dual-track strategy of “Internal Training” and “External Collaboration,” Accton Technology has transformed the issue of biodiversity into a concrete journey of co-learning. Moving from initial daily-life observations and resource circulation practices to ultimate social inclusion, we have fulfilled our original vision: “Understanding nature does not require traveling to remote lands; the city itself is the starting point.” This approach allows ecological care to seamlessly integrate into the daily lives of both our colleagues and the community.

Receiving this award is an encouraging beginning of recognition. Looking ahead, we will continue to play a pivotal role in connection and advocacy. We aim to deepen multi-party exchanges with experts, local communities, and professional institutions. Through cross-border dialogues, we will convert ecological care into robust environmental education capacity, firmly moving forward with our partners toward a sustainable future where humanity and nature coexist in harmony.

Achievements

Accton Friendly Forest

A total of 157 participants joined the daily observation initiative.

“iNaturalist Nature Treasure Hunt” Competition: Successfully recorded 204 research-grade species.

“Accton Nature Photography Contest”: Collected 81 submissions capturing natural records in daily life.

Solitary Bee Initiatives

Hands-on Practice: 3 solitary bee hotels were handcrafted by employees during internal workshops.

Support for Taroko National Park Educational Outreach

A total of 13 solitary bee hotels were donated (including 10 designed by Urban Ark Studio and 3 handcrafted by employees).

Campus & Public Advocacy: Taroko National Park Headquarters organized lectures across 5 local schools, reaching 147 attendees; the remaining 5 hotels were installed at the Cultural and Creative Industries Park.

Volunteer Interpreter Training: Taroko National Park Headquarters partnered with the Society of Wilderness to complete professional training for 120 volunteer interpreters.

“Bats of Hsinchu” Abbreviated Project

Support for Bat Ecological Surveys: Actively responded to the FANR initiative, supporting the Bat Association of Taiwan in conducting bat ecological surveys along the Fèngshān River and Tóuqián River.

Urban Field Surveys: Led employees on field trips across 5 distinct sites in the greater Hsinchu area to observe urban bats.

Thematic Event Participation: Bat-themed activities attracted a cumulative total of 187 participants.

Jiǔgǎng Island Project

Resource Circulation Benefits: Reduced incineration of waste tree branches and leaves by 5.755 metric tons between June 2025 and March 2026.

Food Forest Establishment: Constructed 1 food forest and planted 50 plant species, which included 13 Taiwanese endemic and endangered species.

Environmental Education Promotion Hub: Partnered with Hsinchu Community College to conduct 3 promotional events and designed tailored environmental education curricula for Nan Hua Junior High School, Ching Tsao Hu Elementary School, and Xin Gang Elementary School.
