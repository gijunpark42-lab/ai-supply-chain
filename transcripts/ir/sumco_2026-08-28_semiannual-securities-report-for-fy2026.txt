2. Management analysis of financial position, business results, and cash flow

Forward-looking statements are based on the Group’s estim ate as of the end of the first half consolidated

accounting term.

(1) Financial Position  and Business Results

(i) Business results

During the first half of fiscal year 2026 (January 1, 2026–June 30, 2026), the semiconductor market

grew on a volume basis, driven by AI-related demand and including products for industrial use, for which

recovery had lagged. Strong growth was seen also on a monetary value basis, helped by rising memory

prices.

In the market for 300 mm silicon wafers, shipments increased, driven by strong demand for leading-

edge logic and memory used in AI data centers, despite continued customer inventory adjustments.

Shipments of 200 mm and smaller wafers had remained sluggish due to weak demand for end products,

but recent shipments have grown, with trends varying by customer and product.

In this environment, the SUMCO Group focused on maintaining its high market share of leading-edge

products, carrying on with technology development aimed at meeting customer needs for higher precision

and producing distinctive products. We further made efforts to boost our cost competitiveness, including

by raising productivity with the use of AI.

We have been restructuring our production of 200 mm and smaller wafers, continuing our efforts to

enhance efficiency and profitability.

As a result, for the first half of fiscal year 2026 (January 1, 2026–June 30, 2026), the Group registered

consolidated net sales of ¥215,016 million (up 4.7% year on year), operating loss of ¥6,363 million

(operating profit of ¥7,457 million for the first half of the previous fiscal year), ordinary loss of ¥12,179

million (ordinary profit of ¥4,720 million for the first half of the previous fiscal year), and loss attributable

to owners of parent of ¥12,895 million (profit attributable to owners of parent of ¥3,081 million for the

first half of the previous fiscal year).

Segment information is not described since the Group has only one segment.

(ii) Financial position

At the end of the first half of fi scal year 2026 (June 30, 2026), total assets were ¥1,136,685 million (up

¥8,718 million from the end of fiscal year 2025), total liabilities were ¥495,618 million (up ¥15,436 million

from the end of fiscal year 2025), and total net assets came to ¥641,067 million (down ¥6,718 million from

the end of fiscal year 2025).

While property, plant and equipment decreas ed by ¥39,681 million, the main factors behind the asset

increase were increases of ¥22,469 million in ca sh and deposits, ¥14,000 million in securities, ¥8,087

million in raw materials and supplies, and ¥4,571 mi llion in notes and accounts receivable - trade, and

contract assets.

Although other current liabilities decreased by ¥3,495 million, the main factors behind the increase in

liabilities were an increase of ¥8,636 million in long-term borrowings, an increase of ¥6,645 million in

notes and accounts payable - trade, and an increase of ¥5,050 million in short-term borrowings.

While non-controlling interests increased by ¥3,504 mi llion, foreign currency translation adjustment

increased by ¥2,961 million, and cap ital surplus increased by ¥2,309 million, the main factor behind the

decrease in net assets was a ¥16,397 million decrease  in retained earnings due to loss attributable to

(Trust for the delivery of shares to Officers and employees)

The Company has introduced a performance-based stoc k compensation plan for the Company’s Directors,

Presidents of major domestic subsidiaries, and the Company’s Executive Officers to motivate them to improve the

Company’s performance and increase its corporate value over the medium to long term.

The accounting for such trust agreement is in accordan ce with the “Practical Solu tion on Transactions of

Delivering the Company’s Own Stock to Employees etc. through Trusts” (ASBJ Practical Solution No. 30, March

26, 2015).

(1) Overview of the transaction

The plan is a stock-based compensation system whereby a trust created by funds contributed by the Company

(hereinafter, the “Trust”) acquires the Company’s shares, and the Company’s shares in the number equivalent

to the number of points the Company grants to each Director or other officer are delivered to them through the

Trust.

In principle, each Director or other officer receives the Company’s shares at the time they resign from their

position as the Company’s Director or other officer.

(2) Shares of the Company remaining in the trust

The Company's shares remaining in the trust are record ed as treasury shares under net assets at their book

value in the trust. At the end of fiscal year 2025, the number of such treasury shares was 454,600, and the book

value was ¥894 million. At the end of the first half of fiscal year 2026, the number of such treasury shares was

434,700, and the book value was ¥855 million.

Sale of a portion of the shares of the subsidiary to non-controlling shareholders not resulting in a change in

the scope of consolidation

(4) Overview of the transaction including the purpose of the transaction

FST is currently a consolidated subsidiary of the Company and plays an important role as a production and

sales base for 300 mm and 200 mm wafers in Taiwan. FST is a listed company and a joint venture with

Formosa Plastics Group (hereinafter referred to as “FPG”) in Taiwan, with the Company and FPG currently

participating as equal partners. The Company has been studying the most appropriate form of this capital

relationship, and as a result, has decided to reduce its voting rights ratio by selling a portion of the FST shares

held by the Company Group, with the aim of reclassifying FST from a consolidated subsidiary to an equity-

method affiliate.
