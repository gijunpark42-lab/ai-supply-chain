Beyond 102.4T: Preparing Data Center Networks for the Era of AI Factories

By Raul Lozano-Dominguez • Oct 2026 • ● Live Update

The networking industry is entering the era of 102.4-terabit switching, representing another remarkable step in the evolution of data center infrastructure.

Yet history teaches us something important: every networking breakthrough quickly becomes the new baseline.

AI workloads are expanding at a pace rarely seen in computing history. Model sizes continue to grow, training datasets increase exponentially, and accelerator clusters now scale into tens of thousands of GPUs. Every generation of AI compute creates even greater demand for communication between devices.

From Moving Data to Creating Intelligence

For decades, data centers served as the digital backbone of modern business, storing information, hosting applications, and connecting people, systems, and services around the world. Networking’s role was well understood: move data reliably, securely, and efficiently from one point to another. Success was measured by bandwidth, availability, and the ability to support ever-growing volumes of digital traffic.

Today, Artificial Intelligence has fundamentally expanded the role of infrastructure.

AI Factories have become the operational foundation for creating, refining, and distributing intelligence at unprecedented scale. Their output is no longer limited to applications and transactions, but increasingly includes insights, recommendations, automation, reasoning, and expertise that accelerate innovation across every industry. In this environment, networking is no longer a supporting utility. It is an integral component of the production system that determines how effectively intelligence can be generated, shared, and transformed into business and societal value.

The Four Dimensions of AI Infrastructure Scale

Modern AI infrastructure operates simultaneously across multiple dimensions of scale.

Scale-up architectures enable tightly coupled accelerators to function as a unified computational engine for model training and reasoning workloads.

Scale-out fabrics connect massive accelerator clusters that span thousands of processors working together on increasingly sophisticated AI models.

Scale-in architectures integrate compute, storage, memory, networking, and specialized resources into highly optimized workflows.

Scale-across deployments extend AI production across multiple data halls, campuses, and geographic regions, enabling infrastructure to operate as a globally distributed intelligence platform.

Together, these dimensions define the architecture of today’s AI Factories and establish the foundation upon which future generations of AI systems will operate.

The New Measure of Success: Intelligence Productivity

As AI infrastructure matures, success is no longer measured solely by peak performance. The objective is maximizing the productive output of increasingly valuable compute resources.

Accelerator utilization has become one of the most important indicators of AI Factory effectiveness. Every percentage point of idle compute represents unrealized intelligence production capacity. The role of networking is no longer simply to move packets efficiently, but to ensure that accelerators, storage systems, and applications operate as a coordinated whole.

The question is no longer:

How fast is the network?

The question increasingly becomes:

How effectively does the infrastructure convert compute capacity into low-latency intelligence that compliments and empowers human decision-making AI agents and automation?

When Physics Shapes Architecture

As AI Factories continue to scale, the constraints of physics have become architectural considerations.

Distance now influences performance as much as bandwidth. The placement of accelerators, switches, storage systems, and interconnect technologies directly impacts latency, synchronization efficiency, and workload performance. What were once facility-level considerations have become critical design parameters.

As AI Factories expand across buildings, campuses, and regions, architects must optimize not only bandwidth and resiliency, but also the impact of physical distance on AI training, inference, agent orchestration, and distributed reasoning workloads. Designing modern AI infrastructure increasingly requires optimization across both digital and physical domains.

The AI Factory as a Unified System

The evolution of AI infrastructure has reinforced a fundamental lesson: optimizing individual components no longer guarantees optimal system performance.

The AI Factory functions as a warehouse-scale computer in which silicon, networking, optics, power delivery, cooling systems, software platforms, and operational intelligence work together as a unified system. Improvements in one layer increasingly depend upon innovations across multiple others. As organizations pursue greater scale, efficiency, and sustainability, success can no longer be achieved by advancing compute, networking, or cooling independently. It requires continuously improving the performance and efficiency of the entire intelligence-production platform.

Engineering Beyond the Next Data Rate

Supporting future AI growth requires far more than adopting faster technologies. Every increase in bandwidth introduces new physical challenges and tighter engineering margins. As the industry advances toward higher-speed Ethernet, 800G and 1.6T ecosystems, and emerging Near-Packaged Optics (NPO) and Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) architectures, innovation becomes increasingly dependent on the ability to manage complex interactions among signal integrity, power integrity, thermal behavior, optical performance, mechanical architecture, and manufacturability.

Effects that were once secondary considerations become primary design constraints. Signal loss, jitter, power delivery noise, thermal coupling, optical efficiency, and packaging technologies must all be optimized together to unlock the full potential of future AI infrastructure. Delivering predictable performance therefore requires rigorous engineering and validation across process, voltage, and temperature (PVT) variability, ensuring that systems perform consistently not only in controlled environments, but also across large-volume production and real-world deployments.

Reliability Is Throughput

At AI Factory scale, reliability directly influences productivity. Individual component failures can affect the performance of thousands of interconnected accelerators, reducing utilization and increasing operational costs. Seemingly minor issues in signal quality, thermal behavior, optical connectivity, power delivery, or mechanical integrity can have cascading effects throughout highly interconnected systems.

For this reason, reliability has become a strategic objective rather than a maintenance metric. The most effective AI Factories are designed not only for performance, but for sustained operation throughout the lifecycle of the infrastructure.

At Accton, this begins with a holistic engineering approach spanning system architecture, electrical innovation, signal integrity, power integrity, thermal innovation, CPO/NPO and optical innovation, mechanical engineering, manufacturing excellence, and lifecycle validation. Whether enabling high-density copper platforms, advanced optical fabrics, next-generation air-cooled systems, or liquid-cooled AI infrastructure, every aspect of the solution is engineered to deliver predictable performance under the demanding conditions of hyperscale operation. Equally important, that performance must remain dependable throughout the operational life of the infrastructure. Achieving this level of reliability requires anticipating the effects of component aging, environmental variation, workload evolution, and continuous operation from the earliest stages of development. Ultimately, engineering excellence is measured not only by peak performance, but by the ability to deliver trusted, consistent operation throughout the product lifecycle.

The Network as an Engine of Intelligence and Innovation

As AI Factories continue to evolve, networking is assuming a new role within the technology stack. No longer defined solely by connectivity, it is becoming a foundational enabler of intelligence creation. Future AI Factories will dynamically support training, fine-tuning, inference, reasoning, simulation, agentic AI workflows, and new categories of workloads yet to emerge. Their success will depend upon infrastructure capable of adapting to changing demands while maintaining efficiency, reliability, sustainability, and scale.

In this environment, networking becomes more than infrastructure. It becomes the connective fabric that transforms information into intelligence, intelligence into expertise, and expertise into innovation. The organizations that recognize this shift will be best positioned to lead the next generation of the AI economy.

From Information Age to Knowledge Age

Throughout history, economic progress has been driven by humanity’s ability to amplify physical labor and, more recently, information processing. AI introduces a new possibility: amplifying expertise.

Now physicians have instant access to the collective knowledge of medical science. Engineers can explore thousands of design alternatives in minutes. Teachers may be supported by personalized learning systems for every student based on their needs. Technicians can diagnose complex systems with guidance drawn from millions of similar cases. The ultimate purpose of AI Factories is not to replace human intelligence, but to multiply it.

In many ways, this vision echoes the philosophy of W. Edwards Deming. Deming believed that the purpose of a system was to continually improve its ability to create value for everyone it serves. He envisioned a world in which knowledge, learning, and continuous improvement were available to all, rather than concentrated in a privileged few. AI is has now allowed us in an impressive way to move toward that vision.

The better we become at producing, refining, and distributing intelligence, the more people can gain access to profound knowledge in their chosen field, regardless of geography, organization size, or economic circumstance. The AI Factory is the infrastructure that makes this possible. Let’s enable it with the utmost priority and concern for our environment. This is as important as the design within the AI factory.

Technologies Shaping the Future Beyond 102.4T

Intelligent Ethernet Fabrics

Ethernet has become the foundation of modern AI networking because of its openness, innovation velocity, and broad ecosystem support. The next evolution will incorporate increasingly intelligent capabilities:

Advanced congestion management

Real-time telemetry

Predictive optimization

AI-assisted traffic engineering

Future networks will not simply transport data. They will actively optimize the movement of intelligence.

Optical Innovation

As network speeds continue to increase, optics become central to future scaling. 800G deployments are already expanding rapidly, while the industry looks toward 1.6T, 3.2T and beyond. The latest technologies such as:

Silicon photonics

Co-packaged optics

Advanced optical interconnects

Are playing an increasingly important role in enabling the next generation of AI infrastructure. Future scaling is as much an optics challenge as it is a networking challenge.

Autonomous Operations

Human operators cannot manually manage infrastructure at AI Factory scale. The future network will increasingly operate through:

Predictive analytics

Automated remediation

Intent-based management

Self-optimizing operations

The networks supporting AI will themselves become increasingly AI-driven. Tomorrow’s networks will use AI to support AI.

Open Infrastructure

AI innovation cycles move far faster than traditional infrastructure refresh cycles. Organizations need the ability to adopt new technologies without rebuilding entire platforms. Open infrastructure provides:

Flexibility

Faster innovation

Reduced vendor dependency

Greater ecosystem collaboration

In an environment where change is constant, openness becomes a strategic advantage.

Sustainable Networking

The future of AI is inseparable from the future of energy. As AI deployments expand, sustainability becomes both an environmental imperative and a business necessity. This demands advances in:

Energy-efficient networking

Liquid cooling

Optical power optimization

Rack-level thermal design

Sustainable infrastructure is no longer a secondary consideration. It is becoming a fundamental design principle.

What the AI Factory of 2030 Might Look Like

The AI Factory of 2030 will likely bear little resemblance to today’s data centers. Intelligence production will occur across vast distributed infrastructures. Massive clusters will span multiple facilities. Optical fabrics will connect resources seamlessly across campuses.

Operations teams will increasingly manage systems through policies and objectives rather than device-by-device configurations. Most importantly, these factories will serve a purpose far greater than technology itself. Their mission will be to make expertise more accessible allowing new achievements thanks to the AI era.

Conclusion: Building the Infrastructure for the Knowledge Economy

The transition from traditional data centers to AI Factories represents far more than another technology upgrade cycle. It is a fundamental shift in how intelligence is created, scaled, and shared across society. As organizations move beyond 102.4T networking, success will no longer be defined solely by bandwidth, port density, or compute performance. It will be determined by the ability to build infrastructure that can efficiently support scale-up, scale-out, scale-in, and scale-across architectures while maintaining the reliability, efficiency, and operational simplicity demanded by hyperscale AI deployments.

Meeting these requirements calls for continuous innovation across every engineering discipline that underpins modern networking. With each new generation of bandwidth, engineers encounter new physical challenges in electrical performance, signal integrity, power delivery, thermal management, optics, and system design. Achieving consistent operation across process, voltage, and temperature variability at hyperscale production volumes requires more than advanced components. It requires rigorous engineering methodologies, uncompromising quality standards, and a deep understanding of how entire systems behave under real-world conditions.

At hyperscale, the measure of engineering excellence is not whether a platform performs flawlessly on the day it ships. It is whether that platform continues to deliver predictable performance years later, after billions of transactions, millions of operating hours, and continuous exposure in the real-world. At Accton we believe that the purpose of an AI system is to enable learning, improvement, and knowledge for all. Therefore, our objective is not merely low failure rates, but ensuring sustained operational excellence throughout the entire life span of our products.
