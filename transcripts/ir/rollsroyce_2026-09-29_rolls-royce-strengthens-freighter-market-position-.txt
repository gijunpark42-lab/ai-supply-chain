Rolls-Royce strengthens freighter market position by powering first A350F flight

share

29 September 2026

Rolls-Royce (LSE: RR., ADR: RYCEY) is proud to power the A350F, which has completed its first test flight with Trent XWB-97 engines.

Rolls-Royce (LSE: RR., ADR: RYCEY) is proud to power the A350F, which has completed its first test flight with Trent XWB-97 engines.

The Trent XWB-97 has proven its versatility over eight years of service and more than 4.5 million engine flying hours. Trusted by operators to power the longest and most demanding passenger operations in the world, the engine brings a track record of proven in-service performance to the large widebody freighter sector.

Adam Davies, Director Commercial Aviation, Trent XWB, Rolls-Royce, said:

Select Tabs

For nearly a decade, our largest engine, the Trent XWB-97, has powered some of the world’s most demanding routes. Its proven in-service performance gives freight operators opportunities for increased payload and network expansion, unlocking exciting possibilities offered by this new freighter.

“We are delighted to be part of the next generation of air freighters, and ready to support our customers’ aspirations in this area. Congratulations to Airbus and all partners on achieving this important step towards bringing the A350F into service.”

The Trent XWB-97 has received the first two of its three durability enhancement packages, enabled by Rolls-Royce’s transformation programme. The third phase, which will enter service in 2028, doubles time on wing in challenging environments and provides 50% improvement in benign conditions.

It is certified to operate on a 50% Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) blend today and has been proven to be compatible with 100% SAF for the future.

Rolls-Royce has a growing presence and relevance in the widebody freighter market. The trust in the proven Trent XWB-97 maturity and value for customers has helped secure over 100 A350F orders.

The A330 is a popular aircraft for freighter conversions, with Rolls-Royce capturing almost 90% of all A330ceo conversions over the life of the programme.

Over the next 20 years there, there is an expected demand for 1600 widebody freighter aircraft.

Ewen McDonald, Chief Customer Officer, Rolls-Royce, said:

Select Tabs

Rolls-Royce is proud to be at the forefront of the freighter market, a position underscored by this week’s milestone test flight of the new A350F. With over 100 A350F orders to date, our customers’ confidence in the Trent XWB-97 is clear. We also continue to be a trusted partner in the freighter conversion market, where our Trent 700 powers the majority of A330 passenger-to-freighter conversions worldwide.

“As demand for widebody freighters expands over the next two decades, Rolls-Royce is uniquely positioned to deliver industry-leading technology and service. We remain dedicated to supporting efficient and sustainable growth for cargo operators across the globe.”
