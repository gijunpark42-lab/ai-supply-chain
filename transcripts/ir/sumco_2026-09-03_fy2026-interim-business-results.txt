Interim

I remain deeply grateful to you, our shareholders, for your continued support of SUMCO.

During the first half of fiscal year 2026 (January 1–June 30, 2026), the silicon wafer market

saw a surge in shipments of 300 mm silicon wafers thanks to continuing strong demand for

wafers for leading-edge logic and memory chips used in AI data centers. Shipments of 200

mm and smaller wafers also began to recover, although the pace varied by customer and

product.

AI-related demand has become a significant growth driver. It now extends beyond

leading-edge logic and memory chips to embrace a wide range of semiconductors, such as

power control ICs. Committed to a policy of quality-driven growth, the SUMCO Group has

worked hard to strengthen its framework for consistently supplying advanced technical

features and superior quality. A new factory catering to demand for leading-edge wafers—a

greenfield investment approved in 2021—is steadily coming online, with full-scale operations

expected to begin by 2027. Coupled with upgrades to facilities at existing plants, this will

enhance our ability to cater to the market for leading-edge products.

Meanwhile, the uncertainty in our external environment continues to mount due to a

variety of factors, including exchange rates, commodity prices, climate change, and the

international situation. We will focus on running our business flexibly in response to change.

As a leading manufacturer of silicon wafers, our top priority is to remain a trusted partner in

the eyes of both customers and society at large.

For the fiscal year ending December 2026, we have set an interim dividend amount of 10

yen per share based on an overall consideration of factors such as profit levels in this interim

period, the outlook for the second half of the year and beyond, capital investment funding

needs, free cash flow, EBITDA, and the funds available to pay dividends. Going forward, we

will strive to remain flexible in providing our shareholders with appropriate returns, paying

close attention to the economic climate, industry trends, level of earnings, and funding

needs.

I look forward to your ongoing support and cooperation.

Jiro Ryuta,

Representative Director, President

SUMCO’s Goal: Steady, Quality-Driven Growth

I became representative director and president of SUMCO on

March 27, 2026. Since joining SUMCO, I have worked on the front

lines in many different capacities: in research and development, on

the factory floor, in sales, and running overseas subsidiaries. In the

process, I have learned that in business, the answers are always to

be found on the front lines, and that what ultimately generates

results is candid, open communication with frontline staff,

customers, and suppliers. The more difficult the situation, the more

we must face the facts squarely, be honest with each other, decide

what must be done, and then do it: that shall always be my guiding

principle. While remaining true to the SUMCO Vision propounded by

former Representative Director, Chairman & CEO Mayuki Hashimoto,

I will press ahead quickly and steadily to keep pace with the rapidly

changing times.

The same thinking applies to capital expenditures. Our new factory

for leading-edge 300 mm wafers—a greenfield investment approved in

2021—is steadily coming online. At existing plants, moreover, we are

planning upgrades to production facilities to adapt to the changing

structure of demand, enhancing our ability to cater to the market for

leading-edge products. This will help us build a steady revenue base.

On the financial front, cash flow, rather than just short-term

fluctuations in profits, will remain a prime consideration in the running

of our business. Despite higher depreciation costs resulting from

greenfield investment, we will maintain our ability to generate steady

earnings in the face of significant demand fluctuations and strengthen

the foundations of our business to ensure that we are ready for the

next growth opportunity.

In the silicon wafer market, demand for wafers for leading-edge logic chips and high bandwidth memory (HBM) remains strong, buoyed by

the increasing spread of artificial intelligence (AI). These are areas where SUMCO can flex the impressive technological prowess that it has

developed over the years. On the other hand, in the market for wafers for automobiles, industrial equipment, consumer electronics, and other

non-leading-edge applications, demand is recovering slowly, while Chinese companies are expected to expand supply.

In this climate, the course we should pursue is obvious: achieving quality-driven growth. Quality means being chosen on the virtue of our

technology. It means winning trust with the excellence of our products, having the capability to supply them consistently, and staying on a

growth trajectory while remaining profitable.

Market Outlook

During the six months ended June 30, 2026, the semiconductor market grew on a volume basis, driven

by AI-related demand and including products for industrial use, for which recovery had lagged. Strong

growth was seen also on a monetary value basis, helped by rising memory prices. Shipments of 300 mm

silicon wafers saw a large increase, as strong demand continued for leading-edge logic and memory used

in AI data centers. Shipments of wafers of 200 mm and smaller increased as well, even though demand

varied across customers and products.

The SUMCO Group focused on maintaining its high market share of leading-edge products, carrying on with

technology development aimed at meeting customer needs for higher precision and producing distinctive products. We

further made efforts to boost our cost competitiveness, including by raising productivity with the use of AI. We have been

restructuring our production of 200 mm and smaller wafers, continuing our efforts to enhance efficiency and profitability.

As a result, for the period under review, the Group registered consolidated net sales of 215.0 billion yen, operating loss

of 6.3 billion yen, ordinary loss of 12.1 billion yen, and loss attributable to owners of parent of 12.8 billion yen.

300 mm silicon wafer market is expected to see strong demand for leading-edge logic and DRAM for AI

applications, while demand for NAND use is also projected to grow in line with the expansion of server

SSDs. The supply-demand situation for 300 mm wafers overall is expected to continue improving as

inventory drawdown by customers proceeds. As for wafers of 200 mm and smaller, despite demand

variance among customers and products, gradual recovery is seen.
