NEC C&C Foundation Awards 2026 C&C Prize

- Recognition of Outstanding Contributions to the Realization of the Optical Lattice Clock, and the Establishment of Reinforcement Learning Concepts and Algorithms -

Tokyo, October 15, 2026 - The NEC C&C Foundation today announced that the 2026 C&C Prize will be awarded to two groups for their outstanding contributions to the realization of the optical lattice clock, and to the establishment of reinforcement learning concepts and algorithms.

The Group A recipient is Dr. Hidetoshi Katori, a professor in the Department of Applied Physics, Graduate School of Engineering, The University of Tokyo, who contributed to the realization of the optical lattice clock. The Group B recipients are Dr. Andrew G. Barto, Professor Emeritus at the University of Massachusetts Amherst; Dr. Richard S. Sutton, Professor at the University of Alberta; and Dr. Christopher J. Watkins, Professor at Royal Holloway, University of London, for their contributions to the establishment of reinforcement learning concepts and algorithms. Each recipient will be recognized with a certificate of merit and a plaque. Each group will also receive a cash award of ten million yen.

The C&C Prize was established in 1985 and is awarded to distinguished persons in recognition of outstanding contributions to R&D activities in information processing technologies, communications technologies, electronic device technologies, and technologies integrating these fields, as well as to research in the social sciences related to advances in these fields. Since its establishment in 1985, the prize has been presented to 85 groups (comprising 133 individuals) and two teams.

The Prize Ceremony will be held at the ANA InterContinental Tokyo on Monday, November 30 from 3 pm JST. Members of the public are welcome to attend the Prize Ceremony, Prize Recipient Lectures, and Cocktail Reception at the venue. The Prize Ceremony and Prize Recipient Lectures will also be streamed online.

Citation

Pioneering Contributions to the Realization of the Optical Lattice Clock

Achievements

Precise time measurement is a fundamental technology that underpins modern information and communications technology (ICT) society, including information and communications networks, global navigation satellite systems, electronic transactions, and scientific measurements. The current definition of the "second" is based on the cesium atomic clock, which uses the frequency of microwaves absorbed and emitted by cesium-133 atoms as its standard. On the other hand, by utilizing optical transitions in atoms—which have frequencies approximately 100,000 times higher than those of microwaves—time can be divided into finer intervals, offering the potential to significantly improve the accuracy of clocks. Against this backdrop, research and development of optical atomic clocks has been advancing.

Dr. Hidetoshi Katori proposed the concept of the optical lattice clock, in which a large number of atoms are trapped in an optical lattice formed by laser light and their characteristic optical transition frequencies are measured simultaneously, and, by actually realizing it, established an ultra-high-precision time standard that far surpasses conventional atomic clocks. Furthermore, he extended these results to relativistic geodesy, which uses optical lattice clocks to detect differences in clock rates caused by variations in gravitational potential, as predicted by general relativity, and thereby measure differences in height and gravitational potential, and spacetime sensing that uses relativistic geodesy to monitor temporal and spatial changes such as crustal deformation and volcanic activity, paving the way for applications in next-generation information and communications, quantum technology, and precision positioning.

Dr. Katori’s achievements span the proposal and demonstration of the principles behind ultra-high-precision optical lattice clocks, their application to relativistic geodesy and spacetime sensing, and their practical implementation for societal deployment. These contributions significantly advance the information and communications technologies that underpin precise control, positioning, and synchronization, and serve as a prime example of C&C technology—a highly integrated fusion of high-stability lasers, photonics, electronic control, and vacuum and cooling technologies. These achievements can be highly regarded as outstanding accomplishments worthy of the C&C Prize.

Citation

Pioneering Contributions to the Establishment of Reinforcement Learning Concepts and Algorithms

Achievements

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a technology that enables computers to perform intellectual activities — such as perception, judgment, and decision-making — traditionally carried out by humans. It holds the promise of bringing about significant transformations in society, industry, and people’s lives through increased productivity and the creation of new services. While supervised learning trained on a set of examples labeled with correct responses has been the mainstream approach to learning in traditional AI, a key challenge has been to enable systems to learn through interaction with the external environment and make autonomous decisions even in situations where correct responses are not provided in advance.

Dr. Andrew G. Barto, Dr. Richard S. Sutton, and Dr. Christopher J. Watkins addressed this challenge by establishing the conceptual and mathematical foundations of reinforcement learning, a machine learning approach in which an agent learns through trial and error, using rewards obtained through interactions with its environment, a policy that maximizes long-term cumulative reward, and developing practical, highly versatile reinforcement learning algorithms that do not rely on labeled training examples, thereby making significant contributions to the advancement of modern AI.

Reinforcement learning, pioneered by Dr. Barto, Dr. Sutton, and Dr. Watkins, has become a general-purpose decision-making technology not limited to specific tasks and has evolved into deep reinforcement learning through its integration with deep neural networks. Its applications have expanded to include game AI, communication network control, semiconductor design, and the optimization of logistics and energy management. Furthermore, reinforcement learning that utilizes human feedback has become a technology for improving the quality of generative AI responses, helping to ensure that they are safe, reliable, and aligned with human intent. It is also expected to play a crucial role in the future development of an AI-driven society as a core technology supporting behavioral learning in physical AI and next-generation robot control. The achievements of Dr. Barto and Dr. Sutton, who established the key concepts and mathematical foundations of reinforcement learning, and those of Dr. Watkins, who devised Q-learning and significantly advanced the practical application of reinforcement learning, have had an immense impact on the development of information processing and intelligent information technology. These achievements are highly regarded as outstanding contributions worthy of the C&C Prize.

About the NEC C&C Foundation

The NEC C&C Foundation is a non-profit organization established in March 1985 to foster further growth in the electronics industry by encouraging and supporting research and development activities and pioneering work related to the integration of computers and communications technologies, or C&C, and ultimately to contribute to the world economy and the enrichment of human life. The Foundation is funded by NEC Corporation.
