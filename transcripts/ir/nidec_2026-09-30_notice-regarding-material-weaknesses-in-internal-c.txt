Notice Regarding Material Weaknesses in Internal Control over Financial Reporting and

Disclaimer of Opinion in the Internal Control Audit Report

Nidec Corporation (the “Company”) hereby announces, as described below, that the Company has

stated in the Internal Control Report for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, submitted today to

the Kanto Local Finance Bureau in accordance with Article 24-4-4(1) of the Financial Instruments

and Exchange Act, that there are material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting

and that the Company’s internal control over financial reporting is not effective, and that the

Company has also stated there in that it determined that it could not express the results of the

evaluation of internal control over financial reporting for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026.

1. Details of the Material Weaknesses

As the Company identified suspicions of inappropriate accounting practices that could have a

significant impact on the entire consolidated financial statements, incl uding arbitrary adjustments

to the timing of write-downs of assets that carry evaluation risk, which were conducted under the

involvement or knowledge of certain management members of the Company and its group

companies, the Company resolved on September 3 , 2025, to establish a third -party committee in

accordance with the "Guidelines for Third -Party Committees in Cases of Corporate Misconduct"

established by the Japan Federation of Bar Associations. The Company received an investigation

report from the thir d-party committee on February 27, 2026, and a supplementary investigation

report on April 17, 2026. As a result, a large number of instances of improper accounting were

revealed at various operating bases in the Group, including past fiscal years (hereinafter, the facts

revealed in the investigation report and supplementary investigation report are referred to as th is

"Issue"). The main details of the identified accounting improprieties are outlined below:

• Avoidance of recognizing inventory valuation losses by falsely recognizing asset values in raw

materials and products with extremely low future usage and sales prospects and no asset value.

• Avoidance of recognizing impairment losses by including projects with  low probability of

realization in sales plans used as assumptions for fixed asset impairment tests.

• Avoidance of recognizing impairment losses on fixed assets by providing false explanations

regarding fixed assets that are difficult to repurpose.

• Non-recognition of provisions for loss on contract, arising from the discontinuation of product

development.

• Non-recognition of allowance for doubtful accounts for bad debts.

• Overstatement of inventories by falsifying the results of physical inventory counts.

• Deferral of expense recognition through the improper capitalization of labor costs as incidental

costs, which were subsequently expensed through depreciation.

• Intentional capitalization of quality improvement costs and research and development costs

that had been determined not to contribute to obtaining future economic benefits.

• Premature revenue recognition for unshipped sales transactions that lacked economic

substance.

In addition, for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, the Company recognized trade-related issues

concerning NIDEC FIR INTERNATIONAL S.R.L. (located in Italy; hereinafter "FIR"), a

consolidated subsidiary of the Company, under the control of its Appliance , Commercial and

Industrial Motors Business Unit of the Appliance and Automotive Division.  In response , the

Company engaged external experts to investigate whether there were similar trade compliance

issues outside FIR (hereinafter "Trade Compliance Issues"), and errors in the indication of the

country of origin were identified in the Group's exports to the United States.

Based on reports regarding the facts revealed and its root cause analysis through the investigation

by the third -party committee and the self -assessment investigating the appropriateness of

accounting treatment under the management and supervision of the third -party committee

regarding this Issue, as well as facts revealed and its root cause analysis through the investigation

by external experts and the internal investigation conducted with the support of external experts

regarding the Trade Compliance Issues, the Company re -evaluated its internal control over

financial reporting. As a result, the Company identified deficiencies in entity-level controls (control

environment, risk assessment and response, control activities, information and communication, and

monitoring) of the Company and its consolidated subsidiaries, as well as internal control s over

accounting closing and financial reporting processes appropriate to be evaluated from an entity -

level perspective. The Company determined that these deficiencies constitute material weaknesses

because they have a high probability of having a significant impact on the Company's financial

reporting. Furthermore, because the impact of the deficiencies in entity -level controls  (control

environment, risk assessment and response, control activities, information and communication, and

monitoring) and  internal control s over accounting closing and financial reporting processes

appropriate to be evaluated from an entity -level perspective was material and pervasive, the

foundation for internal controls over business processes to function effectively was lacking.

Therefore, deficiencies were also identified in all business processes evaluated, and all were

determined to constitute material weaknesses due to the high probability of having a significant

impact on financial reporting.

Regarding the material weakness arising from trade -related issues at FIR identified in the fiscal

year ended March 31, 2025 (entity -level controls (information and communication) and internal

controls over accounting closing and financial reporting processes), although recurrence prevention

measures have been implemented, new material weaknesses were identified in the fiscal year ended

March 31, 2026, in entity -level controls (control environment, risk assessment and response,

control activities, information and communication, and monitoring) and internal control s over

accounting closing and financial reporting processes appropriate to be  evaluated from an entity -

level perspective. Therefore, the Company evaluated that the material weakness has not been

remediated.

The Company recognizes the following as material weaknesses regarding this Issue and the Trade

Compliance Issues:

<Material Weaknesses Identified Regarding This Issue>

(Entity-level Controls)

• Control environment, risk assessment and response, and information and communication

Authority over the Automotive Motor & Electronic Control Business Unit, Small Platform

Motor & Solutions Business Unit, Machinery and Automation Business Unit, and domestic

group companies was concentrated in the Company's founder and former head of the Gl obal

Group (resigned as of December 19, 2025; hereinafter referred to as "Former Representative").

Institutionally, the Former Representative  held final decision -making authority regarding

executive officer personnel, remuneration, and approval of request for managerial decision at

domestic group companies. Officers and employees of the Company tended to depend on the

Former Representative's judgment and prioritize his intentions, creating a situation where it

was difficult to express differing opinions and checks and balances did not function sufficiently.

Based on these authorities, the Former Representative placed pressure on the management

executives of the Group to achieve unrealistic and difficult -to-achieve profit targets. The

Former Representative placed excessive emphasis on maintaining and increasing the level of

stock prices and market capitalization. As a result, under an excessive shareholder primacy,

unrealistic and difficult -to-achieve profit targets based on performance levels from an

investor's perspective were set in the Group , rather than profit targets reflecting the actual

capabilities of each business. Achievement of these profit targets was demanded through

excessive performance management by certain members of the Company's management. For

instance, when there was a possibi lity that profit targets would not be met, progress

management and confirmation of numerical target achievement were conducted several times

a day. Even after the end of the fiscal year, if preliminary profit figures fell short, instructions

were given to accumulate figures until the accounting close deadline. Furthermore, excessively

strict guidance was provided when profit targets were not achieved. Under such circumstances,

radical measures could not be taken regarding the expense and loss processing of assets

carrying evaluation risk, known as "legacy liabilities," because securing a certain profit level

or subsequent V -shaped recovery was a prerequisite, resulting in the continued improper

processing of legacy liabilities.

In terms of institutional aspects, a business performance evaluation system that was biased

toward the achievement ratio of operating profit targets, and a personnel evaluation system

that led to a focus on performance and neglect of behavior, especially f or the Company's

executive officers, fostered a corporate culture that gave top priority to short-term profits over

compliance and did not allow profit targets to be missed. This led to the occurrence of

numerous improper accounting practices at wide -ranging bases of the Group aimed at

achieving profit targets.

Furthermore, because the obligation to report material risk information to the Board of

Directors was not clearly defined except for certain items, material risk information and the

progress of business plans were not sufficiently reported to the Board of Directors and the

Audit and Supervisory Committee. Consequently, the Board of Directors and the Audit and

Supervisory Committee were unable to appropriately identify and evaluate material risks, nor

take necessary actions, failing to sufficiently exercise oversight and check functions.

Additionally, outside directors consisted mainly of academic experts and former government

officials, lacking a composition that could confirm and poin t out risks from diversified

perspectives to the execution side, and supervisory functions to promptly grasp and correct

such situations did not function adequately.

• Control activities

Within the Group, the CFOs of many business units and domestic Group companies, as well

as the accounting departments under their supervision, were responsible for achieving

performance targets and did not maintain an independent position from the business divisions.

As a result, they were exposed to significant pressure to achieve such targets . Under these

circumstances, accounting departments and business divisions collaborated to engage in

improper accounting aimed at achieving profit targets, and the monitoring function of the

accounting department as the second line of defense, which should be independent from

business divisions, did not function effectively.

• Monitoring

The Corporate Administration & Internal Audit Department was aware that the root cause of

frequent improper accounting within the Nidec Group was excessive performance pressure

from certain members of the Company's management, but failed to take measures addressing

the root cause, such as conducting in-depth investigations expanding the scope of investigation.

Therefore, the monitoring function of the Corporate Administration & Internal Audit

Department as the third line of defense did not operate sufficiently.

(Internal Controls over Accounting Closing and Financial Reporting Processes Appropriate to Be

Evaluated from an Entity-level Perspective)

The Nidec global Accounting Policy (hereinafter the "NIAP"), the Group's accounting policy,

stipulates guidelines and exception processing for certain accounting treatments in addition to

rules and definitions in compliance with International Financial Reporting Standards.

However, because specific judgment criteria reflecting business characteristics were not

sufficiently described in NIAP,  there was room in practice for overly broad or deviating

interpretations of the NIAP. Specifically, when it was difficult to reach a clear determination based

solely on NIAP descriptions, a mechanism existed to apply for approval from the accounting

department regarding the selected accounting policy. However, such applications became routine,

rendering the operation of NIAP ineffective. Consequently, the accounting department, in line

with the intentions of the business divisions, permitted accounting treatments based on overly

broad or deviating interpretations of the NIAP, which led to improper accounting.

(Internal Controls over Business Processes)

Due to deficiencies identified in entity-level controls (control environment, risk assessment and

response, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring) and internal

controls over accounting closing and financial reporting processes appropriate to be evaluated

from an entity -level perspective under the above conditions, existing internal controls over

business processes were invalidated, failing to prevent numerous improper accounting practices

occurring across wide-ranging business bases and business processes of the Group.

<Material Weaknesses Identified Regarding Trade Compliance Issues>

(Entity-level Controls)

Through the investigation conducted by external experts into the Trade Compliance Issues, errors

in the indication of country of origin were identified in the Group's exports to the United States.

As a result, the Company conducted a statistical sampling survey of all transactions of major

import bases pertaining to exports from the Company’s bases to the United States  during the

period from the fiscal year ended March 31, 2021 through the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026.

The survey revealed certain errors in tariff classifications, country  of origin, application of

preferential treatment based on preferential trade agreements, and import prices in import

declarations to the United Stat es. These errors were attributable not only to  the relevant parties’

insufficient trade know-how and compliance awareness  (an entity -level control deficiency

relating to the control environment), but also to  insufficient business standardization within the

Group (an entity-level control deficiency relating to control activities).

(Entity-level Controls)

Company officers and employees who received information regarding conduct suspected of

violating laws and regulations failed to report such information to the Company’s management

through designated reporting lines. Consequently, necessary internal discussions and reviews

regarding material risk information were not conducted, resulting in a loss of opportunity for early

2. Reasons why remediation could not be completed by the end of the fiscal year ended March

31, 2026

Although some material weaknesses became known prior to the end of the fiscal year ended March

31, 2026, remediation could not be completed by the end of the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026,

because there was insufficient time between discovery and the end of the fiscal year ended March

31, 2026, and certain weaknesses were identified after the end of the fiscal year ended March 31,

2026. Furthermore, regarding the material weakness concerning trade transaction issues at FIR

identified in the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, although recurrence prevention measures have

been implemented, new material weaknesses were identified in enti ty-level controls and internal

control over accounting closing and financial reporting processes appropriate to be evaluated from

an entity-level perspective in the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, leading to the evaluation that

material weaknesses have not been remediated.

4. Description of the significant evaluation procedures that could not be performed and the

reasons why the Company is unable to express an evaluation conclusion on internal control

As described in Item 3, “Matters Concerning Evaluation Results,” of the Internal Control Report,

the Company has been conducting evaluations to determine appropriate personnel-related remedial

measures and implementing such measures with respect to officers and employees involved in

improper accounting practices and other related matters; however, some of these personnel-related

remedial measures has not yet been completed. As a result, there remains a possibility that the

incomplete personnel-related remedial measures could have a material and pervasive impact on

entity-level internal controls, and the Company was therefore unable to evaluate their impact on

the entity -level internal controls  of the Company and its Group companies. Consequently, the

Company determined that it was unable to express an evaluation conclusion on the effectiveness

of internal control over financial reporting for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026.
