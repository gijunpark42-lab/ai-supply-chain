Applied Materials Set to Outperform as AI Drives Sustained Demand for Wafer Fab Equipment

Oct 13, 2026 at 12:45 PM EDT

PDF Version

Company expects strong multi-year growth as global AI infrastructure buildout fuels demand for leading-edge foundry-logic, DRAM and advanced packaging – areas where Applied has built market leadership

Expects wafer fab equipment (WFE) spending to grow faster through 2030 than over the past decade; targets more than 2 percentage points of WFE share gain from calendar 2025 to 2030

Targets higher profitability through 2030 with non-GAAP gross margin target of approximately 54 percent and non-GAAP operating margin target of approximately 40 percent

Committed to distribute 80 to 100 percent of free cash flow to shareholders over time

SAN FRANCISCO , Oct. 13, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- At its 2026 SEMICON West Investor Breakfast Presentation held today, Applied Materials, Inc. presented its longer-term growth expectations and outlined how its leadership in materials engineering positions the company to outperform the wafer fab equipment (WFE) market over the coming years.

“The global buildout of AI infrastructure is driving a new era of growth for wafer fab equipment,” said Gary Dickerson , President and CEO of Applied Materials. “Applied Materials has built leadership in the fastest growing areas of the market that have the greatest impact on AI computing performance, power efficiency and cost, and our advanced services offerings enable chipmakers to increase yield and fab output. Our industry-leading capabilities position Applied for continued growth and outperformance in the years ahead.”

The race for AI computing leadership requires tremendous technology innovation and capacity expansion to support infrastructure scaling, which is fueling sustained WFE investments – bigger than any of the industry’s past growth drivers. The growth and diversity of AI applications from generative to agentic and physical AI require increasing amounts of silicon. Applied expects leading-edge foundry-logic, DRAM and advanced packaging to drive around 75 percent of WFE growth from 2025-2030. Applied has strong number-one process equipment positions in all three areas and is targeting to gain more than 2 percentage points of WFE market share from calendar 2025 to 2030.

To support the industry’s capacity expansion, Applied is utilizing its unique eBeam technology and advanced service solutions to help chipmakers accelerate the ramp of new process nodes and optimize yield, output and cost in high-volume manufacturing. With the industry’s highest resolution and fastest imaging capabilities, Applied’s eBeam equipment portfolio is expected to generate $2 billion in revenue in calendar year 2026. The company is expecting service revenue to grow more than 20 percent this calendar year and to exceed $12 billion in revenue by calendar year 2030, representing a mid-teens compound annual growth rate.

“For the past 10 years, Applied Materials has been making strategic investments to build the industry’s most enabling portfolio for AI computing,” said Brice Hill , Senior Vice President and CFO. “As AI drives wafer fab equipment growth, we are focused on further increasing profitability and operating leverage while investing in R&D programs that will fuel our outperformance into the future.”

Based on the company’s expectations for semiconductor industry growth, Applied is investing to more than double its R&D lab and manufacturing capacity from current levels by 2030. The company yesterday unveiled its new Equipment and Process Innovation and Commercialization (EPIC) Center in Silicon Valley , the world’s largest facility dedicated to collaborative semiconductor process technology and manufacturing equipment R&D. To date, Applied has announced 15 EPIC Center partnerships with leading chipmakers, systems companies, equipment partners and universities. Applied has also recently expanded its manufacturing infrastructure in Austin, Texas , Singapore and Treviso, Italy .

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

Forward-looking non-GAAP gross margin and non-GAAP operating margin targets for 2030 assume non-GAAP adjustments as applicable for future periods, which we are unable to predict without unreasonable efforts due to their inherent uncertainty. Free cash flow is a non-GAAP measure and is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditures. Management uses non-GAAP financial measures to evaluate the company’s operating and financial performance and for planning purposes, and as performance measures in its executive compensation program. Applied believes these measures enhance an overall understanding of its performance and investors’ ability to review the company’s business from the same perspective as the company’s management, and facilitate comparisons of results with prior periods on a consistent basis by excluding items that management does not believe are indicative of Applied’s ongoing operating performance. There are limitations in using non-GAAP financial measures because the non-GAAP financial measures are not prepared in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, may be different from non-GAAP financial measures used by other companies, and may exclude certain items that may have a material impact upon our reported financial results.
