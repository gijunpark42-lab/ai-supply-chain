B. What Is Edge AI? The Fundamental Difference from Traditional Cloud AI

a. What Edge AI Means in Surveillance Workflows

Edge AI is the execution of AI algorithms and computation closer to the source of the data – be it cameras, sensors, IoT devices, or local edge systems. This means the first layer of analysis can happen close to the video stream in an AI monitoring system. So, simple detections, event flags, and routine filtering do not need to make a round trip to a remote data center.

b. How Cloud AI Handles Centralized Intelligence

The main difference between Edge AI vs Cloud AI is not whether one is better, but where the intelligence is running. Cloud AI sends endpoint data to centralized cloud infrastructure for processing which is useful for complex analytics, retrospective review, centralized model management, and multi-site coordination. But cloud-heavy workflows can tax bandwidth demand, storage growth, and transmission-latency pressure when large video volumes need to be uploaded continuously. The research on edge-based video analytics also points out that high bandwidth consumption and latency can be problems for cloud-based video processing.

c. The Hybrid Flow: Local Filtering, Cloud-Level Context

In a well-designed Edge AI computing architecture, the local layer carries out a significant part of initial event detection and filtering, and only relevant events, abnormal footage, or selected data move upward for deeper cloud analysis. For example, a camera or edge node might first evaluate if there is a person, intrusion, or anomalous event, and the cloud may later support cross-camera comparison, long-term pattern analysis, or broader site-level decisions. This keeps time-sensitive action closer to the scene, while saving cloud resources for large-context tasks.

C. How Edge AI Addresses the Key Limitations of Cloud AI

a. Lowering Video Transport and Storage Waste

The most expensive data in cloud-heavy surveillance is often the footage that never becomes an incident, audit record, or investigation lead. Continuous upload can consume network bandwidth and inflate cloud storage requirements, even if most frames have no operational value. Edge-side filtering adds a new dimension to the cost model by filtering normal scenes locally, and only sending selected alerts, clips, or metadata upstream. This is why edge-based video analytics is spoken of as a way to solve high bandwidth consumption in traditional cloud video workflows.

b. Improving Local Alert Timing Where Seconds Matter

When it comes to intrusion detection, safety monitoring, and building security, an alert is useless if the system waits too long for remote processing. However, Edge AI can facilitate faster local detection by running selected AI video analytics tasks closer to the camera, sensor, or local node. Remember, that does not mean zero delay or a promise of being immune from every network hiccup, but it does mean that site-level functions can proceed with less reliance on cloud round trips when the local architecture is designed for it.

c. Scaling Without Starting from a Large Central Server

Initial steps can be onerous for centralized AI processing:

Bigger server capacity

Concentrated power draw

Cooling needs

Increased upfront planning risk

The IEA projects that global data center electricity use will double to some 945 TWh by 2030. That is why organizations are seeking more distributed means of handling AI workloads.

However, with a distributed edge model, select workloads are distributed to the cameras, AI Box nodes, gateways, or local systems. This enables organizations to begin with a controlled site, floor, store, or building zone before a broader roll-out. For LITEON customers this supports a phased path where entry level and higher end edge device options can be matched to real site requirements rather than pushing every deployment into the same server centered design. LITEON’s portfolio, which covers surveillance, access control, smart hub, networking, as well as 5G-related solution areas, also supports this broader deployment flexibility.

D. Redefining the Cloud: Edge and Cloud as a Hybrid Architecture

Gartner predicts that 50% of critical enterprise applications will be outside of centralized public cloud locations by 2027, which proves the need for hybrid infrastructure strategies.

a. Edge Handles Immediate Site Decisions

We start with an edge layer in a hybrid model and we put time sensitive surveillance functions there. Tasks such as intrusion detection, presence detection, initial object classification, event triggers, and local alerts are best placed close to the cameras or sensors as they depend on fast scene level interpretation. This direction is supported by research on edge-based video analytics, where edge architectures perform video processing closer to the source to meet real-time requirements that can be problematic for traditional cloud-only solutions.

b. Cloud Provides Wider Analytical Context

You still want cloud AI when the job needs more compute, shared datasets, or centralized coordination. Cloud infrastructure suits cross-camera search, multi-site analysis, large-scale video comparison, historical pattern review, and centralized model control, as they need a wider perspective than what can be obtained from a single camera, floor, or building zone. Recent work on video analytics at the cloud edge describes hybrid systems that combine edge-centric and cloud-centric techniques through adaptive task offloading and resource-aware scheduling.

A video object-detection study showed that combined edge-and-cloud predictions performed up to 50% better than edge-only and cloud-only approaches. This data lends credence to the idea that the most robust architecture is often collaborative, not just local or just cloud.

c. AI Box Enables Priority-Based Task Offloading

The next step is deciding which AI task deserves computing resources first.

Important events, such as intrusions or immediate safety threats, should be given priority.

On the other hand, lower priority analytics, such as clothing-color analysis or behaviour categorisation, can be processed later or sent to a higher layer of computing.

LITEON’s AI Box architecture can back this modular task-offloading model, prioritizing time-critical AI events and scheduling lower-urgency workloads according to device capacity. This helps to balance responsiveness, system load, and operating cost. Such an edge-to-cloud logic is also reflected in LITEON’s broader AI-RAN direction, which distributes AI workloads between user equipment and edge or cloud environments based on network conditions.

F. The Future of Edge AI: 5G AI-RAN, OTA Automation, Security, and Privacy

a. 5G AI-RAN Extends Intelligence Beyond the Device

The next stage of edge-to-cloud architecture does not just incorporate the components of smarter cameras or local computing nodes. However, it will also include coordinating the placement of AI tasks among devices, network infrastructure, and cloud resources.

Edge devices can do the first-stage detection.

5G AI-RAN can distribute AI workloads and use network-aware bandwidth.

Cloud platforms can stay open for larger analytical workloads.

This trend is supported by the AI-RAN materials that LITEON has developed. These materials highlight scalable AI-native RAN, validated compatibility, reduced complexity of integration, and edge inference through the integration of AI and RAN workloads.

b. A More Integrated Operating Model

The actual value is that, as deployments grow, there will be fewer disconnected handoffs between device suppliers, network teams, and cloud platform owners. LITEON’s Device + Networking + Cloud capability enables enterprises and system integrators to build a more coordinated architecture between edge devices, 5G AI-RAN, cloud connectivity, as well as API integration. Furthermore, LITEON also describes end-to-end AI capability from cloud to edge, which supports this broader integration message.

c. Model Improvement Without Overpromising Accuracy

AI surveillance systems can still produce false alarms in real-world scenarios with fluctuating lighting, shadows, weather, reflections, and complicated human movement. The realistic goal is not a perfect detection claim but to improve model behavior over time with historical data, AI optimization, and better event validation. False alarm reduction is also a continuing technical challenge in research on video-surveillance change detection, not a solved issue.

d. OTA Updates as a Development Direction

For distributed edge deployments OTA update capability is important as devices may be installed across many rooms, floors, branches, or sites. Over time, edge devices may be more capable of assessing local operating conditions and contributing to update decisions, thus reducing manual intervention, but still requiring controlled release, validation, and rollback processes. Remember, remote updates are helpful to add features and fix bugs or vulnerabilities, but they need to consider device constraints, scale, heterogeneity, reliability, and security.

e. Privacy and Security Still Need Governance

By keeping selected video data, metadata, and decisions at the edge, unnecessary cloud exposure can be reduced and data privacy goals can be supported. That is particularly true when the footage contains sensitive people, places, or business activity. But edge processing alone does not remove cybersecurity risk. Access control, data governance, network protection, software update discipline, and security architecture still need to be in place.

G. Conclusion: Choosing the Right Edge AI Solution

The Edge AI vs Cloud AI right strategy is to place each task where it delivers the strongest operational value. Edge AI computing allows surveillance and smart-building teams to cut down on unnecessary video transport, improve local alert timing, support a more privacy-conscious approach to data handling, and scale adoption through a flexible hybrid architecture. Meanwhile, cloud platforms are still important for broader analytics and centralized coordination.

FAQs

What is Edge AI and how is it different from Cloud AI?

Edge AI runs AI algorithms and computing closer to where data is generated, such as cameras, sensors, IoT devices, or local edge systems, while Cloud AI processes data in centralized cloud infrastructure. Edge AI is suited to time-sensitive local processing and filtering, while Cloud AI remains valuable for complex analytics, centralized model management, historical analysis, and multi-site coordination.

When should AI workloads run at the edge instead of in the cloud?

AI workloads are well suited to the edge when applications require faster local responses, generate large volumes of data, or benefit from reducing unnecessary data transmission to the cloud. Cloud computing remains appropriate for workloads requiring greater computing resources, broader datasets, historical analysis, or centralized coordination. A hybrid Edge AI and Cloud AI architecture can therefore place each workload where it delivers the most operational value.
