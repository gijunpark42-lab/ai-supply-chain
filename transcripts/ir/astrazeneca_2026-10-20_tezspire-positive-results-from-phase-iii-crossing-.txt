Tezspire positive results from Phase III CROSSING trial in eosinophilic esophagitis including symptom improvement, histologic and endoscopic remission presented at United European Gastroenterology Week

PUBLISHED

20 October 2026

First and only biologic therapy to achieve statistically significant and clinically meaningful histologic remission and symptom improvements at week 24 that were sustained through week 52 vs. placebo

Tezspire attained statistically significant total endoscopic remission with visible clearance of inflammation and fibrosis, vs. placebo at week 52

Full results from the positive Phase III CROSSING trial showed that AstraZeneca and Amgen’s Tezspire (tezepelumab) demonstrated statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in the co-primary endpoints of histologic remission and the frequency and severity of dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) in adolescent and adult patients with symptomatic and uncontrolled eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE), despite continued treatment with background medications. These improvements were observed at week 24 in both doses tested compared to placebo. 1 Efficacy was sustained in both doses through week 52, as measured in key secondary endpoints. 1,2

Results from the CROSSING trial were presented in a late-breaking oral presentation at United European Gastroenterology Week 2026 in Barcelona, Spain.

Treatment with Tezspire, administered subcutaneously every four weeks, achieved histologic remission for 64.8% (210mg dose) and 68.9% (420mg dose) of patients at week 24, compared to 3.5% of patients on placebo (p<0.001). 1 Tezspire also reduced the frequency and severity of dysphagia symptoms by 18.8 points (210mg dose; p=0.049) and 22.0 points (420mg dose; p<0.001) from baseline vs. 14.7 points on placebo, as measured by the Dysphagia Symptom Questionnaire (DSQ) score, a patient-reported outcome measure. 1 These results were sustained through week 52, assessed in key secondary endpoints: 67.8% (210mg dose) and 63.3% (420mg dose) of patients taking Tezspire achieved histologic remission vs. 5.4% on placebo (p<0.001) and there was continued symptom improvement through week 52, with patients taking Tezspire achieving an average reduction from baseline of 22.1 points (210mg dose) and 24.0 points (420mg dose) in the DSQ score vs. 13.2 points on placebo (p < 0.001). 1 See Tables 1-2 for detailed results and across both doses.

In key secondary endpoints, treatment with Tezspire resulted in statistically significant improvements in endoscopic features and severity (measured with the EoE Endoscopic Reference Score, or “EREFS”) at weeks 24 and 52. 1 Additionally, 34.4% (210mg dose) and 29.5% (420mg dose) of patients achieved total endoscopic remission, meaning no visible features of EoE at week 52 (vs. 4.1% placebo (p<0.001)), as measured in a key secondary endpoint. 1 See Table 2 .

In patients with EoE, inflammation of the esophagus can lead to dysphagia, food impaction and esophageal narrowing, and the risk of food moving slowly or becoming stuck can make daily meals difficult and stressful. 3-4 Nearly half of patients, including adolescents, do not achieve adequate disease control with current standard of care designed to manage symptoms, which include dietary restriction, swallowed topical corticosteroids and proton pump inhibitors. 5-7

Evan Dellon, MD, Professor of Medicine and adjunct Professor of Epidemiology at the University of North Carolina School of Medicine, Chapel Hill, NC, USA, and US primary investigator in the CROSSING trial, said: “The prevalence of eosinophilic esophagitis is on the rise and has become one of the fastest-growing chronic gastrointestinal conditions we see in the clinic. This disease can severely impede a person’s ability to eat and swallow, with many patients experiencing near-daily burden from the risk of food becoming stuck during meals. The CROSSING data suggest that tezepelumab, dosed once every four weeks, would be an important new treatment option that addresses key treatment goals in EoE across symptomatic, histologic and endoscopic outcomes.”

Arjan Bredenoord, MD, Gastroenterologist and professor at the Amsterdam University Medical Center, Amsterdam, The Netherlands, and international coordinating investigator in the CROSSING trial, said: “The CROSSING trial results are compelling because they show consistent, lasting improvements across three key measures of eosinophilic esophagitis — histologic remission, frequency and severity of dysphagia and endoscopic remission — sustained through 52 weeks. Importantly, these meaningful results were seen even in patients who were allowed to remain on their stable background therapy."

Sharon Barr, Executive Vice President, BioPharmaceuticals R&D, AstraZeneca, said: “The exciting results from the CROSSING trial further support the broad potential of Tezspire and its unique mode of action targeting TSLP at the top of the inflammatory cascade. These data build on the strong body of evidence for Tezspire and support its potential to transform outcomes for patients across a range of epithelial-driven inflammatory diseases. We look forward to sharing the CROSSING data with regulatory authorities in order to bring Tezspire to patients living with eosinophilic esophagitis as quickly as possible.”

In the trial, patients were allowed to remain on background medications for EoE, including proton pump inhibitors and swallowed topical corticosteroids, provided that they were stable prior to entry and during the treatment period. 2

Tezspire had a safety profile consistent with its approved indications. 1 The most frequently reported adverse events for Tezspire in the CROSSING trial were upper respiratory infection (nasopharyngitis), headache and diarrhoea. 1

Tezspire is a first-in-class human monoclonal antibody that inhibits the action of thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP), a key epithelial cytokine that sits at the top of multiple inflammatory cascades. Tezspire is currently approved for the treatment of severe asthma in the US, EU, China, Japan and more than 70 countries across the globe, and for the treatment of chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) in the US, EU, China and Japan. The Phase III JOURNEY and EMBARK trials evaluating Tezspire in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) are ongoing. 8,9

Notes

​​ Eosinophilic Esophagitis (EoE) ​

EoE is a chronic and progressive epithelial-driven inflammatory disorder of the esophagus affecting more than 470,000 people in the US, with the prevalence increasing five-fold since 2009. 3,10 It is characterised by inflammation, remodelling and esophageal epithelial dysfunction. 3 Epithelial dysfunction and inflammation are important characteristics of EoE and impede the ability of the epithelium to act as a physical and immunological barrier against the external environment. 3

The most common symptoms of EoE include difficulty and pain when swallowing, food becoming stuck in the esophagus (which may require emergency medical interventions), nausea and vomiting. 3,11,12 Many patients, including adolescents, report challenges with swallowing and eating, including concerns about choking. 11,13,14

Patients are often treated with proton pump inhibitors or swallowed topical corticosteroids to manage inflammation. 3,5 Nearly half of patients with EoE will not respond to standard first-line therapies or dietary treatment. 6,7

CROSSING

CROSSING was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multi-centre, parallel-group, Phase III trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Tezspire administered subcutaneously every four weeks, compared to placebo in patients aged 12-80 years with symptomatic and histologically active EoE. 2 A total of 368 patients were randomised in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive either a 210mg or 420mg dose of Tezspire or placebo. 2

The co-primary endpoints analysed at week 24 were the proportion of patients with histologic remission, defined as a peak esophageal eosinophil count less than or equal to six eosinophils per high-power field, and mean changes from baseline in the DSQ. 2 The peak eosinophil count is obtained when biopsies of the tissue of the esophagus are examined under a microscope. 2 A count of 15 or more peak eosinophils per high power microscopic field measured by esophageal biopsy is often the cutoff used to diagnose EoE. 15,16 The DSQ captures the presence and severity of dysphagia symptoms in a daily diary with a four-item patient-reported questionnaire; the score is calculated over 14-day periods, ranging from zero to 84, with a higher score indicating more severe dysphagia. 2

Key secondary endpoints assessed histologic remission and dysphagia symptoms at week 52; changes in endoscopic disease features (EREFS) and histologic severity and extent features (EoE-HSS) at weeks 24 and 52; as well as endoscopic response, inflammatory remission and total endoscopic remission at week 52. 2 EREFS is a standardised scoring system used to assess five visible features of EoE in the esophagus during endoscopy: swelling (edema), rings, white patches (exudates), furrows and narrowing (strictures) where a higher score indicates more severe endoscopic disease. 17 A threshold for clinically meaningful improvements in EREFS change from baseline has not been established.

In the trial, patients were allowed to remain on background medications for EoE, including proton pump inhibitors and swallowed topical corticosteroids, provided that they were stable prior to entry and during the treatment period. 2

​​Tezepelumab and TSLP​

Tezepelumab is being developed by AstraZeneca in collaboration with Amgen as a first-in-class human monoclonal antibody that inhibits the action of TSLP, a key epithelial cytokine that sits at the top of multiple inflammatory cascades. 18 TSLP is critical in the initiation and persistence of allergic, eosinophilic and other types of epithelial-driven inflammation associated with severe asthma, CRSwNP, COPD and EoE. 19,20

TSLP is released by the epithelium in response to environmental triggers. 18 Across these disease states, the expression of TSLP is increased and correlates with disease severity. 18-22

Tezspire is approved as a single-use pre-filled syringe and auto-injector for self-administration in the US, EU, China and Japan. Since 2021, more than 100,000 patients have been treated with Tezspire for severe asthma. 23

In October 2021, Tezspire was granted Orphan Drug Designation by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of EoE. 24 Beyond EoE, Tezspire is also being explored in Phase III trials in COPD. 8,9

Amgen Collaboration

The 2012 Collaboration Agreement between Amgen and AstraZeneca has been amended and updated over time. For Tezspire , both companies continue to share costs and profits equally after payment by AstraZeneca of a mid-single-digit inventor royalty to Amgen. AstraZeneca continues to lead development and Amgen continues to lead manufacturing. All aspects of the collaboration are under the oversight of joint governing bodies. Under the agreement, Amgen and AstraZeneca jointly commercialise Tezspire in the US. Amgen records product sales in the US, with AZ recording its share of US profits as Alliance Revenue. Outside of the US, AstraZeneca records product sales.
