REC-4881 Phase 1b/2 Study




On October 15, 2026, we announced updated topline data from our Phase 1b/2 TUPELO study of REC-4881, an investigational MEK1/2 inhibitor for familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) that targets both Wnt/β-catenin initiation and MAPK evolution pathways via a mechanism identified by Recursion's AI discovery platform. REC-4881 is a potential first-in-class oral therapy granted FDA Orphan Drug and Fast Track designations.




FAP is a genetically defined driver of GI cancer with no approved therapies that affects more than 50,000 patients, 35,000 of which are post-colectomy, in the US and EU5, representing a roughly $15 billion overall addressable market opportunity. In these patients, upper GI (UGI) polyps represent the highest unmet need in FAP due to higher rates of complications such as bleeding and perforations from surgical procedures used to manage the disease. 




Study Design and Population




We are conducting a multi-center, open-label trial of REC-4881 in genetically confirmed FAP patients age 18 and over with no active cancer and polyps in the upper or lower GI tracts. Patients are given 4 mg once daily for twelve weeks followed by no treatment for twelve weeks. Polyp burden was defined as the sum of all polyp diameters and evaluated at baseline and weeks 13 and 25. With a data cutoff of September 10, 2026, we reported on sixteen (16) efficacy-evaluable patients, defined as participants with measurable disease (non-zero polyp burden) at baseline endoscopy, who received at least 75% of study drug, and had at least one post-baseline on-study endoscopic assessment. These 16 reflect 4 additional efficacy-evaluable patients from the last update. We also reported on 26 safety evaluable patients, 5 from the Phase 1b and 21 from the Phase 2 portion of the TUPELO study.




Results




Of the 16 efficacy-evaluable patients, 87% of patients achieved reductions in total polyp burden, with a 47% median reduction in total polyp burden at week 13, consistent with the 43% (n=12) reported previously. Of the four additional patients, all had reductions in overall polyp burden. In the upper GI tract, 92% of 13 evaluable patients experienced reductions, with 46% achieving equal to or greater than a 65% reduction. Two patients achieved reductions in the UGI of equal to or greater than 98%. 




After twelve weeks off treatment, 85% of patients remained below their overall baseline polyp burden (n=11/13). In the UGI tract, 92% of patients remained below baseline, with 6 out of 11 with equal or greater reductions from their 13 week assessments. We view the durability results as potentially allowing for dosing flexibility, which may be important for long term disease management. 
















Half of patients showed Spigelman downstaging at week 13 (n=12), which is in line with our expectation given the 55% median reduction in upper GI polyp burden. Of the 6 patients downstaged at week 13, 4 maintained the greater than or equal to one stage improvement after twelve weeks off treatment; the remaining two were not assessed. 




The safety profile remains favorable and supports the potential for chronic dosing. Of 26 safety-evaluable patients, 21 (80.8%) patients had treatment related adverse events (TRAEs) less than or equal to Grade 2, which were consistent with MEK1/2 inhibition and included dermatitis acneiform, CPK increases, rash, diarrhea, and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) decrease. Of those who experienced LVEF, they were asymptomatic and the decrease was reversible, with one patient lost to follow up. Of 4 reports of Grade 3 TRAEs, two were dermatitis acneiform and rash and 2 were a blood CPK increase. None of the patients with Grade 3 TRAEs discontinued and three of the four resolved prior to the data cutoff. No Grade 4 or 5 TRAEs were reported. 




The full Phase 1b/2 trial dataset will be presented at the Collaborative Group of the Americas on Inherited Gastrointestinal Cancer (CGA-IGC) Annual Meeting on Monday, November 2 presented by Lisa Boardman, M.D., Mayo Clinic.

Recursion has had conversations with the FDA for initiating a registrational trial. Pending the outcome of ongoing discussion with the FDA, the Company anticipates initiating a registrational trial in 2027 and will provide additional details later this year.







REC-1245 Phase 1/2 Study




On October 15, 2026, Recursion announced initial data from the Phase 1 monotherapy dose escalation portion of DAHLIA, our ongoing open-label Phase 1/2 clinical trial of REC-1245, a potential first-in-class oral molecular glue degrader designed to exploit a synthetic-lethal vulnerability in genomically unstable cancers by degrading RBM39, a splicing factor that regulates genes involved in DNA repair and stress response. The target emerged from Recursion’s platform, which found that degrading RBM39 mimics the loss of CDK12, a cancer vulnerability that has been difficult to target selectively because of its close similarity to CDK13, whose co-inhibition risks added toxicity. We estimate the total addressable patients for the therapy, should it be approved, to be greater than 100,000 in the US and EU5.




Study Design and Population




The dose escalation portion of DAHLIA enrolled patients with unresectable, locally recurrent or metastatic select solid tumors, or select relapsed or refractory lymphoma, who had progressed following, or were intolerant to, available standard-of-care treatments. Patients received REC-1245 on a continuous once-daily basis at escalating dose levels. The primary objectives of the phase 1 portion of the trial are safety and determining an MTD and/or recommended phase 2 dose, and the secondary objectives are pharmacokinetics and anti-tumor activity. As of the data cutoff date, 30 patients across all dose levels were safety-evaluable, and 12 patients who started treatment at 45 mg (dose level 4) or 65 mg (dose level 5) were efficacy-evaluable. Patients who had not yet reached their first efficacy assessment were excluded from the efficacy-evaluable population.




The data reflect a data cutoff date of August 21, 2026.




Dose Escalation Results




Among the 12 efficacy-evaluable patients who started treatment at 45 mg or 65 mg, 11 patients (91.7%) achieved disease control (complete response, partial response or stable disease) at eight weeks. Across all dose levels, 14 of 21 efficacy-evaluable patients (66.7%) achieved disease control at week 9.
















Among the patients who started treatment at 45 mg or 65 mg, three patients achieved a partial response (PR) as measured by target lesion change under RECIST 1.1 criteria, two with endometrial cancer and one with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Two of the responses have been confirmed. One patient with endometrial cancer, who started treatment at 65 mg and was reduced to 45 mg after six days of dosing (90% of dosing was 45 mg), achieved a PR with a 32.3% reduction in target lesions at Week 9, which was confirmed at Week 17 with a 48.9% reduction. A second patient with endometrial cancer, who started treatment at 45 mg, achieved a PR with a 33.9% reduction at Week 9 and a 38.2% reduction at Week 13. Both patients remained on treatment at 45 mg as of the data cutoff date. The patient with HCC, who started treatment at 65 mg, achieved an unconfirmed PR with a 55.9% reduction at Week 9, but withdrew from the trial due to toxicity, including Grade 3 stomatitis, Grade 4 thrombocytopenia and febrile neutropenia.




Safety and Tolerability Results




Across all dose levels, 22 of 30 patients (73.3%) experienced treatment related adverse events (TRAEs) of any grade, and eight patients (26.7%) experienced Grade 3 or higher TRAEs. The most common TRAEs were fatigue (30%), anemia (30%), nausea (20%), thrombocytopenia (20%) and stomatitis (16.7%). Grade 3 or higher TRAEs included anemia (10%), thrombocytopenia (10%), stomatitis (10%) and nausea (3.3%). At the 45 mg dosing level, 70% of patients experienced TRAEs (fatigue and anemia (30% each), all of which were Grade 1 or 2) with two patients (20%) experienced Grade 3 TRAEs, consisting of elevated creatinine and hypokalemia. No dose limiting toxicities (DLTs) or serious TRAEs were observed at that dose. At 65 mg, three of four DLT-evaluable patients experienced DLTs, including stomatitis, neutropenia or febrile neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia, and the MTD was exceeded. 




Next Steps




Based on these data, we selected 45 mg once daily as the dose for monotherapy expansion cohorts, including in endometrial cancer, where second-line treatment options are limited and expect preliminary data in the second half of 2027. We are exploring additional tumor types for dose expansion. Additional Phase 1 data from DAHLIA are anticipated in the second half of 2026.

Additional Phase 1 monotherapy data are expected in the second half of 2026, and preliminary dose expansion data in endometrial cancer are expected in the second half of 2027. Dose escalation will also continue in other solid tumors. Early data from the Phase 1 dose-escalation portion of DAHLIA, our ongoing open-label Phase 1/2 study of REC-1245, show the following (data cutoff: August 21, 2026): ●Three partial responses per RECIST 1.1 criteria (2 confirmed, 1 unconfirmed) in heavily pretreated patients with endometrial cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma, all at doses of 45 mg once daily (QD) or higher, with a disease control rate of 91.7% at those doses (n=12). ●Recursion has selected 45 mg QD for expansion cohorts, including endometrial cancer, where limited second-line treatments are approved; at the 45 mg QD dose (n=10), no dose-limiting toxicities or treatment-related serious adverse events were observed, and treatment-related adverse events were predominantly Grade 1 or 2. Most frequent TRAEs were Grade 1 or Grade 2 fatigue and anemia (30% each). We define operating cash expense as the net cash used in operating activities, excluding non-ordinary course transaction costs and partnership cash inflows. This non-GAAP financial measure is not based on any standardized methodology prescribed by U.S. GAAP and is not necessarily comparable to similarly-titled measures presented by other companies. We believe operating cash expense to be a liquidity measure that provides useful information to management and investors about the amount of cash consumed by the operations of the business. A limitation of using this non-U.S. GAAP measure is that operating cash expense does not represent the total change in cash and cash equivalents for the period because it excludes cash provided by or used for other investing and financing activities. We account for this limitation by providing information about our capital expenditures and other investing and financing activities in the statements of cash flows in our financial statements. Additionally, we reconciled operating cash expense above to net cash used in operating activities, the most directly comparable U.S. GAAP financial measure. In addition, it is important to note that other companies, including companies in our industry, may not use operating cash expense, may calculate operating cash expense in a different manner than we do or may use other financial measures to evaluate their performance, all of which could reduce the usefulness of operating cash expense as a comparative measure. Because of these limitations, operating cash expense should not be considered in isolation from, or as a substitute for, financial information prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP.
