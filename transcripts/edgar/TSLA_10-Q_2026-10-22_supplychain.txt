We are presumed to have significant influence over our equity method investment in SpaceX under ASC 323, Equity Method Investments and Joint Ventures, as our CEO also serves as the CEO of SpaceX but as we do not have control over the investee, we have elected the fair value option in accordance with ASC 825, Financial Instruments, to provide a more relevant measure of the investment’s current economic value to financial statement users. The fair value is determined on a quarterly basis in accordance with ASC 820, Fair Value Measurement, based on market observable inputs. The equity investment is presented within Other non-current assets on our consolidated balance sheet. Realized and unrealized gains and losses are recorded to Other (expense) income, net in our consolidated statement of operations. See Note 13, Related Party Transactions, for further information regarding our equity investment.

We are dependent on our suppliers, including single source suppliers, and the inability of these suppliers to deliver necessary components of our products in a timely manner at prices, quality levels and volumes acceptable to us, or our inability to efficiently manage these components from these suppliers, could have a material adverse effect on our business, prospects, financial condition and operating results.

ASC 820, Fair Value Measurement, states that fair value is an exit price, representing the amount that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants. As such, fair value is a market-based measurement that should be determined based on assumptions that market participants would use in pricing an asset or a liability. The three-tiered fair value hierarchy, which prioritizes which inputs should be used in measuring fair value, is comprised of: (Level I) observable inputs such as quoted prices in active markets; (Level II) inputs other than quoted prices in active markets that are observable either directly or indirectly and (Level III) unobservable inputs for which there is little or no market data. The fair value hierarchy requires the use of observable market data when available in determining fair value. During the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, we recognized total revenues of $28.05 billion and $78.67 billion, respectively, representing a decrease of $49 million and an increase of $8.74 billion, respectively, compared to the same periods in the prior year. During the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, our net income attributable to common stockholders was $787 million and $2.38 billion, respectively, representing decreases of $586 million and $576 million, respectively, compared to the same periods in the prior year. We continue to ramp production and build and optimize our manufacturing capacity, expand our operations while focusing on further cost reductions and operational efficiencies, including through vertical integration of our battery and semiconductor supply chains, to enable increased deliveries and deployments of our products. We are also investing heavily in research and development to accelerate our SI, software and fleet-based profits for further revenue growth, which will negatively impact our profitability during this phase.

We ended the third quarter of 2026 with $45.45 billion in cash and cash equivalents and short-term investments, representing an increase of $1.39 billion from the end of 2025. Our cash flows provided by operating activities were $14.90 billion during the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared to $10.93 billion during the same period ended September 30, 2025, representing an increase of $3.96 billion. Capital expenditures amounted to $13.01 billion during the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared to $6.13 billion during the same period ended September 30, 2025, representing an increase of $6.88 billion. Overall growth has allowed our business to generally fund itself. As we make critical high-value investments in our SI initiatives and expand manufacturing capabilities, including for our semiconductor, Optimus and solar operations, we intend to manage the business such that we maintain a strong balance sheet and sufficient liquidity, which may include additional funding.

We are focused on growing and optimizing our manufacturing capacity, which includes capacity for manufacturing newer vehicle models and future vehicles utilizing aspects of our next generation platform, while maximizing production rate and efficiency at our Gigafactories. The next phase of production growth will be initiated by advances in autonomy and the introduction of new products, including those built on our next generation vehicle platform, as well as our ability to efficiently manufacture and rapidly scale our own cells that we are developing to have high-volume output, lower capital and production costs and longer range. In the third quarter of 2026, we continued to progress towards these objectives through the launches of Cybercab and the Tesla Semi, along with production ramps across our new battery and material factories, including cathode material and lithium refining in Texas. We are focused on improving vehicle performance, decreasing production costs and increasing affordability and customer awareness. Leveraging our autonomy-enabled products, our goal is to redefine and lead transportation across all modes, advancing toward a future of safer, more accessible, efficient and sustainable mobility. We are also capitalizing on our strengths in real-world SI data to advance the development of Optimus, a general purpose, autonomous humanoid robot, as we make preparations and investments in large-scale production.

Our capital expenditure projections are subject to variability given the number and breadth of our projects at any given time. Changes in, among other things, global market conditions, trade and fiscal policies, and strategic timing and direction, may necessitate adjustments to our project timelines, potentially impacting our capital expenditure expectations. We are focused on long-term growth opportunities through critical, high-value investments. We are simultaneously developing and ramping new products, building or ramping manufacturing facilities on three continents, piloting the development and manufacture of new battery cell technologies, expanding our Supercharger network and investing in autonomy, robotics and other super intelligence enabled training and products and its supporting infrastructure, and the pace of our capital spend may vary depending on overall priority among projects, the pace at which we meet milestones, production adjustments to and among our various products, increased capital efficiencies and the addition of new projects. We currently expect our capital expenditures to be in excess of $20 billion in 2026, driven by our SI initiatives, including investments in compute infrastructure and data centers, the expansion and ramp of our manufacturing and R&D production lines and facilities, and growth in our fleet of company-operated SI-enabled assets and our retail, service and charging footprint. We believe this strategy will position our Company for further growth as we make investments in a capital efficient manner.

Our business has generally been consistently generating cash flow from operations in excess of our level of capital spend, and with better working capital management resulting in shorter days sales outstanding than days payable outstanding, our sales growth is also generally facilitating positive cash generation. We have and will continue to utilize such cash flows, among other things, to invest in autonomy and robotics, further vertically integrate our supply chain, expand our product roadmap and provide financing options to our customers. At the same time, periods of heightened levels of capital expenditures due to capital-intensive projects and other potential variables such as rising material prices and increases in supply chain and labor expenses resulting from changes in global trade conditions and labor availability, will necessitate additional funding beyond our operating cash flow. For example, in September 2026, we entered into three credit agreements providing aggregate commitments of $30 billion, which afford us additional flexibility to support our planned investments, as described above.

Other (expense) income, net, changed unfavorably by $89 million in the three months ended September 30, 2026 as compared to the three months ended September 30, 2025. Other (expense) income, net, changed unfavorably by $235 million in the nine months ended September 30, 2026 as compared to the nine months ended September 30, 2025. The changes were primarily due to mark-to-market adjustments on our SpaceX equity investment, which we entered into in March 2026, and adjustments on our bitcoin digital assets, as well as fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates on our intercompany balances. As our intercompany balances are significant in nature and we do not typically hedge foreign currency risk, we can experience significant fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rate gains and losses from period to period.

In addition, because a large portion of our future expenditures will be to fund our growth, we expect that if needed we will be able to adjust our capital and operating expenditures by operating segment. For example, if our near-term manufacturing operations decrease in scale or ramp more slowly than expected, including due to global economic, tax, trade or business conditions, we may choose to correspondingly slow the pace of our capital expenditures. We continually evaluate our cash needs and the evolving nature of our business and may decide it is best to raise additional capital or seek alternative financing sources to fund the rapid growth of our business, including through drawdowns on existing or new debt facilities or financing funds. For example, in September 2026, we entered into three credit agreements providing aggregate commitments of $30 billion, which afford us additional flexibility to support our planned investments. Conversely, we may also from time to time determine that it is in our best interests to voluntarily repay certain indebtedness early. Accordingly, we believe that our current and potential sources of funding will provide us with adequate liquidity during the 12-month period following September 30, 2026, as well as in the long-term.
