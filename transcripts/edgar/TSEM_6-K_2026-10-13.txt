Proposal 1 APPROVAL OF AMENDED AND RESTATED COMPENSATION POLICY FOR DIRECTORS AND EXECUTIVE OFFICERS Executive Summary What’s being voted on: approval of an amended and restated compensation policy for Tower’s directors and executive officers, as required every three years under the Israeli Companies Law. Why now: the Company’s most recent compensation policy for officers and directors, approved by the shareholders at the annual general meeting held in July 2023 and subsequently amended by the shareholders in July 2024 (the “2023 Compensation Policy”) reached the end of its three-year term, and a revised compensation policy presented at the July 2026 annual general meeting of shareholders (the “July 2026 Proposed Policy”) did not receive the required shareholder approval. What changed: following an extensive shareholder engagement process and review of benchmark and related information, the Compensation Committee and the Board of Directors withdrew two proposed modifications from the July 2026 Proposed Policy: (1) an increase in the Chief Executive Officer’s maximum potential bonus upside opportunity, and (2) an increase in the maximum permitted sign-on bonus for executive officers, maintaining both elements at the 2023 Compensation Policy levels. No other provision of the July 2026 Proposed Policy was modified and no new provision has been proposed. Board recommendation: FOR. | [image: img112_v1.jpg — not text] [image: img104_v1.jpg — not text] Background Under the Israeli Companies Law, a public company is required to adopt a compensation policy, which sets forth the terms of service and employment of directors and officers, including the grant of any benefit, payment or undertaking to provide payment, any exemption from liability, insurance or indemnification, and any severance payment or benefit. The compensation policy must be approved at least once every three years by the board of directors, after considering the recommendations of the compensation committee, and by the shareholders by the Special Majority (see above, “Vote Required for Approval of the Proposal”). In addition, the board of directors is required to periodically examine the compensation policy, as well as the need to adjust the policy in the event of a material change in the circumstances prevailing at the time of the adoption of the compensation policy or for other reasons. The Company’s most recent compensation policy, the 2023 Compensation Policy, approved by the shareholders at the annual general meeting held in July 2023 and subsequently amended by the shareholders in July 2024, reached the end of its three-year approval term in July 2026, and therefore, the Company is required under the Israeli Companies Law to obtain renewed shareholder approval of a compensation policy. Accordingly, during the first half of 2026, the Compensation Committee and Board of Directors conducted an extensive review of the 2023 Compensation Policy and retained FW Cook, an independent global compensation consultant, to perform executive compensation benchmarking, provide advisory services regarding prevailing global trends in executive compensation, and recommend changes to the 2023 Compensation Policy that aim to enable Tower to attract and retain the talent it needs to continue executing on the Company’s business strategy and growing shareholder value. Following this review, the July 2026 Proposed Policy was presented for shareholder approval at the July 2, 2026 annual general meeting of shareholders (“July 2026 Meeting”); however, the proposal for its approval did not receive the requisite majority under the Israeli Companies Law at the July 2026 Meeting and, accordingly, was not approved by the shareholders. Tower’s Growth Since Approval of the 2023 Compensation Policy Since the adoption of the 2023 Compensation Policy in July 2023 and the subsequent shareholder-approved amendment in July 2024, the Company has experienced significant growth and transformation in market share, capacity, and revenue, driven in large part by the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence (AI), data center infrastructure, and radio frequency (“RF”) infrastructure. During this period, the scale of the Company’s business, the complexity of its operations, the differentiation and strategic importance of its technology and intellectual property, and its importance to market leaders and top-tier customers have all increased dramatically. Amended Compensation Policy The Amended Compensation Policy being submitted for approval at the Meeting is substantially similar to the July 2026 Proposed Policy, with two important changes, each of which withdraws a proposed amendment and preserves the corresponding provision included in the 2023 Compensation Policy, following discussions with shareholders and review of other information. No new provision has been introduced, and no other provision of the July 2026 Proposed Policy has been modified. What Changed Since the July 2026 Meeting ✓ Withdrawn: Chief Executive Officer increase in maximum potential bonus upside opportunity. The Amended Compensation Policy withdraws the increased potential bonus upside for the Chief Executive Officer that was proposed in the July 2026 Proposed Policy. Accordingly, the Chief Executive Officer’s maximum bonus upside opportunity will remain at the level provided in the 2023 Compensation Policy, of 21 months’ salary for the “A” portion (based on measurable Company criteria) and 3 months’ salary for the “B” portion (based on individual performance), which level has not changed since the first compensation policy that was adopted by the Company in 2013. ✓ Withdrawn: Increased maximum permitted sign-on bonus grant. The Amended Compensation Policy withdraws the increased maximum permitted sign-on bonus that was proposed in the July 2026 Proposed Policy. Accordingly, the maximum permitted sign-on bonus will remain at the level provided in the 2023 Compensation Policy, namely, cash or equity with an aggregate value of up to one annual base salary, which level includes any potential make-whole awards and has not changed since the first compensation policy that was adopted by the Company in 2013. | The compensation policy establishes a framework of maximum permitted limits and does not require the Company to approve or pay any specific amount of actual compensation. The actual compensation granted to the Company&rsquo;s executive officers has never reached the maximum permitted total compensation limits. The compensation policy provides the Compensation Committee and the Board of Directors with the ability to design competitive compensation arrangements within these caps, providing the flexibility needed to navigate the highly competitive global talent landscape and support shareholder value creation. The Amended Compensation Policy being brought for approval at the Meeting is intended to ensure that the Company can continue to attract, retain, and motivate the leadership talent necessary to support the Company&rsquo;s continued growth and enhance its long-term value.
