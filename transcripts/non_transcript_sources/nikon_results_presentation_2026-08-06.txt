 I will begin with our first-quarter results.
 First-quarter revenue was 164.1 billion yen. Operating profit was a loss of 
0.9 billion yen, while profit attributable to owners of parent was a profit of 
1.2 billion yen.
 Revenue increased by 6.0 billion yen year-on-year, supported by increased 
sales in business segments outside Imaging Products and foreign 
exchange tailwinds.
 Operating profit benefited from higher sales and a 4.0 billion yen US tariff 
refunds. However, we recorded an operating loss roughly in line with the 
prior year, reflecting lower sales volumes and higher memory prices in 
Imaging Products and 4.1 billion yen inventory write-downs in Precision 
Equipment.
 Profit attributable to owners of parent returned to profit due to gains from 
the financial P&L and equity-method investments. The year-on-year 
decline of 8.2 billion yen primarily reflects the disappearance of a 9.3 billion 
yen tax benefits booked in the previous year resulting from the resolution 
to dissolve and liquidate Nikon Metrology NV.

 Next, I will explain the full-year forecasts for each segment.
 First, the Imaging Products.
 We expect the DCIL market to remain robust over the medium- to long-
term. In the near term, however, demand is softening, particularly in China, 
due to the economic slowdown. Accordingly, we revised our market 
forecast to 6.7 million units, down 0.3 million units from our previous 
forecast.
 We also revised our sales volume forecasts: camera bodies down 50 
thousand units from the previous forecast to 850 thousand units and 
lenses down 50 thousand units to 1.25 million units.
 Based on first-quarter results and the revised sales plan, we lowered our 
revenue forecast by 13 billion yen from the previous projection to 290.0 
billion yen.
 We revised downward operating profit by 3 billion yen from the previous 
forecast, as the impact of the revised sales plan more than offsets the 
benefit of US tariff refunds.
 On a year-on-year basis, revenue should remain roughly flat, as foreign 
exchange tailwinds will offset the impact of lower sales volumes. Operating 
profit should decline due to the impact of lower sales volumes and soaring 
memory prices, despite foreign exchange effects, the absence of the 3.2 
billion yen in one-time costs recorded in the previous year, and the US 
tariff refund.
