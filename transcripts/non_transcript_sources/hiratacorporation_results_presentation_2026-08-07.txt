• A packaging technology in which numerous individual chips, diced after 
circuit formation, are rearranged onto thin, rectangular panel substrates 
and molded collectively in the packaging process.
• PLP generally uses panel substrates larger than the standard 300 mm 
wafer size commonly used in semiconductor manufacturing.
• Printed circuit boards, as well as glass and copper panels used in LCD 
panel manufacturing, are employed as panel substrates.
What is PLP (Panel Level Packaging) :
Differences in Packaging Processes
Conventional 
Packaging:
Circuits are formed on a wafer, after which the wafer 
is diced into individual chips. Each chip is then 
individually mounted on a substrate and encapsulated 
to form finished products.
WLP
（Wafer Level Package）
After chips are diced, only known-good dies are 
rearranged onto a wafer. While the chips remain on 
the wafer, they are mounted on a substrate and 
encapsulated, and then finally singulated.
PLP
（Panel Level Package）
After chips are diced, only known-good dies are 
rearranged onto a rectangular panel. With the chips 
placed on the panel, they are mounted on a substrate 
and encapsulated, and then singulated into individual 
products.
