◆ YoY Changes in Q1 Results and Details of the Forecast Revision 
Q1. Operating profit in the Telecommunication Systems business increased significantly year on year. 
Could you provide a breakdown of optical components, optical cables, and other products? 
A1. In the Telecommunication Systems business, net sales increased significantly from JPY 143.8 
billion in Q1 of FY2025 to JPY 264.4 billion in Q1 of FY2026, while operating profit increased 
from JPY 34.0 billion to JPY 98.0 billion. The majority of the increase in net sales was attributable 
to optical components, which were also highly profitable due to short lead -time responses. In 
addition, profitability i mproved more than the increase in net sales, supported by growth in 
engineering business for data center in the U.S. and an improved product mix for optical fiber 
cables. 
  
Q2. What changed after the announcement of the forecast revision dated June 18? The 1st half forecast 
has also been revised upward substantially. What happened during this period? Could you 
quantify, as much as possible, the breakdown of the factors behind the 1st half and full-year 
forecast revisions, including volume increases in optical components due to data center project 
orders, the impact of hydrogen, price increases and improved product mix? 
A2. The upward revision to the 1st half forecast was mainly since orders for a large optical component 
project expanded beyond our initial forecast. For the full -year forecast, we revised the forecast 
by incorporating a large project into the second half as well, whereas they had previously been 
factored in only for the 1st half. Quantifying the factors behind the 1st half and full-year forecast 
revisions is difficult as of today, but we will consider providing such information in the future 
based on your request. The impact of hydrogen has eased considerably, and its impact is much 
smaller than that of the large order for optical components. 
  
◆ Large Optical Component Project 
Q3. Could you explain the background to the large optical component order that was the main factor 
behind the latest forecast revision? Was this order received suddenly, or were they strategically 
targeted? Also, are they expected to continue into FY2027 and beyond, or will additional orders 
be difficult due to capacity constraints? 
A3. The latest forecast revision was mainly attributable to the sudden receipt of new large optical

A4. As this relates to customer information, we are unable to provide details. 
  
Q5. Could you explain the content of optical components, the production structure, including in-house 
and outsourced production as well as domestic and overseas production, and how you respond to 
short lead-time and large-volume order? Do you strategically hold inventory, or do you produce 
in response to orders? Also, how should we understand this in light of the fact that there did not 
appear to be a significant inventory build-up at the end of FY2025? 
A5. Optical components are connectivity and cabling -related products used within data centers, 
including pre -connectorized cables, patch cords, cabinets, patch panels, splice cassettes, and 
similar products. Production is not conducted in Japan; rather, we utilize production sites around 
the world. We refrain from commenting on inventory strategy or specific approaches to handling 
individual projects, as these relate to our business strategy. However, we are responding to 
customer requirements by utilizing our global production and supply structure. 
  
Q6. You previously stated that, while optical fibers and optical cables capacity remain constrained, 
you would like to make optical components and similar products a third pillar of earnings. Since 
when have you been targeting this opportunity? 
A6. Since new plants for optical fibers and optical cables take time to start up, we have been focusing 
since last year on expanding sales of optical components, which use relatively smaller amounts 
of optical fibers, as one measure to address this issue. In addition, by allocating limited optical 
fibers to higher value-added products, average selling prices have also increased.

◆ Prices, Price Increases and Product Mix 
Q8. To what extent have prices increased year on year for each product? Could you provide a rough 
breakdown of the “price increases and improved product mix +JPY 23.4 billion” shown on page 
7 of the presentation  material for 1Q of FY2026 ? Are you also raising prices for the same 
products? 
A8. We are making progress on price revisions for the same products, including those associated with 
short lead-time responses. At the same time, the product mix is improving as we allocate optical 
fiber to high value -added products in areas where we have stre ngths, such as ultra -high-fiber-
count cables and Air Blown WTC™. Therefore, it is difficult to clearly separate the effect of price 
increases from the effect of an improved product mix. As one example of a price revision for the 
same product, certain customers accepted a price increase of approximately 25%. 
  
◆ Optical Fiber Procurement and Supply Environment 
Q9. Regarding external procurement of optical fiber, some market participants believe that the supply-
demand environment may improve globally as demand for drone applications settles down. Have 
you seen any change in procurement conditions? Also, from a medium - to long -term growth 
perspective, is it correct to understand that top-line growth from FY2027 onward will depend on 
the volume of external procurement? 
A9. Since we do not handle optical fiber for drone applications, we do not have information on supply-
demand trends for that application. On the other hand, global optical fiber prices remain on an 
upward trend, and demand is strong not only from data centers but also from telecommunications 
carriers. Therefore, at present, we do not feel that supply -demand tightness has eased. From a 
medium- to long-term perspective, we expect demand to continue expanding steadily. If we can 
increase external procurement volum es before the new plant starts up, we believe this will 
contribute to top-line growth. Accordingly, external procurement can be both an upside factor and 
a risk factor. Although we will continue working to improve productivity and yields at existing 
facilities, we expect to remain dependent to a certain extent on external procurement until the 
new plant is completed.

project order received FY2026? 
A10. We continue to expand optical connector s capacity appropriately and in a timely manner, as 
before. The new optical fiber plant in Sakura began operations last year and is currently operating

◆ Sources of Competitiveness and Profitability, and Review of the Mid-Term Plan 
Q11. What are the sources of competitiveness and excess profitability in optical components and 
engineering business for data center? If the advantage is only short lead-time response, it may be 
difficult to maintain superiority. Can know -how such as shortening construction periods be 
maintained going forward? 
In addition, in the Mid-Term Plan, you separated upstream operations, where capacity expansion 
takes time due to constraints, from downstream operations, where capacity can be expanded 
flexibly, and presented a growth scenario for downstream operations. In light of the large project 
order received this time, has there been any change in the thinking behind the Mid-Term Plan? 
A11. Our strengths lie in high value -added products such as SWR ™/WTC™, flame-retardant cables, 
and Air Blown WTC ™, as well as in our technological capabilities that enable us to continue 
developing new products. We believe we can maintain this differentiated advantage. Another 
strength is our ability to provide  optical fiber cables, optical connectors, fusion splicers, and 
engineering business for data center as an integrated solution. In particular, providing engineering 
business together with products creates significant value for customers by shortening construction 
periods. Short lead -time response is also becoming one of our strengths, as customers are 
increasingly prioritizing delivery schedules over prices amid tight procurement conditions. 
At this point, we are not considering a review of the Mid-Term Plan. Since this project is project-
based and some aspects of the future remain uncertain, we would like to take a little more time 
before discussing medium- to long-term future plans. 
In the short term, we will seek top -line growth in optical components, engineering  business for 
data center, and other areas that are less constrained by optical fiber production capacity, while 
supplementing capacity shortages through external procurement, including preforms and optical 
fibers, and in this case cords and cables. Over the medium to long term, we will also focus on 
increasing optical fiber production capacity through the new plant. This new plant is not merely 
a repeat investment to increase capacity; rather, it aims to achieve h igh productivity through 
innovative manufacturing technologies. We are targeting a cost -competitive structure that will 
allow us to compete sufficiently even if the supply -demand environment eases and price 
competition intensifies in the future.
