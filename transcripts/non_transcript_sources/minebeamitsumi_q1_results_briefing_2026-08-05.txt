Key Highlights

                                Reached record highs in 1Q net sales and OP,
                      supported by continued strong demand from AI data centers

                        OP significantly surpassed the previous record high (19.6 bn yen in FY3/22) by 6.7 bn yen,

                            absorbing approximately 2.2 bn yen in special factors (structural reform expenses) in AS.
                        Strong demand for data center products in PT and MLS.
                        Although AS was affected by the slowdown in the automotive market, automotive content growth in

                           bearings and motors remained solid.
                        Five growth areas +1: Updated `+1' from "Aircraft" to "Aerospace" to accelerate business expansion

                           in the growing space industry.

  & Electronics (SE)       Sales of EEPROM* for AI servers expanded, and integration of ICs for fan motors also progressed.

                            Q&A (Summary)
              Investor Meeting for 1Q of FY 3/2027

                        MinebeaMitsumi Inc.

Date & Time:  Wednesday, August 5, 2026, 5:30 p.m. - 6:30 p.m.
Speaker:      Mr. Yoshihisa Kainuma / Representative Director, Chairman, CEO
              Mr. Katsuhiko Yoshida / Director, President, COO & CFO
              Mr. Yusuke Kataoka / Business Officer, Head of Accounting, Finance & Business
              Administration Div. In charge of Investor Relations

*This material has been summarized from the original dialogue.

Q: Regarding ball bearing pricing, Chairman Kainuma mentioned that prices would naturally follow an
    upward trend as a "law of nature." Could you elaborate on the specific nuance behind this? What
    kinds of negotiations are currently taking place with customers, and how do you expect price
    determinations to impact profitability?

A: (Kainuma) Regarding bearing price adjustments, we have not officially announced or negotiated a flat
    percentage price hike across the board. Currently, production capacity is tight across both our
    operations and those of our competitors, resulting in a market-wide supply shortage. Under these
    conditions, customers seeking to expand their business are requesting increased supply volumes from
    us. While we continue to value and maintain relationships with our highest-priority customers who
    have trusted us as their primary vendor over the long term, we have initiated appropriate price
    negotiations at the field level for urgent additional demand. Driven by this tight supply-demand
    environment, we expect average selling prices to gradually rise as a "law of nature."

Q: Regarding the cases where additional demand is occurring, are there specific main applications or
   customer characteristics?

A: (Kainuma) The additional demand is centered primarily on applications and customers where demand
   is currently most robust.

Q: Regarding the approximately 2.0 billion yen in restructuring expenses in the Access Solutions (AS)
    segment, please explain the difference from the approximately 1.7 billion yen for Q1 restructuring
    cost indicated in the previous slide. Also, given that Q1 results for the AS segment fell below initial
    plans, yet full-year forecasts remain unchanged, what is your scenario for recovery going forward?

A: (Yoshida) The factor behind AS segment's Q1 results falling short of initial plans was not the
    restructuring expenses. Restructuring costs were already factored into our guidance. From a business
    standpoint, automotive market conditions in Europe and China proved tougher than initially
    expected. Regarding the approximately 1.7 billion yen previously explained, we had anticipated
    roughly 0.5 billion yen in gain on sale of sites against 2.2 billion yen in restructuring expenses.
    However, because the timing of site sales shifted, the recognition of gain was delayed, resulting in
    recording only the cost side of over 2.0 billion yen in Q1. Nevertheless, the sale of sites is expected to
    be executed during this fiscal year, so on a full-year basis, it remains on track with the initial plan.
    Therefore, this special factor does not alter our full-year outlook.

              1
A: (Kainuma) We maintained our full-year forecast because mass production of the "Winglet" (formerly
    Wing Handle), a new product for the BMW X5, is already ramping up. Profit contributions will begin
    over the next month or two, and as the program expands, both shipment volumes and profits are
    expected to grow toward the second half. In addition, while overall automotive market conditions
    remain challenging, particularly in China and Europe, performance among Japanese OEMs varies by
    manufacturer. On the other hand, the AS segment consists of eight business units; beyond sluggish
    markets in China and Europe, regions like the United States and Asia-Pacific remain resilient.
    Furthermore, beyond four-wheel business, we operate solid businesses in industrial machinery and
    thriving two-wheel business. Thanks to this diversified portfolio across regions and applications that
    mutually offset weakness, we possess high resilience and believe the full-year forecast is fully
    achievable.

Q: Considering that other segments, particularly Precision Technologies (PT), are performing strongly,
    wouldn't it have been appropriate to raise full-year guidance?

A: (Kainuma) In the past, when Q1 performance exceeded expectations, we revised our earnings forecasts
    relatively early. However, we previously experienced market criticism when we raised forecasts early
    in the term but subsequently failed to meet targets. Given the uncertain macroeconomic environment,
    we believe forecast revisions should be evaluated prudently, and out of an extremely conservative
    approach, we decided to keep initial full-year guidance unchanged this time.

Q: Regarding the three sub-segments in the Semiconductor & Electronics (SE) segment (Semiconductors,
    Optical Devices, and Mechanical Components), could you update us on the direction from this term
    into the next, as well as your confidence level and outlook for next term based on Q1 progress?

A: (Yoshida) In the semiconductor business, analog semiconductors are performing steadily, whereas
    power semiconductors are struggling, primarily due to capacity utilization losses at the Shiga Plant.
    However, the Shiga Plant is expected to surpass its break-even point and achieve single-month
    profitability within this fiscal year. Looking into next term, alongside operational improvements, we
    expect sales recovery and expansion, as well as a rebound in power semiconductors. New business
    negotiations are making steady headway, and we expect the overall semiconductor business to recover
    from next fiscal year onward, staying on track toward achieving our medium-term forecast.
    For optical devices and mechanical components, results from various internal profitability
    improvement measures are beginning to materialize. However, regarding plans for next term, we will
    carefully assess market conditions while continuing to incorporate conservative assumptions.

Q: Given that HDD-related components are currently performing very strongly, is there a possibility of
    negotiating price adjustments with customers to pass on rising raw material and component costs?

A: (Kainuma) Our customer contracts include mechanisms to reflect raw material price increases into
    selling prices, and we are implementing price pass-throughs within that framework. While we
    recognize that major HDD customers are performing strongly, we have not raised base prices
    themselves at this point.

Q: With AI demand appearing to intensify further over the past three months, what is your progress
    regarding AI server-related sales against your plan for a CAGR of 22% from FY3/26 to FY3/29? Also,
    the presentation material notes that "inquiries for liquid cooling system-related products are gaining
    momentum." How much earnings contribution do you anticipate from this in the future?

A: (Yoshida) Demand related to air-cooling fans remains strong. Orders for ball bearings and fan motors
    are extremely solid, driving expansion in external bearing sales volumes.
   Meanwhile, for liquid cooling systems, business negotiations for new products such as coolant control
   valves, pump motors, and water leak sensors are advancing in line with customer development
   schedules. Because market adoption for liquid cooling has a time lag compared to air cooling, we have
   not factored it heavily into our current full-year forecast. Negotiations are progressing steadily, and we
   intend to reflect contributions to performance once commercialization timelines become clear.

Q: Is it correct to assume that overall AI server-related performance is progressing ahead of initial plans?

A: (Yoshida) The PT segment and air-cooling fans in particular are running significantly above initial
    targets. As a result, bearing operational profitability has improved substantially, and we recognize
    that progress is moving ahead stronger than anticipated.

Q: Amid changing external environments, various costs such as labor and energy are rising. How are you
    absorbing these cost increases to maintain strong performance? Also, if cost pressures or macro
    conditions worsen further, can we assume that your diversified portfolio and internal measures will
    enable stable performance?

A: (Kainuma)We continuously work on productivity improvements. Specifically, we are advancing labor-
    saving initiatives by globally automating low-value-added processes like material transport, yielding
    steady results. In addition to major initiatives, we continually engage in daily, detailed expense
    reductions.
    Furthermore, at our Cambodia plant, introducing solar panels has achieved 100% carbon neutrality,
    supported by a system that re-invests profits generated from energy savings back into shop-floor
    operations. This framework fosters field-level awareness and motivation toward cost reduction.
    On the other hand, for material price increases, we pass costs on based on customer contracts while
    focusing on expanding sales of high-value-added products. Accumulating these efforts allows us to
    improve operating margins.
    Additionally, customer focus in B2B markets is shifting beyond price toward delivery speed and
    performance. We recognize that the business environment is evolving into one where technical
    capabilities and supply reliability are highly valued, moving away from purely price-centric
    competition.

Q: Regarding the semiconductor business in the SE segment, you mentioned that analog semiconductors
    are performing very strongly while power semiconductors face headwinds. Could you provide specifics
    on their business environments and current conditions? Also, given price revision movements in the
    industry, what is your pricing strategy and outlook for analog and power semiconductors?

A: (Kainuma) Regarding price adjustments in semiconductors, I issued instructions to operational teams
    during our Executive Officers' meeting prior to today's briefing. We believe price adjustments are

                                                                     3
    necessary in semiconductors, and I instructed teams to request price revisions from customers,
    particularly for analog semiconductor products where we possess competitive advantages.
    Meanwhile, regarding factors behind power semiconductors struggling, while it is true that product
    competitiveness needs improvement on our part, the largest driver was the complete cancellation of
    an EV power semiconductor program for a specific customer. Following that impact, our product
    competitiveness is not yet at a level to quickly re-route capacity toward other customers.
    Moving forward, we will accelerate the development of "Fin-SiC," our next-generation power
    semiconductor using SiC, while expanding sales and channels across industrial and consumer
    applications--leveraging power semiconductors for power supply peripherals built on our analog
    semiconductor expertise, beyond EVs.
Q: Regarding optical devices, you explained that the "ramp-up is smoother than expected." Does this
    imply higher volume increases than originally planned, or market share expansion? What is behind
    this strong performance?
A: (Kainuma)At present, we do not perceive major shifts in market share between us and the competitor.
    When I say "smoother than expected," I mean that line switching from older to newer models is
    progressing at a pace exceeding initial plans. Two years ago, we struggled during launch and caused
    customer inconvenience, leading to temporary market share loss; however, no such issues occurred
    this year, resulting in a smooth ramp-up. Going forward, we need to monitor final sales volumes, price
    trends, and market reactions for new models.
