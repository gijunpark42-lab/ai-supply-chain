Kamata [A]: My name is Kamata. Thank you for the question. I’d like to answer the question you asked earlier, 
but I apologize that I’ll have to explain using materials you don’t have in front of you. I believe it’s on slide 34. 
You mentioned earlier the term “scale across.” While data centers are currently connected via fiberoptic links, 
as AI continues to evolve, it has become increasingly difficult for a single data center to provide the necessary 
computing power on its own. 
First, the server itself will be updated , which is called “scale up ,” and then those servers will be grouped 
together to use them, which is called “scale out.” In this context, solutions like “scale across,” which connect 
data centers, are now emerging. 

--- page 15 ---

On slide 36, I’ve included a diagram illustrating where we currently stand in terms of the computing power 
required. 
Generative AI is still in its infancy; we’re just beginning to see inference, and a broad roadmap for AI has 
already been laid out, from agent-based AI and physical AI to, ultimately, AGI and even “super AI.” 
Among these, specifically the areas shown on the right, such as scale up, out, and across, the sections 
highlighted in yellow are the ones that will serve as tailwinds for us. 
The reason for this is that, first of all, we are currently selling a large number of photodiodes for optical 
transceivers. However, in data centers, the closer you get to the computing section, the more electrical 
interconnects remain in use , specifically, at distances below 1 km , it’s still electricity. Our opportunity lies 
when this electricity changes to optical. 
The scale across mentioned earlier is for long-distance connections. Whereas in the past, it was sufficient for 
systems to simply be connected, now, since we’re using them in sync for computational power, it’s no longer 
enough for them to be merely connected; they must be synchronized with low latency. The result is a setup 
where geographically dispersed data centers operate as a single computer. 
Rather than the performance of conventional long -distance optical transceivers, there is now a demand for 
even higher-quality, higher-performance products, and we believe this trend is a major boon for our company. 
As things stand now, we are moving forward with our efforts with a very high degree of confidence regarding 
medium- to long-term market demand.
