Q: Regarding the adoption of cooling fans for rear-door server racks, are your fans 
used by major North American server manufacturers such as Dell and HP? If you 
cannot disclose customer names, could you tell us whether Supermicro is the only 
customer adopting them, or whether you also do business with other major server 
manufacturers? 
A: In line with our policy, we do not disclose customer names. However, we have 
business relationships with multiple major server manufacturers. We also supply 
products for server applications other than rear-door systems, including GPU server 
manufacturers in China and customers in many other countries. 
 
Q: Regarding capital allocation, the 10th Mid- Term Management Plan states that 
cash inflows will include the strategic use of interest -bearing debt. In the 9th Mid-
Term Management Plan, I believe the policy was to repay interest-bearing debt. Has 
your view on the use of interest -bearing debt changed between the previous plan 
and the current one? 
A: Under the 9th Mid -Term Management Plan, we were focused on repaying 
interest-bearing debt. Under the 10th Mid- Term Management Plan, while utilizing 
earnings from our core businesses and funds within the Group, we will also make 
strategic use of interest-bearing debt as needed. 
 
Q: Please share your thoughts on implementing a stock split. 
A: We do not take an extreme position either way —neither ruling out stock splits

entirely nor committing to them proactively. Our policy is to consider them flexibly 
and in a timely manner whenever appropriate. 
 
Q: Regarding orders at the San Ace Company, a strong figure of 13.3 billion yen 
was reported for 4Q FY2025. Does that include orders for cooling fans for rear-door 
systems? Also, as the range and production volume of large fans increase s going 
forward, should we expect orders for cooling fans for rear -door systems to rise as 
well? 
A: Demand related to GPU servers and AI will continue to grow. We are also 
proactively advancing product development for large fans, so we expect operating 
revenue to continue rising steadily. 
 
Q: I attended NVIDIA's conference earlier this year and saw a presentation on 
technology that can handle heat generation at room temperature without the need 
for chillers. How do you think this will affect demand for cooling fans? 
A: At this point, we do not expect any impact. GPU servers inevitably generate heat, 
so cooling remains essential. 
 
Q: Regarding the Motion Company, you explained that the operating margin in 4Q 
FY2025 remained at around 1% due to the impact of higher material costs. However, 
your forecast shows a significant improvement to 8% in the first half of FY2026. 
What measures will enable this improvement? 
A: Material costs have continued to rise since the second half of FY2025. We have 
been passing these increases on through selling prices, but because some orders 
remain in backlog and price negotiations take time, there is a lag before higher costs 
can be fully absorbed. As a result, the operating margin in 4Q FY2025 declined. Our 
plan for the first half of FY2026 already reflects these factors. Since material cost 
inflation may continue, we will keep implementing price revisions as needed. Our 
FY2026 forecast also incorporates price revisions, a shift toward higher-margin new 
products, and increased demand. 
 
Q: You mentioned that for AI servers, the key is how efficiently heat can be reduced 
with as little power as possible. For the large fans and high -static-pressure fans 
currently under development, are there any new technical priorities or design 
changes that are being emphasized compared with conventional server products? 
A: The biggest issue in servers and data centers is power consumption. To address 
--- page 3 ---
this challenge, our top priority is to develop fans that deliver low power consumption 
together with high airflow and high static pressure. We will continue advancing R&D 
to achieve world-leading products. 
 
Q: In particular, are there areas where the level of difficulty is increasing, such as 
aerodynamic design, motor efficiency, or control technologies? 
A: To improve cooling fan performance, both aerodynamic performance and motor 
performance must be enhanced, but there has been no major change in our basic 
approach. We will continue our R&D efforts to achieve further performance 
improvements. 
 
Q: Regarding the profit margins of the San Ace Company and the Electronics 
Company, your FY2026 forecast shows a lower margin for the San Ace Company, 
while the Electronics Company is expected to post a higher margin. Could you 
explain the assumptions behind this? 
A: The assumption behind the lower margin at the San Ace Company is that 
component prices are rising due to market conditions. We plan to pass these costs 
on through selling prices, but depending on timing, we may not be able to fully 
absorb the increases,  and our forecast reflects that risk. The main assumption 
behind the higher margin at the Electronics Company is increased operating revenue. 
We expect stronger semiconductor-related demand for SANMOTION. Because the 
Electronics Company's business has a hi gh marginal profit ratio, higher operating 
revenue will contribute significantly to profit. 
 
Q: At the Electronics Company, are you not planning to implement price increases 
or other measures to improve profit margins? 
A: Because material costs for purchased components are trending upward, we are 
working to reduce procurement costs and pass higher costs on through selling prices, 
taking market conditions into account.
