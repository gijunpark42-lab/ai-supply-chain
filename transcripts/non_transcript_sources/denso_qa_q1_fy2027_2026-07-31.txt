FY2027 Financial Results (2026/4–2027/3) 
Summary of Q&A 
 
Financial results for the FY2027 1st Quarter 
Q: Parts costs deteriorated significantly by ¥39.5 billion. What is driving the increase in parts costs? 
A: The increase is primarily attributable to higher prices for raw materials such as copper, aluminum, and 
platinum, as well as parts including semiconductor memory. While the impact of rising material costs 
had already been incorporated into our initial plan, market prices have increased further than 
anticipated, resulting in additional cost pressure. 
 
Outlook for the FY2027 (2026/4-2027/3) 
Q: Regarding the full-year outlook, there were various changes in the operating environment during 
the first quarter, including the Kumamoto earthquake and foreign exchange fluctuations. Why 
did you revise only revenue upward while leaving the other guidance items unchanged? 
A: In formulating our full -year outlook, we had already planned the financial impact on highly 
uncertain risks such as the geopolitical situation in the Middle East and rising material costs at 
the beginning of the fiscal year. We believe that the recent changes in the operating environment 
can be absorbed within the range of these assumptions, and therefore we have left our operating 
profit forecast unchanged. On the other hand, with the Japanese yen currently trending weaker 
against major currencies, we have reflected the favorable foreign exchange impact realized in 
the first quarter, as well as volume effects, in our revenue forecast.  
 
Q: Is there any impact on tariffs in North America? 
A: We don’t expect a significant difference in the full-year tariff impact between FY2026 and FY2027. 
Since last year, through ongoing discussions with our customers, we have been continuously 
advancing localization initiatives across North America, including Mexico and Canada, to 
minimize tariff exposure. As a result, we expect tariff -related costs to remain within the scope 
of our current plans. 
 
Impact of the Kumamoto earthquake 
Q: What has been the impact and future outlook of the Kumamoto earthquake?  
A: DENSO has three manufacturing sites in Kyushu (MORTEC, DENSO Kyushu, and DENSO Miyazaki). 
Fortunately, there has been no impact on production operations and no injuries to employees, 
and all facilities continue to operate normally. However, some Toyota Group companies have 
facilities located closer to the earthquake's epicenter. In addition, we are currently assessing the 
impact on approximately 30 suppliers. As of July 31, 2026, we have confirmed that 16 suppliers 
have not been affected, 13 suppliers are still under assessment, and 1 supplier has been affected. 
At this stage, we are unable to provide a quantitative assessment of any potential impact on 
production or financial performance. We will continue to closely monitor the situation and 
provide the necessary support to help Toyota Group companies and our suppl iers achieve an 
early recovery.
