Summary of the Q&A Session at the Financial Results Briefing for the  
First Quarter of the Fiscal Year Ending March 2027 
 
 
Date and time: August 7 (Fri.), 2026 10:00-10:40  
 
Notice: This summary is for reference purposes and designed for the convenience of those     
who could not attend the financial results briefing stated above. Please note that this is not a     
word-for-word transcript of all remarks made at the above session but a summary prepared     
by Kurita Water Industries Ltd.   
 
Q.  How does Kurita assess the pace of orders, net sales and business income in 1Q and 
the progress relative to its 1H and full-year forecasts?  
A.  While there were differences in progress between the business segments in 1Q, overall 
results were in line with our assumptions.  
  
Inquiry levels in the Electronics Industry segment remained high on the back of proactive 
customer investments. We were able to win orders that had not been factored into our 
initial forecast, including projects in each of the overseas regions. Relative to our 1H 
forecast for orders, the pace of progress is fast er than expected. Net sales is growing 
steadily, reflecting advances in  construction for previously won facilities' orders. Net 
sales from recurring contract-based services are also running ahead of our assumptions, 
driven by customers' utilization rates. However, as a result of additional costs for a large-
scale facilities project currently under construction, the progress rate for 1Q b usiness 
income relative to 1H forecast was slow. 
     
For the General Industry segment, despite our concerns about the conflict in the Middle 
East the impact  was minimal . Boosted also by forex impact on the back of yen 
depreciation, 1Q progress was better than we had expected. CSV business net sales 
also grew, continuing to contribute to improved profitability. While the situation in the 
Middle East remains highly unpredictable, based on the current operating environment 
it is increasingly likely that the actual negative impact as of 1H will not be as large as we 
had anticipated. 
   
With the General Industry segment making up for the slight lag in the Electronics Industry 
segment, I believe we are tracking in line with plan in terms of progress toward the overall 
1H forecasts. At this point in time, we do not anticipate changes that would necessitate 
a review of our initial assumptions for 2H either; our assessment is that we are making 
solid progress toward our full-year forecasts as well.   
  
Q.  Can you provide more details on conditions for facilities orders in the Electronics Industry 
segment?  
A.  Kurita has won multiple mid - and large -scale orders in China, S outh Korea, North 
America and Europe, mainly from global accounts. We are also expecting to win large-
scale orders in Japan and the US in 2Q. We have already had some order wins in Japan, 
supporting solid progress for 1H. In terms of Kurita's stated strategy of demonstrating

Q.  On the additional costs incurred in the Electronics Industry segment, please elaborate 
on the background, the scale of the costs incurred and whether there is a risk that there 
could continue to be cost overruns.  
A.  The additional costs are related to one specific project in North America. Including 
changes to specifications, we incurred around 2 billion yen in additional costs. This was 
our first project in North America. In addition to some reworking as a result of our 
unfamiliarity with standards and specifications unique to the US, related to the overall 
construction of the project the customer replaced the design and construction company, 
which disrupted the design process.     
 
We are strengthening our response, including setting up a system for support from 
Japan, to minimize the incidence of additional costs . However, we  expect a slight 
lingering impact in 2Q. We have already taken into account the costs we can project and 
expect that the impact will be limited to 1H. While construction will continue in 2H, we 
have stepped up our management of the project with response  measures that are 
already being implemented to ensure that further additional costs are not incurred. 
   
Q.  Progress on facilities orders so far is firm but is there a risk that construction completion 
could be pushed out as a result of shortages in construction workers or materials , or 
longer production lead times? Are you concerned about the possibility that the situation 
could deteriorate going into next fiscal year?  
A.  On the back of high market expectations, order intake is rising at a rapid pace but we 
believe that our current production capacity is sufficient to address the orders that we 
win during the current fiscal year. That said, production capacity remains tight. We are 
stepping up the transformation of the production process through measures such as 
automating the design process. We are also simultaneously moving forward with 
measures to build out our business foundation to support efficient operations of our 
recurring business which we expect will develop into profit pillars in the future.   
  
Q.  If the impact of additional costs is excluded, Kurita made solid progress in improving the 
Electronics Industry segment business profit margin  (BPM) in 1Q. Is this the result of 
contributions from internal efforts, such as the automation of design for water treatment 
facilities?  
A.  The primary reason for the better -than-expected improvement in BPM excluding the 
impact of additional costs is the strength of the recurring contract-based services within 
the Electronics Industry segment. However, we are making progress on a wide variety 
of initiatives to improve profitability including the measures to automate design. Going 
forward, we expect such initiatives will contribute overall to improving margins.

Q.  Why are recurring contract-based services running ahead of your assumptions? Is it a 
reflection of the impact of higher capacity utilization rates at existing customers or is it 
the result of new contract starts?  
A.  The main factor is the high capacity utilization rates at existing customers' plants.  
  
Q.  The faster-than-expected order intake pace for facilities, I think there is a possibility that 
you may experience a higher need for funds given the time lag between the initial need 
for working capital and the actual cash inflow on receivables. You assum e your debt 
capacity for the current and next fiscal year combined  is around 60 billion yen, but is it 
possible to raise funds in excess of this level? Also, would you view non-debt financings, 
including convertible bonds, as a possible choice?   
A.  Based on how we view cash allocation for the next 2 years, as discussed when we 
announced full-year results for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, we are prepared 
to be flexible in considering and undertaking debt financing up to a level where we are 
able to maintain a single A credit rating. With regard to the equity ratio, we would be 
generally comfortable at a level of around 55% during the PSV -27 plan period and 
around 50% in the following medium-term management plan period.   
  
Q.  Will the additional costs incurred in the Electronics Industry segment have an impact on 
either orders you have already received or orders that you are expecting to win?  
A.  The additional costs incurred this time are the result of disruption due to reasons specific 
to an individual customer and also specific to this particular project, given this is the first 
time we are dealing with specifications unique to the US. For this reason we don't expect 
the impact to spill over into other projects. Construction for other projects is proceeding 
smoothly, in line with our assumptions.  
  
Q.  Is it not possible to invoice the customer for the additional costs incurred?  
A.  Going forward, we will thoroughly review the reasons for the additional costs; I would 
like to invoice the customer where possible.   
  
Q.  I am concerned that the inappropriate incident involving your analytics service could 
damage faith in business models for the CSV business or others. What effect has it had?  
A.  With regard to this, I must first sincerely apologize to all our stakeholders for any concern 
and inconvenience this may have caused. We are currently formulating initiatives to 
ensure that this does not happen again. We are in the midst of conducting a rigorous 
investigation and analysis of the facts of the case and its cause, and are reviewing our 
management system, including data recording, management structure, operational  
processes, the division of labor, the approvals process, the review framework and our 
internal audit system.   
 
We are committed to further strengthening our compliance system and implementing 
rigorous measures that will prevent a recurrence. Analysis of waste water, soil and 
industrial waste is an area that we target with the CSV business. We recognize there is 
a risk of damaging our reputation even in areas that are not directly involved in this case. 
As such, we firmly undertake to execute measures to prevent a recurrence and are doing 
our utmost to win back the trust of our stakeholders.

Q.  With regard to the impact of the Middle East conflict, it seems as if there was almost no 
impact when looking at 1Q results. If we look at individual regions, were there areas that 
were impacted? Also, the initial forecast projected a negative impact of 2. 5 billion yen 
for business income in 1H but you did not assume any impact in 2H. Please discuss the 
risks going into 2H that you see at this point, taking into account factors such as 
conditions for passing on higher costs.   
A.  For the General Industry segment, which was the area where we had been most 
concerned about the impact of the Middle East  conflict, there was a direct impact on 
demand in EMEA, reflecting the uncertain situation. On top of this, EMEA was also 
impacted by rising raw materials, transportation and energy costs.  
 
That said, the overall General Industry segment was not only up YoY in 1Q but is also 
running ahead of our assumptions, so we don't feel that the Middle East situation is 
having a negative impact on our earnings. The backdrop to this is that while procurement 
costs increased on the back of higher raw materials and packaging costs and logistics 
expenses in Japan, our largest market for water treatment chemicals, we were early in 
engaging with our customers. We are extremely grateful that a high proportion of  our 
customers were understanding of the situation, which allowed us to smoothly pass 
higher costs through to our customers.  
 
As a result, as of 1Q, there was almost no deterioration in the cost-of-sales ratio for this 
business. At the same time, the situation in the Middle East is highly volatile. Given 
concerns that the conflict could be prolonged or could deteriorate, we remain focused 
on continuing to monitor the impact on earnings going forward, including 2H. We will 
also consider additional measures as necessary.  
  
Q.  You have indicated that the pace of order intake in 1Q was faster than expected. Why 
didn't you revise up your order forecast this time?  
A.  Order intake progress in 1Q exceeded our assumptions . In some regions, progress 
overshot our 1H forecast. However, the trend for orders in the Electronics Industry 
segment can be highly variable. We would like to monitor trends in 2Q before reviewing 
our full-year forecast.
