Summary of Q&A 

Questioner 1 

Q:  Regarding  2Q  operating profit in the Energy business, could you provide more 

details on the cost increase and profit impact caused by operational losses at the 

Jeannette  plant in the U.S., as mentioned on page 22 of the presentation materials? 

What specific costs were incurred? 

Hosoda :

As we continue to operate and maintain manufacturing facilities in the U.S., we have been 

making ongoing capital investments based on the view that modernization and automation 

are essential over the medium to l ong term to enhance productivity and reduce reliance on 

manual operations . Due to differences between labor environments in the U.S. and Japan, 

recovering from disruptions through manual effort is more difficult in the U.S., which 

resulted in several loss -related costs. The impact in  1H  amounted to a little over JPY 1 

billion. However, we have largely resolved these issues during  1H  and  therefore do not 

expect any significant negative impact in  2H. 

Miyaki :

The direct causes of the operational and additional costs were very basic machining errors 

and process management mistakes. Behind these issues were large -scale equipment 

replacements and personnel reallocations associated with the automation and labor -sav ing 

initiatives currently underway. While these factors were incorporated into our initial plan, 

our response proved insufficient. Equipment replacement and automation projects under E -

Plan 2028 are being completed step by step. Although significant losses  were incurred in 

1H , we now have a clear path toward resolution. In  3Q and  4Q , we will focus on ensuring 

that we achieve the operating profit targets we have presented.  The JPY 3 billion 

downward revision to our full -year operating profit forecast reflects a combination of 

operational losses at the U.S. plant and the impact of the Middle East situation. 

Q:  Of the JPY 3 billion downward revision to full -year operating profit, is it correct to 

assume that roughly two -thirds, or about JPY 2 billion, is attributable to operational 

losses and approximately JPY 1 billion to additional Middle East -related impacts? 

Hosoda :

Yes, that understanding is correct.

Q:  Regarding order trends in the Precision Machinery business, there appeared to be 

notable movements in both equipment and components orders between  1Q  and  2Q .

Could you discuss changes by region, customer type, and memory versus logic 

customers, as well as your outlook for  2H  and next fiscal year? 

Hosoda :

Equipment orders increased rapidly i n 1Q  and moderated somewhat in  2Q . Part of the  2Q 

demand was brought forward into  1Q , but overall order levels remain very strong. 

Component orders, while less visible due to their smaller scale, have increased for four 

consecutive quarters. Components typically benefit from market expansion with some 

delay compared to equipment, and we b elieve we are steadily capturing that growth.  We 

expect order levels in  2H  to remain at least as strong as in  1H , which is why we revised our 

full -year forecast upward. Based on this order growth, our priority is to ensure steady 

production execution. 

Nambu :

As with our peers, we saw substantial demand from customers during  1H . In addition to 

logic customers, where we have traditionally been strong, memory customers have also 

placed very robust orders. 

Equipment orders have increased more sharply than component orders because 

customers have tended to place orders earlier to secure production slots as investment 

appetite rises. We also expect component orders to remain on an upward trajectory. 

For  2H , we continue to expect strong demand, as reflected in our revised full -year orders 

target of JPY 550 billion. We are also gaining confidence that next year could be a very 

strong year, although we will continue to update our outlook based on market condit ions.

Questioner 4 

Q:  Regarding Revenue in the Precision Machinery business, is there potential for 

further upside in the  2H ? Also, how do you assess your outlook compared with WFE 

market growth expectations of +18% and peers forecasting growth in the high -30% 

range? 

Hosoda :

The forecast we presented represents what we believe is the most probable  revenue 

outcome at this stage. While market expansion and order growth are clearly evident, lead 

times have also lengthened. Accordingly, we have reflected only the portion we believe can 

realistically be recognized as  revenue during the current fiscal year .

Nambu: 

Equipment products require a certain delivery lead time, which means that most of the 

orders received during  1H  will contribute to  revenue in the next fiscal year. While we 

continue working to shorten lead times, we believe our current forecast is the most 

appropriate.  In addition, while our WFE market growth assumption is +18%, our own 

planned  revenue growth rate is +24.2%, which we believe compares favorably with the 

market. 

Questioner 5 

Q:  What are the current lead times for equipment and components? Also, why was 

the upward revision to operating profit limited to JPY 3.5 billion despite a JPY 25 

billion upward revision to full -year  revenue? 

Nambu :

Lead times vary depending on specifications and order  conditions but  currently range from 

approximately six to twelve months. For equipment, customers have increasingly placed 

orders earlier to secure production capacity. Component lead times are generally shorter 

than equipment lead times, but customer factory utilization  rates remain very high. 

Regarding profitability, a significant portion of the  revenue increase comes from 

components, which generally have lower margins than equipment. In addition, project mix 

affected profitability. We will continue making efforts to improve the operating profit ratio, 

but JPY 3.5 billion is currently our most real istic estimate for the increase in operating 

profit. 4 / 7

Q:  According to page 28 of the presentation materials, the Middle East situation had 

a negative impact of JPY 1.6 billion in  1H, with some recovery expected in  2H  and a 

full -year impact of negative JPY 1 billion. Given that the situation in the Middle East 

has not fully stabilized, could you discuss the impact on the Energy business, the 

likelihood of achieving  2H plan, and your outlook for  2H orders? 

Hosoda :

While uncertainty in the Middle East remains, customers are adapting to the situation, and 

activities such as the acceptance of parts for the Service & Support (S&S) business, which 

had previously been postponed, are beginning to resume. As a result, we ex pect gradual 

revenue recognition in  2H .

Orders are improving both in quantity and quality, including profitability at the time of order 

intake. Although the timing of large orders can create fluctuations between halves, the 

medium -term outlook remains very favorable. 

Miyaki: 

The JPY 1.6 billion negative impact in  1H  was primarily due to products that had already 

been ordered but could not be shipped because of customer -side logistics constraints. 

These  are expected to be recognized in  2H .

The reason we forecast a full -year impact of negative JPY 1 billion is that our original plan 

assumed emergency inspection and service work would occur in  2H , as in a typical year. 

However, given the current uncertainty, we have conservatively  assumed that such upside 

will be less likely. 

More broadly, order trends differ between the Products business and the Service & Support 

business. In the Products business, several large projects originally expected in  2H  were 

brought forward into  1H  at customers' request, resu lting in a  1H  weighting. In the S&S 

business, particularly parts -related operations, orders were also stronger than expected in 

1H  as customers accelerated purchases to secure inventory amid market uncertainty. 

However, because maintenance plans and parts demand are generally based on long -term 

inspection cycles, we expect  2H  order trends to remain within our original full -year 

assumptions. 5 / 7

Q:  In the Environmental Solutions business, the operating profit ratio has remained 

around 13%, supported partly by contingency reversals. Can this be considered a 

sustainable profit level? 

Hosoda :

Given the nature of construction projects, contingency reversals occur on an ongoing basis 

when actual project costs come in below assumptions. Improved profitability in life -

extension projects has also contributed. However, further analysis is needed befo re 

concluding that an operating profit ratio of around 13% can be maintained consistently. 

Ota: 

Compared with last year, contingency reversals contributed less to profit growth, while 

expansion in life -extension and maintenance businesses provided a stronger earnings 

boost. Since profitability is influenced by project mix, we are cautious about assum ing that 

a 13% operating profit ratio can be maintained on a continuous basis. We will continue 

implementing initiatives to improve profitability across the business.

It is true that we are tracking ahead of schedule from a  revenue perspective, but we remain

Regarding production capacity, the expansion of the Kumamoto plant completed last year 

provides sufficient capacity for the foreseeable future. Nevertheless, we are continuously 

evaluating additional capital investments ahead of future market growth. 6 / 7

Questioner  10 

Q:  Regarding the new chiller product capable of operating at minus 60 degrees 

Celsius, is this intended as a replacement for existing products, or is it an entirely 

new product category? Also, what are its competitive advantages? 

Hosoda :

This new chiller is a new market entry for us. It leverages  EBARA ’s  thermal management 

and pump technologies to provide precise temperature control in ultra -low -temperature 

applications such as cryogenic etching. 

Nagata: 

Traditional etching processes typically operate at temperatures around minus 30 degrees 

Celsius. However, demand for even lower temperatures is increasing to enable more 

precise etching processes. 

Against this backdrop, we launched a new product capable of operating at minus 60 

degrees Celsius. This temperature range is also a relatively new frontier for competitors, 

and many companies are currently developing products in this area. 

The product's greatest differentiator is its energy efficiency. Compared with conventional 

competing products, power consumption can be reduced by 30% to 50%, providing a 

significant competitive advantage.
