First, please turn to page four. 
In Q1, both net sales and operating profit reached record highs. 
Amid issues such as the Middle East crisis, currency depreciation in emerging markets, additional US tariffs, 
and soaring raw material prices —all of which caused cost increases to exceed initial projections —we drove 
growth in high -value-added products, imp lemented pricing strategies, and pursued comprehensive cost 
reductions. As a result, operating profit in absolute terms increased YoY, including the impact of the weak yen 
on foreign exchange conversions and refunds from US tariffs. This exceeded the Company's internal target. 
On the other hand, the operating profit margin decreased by 0.8 percentage points compared to the previous 
year. We were unable to fully offset the impact of cost increases —which exceeded our initial projections as 
mentioned earlier—within the three months of Q1 alone. In addition, during Q1 of the previous fiscal year, 
the effects of pricing strategies and cost -cutting measures took precedence over cost increases caused by 
soaring raw material prices and US tariffs, resulting in higher profit levels. These are the main factors behind 
the decline in profit margins this period. However, given that the effects of this fiscal year’s cost -reduction 
measures will begin to materialize in earnest starting in Q2, we assess that progress as of Q1 was generally in 
line with our plans. 
The negative impact of rising costs is expected to grow even further in the future. However, throughout the 
year, while we will continue to steadfastly implement pricing strategies and cost -cutting measures, we will 
also rapidly roll out additional measure s in light of changes in the business environment, with a focus on

Takahashi [A]: First, regarding our assessment of the Q1 financial results, as I mentioned at the outset, we
