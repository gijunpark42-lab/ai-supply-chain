Question & Answer 
 
Q1. Could you comment on order trends excluding the JPY 5 billion quantum computing order, as well as 
order conditions in July and August and the medical equipment business? 
A1. Third-quarter order intake totaled JPY 83.0 billion, including a large order of approximately JPY 5.0 billion 
related to quantum computing. Excluding this order, order intake was largely in line with the second quarter, 
indicating that demand remained solid. While we do not disclose monthly order figures, we have not observed 
any significant changes in recent months, and overall business conditions remain stable. Orders in industrial 
applications continue to be strong, while demand in medical and life scien ce applications has remained 
generally flat over the past year, reflecting a stable order environment. 
 
Q2. What was the impact of depreciation expenses associated with new Building No. 5  at opto -
semiconductor factory, and what increase in depreciation expenses should be expected next fiscal year? 
A2. Building No. 5 began operations in June, resulting in approximately JPY 0.5 billion of depreciation expenses 
during the third quarter. In addition to depreciation, various start-up and operating costs were incurred during 
the quarter. In the fourth quarter , the primary impact will be depreciation expenses, which are expected to 
total approximately JPY 1.5 billion. The commencement of operations has proceeded according to plan. 
Regarding the increase in depreciation expenses next fiscal year, we continue  to expect a level close to the 
previously indicated increase of approximately JPY 5.0 billion from this fiscal year, although the final amount 
may be somewhat lower. We plan to provide further details together with our next fiscal year's guidance. 
 
Q3. Have there been any changes in products or demand trends for wafer inspection, failure analysis 
systems, and X-ray non-destructive inspection equipment in the semiconductor market? 
A3. There have been no significant changes in the components we supply for wafer inspection applications. 
However, as advanced semiconductor manufacturing processes become increasingly complex, the number of 
inspection steps continues to rise, driving demand for our customers' inspection equipment. In failure analysis 
systems, large-scale deployment in mass production remains at an early stage, with current demand primarily 
related to the analysis of leading-edge devices such as HBM. For X-ray non-destructive inspection, orders have 
continued to increase, supported by growing demand for more detailed inspection of circuit boards, 
particularly for AI server applications. 
 
Q4. Could you comment on the size of AI data center-related demand within the non-destructive inspection 
market? 
A4. As we mainly supply components , we do not have full visibility into end -use applications. However, we 
believe that investment related to AI data centers has been a major driver behind demand growth over the 
past two years. In particular, increased inspection demand for circuit boards used in AI server has contributed 
significantly to growth in non-destructive inspection-related applications. 
 
 
 
 
Q5. Is demand related to electric vehicles (EVs) recovering? 
A5. Demand for battery inspection systems used in EV applications has been increasing. However, demand 
has not yet returned to the peak levels observed during the strongest period of EV market expansion, and we 
view the market as still being in a recovery phase. 
 
Q6. What factors have driven the improvement in gross profit margin, and what is the outlook going 
forward? 
A6. Gross profit margin improved from 47.8% in the previous fiscal year to above 50% in the second quarter 
of the current fiscal year. Since the third quarter, however, depreciation expenses associated with newly 
commissioned facilities have become a factor w eighing on margins. At the same time, higher sales volumes 
have improved operating efficiency and capacity utilization, contributing positively to both gross profit margin 
and operating margin. We will provide further details regarding the outlook in our medium-term management 
plan scheduled for release in November. 
 
Q7. How is the profitability improvement of Hamamatsu Photonics A/S (formerly NKT Photonics  A/S) 
progressing? 
A7. We have been pursuing a plan to improve operating profit before goodwill amortization to break -even 
within three years, and progress remains on track. Our target has been to improve profitability by 
approximately JPY 1.0 billion annually. This fiscal year, however, the increase in quantum computing-related 
R&D expenses is expected to reduce the amount of improvement somewhat. Nevertheless, the underlying 
business continues to improve steadily, and we aim to achieve an operating profit improvement of more than 
JPY 1.0 billion next fiscal year. 
 
Q8. What caused the decline in profitability in the Imaging and Measurement Instrument segment business 
compared with the forecast announced in May and the losses in the Laser segment business? 
A8. In the Imaging and Measurement Instruments business, shipments of high-margin failure analysis systems 
were delayed, affecting both sales and profitability. In addition, higher material costs and changes in product 
mix negatively impacted margins. In the Laser business, increased R&D e xpenses related to quantum 
technologies, together with foreign exchange effects, contributed to the loss. 
 
Q9. Why is profit growth expected to be limited in the fourth quarter despite higher sales? 
A9. Our forecast includes approximately JPY 1.0 billion in inventory disposal costs. Furthermore, we have 
adopted conservative assumptions for operating expenses. As a result, profit growth is expected to be more 
modest than sales growth in the fourth quarter. 
 
Q10. Why was the gross profit margin forecast revised from 48.1% in the previous forecast to 47.9% in the 
current forecast ? 
 
 
 
? 
A10. The revision primarily reflects inventory disposal costs and more conservative assumptions regarding 
expenses. On the other hand, the impact of price revisions has developed as planned, and shipments from 
April onward have been contributing positively to profitability. 
 
Q11. Have customers placed advance orders in semiconductor-related markets? 
A11. Based on our ongoing discussions with customers, we have not observed the type of inventory -driven 
advance purchasing seen during the COVID-19 period. We believe current demand is primarily supported by 
underlying end-market demand. 
 
Q12. Could you comment on the photodetector business for optical transceiver applications? 
A12. Annual sales related to optical communications are approximately JPY 2.0 billion and currently account 
for a relatively small portion of our total sales. We supply high -speed photodiodes to optical transceiver 
manufacturers. As AI data center investments c ontinue to expand, the optical communications market is 
growing, and we are also developing products for next-generation, higher-speed communication systems. 
 
Q13. What is the Company's strategy for the optical communications market? 
A13. We recognize the strong growth potential of the optical communications market. Leveraging our 
strengths in photodetection technologies, we aim to expand business opportunities in this field and further 
strengthen our presence in optical communications-related applications. 
 
Q14. What factors contributed to the larger loss at Hamamatsu Photonics A/S? 
A14. The primary factor was increased R&D investment in quantum technology-related projects. In addition, 
the business includes a significant number of project-based orders, which can result in substantial fluctuations 
in quarterly sales and profitability. 
 
Q15. What is the purpose of the name change to Hamamatsu Photonics A/S from NKT Photonics A/S? 
A15. The name change is intended to reinforce the sense of unity within the Hamamatsu Photonics Group. 
We are integrating sales activities and leveraging Hamamatsu Photonics' global sales network to expand 
market opportunities. In addition, we expect to generat e technological synergies by combining the former 
NKT Photonics' expertise in light generation with Hamamatsu Photonics' strengths in photodetection 
technologies, while also promoting closer collaboration among engineers. 
 
Q16. How do you view neutral-atom quantum computing within the broader quantum computing market? 
 
 
 
A16. We believe interest in neutral -atom quantum computing is increasing across the industry. However, it 
remains unclear which technology platform will ultimately emerge as the dominant approach. Accordingly, 
we are supporting not only neutral -atom systems but  also photonic and ion -trap quantum computing 
platforms. 
 
Q17. Which quantum computing approach is most compatible with Hamamatsu Photonics' technologies? 
A17. While we have high expectations for the neutral-atom approach, our laser products are also widely used 
in ion-trap quantum computing systems. Therefore, rather than focusing on a single technology platform, we 
intend to support multiple quantum computing a rchitectures and pursue a broad range of business 
opportunities.
