Hitoshi Saito (hereinafter “Saito”): I am Hitoshi Saito, President and CEO. I would like to present our 
financial results for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026 (FY2026/3). 
 
Our Group’s three business segments are the Electronics business (Electronics), the Medical and 
Pharmaceuticals business (Medical and Pharmaceuticals), and the ICT and Sustainability business (ICT&S). 
 
  

==== page 3 ====
 
 
 
This slide presents topics for the fiscal year ended March 2026 (FY2026/3). Net sales in the Electronics 
business increased 17% year on year (YoY). This was due to higher sales volumes, particularly for materials 
for semiconductor package (PKG) substrates and for rigid boards. 
 
In materials for PKG substrates, positive factors included dramatic volume growth in dry film (DF) products 
for memory applications under higher demand backed by the proliferation of AI. 
 
In materials for rigid boards, sales volume increased mainly in automotive and smartphone-related 
materials. At the same time, although we have achieved considerable sales of white DF products in display-
related materials for Mini LEDs, demand declined due to fluctuations in demand for end products, resulting 
in a YoY decrease in sales volume. 
 
Looking at foreign exchange rate effects, the yen appreciated by 1.6 yen YoY from 152.5 yen to the U.S. 
dollar, reaching 150.9 yen to the dollar. 
 
Net sales in the Medical and Pharmaceuticals business grew 16% YoY . This was primarily due to an increase 
in contract volume in the contract manufacturing business. 

==== page 4 ====
 
 
In the contract manufacturing business, full-scale contract manufacturing for new customers has started 
while contract volume from existing customers also increased. 
 
In the manufacturing and marketing business, sales volume increased due to higher demand that resulted 
from supply shortages of competing drugs with equivalent efficacy as well as National Health Insurance 
(NHI) drug price revisions that raised the prices of some products. On the other hand, as a negative factor, 
the elective care scheme for long-listed products that began in October 2024 caused a decline in the sales 
volume of affected items. 
 
We expressed our opinion in support of the planned commencement of the tender offer for our shares by 
KJ005 Co., Ltd., an entity owned by a fund under investment firm KKR, and neutral position regarding 
tendering of shares.

Q&A: Impacts of the Middle East situation, and the status of procurement 
 
Questioner: You mentioned that you have not incorporated impacts of the conflict involving Iran into the 
earnings forecast at present due to the difficulty of quantitative analysis. I have heard that in the 
Electronics business, some manufacturers are moving up their procurement schedules. What is the 
situation at your company? 
 
Saito: At present, there is no particular notable movement in that direction. To maintain a stable and fair 
supply system, we are securing capacity and, when changes occur in supply conditions, undertake 
necessary checks at that time. 
 
Sayaka Tomioka (hereinafter “Tomioka”): I am Sayaka Tomioka, Managing Executive Officer and CFO. To 
add to what was said, we recognize that demand has been very high even before any impacts from the 
conflict involving Iran, and that, with the start of the war, demand is more likely to remain high than to 
further strengthen. 
 
Questioner: Regarding the procurement of raw materials, I believe the Electronics business is being 
affected by resins, pigments, and solvents, and the Medical and Pharmaceuticals business by raw materials 
and packaging materials. I would like to hear your thoughts on quantitative terms, even if numbers are 
difficult to present, about whether concerns are arising over the company’s current procurement, and the 
state of the company's current inventory. 
 
Saito: Regarding impacts of the situation in the Middle East, concerns over resins and solvents are 
appearing in some areas. However, we are taking action early and are currently in the stage of evaluating 
substitutes. 
 
Other sub-materials including plastic containers and films are also experiencing impacts, but we are 
addressing these through inventory buildup and through substitutes. We have also sent letters to 
customers informing them that supply concerns may arise between June and July. 
 
We are seeing a path appear with regard to substitutes, and are now evaluating changes in containers, an 
area that will require careful handling. 
 
Although some sub-materials and chemical products are also used in the Medical and Pharmaceuticals 
business, we will engage in inventory buildup and do not foresee major problems in the near term.

Q&A: Net sales in the Electronics business for the fiscal year ended March 2027 
 
Questioner: My question concerns your thoughts on net sales in the Electronics business for the fiscal year

Also, regarding the display sector, you mentioned that you expect a decline in demand for white DF

The sales volume of materials for PKG substrates for memory applications is increasing under AI-related 
demand. Materials for rigid boards are also growing considerably. In the latter, the same products are used 
in a variety of applications, including automotive and AI server applications, which makes it difficult to 
ascertain the actual degree of growth.

As we have not disclosed how much the increase will be in products for memory applications, you can 
assume that the increase in sales of DF products for PKG substrates will be largely due to AI-related 
demand. 
 
The decrease in white DF products stemmed from lower demand for finished products. 
 
Questioner: Does that mean that white DF products will not fall to zero and will continue to some extent in 
the future? 
 
Tomioka: That is correct. We have been told that current models of the final products still use Mini LEDs,

Q&A: Status of and changes to procurement of materials 
 
Questioner: With regard to materials, you mentioned buildup of inventory. Should we understand that

Q&A: Approaches to price pass-through, for each business 
 
Questioner: You mentioned that procurement costs are rising and that inflation is having an impact in the 
Medical and Pharmaceuticals business. I understand that the current rise in costs for direct materials and 
sub-materials will put pressure on margins to some extent unless these higher costs are passed through to 
selling prices. 
 
I believe that in line with rising procurement costs and inflation, salary increases will also take place in the 
future, so labor and electricity costs will also go up. Regarding your approach to price pass-through, it 
sounded to me that the situation differs between the Electronics business and the Medical and 
Pharmaceuticals business. I would like to hear more details for each business. 
 
Saito: Regarding the Electronics business, I mentioned earlier that we have sent letters to customers 
regarding supply. In the letter, we clearly state that we intend to pass these higher costs on through prices, 
that is, that we intend to raise the prices of our products. 
 
Tomioka: Regarding the Medical and Pharmaceuticals business, as you know, Taiyo Pharma is not able to 
raise prices because drug prices are fixed. Regarding the amount by which costs have increased, the 
procedure is that, at the time of annual NHI drug price revisions, we submit applications to raise the prices 
of products that have become unprofitable. 
 
Regarding contract manufacturing, the approach depends on the magnitude of the price increases, but if 
the price increases are large, it is normal practice to negotiate to reflect those increases in the contract 
manufacturing unit price. 
 
Questioner: As you noted, because prices are fixed, Taiyo Pharma has to unilaterally accept cost increases, 
which I think is unavoidable. 
 
As for Taiyo Pharma Tech, I believe that under normal business practice, conditions such as  the number of 
lots, delivery dates, and overall volume are agreed upon before transactions begin. 
 
Tomioka: That is correct. As the business does not have a great number of customers and products are tied 
to customers in the form of "this product is for this customer,” the structure is fairly easy to understand. 
 
In comparison to the Electronics business, however, in the Medical and Pharmaceuticals business drug 
prices are fixed for manufacturers and distributors, which makes price pass-through more difficult, and the 
situation depends on the product. 
 
Questioner: So, the position is opposite that of Taiyo Pharma, isn’t it.

Q&A: NHI Drug price revisions 
 
Questioner: You mentioned that drug prices increased for some products in the manufacturing and 
marketing business of the Medical and Pharmaceuticals business in the fiscal years ended March 2026 and 
March 2027. Can you provide some background for this? 
 
Also, while some products have increased in price, I believe there are still some products that are 
decreasing in price. Please explain the impact on the manufacturing and marketing business overall. 
 
Tomioka: First, in our case, there are some products for which the drug price is already at the minimum NHI 
price level. For these products, the drug prices will not fall any further. There is also a certification system 
for basic pharmaceuticals, that is, pharmaceuticals considered so vital that drug price supports exist to 
ensure stable supply. Drug prices for these are maintained as long as certain criteria are met. 
 
In NHI drug price revisions, there is also a mechanism for the recalculation of prices for unprofitable 
products. This allows applications for drug price increases to be submitted to the Ministry of Health, Labour 
and Welfare when a product is unprofitable or not profitable enough. 
 
 

==== page 34 ====
 
 
In principle, drug prices are revised every two years, but off-year revisions have also been made in recent 
years, and recalculation of unprofitable products is carried out every year. Submission of applications 
begins in the summer, and a product’s price increases if its application passes review. In our case, the NHI 
drug price revisions of April 2026 have a positive impact on the manufacturing and marketing business 
overall, resulting in an average increase in the low single-digit percentage range. 
 
Questioner: So, although there are some products for which drug prices will decrease, overall, drug prices 
are expected to increase for many products, with an average price increase in the low single-digit 
percentage range. Is that understanding correct? 
 
Tomioka: Yes. Drug prices were changed as of April. Although there has not been a noticeable effect, the 
overall prices are increasing. 
 
Questioner: However, the effect of cost increases will be greater, leading to a decrease in profit. Is that 
correct? 
 
Tomioka: That is correct. It should also be noted that, in pharmaceuticals, taking immediate action is 
difficult. For example, in the Electronics business, we can flexibly respond to rising costs of raw materials by 
raising our unit prices accordingly. However, NHI drug price revisions can only be made on April 1, so there 
is no choice but to wait until that time. Taking speedy action is difficult.
